> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Xie et al.** (Johns Hopkins University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27848) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✗
>
> Measures how much word-level pitch and energy contour, and utterance-level timing, carry over between professionally dubbed source and target speech in three European language pairs, as design input for expressive speech-to-speech translation.

## Problem

Expressive speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) and cross-lingual prosody transfer systems assume that some prosodic structure in the source speech should survive translation. How far prosody actually corresponds across languages is poorly measured. Prior studies of dubbing and cross-lingual prosody work at the utterance level (speaking rate, timing, aggregate statistics), while emphasis and emotional cues often sit at the word level. The paper asks whether, where and how strongly fine-grained prosody is preserved when professional voice actors dub speech into another language. The authors state that the paper is accepted to EMNLP 2026 Findings; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Method

The study builds no synthesis or translation model. It is an analysis pipeline over human speech:

- **Data.** Parallel utterance pairs from professionally dubbed entertainment television (mostly multi-character dialogue), mined with the SpeechVecAlign pipeline for DE-EN, EN-ES and EN-FR. From 100,000 raw pairs per language pair, filtering keeps 15,957 to 16,939 pairs (about 36 to 38 hours per pair). Filters are SQUIM objective quality (SI-SDR ≥ 7.5, STOI ≥ 0.91, PESQ ≥ 1.25), pyannote diarization (single speaker on both sides), SONAR cross-lingual semantic similarity (cosine ≥ 0.63), and removal of pairs with no valid non-constant pitch contour (Tables 1 to 3). Thresholds are the 75th percentile on a 100-pair German-English pilot and are reused for all pairs.
- **Words and alignment.** whisper_timestamped gives word-level timestamps. FastAlign with intersection symmetrization, augmented with OPUS TED2013, aligns words across languages. Intersection is chosen for its precision (82.2%) over grow-diag, which has the lowest alignment error rate (37.4% vs. 39.6%) (Table 7).
- **Prosody representation.** Frame-level F0 comes from pYAAPT with linear interpolation over unvoiced regions, and frame-level energy from librosa. Aligned words are grouped into clusters (one-to-one up to many-to-many), each cluster's contour is resampled to 20 frames, and clusters are reordered into semantically corresponding order before concatenation. Each utterance contour is divided by its mean to remove absolute speaker level.
- **Measures.** Per-utterance Spearman rank correlation between source and target contours, compared with a shuffled baseline that permutes the target contour within the utterance. A leave-one-group-out analysis removes all frames of one English POS class (nouns, verbs, modifiers, function words) and defines Δ = r_full − r_without, so a positive Δ means the class raises the measured correspondence. A coarse temporal analysis correlates utterance duration and phoneme count (phonemizer) between source and target.

## Key Results

**Contour correlation (Table 4).** All six correlations are positive and far above the shuffled baseline (paired Wilcoxon, p < 10⁻⁸⁰), but moderate in size:

| Feature | DE-EN | EN-ES | EN-FR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitch, mean r | 0.229 | 0.241 | 0.246 |
| Pitch, shuffled | 0.007 | 0.005 | 0.010 |
| Energy, mean r | 0.183 | 0.174 | 0.176 |
| Energy, shuffled | 0.043 | 0.030 | 0.026 |

Pitch correlation exceeds energy correlation in every pair. The mean paired difference is +0.046 (DE-EN), +0.067 (EN-ES) and +0.070 (EN-FR). Pitch is higher than energy in 57%, 60% and 60% of utterances (Table 9). Differences between language pairs are partly statistically significant but negligible in effect size (|Cliff's δ| ≤ 0.031, Table 8). DE-EN has slightly lower pitch and slightly higher energy correlation than the other two pairs.

**POS-class ablation (Tables 5, 11 to 13).** Δ values per class (Docling split the pitch rows of Table 5; values here come from the full statistics in Table 11, which match the legible cells):

| Feature | Class | DE-EN | EN-ES | EN-FR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pitch | Nouns | +0.0228 | +0.0208 | +0.0203 |
| Pitch | Verbs | +0.0034 | +0.0156 | +0.0045 |
| Pitch | Modifiers | +0.0083 | +0.0057 | +0.0055 |
| Pitch | Function | −0.0065 | +0.0108 | +0.0132 |
| Energy | Nouns | +0.0355 | +0.0396 | +0.0402 |
| Energy | Verbs | −0.0041 | −0.0064 | −0.0128 |
| Energy | Modifiers | +0.0027 | +0.0062 | +0.0052 |
| Energy | Function | −0.0324 | −0.0203 | −0.0204 |

Nouns have the largest positive Δ in every feature and pair. Removing function words raises energy correlation in all three pairs (negative Δ), while for pitch the function-word effect is negative for DE-EN and positive for EN-ES and EN-FR. The noun minus function contrast is significant for all energy analyses and for DE-EN and EN-ES pitch; EN-FR pitch is marginal (0.0056, 95% CI [−0.001, +0.012]) (Table 13). Median per-utterance Δ is 0 for most pitch cells, and per-utterance standard deviations (about 0.14 to 0.27) are large relative to the means (Table 11).

**Temporal features (Table 6).** Duration correlations are 0.891 (DE-EN), 0.890 (EN-ES) and 0.884 (EN-FR). Phoneme-count correlations are 0.885, 0.875 and 0.871. Duration is slightly higher than phoneme count in every pair. Dubbing has explicit timing constraints, so these high values are expected.

**Robustness (Appendix D).** Mean pitch correlation changes by at most 0.0008 across 10 to 40 frames per word (Table 14, 3,000-pair subsets). On the EN-ES subset of Table 15, raw (uninterpolated) F0 contours give lower correlation than interpolated contours at every resolution (for example 0.1671 vs. 0.2315 at 20 frames per word).

All comparisons are against a shuffled baseline and across language pairs. No S2ST system output is analysed, so the results describe human dubbing, not machine translation quality.

## Novelty Assessment

The pipeline combines established tools: SQUIM, pyannote, SONAR, whisper_timestamped, FastAlign, pYAAPT and Spearman correlation. The new element is the measurement design: alignment-aware reordering of word-cluster contours, per-word resampling and a leave-one-POS-group-out ablation, applied at scale (about 49,000 filtered pairs) to professional dubbing. The statistical reporting is careful (episode-clustered bootstrap intervals, effect sizes, Holm correction, resolution and interpolation checks). The scope is narrow: three closely related European pairs, one corpus that cannot be released, and only pitch, energy and coarse timing. The main finding (weak but reliably positive contour correspondence, strong timing correspondence) is a quantification of an intuition rather than a surprise.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper gives concrete reference values for how much word-level pitch and energy correspondence a human dubbing target keeps relative to its source, which an expressive S2ST or cross-lingual prosody transfer system could be compared against. It also shows where that correspondence is concentrated (noun regions) and where it is unstable (function words for energy), which is direct design input for word-level prosody transfer. Its value is as a measurement baseline; it does not test any generation system.

## Claims

- **supports:** Fine-grained pitch and energy contours keep a weak but systematic correspondence across languages in expressive human translation, so cross-lingual prosody transfer has a real signal to exploit.
  > *Evidence:* Mean utterance-level Spearman correlation between reordered source and dubbed target contours is 0.229 to 0.246 for pitch and 0.174 to 0.183 for energy across DE-EN, EN-ES and EN-FR, against shuffled baselines of 0.005 to 0.043 (paired Wilcoxon p < 10⁻⁸⁰). *(§4.1, Table 4, Figure 3)*
- **refines:** Cross-lingual pitch correspondence is stronger than energy correspondence, while the language pair matters little among closely related European languages.
  > *Evidence:* Pitch exceeds energy by +0.046 to +0.070 mean paired difference in all three pairs, with bootstrap intervals excluding zero; pairwise language-pair differences have |Cliff's δ| ≤ 0.031. *(§Appendix B, Tables 8 and 9)*
- **refines:** Cross-lingual prosodic correspondence is concentrated on content words, especially nouns, while function words carry little transferable energy pattern.
  > *Evidence:* Removing noun frames lowers correlation in every feature and pair (Δ +0.0203 to +0.0228 pitch, +0.0355 to +0.0402 energy); removing function-word frames raises energy correlation (Δ −0.0203 to −0.0324); the noun minus function contrast is significant for all energy analyses but only marginal for EN-FR pitch. *(§4.2, Tables 5, 11, 13)*
- **complicates:** Utterance-level timing is preserved across translation far more strongly than fine-grained contour shape, so duration agreement alone overstates how much prosody a translation keeps.
  > *Evidence:* Source-target duration correlations are 0.884 to 0.891 and phoneme-count correlations 0.871 to 0.885, against contour correlations below 0.25 for the same pairs. *(§4.3, Table 6; §4.1, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Measured cross-lingual pitch correspondence depends on how unvoiced regions are handled, because languages differ in voiced and unvoiced distributions.
  > *Evidence:* On the EN-ES subset, raw F0 contours give correlations of about 0.167 against about 0.232 for interpolated contours at every frames-per-word setting. *(§Appendix D, Table 15)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The analysis is limited to human professional dubbing in three European language pairs from one corpus that cannot be publicly released. It measures no S2ST system output, so it gives reference values for expressive translation without showing how any system compares to them.

- Dubbing only approximates prosody transfer: voice actors change timing, emphasis and intonation for lip synchronization and fluency, and source and target are different speakers.
- Only pitch, energy and coarse timing are analysed; pauses, rhythm and voice quality are left out.
- POS classes come from English words only, and automatic transcription and word alignment (82.2% precision on a German-English benchmark) add noise to the contour pairing.
- Typologically distant and tonal languages are not covered.
- The authors plan to release the analysis pipeline; neither code nor data is available at the time of this version.
