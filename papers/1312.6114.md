## Context in Speech Generation

The paper targets directed models with continuous latent variables whose posterior is intractable, and introduces the Stochastic Gradient Variational Bayes (SGVB) estimator: by writing a latent sample as a deterministic function of the input and auxiliary noise (for a Gaussian, z = mu + sigma * epsilon), the Monte Carlo estimate of the variational lower bound becomes differentiable with respect to the encoder parameters (§2.3, §2.4). Applied with a neural probabilistic encoder q(z|x) and decoder p(x|z), a centered isotropic Gaussian prior, and an analytic KL term, this gives the variational auto-encoder, whose objective is a reconstruction log-likelihood plus a KL regularizer on the latent code (§3, Appendix B). The experiments are on images only (MNIST and Frey Face), where AEVB optimizes the lower bound faster than the wake-sleep algorithm across latent dimensionalities (Figure 2). For speech generation, the paper provides the evidence-lower-bound training recipe and posterior-encoder/prior/decoder decomposition that end-to-end TTS, voice conversion and discrete-latent autoencoders can build on, and the encoder output can serve as a latent representation of the input.
