> [!abstract] Interspeech · 2023 · Conference
> **Yuma Koizumi et al.** (Google / Tokyo University of Agriculture & Technology) · [→ Paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/koizumi23_interspeech.html) · [→ Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18802) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✗
>
> Releases LibriTTS-R, a sample-for-sample restored version of the 585-hour, 2,456-speaker LibriTTS corpus, and shows that a multi-speaker TTS model trained on it rises from roughly 2.5-3.4 MOS to roughly 3.7-4.3 MOS with no change to the model.

## Problem

Large studio-quality TTS corpora (100+ hours) exist inside companies but are not public, so results trained on them are hard to reproduce. The largest public multi-speaker TTS corpus, LibriTTS, comes from LibriVox audiobook recordings and carries downsampling, environmental noise, reverberation and non-linear enhancement artefacts. The paper cites earlier evidence that TTS trained on LibriTTS falls well short of its own ground truth (Glow-TTS: 3.45 MOS against 4.22 for vocoded ground truth, compared with a much smaller gap on LJSpeech) *(§2)*. The question is whether modern speech restoration can turn this existing public corpus into usable high-quality TTS training data.

## Method

The paper applies an existing text-informed speech restoration model, Miipher (authors' prior work), to every LibriTTS utterance while keeping the text, speaker labels and splits unchanged. Miipher is a parametric re-synthesis model. It downsamples the input to 16 kHz and extracts w2v-BERT features (frozen). A DF-Conformer feature cleaner then predicts the w2v-BERT features of clean speech, conditioned on PnG-BERT features from the transcript and a speaker embedding computed from the original 24 kHz waveform. A WaveFit-5 neural vocoder synthesizes the restored 24 kHz waveform *(§3.1, §3.3)*. Utterances shorter than 2 seconds are repeated before the speaker embedding is computed. The authors chose Miipher because its text conditioning and self-supervised input features target two degradations common in LibriTTS: phonemes masked by noise or reverberation, and phonemes partly deleted by down-sampling or non-linear processing *(§3.1)*.

The authors train their own Miipher instance on a proprietary set of 2,680 hours of noisy/clean pairs. The clean side is 670 hours of studio-recorded English in five accents at 24 kHz. The noisy side mixes in TAU Urban scenes and internal noise at 5-30 dB SNR, with optional simulated reverberation (image-method RIRs) and codec artefacts (MP3, Vorbis, A-law, AMR-WB, OPUS) *(§3.2)*. The feature cleaner is pre-trained for 150k steps and WaveFit for 1M steps on clean features; WaveFit is then fine-tuned for 350k steps on cleaned features.

To test the corpus as training data, the paper trains one fixed multi-speaker TTS system on LibriTTS and on LibriTTS-R with identical hyperparameters: a duration-unsupervised Non-Attentive Tacotron with a fine-grained VAE (NAT-FVAE) as acoustic model and a WaveRNN vocoder, cascaded without joint fine-tuning. Both corpora are used at two sizes: Train-460 (train-clean-100 + train-clean-360) and Train-960 (adding train-other-500) *(§4.2.1)*. The TTS system is a measurement probe for the data, not a proposed model.

## Key Results

Human ratings use paid native US English listeners on headphones, with each stimulus rated by one listener and over 100 listeners per condition. Tests are 5-point MOS on naturalness and 7-point side-by-side (SxS) preference on sound quality *(§4.1.1)*.

- **Ground-truth quality** *(Table 1)*: naturalness MOS rises from 4.36 to 4.41 on test-clean and from 3.94 to 4.09 on test-other, but the paper reports neither difference as significant. SxS sound-quality preference is significantly in favour of LibriTTS-R on both splits (+0.80 ± 0.15 test-clean, +1.42 ± 0.14 test-other).
- **Content and speaker preservation** *(§4.1.2)*: WER changes from 3.4 to 3.2 (test-clean) and stays at 5.1 (test-other). Same-speaker cosine similarity between different utterances drops slightly, from 0.784 to 0.762 (test-clean) and 0.755 to 0.745 (test-other). Both stay far above the 0.302 different-speaker level. The paper reads this as speaker identity preserved.
- **TTS trained on the corpus** *(Table 2)*: with LibriTTS, MOS across the six evaluation speakers ranges 2.49-3.40 (Train-460) and 2.59-3.35 (Train-960). With LibriTTS-R it ranges 3.67-4.11 (Train-460) and 4.06-4.31 (Train-960). LibriTTS-R Train-960 gives the highest MOS for five of six speakers (103: 4.31, 1841: 4.20, 204: 4.11, 1121: 4.23, 5717: 4.08). For speaker 19, Train-460 is higher (4.11 against 4.06), a difference the paper calls not significant.
- **More data helps only after restoration** *(Table 2)*: adding train-other-500 raises MOS for five of six speakers on LibriTTS-R (speaker 19: 4.11 to 4.06, not significant). On LibriTTS it lowers MOS for three speakers (103: 2.94 to 2.75, 1841: 3.40 to 3.35, 204: 2.88 to 2.74) and raises it slightly for the other three.
- **SxS on TTS output** *(Table 3)*: listeners prefer the LibriTTS-R-trained model strongly for speakers 19 (+2.38 Train-460, +2.51 Train-960) and 204 (+1.84, +2.20). SxS was run only for these two speakers.

The paper's statement that LibriTTS-R Train-960 TTS reaches "on par with" ground-truth naturalness compares Table 2 against Table 1 (LibriTTS ground truth, 4.36 on test-clean). These are separate tests on different stimuli and speaker sets, not one paired comparison. The baseline comparison itself is fair: same architecture, hyperparameters, steps and test sentences, with only the training audio changed.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is the corpus and the evidence that it works as training data. The restoration model is the authors' earlier Miipher, retrained on in-house data. The TTS models (NAT-FVAE, WaveRNN) are existing architectures used unchanged. Applying restoration to a whole public TTS corpus and releasing the result under the original licence is the new step. The controlled A/B design (same model, same recipe, original against restored audio, two data sizes) cleanly isolates the effect of audio quality. Evaluation is purely subjective plus two automatic sanity checks (WER, speaker similarity). There is no objective quality metric and no test with a modern zero-shot or codec-based TTS model.

## Field Significance

> [!tip]
> High: LibriTTS-R provides a public, permissively licensed 585-hour multi-speaker English TTS corpus with restored audio, aligned one-to-one with LibriTTS so earlier splits and transcripts carry over. It shows that dataset-level speech restoration can close much of the quality gap between public found data and studio recordings for TTS training. It also shows that noisier subsets become useful extra data once restored.

## Claims

- **supports:** Training-data audio quality is a primary limit on multi-speaker TTS naturalness, and improving it with a fixed architecture yields large naturalness gains.
  > *Evidence:* The same NAT-FVAE + WaveRNN system rises from 2.49-3.40 MOS when trained on LibriTTS to 3.67-4.31 MOS when trained on restored LibriTTS-R across six speakers, and SxS preference favours the restored-data model by +1.84 to +2.51. *(§4.2.2, Table 2, Table 3)*
- **supports:** Generative speech restoration applied to found speech corpora can produce TTS training data that improves sound quality while preserving transcript content and speaker identity.
  > *Evidence:* Restored ground truth is significantly preferred in SxS (+0.80 test-clean, +1.42 test-other), WER is unchanged or lower (3.4 to 3.2, 5.1 to 5.1), and same-speaker similarity stays far above the different-speaker level (0.762/0.745 against 0.302). *(§4.1.2, Table 1)*
- **refines:** Adding more but noisier found speech to TTS training helps only when the added data is clean enough; otherwise it can reduce output naturalness.
  > *Evidence:* Adding train-other-500 lowers MOS for three of six speakers when training on LibriTTS, but raises MOS for all six when training on restored LibriTTS-R (for example speaker 103: 2.94 to 2.75 on LibriTTS against 4.09 to 4.31 on LibriTTS-R). *(§4.2.2, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** Naturalness MOS on real recordings is insensitive to recording-quality differences that preference tests and downstream TTS training expose.
  > *Evidence:* Ground-truth MOS differences between LibriTTS and LibriTTS-R are not significant (4.36 against 4.41, 3.94 against 4.09), while SxS sound-quality preference is significant on both splits and the TTS MOS gap reaches about 1.5 points. *(§4.1.2, Table 1, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** Restoration by re-synthesis introduces its own failure cases and can slightly shift speaker characteristics.
  > *Evidence:* Two of 640 evaluated restored samples were distorted by restoration failure, the training splits were not checked by hand, and same-speaker cosine similarity drops slightly after restoration (0.784 to 0.762 test-clean, 0.755 to 0.745 test-other). *(§4.1.2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The restoration model is trained on a proprietary 2,680-hour paired dataset, and the paper does not report a release of the restoration model or code. So the corpus is reproducible as a download, but the processing pipeline cannot be rerun or applied to other corpora from this paper alone.

- Restoration failures are present but uncounted: 2 of 640 evaluated test samples were distorted, and the training splits were not inspected *(§4.1.2)*. The TTS results suggest the residual failures have little effect, but the paper gives no rate for the full corpus.
- Speaker similarity falls slightly after restoration. The paper argues identity is preserved from same-speaker against different-speaker similarity, but does not test whether fine speaker traits (recording-environment cues, voice quality) are lost.
- The TTS evaluation covers one architecture family (NAT-FVAE + WaveRNN) and six training-set speakers on test-clean sentences. It does not show how restored data affects zero-shot cloning, codec-based or large-scale TTS models.
- The "on par with ground truth" conclusion compares separate listening tests, and SxS on TTS output covers only two speakers.
- No objective quality metric (for example DNSMOS or PESQ-style measures) is reported alongside the subjective results.
