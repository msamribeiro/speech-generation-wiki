> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Zeyang Song et al.** (National University of Singapore / Tencent / LIGHTSPEED / Nanyang Technological University / The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen / Shenzhen Loop Area Institute) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28970) · Demo: ? · Code: ✓
>
> LoopTTS turns single-pass TTS into an offline closed loop: an AudioLLM Judge diagnoses word-level prosodic defects (missing stress, missing pauses, wrong global style) in a base system's output and writes a structured instruction, and a fine-tuned instruction-following Refiner re-synthesizes the flagged utterance, conditioned on the original audio, so that listeners prefer it over raw output and over untargeted re-generation.

## Problem

Deployed TTS pipelines are open-loop: an utterance is either accepted or regenerated from scratch with a new seed. Stochastic autoregressive and flow-based decoding still produces sporadic local prosodic defects, such as stress on a function word, a missing pause or flattened intonation, and utterance-level metrics (WER, UTMOS, speaker similarity) barely register them. Re-generation is untargeted and can reproduce the defect or change other attributes. Speech editing systems such as VoiceCraft and SpeechX operate on content (word replacement or insertion) and expose no control like "add emphasis to word X". AudioLLM judges used for preference alignment collapse their diagnosis into a scalar reward, discarding the word-level information a corrective model would need.

## Method

LoopTTS is a three-stage offline quality-assurance cascade around a base TTS model (CosyVoice 2 in all experiments). Stage 1 (Filter) accepts an utterance only if Whisper-large-v3-turbo WER is at most 3.0% and UTMOS exceeds 3.0; failing utterances get up to two re-generations with new seeds and are dropped if they still fail. Stage 2 (Judge) asks an AudioLLM (Gemini-3-pro in the main system) to score prosodic naturalness and emotional fidelity from 1 to 10. Utterances scoring 5 or more are accepted. Lower-scoring ones receive a strict-JSON diagnosis: global style targets (emotion, speed and pitch from fixed enums), 3 to 5 word-level local edits typed as `stress` or `pause`, and a one-sentence natural-language refine instruction. Stage 3 (Refiner) re-synthesizes the flagged utterance from the instruction, the initial audio and the target text, and its output can be fed back to the Judge for further rounds. The authors stress that this is guided expressive re-synthesis, not waveform-local editing: unrequested regions and the speaker embedding can change.

![Figure 1: Overview of the LOOPTTS pipeline. Stage 1 (Filter): coarse-grained metrics (WER, UTMOS) discard catastrophically degraded outputs and trigger full re-generation. Stage 2 (Judge): an AudioLLM evaluates prosody naturalness and emotional fidelity, producing an overall score (1-10) along with structured refine instructions that specify both global attributes (emotion, speed, pitch) and local operations (stress, pause). Utterances meeting the threshold are accepted as final output. Stage 3 (Refiner): the Refiner takes the initial utterance together with the refine instructions and performs guided expressive re-synthesis, producing corrected speech with optional iterative refinement for residual defects.](assets/2608.28970/figure-1.png)

The Refiner's training data, REFINER-DB, comes from contrastive AudioLLM annotation. Each expressive recording from ESD, RAVDESS, SAVEE, MESS, EmoVoice-DB or LibriTTS is paired with a neutral zero-shot clone of the same text and speaker generated by CosyVoice 2 (kept only when WER is 0 and UTMOS exceeds 3). Gemini-3-pro then lists the 3 to 5 most salient word-level stress and pause differences between the two. These local edits are merged with dataset-level emotion, speed and pitch metadata (from VccmDataset where available) into an instruction, giving about 42K tuples of (neutral audio, instruction, expressive target, text). The neutral clone plays the role that the defective initial utterance plays at inference. The authors estimate the annotation cost at about $907 of Gemini API usage.

The Refiner initializes from EmoVoice-1.5B, a codec language model (Qwen2.5-1.5B backbone) that autoregressively predicts 50Hz CosyVoice semantic tokens, which a flow-matching decoder and HiFi-GAN turn into waveforms. The input sequence is (instruction, initial utterance tokens, target text), with the instruction first so that it conditions everything under causal attention. All LLM parameters are fine-tuned with the encoder frozen. The paper describes no training of the flow-matching decoder or vocoder, so these appear to be inherited from EmoVoice unchanged. Training uses two losses:

- **Position-weighted cross-entropy.** Whisper word timestamps map each instructed stress word and pause position to codec-token spans. Tokens in stress spans get weight 1 + α (α = 0.2) and tokens in pause spans get 1 + β (β = 0.3), against a baseline weight of 1. This targets the 10 to 30 of 200+ tokens that carry the edit.
- **Position-aware structural alignment.** For each stress span (5 to 20 tokens), a representational similarity matrix of cosine similarities between positions is built from the model's hidden states and from the target code embeddings. The loss is the size-normalized squared Frobenius distance between the two, weighted by λ = 0.1. Pause spans are excluded because they are mostly silence tokens.

![Figure 3: Refiner architecture and position-aware training objective. The Refiner takes a refine instruction I, the initial utterance y init, and the target text T as input. Two losses are applied in training: a position-weighted CE loss L CE that amplifies gradients at stressed and pause positions, and a structural alignment loss L struct that aligns span-level consistency.](assets/2608.28970/figure-3.png)

Training uses AdamW at learning rate 5 × 10⁻⁶ for 300K steps with batch size 8. Inference uses greedy decoding with at most 256 new tokens.

## Key Results

**Judge selection (§5, Tables 1 and 2).** Five AudioLLMs diagnose 100 TTS utterances (50 Regular, 50 Emotional), and six non-technical listeners rate how well each diagnosis matches their own judgment on a 1 to 4 scale. Gemini-3-pro aligns best on both subsets (3.3 Regular, 3.4 Emotional), ahead of Gemini-3-flash (3.1/3.2), Qwen3-Omni Thinking (2.9/3.0), Qwen3-Omni (2.6/2.7) and Kimi-Audio (2.2/2.5). Against professional stress and pause labels on 200 neutral-expressive pairs, Gemini-3-pro also has the highest micro recall and F1 on all four columns (stress recall 0.884, F1 0.673; pause recall 0.697, F1 0.532). The authors note that pause F1 stays moderate and treat the labels as weak supervision, not ground truth.

**Pipeline recovery (§6.2, Table 3, Figure 4).** On 100 Stage 2 flagged utterances per condition, rated by ten professional listeners:

| System | Neutral WER / SIM / MOS | Emotional WER / SIM / MOS |
|---|---|---|
| Before refinement | 2.04 / 0.73 / 3.21 | 2.11 / 0.69 / 3.01 |
| CosyVoice 2 re-generation | 1.95 / 0.71 / 3.45 | 2.03 / 0.70 / 3.15 |
| EmoVoice re-generation | 1.83 / 0.68 / 3.90 | 1.72 / 0.65 / 3.85 |
| LoopTTS, Global-only | 1.87 / 0.68 / 3.96 | 1.82 / 0.66 / 3.72 |
| LoopTTS, 1 round | 1.86 / 0.68 / 4.17 | 1.79 / 0.67 / 4.01 |
| LoopTTS-Q (Qwen3-Omni Thinking Judge), 1 round | 1.90 / 0.67 / 4.09 | 1.84 / 0.66 / 3.98 |
| LoopTTS, 2 rounds | 1.85 / 0.66 / 4.16 | 1.80 / 0.64 / 4.17 |
| Human-written instruction | 1.79 / 0.67 / 4.20 | 1.78 / 0.67 / 4.26 |

One LoopTTS round lifts MOS by 0.96 (Neutral) and 1.00 (Emotional) over the raw flagged audio and beats the stronger re-generation baseline, EmoVoice, by 0.27 and 0.16. The Emotional margin over EmoVoice lies within the overlapping 95% intervals (4.01 ± .14 vs. 3.85 ± .12). Dropping the word-level stress and pause cues (Global-only) costs 0.21 and 0.29 MOS at similar WER and SIM, which is the paper's main evidence that local instructions matter beyond global style. Human-written instructions remain the soft upper bound on both conditions, and a second round helps only in the Emotional condition. In blind A/B tests over 30 samples per comparison, listeners prefer LoopTTS over raw flagged audio in 85.67% of judgments, over CosyVoice 2 re-generation in 77.00% and over EmoVoice re-generation in 72.3%. Swapping in the open Qwen3-Omni Thinking Judge at inference (the Refiner is still trained on Gemini labels) keeps MOS at 4.09/3.98 and a 79.67% preference over raw audio. SIM against the neutral speaker reference falls relative to the raw audio (0.73 to 0.68 Neutral, 0.69 to 0.67 Emotional) and falls again after a second round (0.66/0.64). A separate iteration-budget run (Table 12) shows SIM declining from 0.66/0.65 at one round to 0.60/0.58 at three, with no WER gain.

**Instruction following (§6.3, Table 4).** Given the same instruction and text (the Refiner also gets the initial audio), the Refiner has the highest MOS-I on pitch (4.01 vs. 3.78 for CosyVoice2-marker), speed (4.13 vs. 3.76 for EmoVoice), stress (4.57 vs. 4.32 for CosyVoice2-marker) and pause (4.28 vs. 3.88 for CosyVoice2-marker). Unmodified EmoVoice stays ahead on emotion MOS-I (4.13 vs. 4.04), overall MOS (4.32 vs. 4.21), WER (2.81 vs. 2.90) and UTMOS (3.86 vs. 3.60). CosyVoice 2 with free-form instructions often reads the instruction aloud, so its WER is not reported. Inter-rater reliability (ICC(2,k), Table 13) for the Refiner's ratings ranges from 0.677 to 0.886, in the range the authors call good or excellent; some baseline dimensions fall below it (EmoVoice MOS 0.496, CosyVoice 2 stress 0.496).

**Ablations (§6.4, Table 5; Appendix D.1, Table 9).** Removing the structural alignment loss drops average MOS-I from 4.21 to 4.01. Removing position weighting drops it to 4.13, and removing both drops it to 3.75. WER is slightly lower without the structural loss (2.78 vs. 2.90), and the authors call the evidence modest. Removing the initial-utterance input barely moves MOS (4.21 to 4.17) but lowers SIM from 0.68 to 0.62 and average MOS-I from 4.21 to 3.94.

**Full distribution (Appendix A, Tables 6 and 7).** On 3,000 emotional synthesis requests, the full cascade has the lowest WER (2.73, vs. 2.81 for EmoVoice re-generation) and highest UTMOS (3.93, vs. 3.82 for Judge-reranked best-of-5) of the seven systems compared. Its SIM is 0.73 against 0.74 for Stage 1 alone, CosyVoice 2 re-generation and best-of-5. Only 237 utterances (7.90%) are flagged by the Judge, and 103 (3.43%) remain flagged after one refinement round. These full-distribution numbers are objective only; human ratings cover the flagged subset.

## Novelty Assessment

The new parts are the training objective and the inference procedure. Position-weighted cross-entropy is a simple reweighting. The span-level RSM alignment loss, borrowed from relational knowledge distillation, is the more distinctive piece and gives the largest single ablation drop. The Judge-to-instruction-to-Refiner loop goes further than prior AudioLLM-judge work, which used judgments as scalar rewards or reranking scores: here the diagnosis is an executable word-level instruction. The contrastive annotation recipe (expressive recording vs. neutral zero-shot clone, AudioLLM lists the differences) is a cheap way to get weak word-level prosody supervision. Its labels inherit Gemini's moderate pause F1.

The components themselves (EmoVoice, CosyVoice 2, Whisper, UTMOS, a commercial AudioLLM) are existing systems. The Refiner is a fine-tuned EmoVoice, not a new architecture. The evaluation is careful about its own scope: budget-matched re-generation baselines, a Global-only control, a human-instruction upper bound, an open-judge swap and a best-of-5 reranking comparison. However, the human MOS covers only flagged utterances, and some key margins sit within confidence intervals.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper shows that an AudioLLM's prosodic diagnosis can be turned into an executable correction signal for a separate TTS model, and that targeted re-synthesis recovers flagged utterances better than budget-matched blind retries. It also provides a reusable recipe for weak word-level stress and pause supervision. The evidence is limited to English, to one base system and to a small human-rated failure subset.

## Claims

- **supports:** Word-level prosodic instructions (where to stress, where to pause) give measurable naturalness gains in corrective re-synthesis beyond global style controls alone.
  > *Evidence:* With the same Refiner, removing the Judge's word-level stress and pause cues while keeping emotion, speed and pitch lowers MOS on flagged utterances from 4.17 to 3.96 (Neutral) and from 4.01 to 3.72 (Emotional) at comparable WER and SIM. *(§6.2, Table 3)*
- **supports:** Diagnosis-guided re-synthesis recovers prosodically defective TTS outputs more reliably than untargeted re-generation under the same correction budget.
  > *Evidence:* On Judge-flagged CosyVoice 2 utterances, one LoopTTS round reaches MOS 4.17/4.01 vs. 3.45/3.15 for CosyVoice 2 re-generation and 3.90/3.85 for EmoVoice re-generation, and listeners prefer it in 72.3% to 85.67% of blind A/B judgments. The Emotional margin over EmoVoice falls within overlapping 95% intervals. *(§6.2, Table 3, Figure 4)*
- **supports:** Upweighting and relationally aligning the sparse token spans named in an instruction improves a codec language model's execution of local prosodic edits.
  > *Evidence:* Removing the span-level RSM structural alignment loss, position weighting, or both lowers average MOS-I from 4.21 to 4.01, 4.13 and 3.75. The fine-tuned Refiner reaches stress and pause MOS-I of 4.57 and 4.28 vs. 3.83 and 2.92 for its unmodified EmoVoice backbone. *(§6.3–6.4, Tables 4 and 5)*
- **complicates:** AudioLLMs can serve as scalable sources of weak prosodic labels and diagnoses, but their agreement with human prosody judgments is incomplete, especially for pauses.
  > *Evidence:* The best AudioLLM (Gemini-3-pro) reaches stress F1 0.673 and pause F1 0.532 against professional labels and a mean diagnosis-alignment rating of 3.3 to 3.4 out of 4. Open models score lower: Kimi-Audio and Qwen3-Omni have pause F1 of 0.316 to 0.33. *(§5.1–5.2, Tables 1 and 2)*
- **complicates:** Prosodic re-synthesis that strengthens expressiveness trades away speaker similarity to the original reference, and the loss accumulates over repeated refinement rounds.
  > *Evidence:* SIM against the neutral reference drops from 0.73/0.69 for raw flagged audio to 0.68/0.67 after one round and 0.66/0.64 after two. In a separate iteration-budget run, SIM falls from 0.66/0.65 at one round to 0.60/0.58 at three while WER stays flat. The authors argue that an expressive-reference SIM (0.77 for the Refiner vs. 0.75 for EmoVoice) shows the drop is mostly intended emotional variation. *(§6.2, Table 3; Appendix D.3, D.5, Tables 12 and 14)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> Human MOS and preference results cover only Judge-flagged utterances (100 per condition, 30 per A/B comparison), and that subset is selected by the same Stage 2 AudioLLM Judge whose diagnosis guides the refinement. Full-distribution evidence (3,000 utterances) is objective-only, so the gain a listener would notice across all outputs, where only about 8% are flagged, is not measured.

- The Refiner's training labels come entirely from Gemini-3-pro, a proprietary endpoint the authors note can change. LoopTTS-Q swaps in an open Judge only at inference.
- Experiments cover English read and emotional speech from a limited set of corpora and speakers, with CosyVoice 2 as the only base TTS system. Tonal languages are named as future work.
- Stress and pause are binary labels, so stress intensity and pause duration cannot be controlled.
- Because the Refiner re-synthesizes the whole utterance, unrequested regions can change. The counterfactual test (40 utterances, Tables 10 and 11) finds the wrong-position edit executed in 10% (stress) and 28% (pause) of cases, while the correct cue dropped from the instruction survives in 34% and 52%. The authors say this test does not measure false-edit rates at natural positions.
- Several margins are small relative to the reported 95% intervals, and ICC reliability falls below the authors' "good" range on some MOS-I dimensions for baseline systems.
- The pipeline is offline: each flagged utterance costs one AudioLLM call plus one Refiner generation per round, so it does not apply to streaming synthesis.
- The paper's comment states acceptance at the EMNLP 2026 main conference (authors' statement). This page summarizes the arXiv v1 preprint.
