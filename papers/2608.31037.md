> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Park et al.** (The University of Tokyo / Keio University / University of California, Berkeley) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31037) · Demo: ? · Code: ?
>
> Across 13 neural audio codecs, three corpora and three acoustic conditions, the Zipf, Heaps and entropy statistics of codec tokens are shaped by quantizer topology and noise, not by corpus. Token-level noise signatures (collapse, explosion) are family-specific rather than universal.

## Problem

Earlier work by the same group (ASRU 2025, not in the corpus) reported that 3-gram neural audio codec (NAC) tokens follow Zipf and Heaps laws, and that Zipfian adherence correlates weakly (|r| < 0.3) with resynthesis quality. That analysis covered six RVQ codecs, a single clean corpus and one fixed sample size. It did not test estimation reliability, cross-corpus generalization, noise robustness, or whether the conventions (first RVQ level, 3-gram order, deduplication) carry over to single-codebook and non-VQ tokenizers. No prior study had characterized how the distributional structure of codec tokens changes under noise, even though such statistics can be computed without decoding audio.

## Method

The study is a fully crossed analysis grid with no model training: 13 released codecs × 3 corpora × 3 conditions = 117 cells. Codecs are grouped into three quantizer meta-categories (Table I):

- **Multi-codebook RVQ (R):** SpeechTokenizer, AcademiCodec, AudioDec, EnCodec, FunCodec, DAC-24k (1,024 entries per level) and Mimi (2,048 entries, hybrid RVQ with semantic objective).
- **Single-codebook VQ (S):** BigCodec (8,192), WavTokenizer (4,096), XCodec2 (65,536).
- **Non-VQ (N):** SQCodec (scalar quantization), FocalCodec (focal modulation, 8,192), and S3Tokenizer (semantic-only, 4,096, no decoder).

Corpora are matched at about 10 hours each: LJSpeech (single speaker), VoxCeleb (multi-speaker) and TrIJEK (one speaker reading Japanese, Korean and English). Noisy variants add Gaussian white noise or DEMAND environmental noise (five environments drawn at random) at 0 dB SNR, generated once with a fixed seed so every codec sees identical waveforms. Each codec runs in its default released configuration.

Tokens are extracted through the Codec-SUPERB framework. For RVQ codecs only the first level is analyzed; consecutive duplicate tokens are collapsed; and n-grams are formed with a family-conditional order (n = 3 for classic RVQ and S3Tokenizer, n = 2 for Mimi, BigCodec and WavTokenizer, n = 1 for XCodec2, SQCodec and FocalCodec). The metric panel is the Zipf frequency-distribution exponent α (MLE via the `powerlaw` package) with KS goodness of fit, the Heaps exponent β and intercept K, unigram entropy H, redundancy R and token perplexity 2^H, codebook occupancy, and clean-to-noise Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD). Two fit safeguards apply throughout: refitting over α ∈ [1.01, 8] when the default bound truncates at 3, and treating a fit as undefined when the type-token ratio exceeds 0.9.

The analysis adds three layers. Chunk analysis (5K to 500K tokens) quantifies finite-sample stability against a matched 500K-token reference. A three-way Type-II ANOVA over meta-category, corpus and condition partitions variance, backed by a linear mixed model with a codec random intercept. Sequence-level degradation signatures are defined from pre-deduplication streams: collapse is Δr > 0.01 with ΔH_trans < -0.1 (more repetition, lower transition entropy), explosion is the reverse. Resynthesis quality for the 12 decodable codecs is measured by UTMOS, MCD (WORLD, DTW, level-matched), F0 RMSE and median absolute error (Praat), differential CER from Whisper-large-v3 transcripts, and unit error rate (UER) between clean and noisy token sequences.

## Key Results

**Estimation reliability (§V-A, Figure 2).** KS converges fastest: its median deviation falls from 0.55 of the cross-cell spread at 5K tokens to 0.07 at 200K. β and α remain at 0.17 and K at 0.19 at 200K, and α at 5K tokens deviates by more than the full cross-cell spread (1.06). The paper therefore compares all cells at matched 500K-token samples.

**n-gram order is family-dependent (§V-B, Tables III and IV).** KS-optimal orders are 3 or 4 for most RVQ codecs (DAC-24k: 2) and 1 to 2 for single-codebook codecs, whose higher-order inventories hit the V/N validity limit. The adopted order matches the corpus-mean optimum for 8 of 13 codecs. At the common unigram order the meta-category effect on α is not significant (η² = 0.05, p = 0.37); at n = 2 it is large (η² = 0.63) but only 25 of 39 cells remain valid. The first RVQ level fits better than later levels (KS 0.012 vs 0.049 to 0.110), and deduplication raises α and β with a medium effect (Cohen's d ≈ 0.5) without changing KS or K (Table V).

**Clean-speech landscape (§VI-A, Table VI).** Under the adopted orders, RVQ codecs span α ≈ 2.0 to 2.7, with Mimi (1.97) and SpeechTokenizer (2.01) lowest. Waveform-reconstructing single-codebook and non-VQ codecs sit at 2.8 or above, and WavTokenizer is highest at 4.48 (one valid corpus only). FocalCodec has the lowest Heaps exponent (β = 0.27), reflecting near-complete use of its codebook. Cross-family α comparisons use different n-gram orders, and the paper itself treats them as descriptive.

**Entropy (§VI-B, Table VII).** Unigram entropy tracks vocabulary scale, from 6.3 bits (AcademiCodec) to 16.3 bits (SQCodec); redundancy is low for every codec (at most 0.237, AudioDec), far below the ≈0.5 estimate for printed English. White noise removes 1 to 4.4 bits for every codec except S3Tokenizer and SQCodec (changes under 0.5 bits), with the largest losses for FunCodec (-4.40) and AudioDec (-4.35); grid-mean redundancy rises from 0.08 to 0.27. DEMAND noise changes entropy little (-1.54 to +0.45 bits).

**Variance decomposition (§VI-D, Table VIII).** Corpus identity explains at most 0.5% of variance for any metric (η² ≤ 0.005). Acoustic condition is the largest main effect for α (0.278 vs 0.134 for meta-category), β (0.120 vs 0.086) and R (0.351 vs 0.320). Meta-category leads for KS (0.158), K (0.240) and above all H (0.551). Residuals stay large (0.241 to 0.745). In the mixed model, noise effects and the meta × condition interaction remain significant for every metric (p ≤ 0.001), and corpus reaches significance only for β.

**Noise shifts and rankings (§VI-C).** White noise depresses α most for single-codebook VQ (mean Δα = -1.02 over six valid cells) versus -0.36 for RVQ and 0.00 for non-VQ. Codec robustness rankings agree across noise types for UER (Spearman ρ = 0.81), partly for occupancy shift (ρ = 0.65), and not at all for α shift (ρ = -0.04).

**JSD and quality (§VII-B, Table X).** Over the 12 decodable codecs (corpus-averaged), clean-to-noise unigram JSD correlates with UTMOS ([[2204.02152|UTMOS]]) under DEMAND noise (r = -0.76, ρ = -0.65), and the association survives removing the two non-VQ codecs (r = -0.68, p = 0.029). DEMAND JSD also tracks median F0 error (r = 0.57, ρ = 0.34). Under white noise no audio-quality association survives both statistics: UTMOS r = 0.15, ρ = 0.06, and the MCD correlation (r = 0.62, ρ = 0.06) reverses sign without the non-VQ codecs. JSD correlates negatively with UER under white noise (r = -0.56, ρ = -0.73) but not under DEMAND (r = -0.03, ρ = -0.23), where UER is uniformly high (0.66 to 1.00).

**Clean resynthesis quality (§VII-A, Table IX).** DAC-24k has the lowest reference-based errors on every reference metric (MCD 1.3 dB, F0 RMSE 7.0 Hz, F0 median error 0.1 Hz, dCER 0.01), with UTMOS at the reference level (3.06 vs 3.09). FocalCodec (3.33), XCodec2 (3.32) and BigCodec (3.32) have the highest UTMOS, above the reference recordings; the paper attributes this to regenerative decoders moving output toward clean training data while raising spectral distance. WavTokenizer is the only codec with substantial transcription damage (dCER 0.21 vs at most 0.06 for all others). EnCodec has the lowest UTMOS (2.26).

**Degradation signatures (§VIII, Table XI).** Of 15 collapse cells, 14 are RVQ under white noise (14 of 21 RVQ-white cells); 13 of 18 explosion cells are RVQ under DEMAND noise (13 of 21 RVQ-DEMAND cells). All 18 single-codebook VQ cells are neutral, although they lose 57 percentage points of occupancy on average under white noise (RVQ: -40). In the non-VQ group, only S3Tokenizer shows explosion (four of its six cells); FocalCodec and SQCodec are neutral throughout. The RVQ pattern holds when the deadband thresholds change: halving them gives 15 RVQ-white collapse and 15 RVQ-DEMAND explosion cells of 21 each, doubling them gives 14 and 11.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is empirical and methodological rather than architectural. The statistics (Zipf, Heaps, entropy, JSD, ANOVA) are standard. What is new is the breadth of the grid (13 codecs across three quantizer families, three corpora, two noise types), the explicit reliability safeguards (fit-validity rules, matched-sample chunk analysis, a mixed-model robustness check), and two findings that revise the authors' own earlier conventions: the 3-gram optimum is family-dependent, and the collapse/explosion signatures previously seen in RVQ codecs do not transfer to single-codebook VQ codecs. Clean-to-noise JSD as a decoder-free diagnostic is a modest proposal: it needs a clean reference, works only at the unigram order across families, and its quality association holds under one of the two noise types.

The analysis treats codecs as black boxes in their default configurations, so vocabulary size, bitrate and training objective are confounded with meta-category. The paper states this openly and frames the codebook-size patterns as associations.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper gives codec-token analysis a reproducible protocol (family-conditional n-gram orders, fit-validity rules, matched sample sizes) and shows that conclusions drawn from one codec family or one noise type do not generalize. It provides cautionary evidence that "language-like" codec statistics should be read within quantizer families, not as a universal target near the word-level exponent α ≈ 2. Its quality links rest only on automatic estimators and 12 codec-level means.

## Claims

- **supports:** The token-frequency statistics of neural audio codecs depend on quantizer topology and acoustic condition, while the speech corpus they encode contributes almost no variance.
  > *Evidence:* In a three-way ANOVA over 117 codec-corpus-condition cells, corpus η² is at most 0.005 for every metric; meta-category explains 0.551 of unigram-entropy variance, and condition is the largest main effect for α (0.278), β (0.120) and redundancy (0.351). Corpus terms reach significance only for β in the codec-random-intercept mixed model. *(§VI-D, Table VIII)*
- **refines:** Zipf-law analysis of codec tokens requires family-specific n-gram orders, because large single-codebook vocabularies make higher-order n-gram inventories nearly unique and their power-law fits degenerate.
  > *Evidence:* KS-optimal orders are 3 to 4 for most RVQ codecs but 1 to 2 for BigCodec, WavTokenizer, XCodec2, FocalCodec and SQCodec; at n = 2 only 25 of 39 clean codec-corpus cells keep valid fits, and the cross-family α effect is significant at n = 2 (η² = 0.63) but not at n = 1 (η² = 0.05, p = 0.37). *(§V-B, Tables III and IV)*
- **complicates:** Decoder-free token-distribution shift can track the perceived quality of codec resynthesis under noise, but the relationship depends on noise type.
  > *Evidence:* Clean-to-noise unigram JSD correlates with UTMOS under DEMAND noise (r = -0.76, ρ = -0.65 over 12 codecs; r = -0.68 without the non-VQ codecs) but not under white noise (r = 0.15, ρ = 0.06), where the MCD correlation holds only in Pearson terms and reverses sign without the non-VQ codecs. Quality is measured only with automatic estimators. *(§VII-B, Table X)*
- **complicates:** Noise-induced degradation of codec token sequences takes different forms across quantizer families, so robustness diagnostics developed on RVQ codecs do not transfer directly to single-codebook or semantic tokenizers.
  > *Evidence:* 14 of 15 collapse cells are RVQ under white noise and 13 of 18 explosion cells are RVQ under DEMAND noise; all 18 single-codebook VQ cells are neutral despite a mean 57-point occupancy loss under white noise, and S3Tokenizer accounts for all four non-VQ explosion cells. *(§VIII-B, §VIII-C, Table XI)*
- **complicates:** Codec noise-robustness rankings depend on the diagnostic chosen, so no single token-space metric gives a general robustness ordering.
  > *Evidence:* Across the 13 codecs, white-noise and DEMAND-noise rankings agree for unit error rate (ρ = 0.81), moderately for occupancy shift (ρ = 0.65), and not at all for Zipf-exponent shift (ρ = -0.04 over 12 codecs with valid fits). *(§VI-C)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The link between token statistics and quality rests on automatic estimators only (UTMOS and Whisper-large-v3, whose biases may correlate with codec architecture), on 12 codec-level means, and on a single 0 dB SNR for two noise types. No listening test is reported.

- Cross-family comparisons of α and β use different n-gram orders, so absolute values are not directly comparable across families; the paper reports them descriptively.
- Vocabulary size is confounded with architecture, bitrate and training objective, so the codebook-size explanation of the α ordering is an association, not an isolated effect.
- Only the first RVQ level is analyzed for multi-codebook codecs, while resynthesis uses all levels; the distributional statistics therefore describe only part of what the decoder consumes.
- The ANOVA cells repeatedly sample the same 13 codecs, so η² values are descriptive and the mixed-model meta-category tests rest on 13 codec-level units.
- Collapse and explosion counts depend on operational deadband thresholds (tested over a two-fold range).
- S3Tokenizer has no decoder, so it is excluded from all quality analyses.
- The study does not test whether these token statistics predict downstream performance of a language model trained on the tokens (for example codec-LM TTS quality), which is the main motivation for caring about them.
- Reverberation, channel distortion and continuous SNR sweeps are left to future work.
