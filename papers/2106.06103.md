> **Jaehyeon Kim et al.** (Kakao Enterprise / KAIST) · [→ Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kim21f.html) · [→ Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06103) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> VITS trains a single parallel model from phonemes to raw waveform as a conditional VAE with a flow-enhanced prior and an adversarially trained waveform decoder, and reaches LJSpeech MOS close to ground truth without a separately trained vocoder.

## Problem

Neural TTS in 2021 mostly used two stages: an acoustic model that predicts mel-spectrograms, then a vocoder that turns them into waveforms. The two models are trained separately. Top quality usually needs the vocoder to be fine-tuned on the acoustic model's outputs. The fixed mel-spectrogram interface also blocks the use of learned intermediate representations. Earlier single-stage parallel systems (FastSpeech 2s, EATS) removed this pipeline, but their quality stayed below two-stage systems. Parallel systems also used deterministic duration predictors, so they could not produce the different rhythms a speaker uses for the same sentence (the one-to-many problem).

## Method

VITS (Variational Inference with adversarial learning for end-to-end Text-to-Speech) is a conditional VAE that maximizes an evidence lower bound on log p(x | c). It has five components (§2.5):

- **Posterior encoder.** Non-causal WaveNet residual blocks take a *linear-scale* spectrogram and output a Gaussian posterior over 192-channel latents z. It is used only in training.
- **Prior encoder.** A transformer text encoder with relative positional representations maps IPA phonemes to per-phoneme means and variances. A normalizing flow (four volume-preserving affine coupling layers built from WaveNet blocks) transforms z so that the prior is more expressive than a factorized Gaussian.
- **Decoder.** It is essentially the HiFi-GAN V1 generator and upsamples z directly to the waveform. The reconstruction loss is an L1 distance between the mel-spectrogram of the generated waveform and the target mel-spectrogram (§2.1.2).
- **Discriminator.** It is the HiFi-GAN multi-period discriminator plus one raw-waveform sub-discriminator, giving periods [1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11] (Appendix B.2). Training uses a least-squares adversarial loss plus a feature-matching loss (§2.3).
- **Stochastic duration predictor.** This flow-based model (neural spline flows, dilated depth-separable convolutions) learns a distribution over phoneme durations. Variational dequantization handles integer durations. Variational data augmentation lifts the scalar duration to a higher dimension so that invertible transforms can act on it (§2.2.2). Stop-gradient on its input keeps it from affecting the other modules.

Alignment comes from Monotonic Alignment Search (MAS), taken from Glow-TTS and redefined to maximize the ELBO. This reduces to maximizing the log-likelihood of the flow-transformed latents under the text-conditioned prior, so the original dynamic-programming implementation is reused unchanged (§2.2.1). The total loss is the sum of the reconstruction, KL, duration, adversarial and feature-matching losses (Eq. 11). Windowed generator training decodes random 32-frame latent segments to save memory. Training runs 800k steps on 4 V100 GPUs (§3.3). For multi-speaker training (VCTK), a learned speaker embedding is added through global conditioning to the posterior encoder, flow, decoder and duration predictor. The text encoder gets no speaker input.

At inference, the duration predictor samples durations from noise (noise scale 0.8). The text-conditioned prior is expanded by these durations and sampled (prior standard deviation scaled by 0.667). The inverse flow and the decoder then produce the waveform (§3.4). The paper does not report the parameter count.

## Key Results

All quality numbers come from crowd-sourced 5-point naturalness MOS tests with 95% confidence intervals (§4.1). The baselines are public pretrained Tacotron 2 and Glow-TTS checkpoints paired with HiFi-GAN. They include HiFi-GAN fine-tuned for 100k steps on Tacotron 2 teacher-forced outputs; the authors report that this fine-tuned vocoder worked better for both acoustic models (§3.4).

- **LJSpeech (Table 1).** VITS scores 4.43 (±0.06), against 4.46 (±0.06) for ground truth. The strongest two-stage system, Glow-TTS + fine-tuned HiFi-GAN, scores 4.32 (±0.07), and Tacotron 2 + fine-tuned HiFi-GAN scores 4.25 (±0.07). VITS with Glow-TTS's deterministic duration predictor (DDP) scores 4.39 (±0.06). VITS has the highest MOS of the six synthetic systems, and its confidence interval overlaps that of ground truth.
- **Ablations (Table 2, 300k steps).** Removing the prior normalizing flow drops MOS from 4.50 to 2.98 (−1.52). Feeding a mel-spectrogram instead of a linear spectrogram to the posterior encoder drops it to 4.31 (−0.19).
- **VCTK multi-speaker (Table 3).** VITS scores 4.38 (±0.06), equal to ground truth (4.38 ±0.07). Glow-TTS + fine-tuned HiFi-GAN scores 3.82 and Tacotron 2 + fine-tuned HiFi-GAN scores 3.19. The authors trained these baselines on VCTK themselves. The gap to the best two-stage system (+0.56) is much larger than on LJSpeech (+0.11).
- **Side-by-side comparison (Appendix C, Table 5).** A 7-point CMOS of VITS against ground truth (500 ratings on 50 items) gives −0.106 on LJSpeech and −0.262 on VCTK. A negative score means raters prefer the ground truth. The appendix text gives −0.270 for VCTK; Table 5 in the PDF gives −0.262. This page uses the table value. Either way, a small preference for real speech remains, even where the MOS values match.
- **Diversity (§4.3, Figures 2–3).** For 100 samples of one sentence, VITS gives a spread of utterance lengths similar to Tacotron 2, while Glow-TTS gives a single fixed length. The F0 tracks vary in pitch and rhythm. In the multi-speaker model, durations and pitch differ by speaker identity.
- **Speed (Table 4).** On one V100 with batch size 1, VITS generates 1480.15 kHz (×67.12 real time), against 606.05 kHz (×27.48) for Glow-TTS + HiFi-GAN, which is about 2.4 times faster. VITS (DDP) is the fastest at 2005.03 kHz (×90.93), so the stochastic duration predictor costs speed.

There are some limits to the comparisons. The paper does not state the number of MOS raters, and it reports no objective metrics (no WER or speaker similarity). The LJSpeech baselines use public checkpoints, and the paper does not say whether their training data excludes the authors' random 500-utterance test split.

## Novelty Assessment

The architectural contribution is real. Many components are borrowed: the WaveNet blocks and MAS from Glow-TTS, and the generator and discriminators from HiFi-GAN. The new step is to join them through a conditional-VAE latent, trained end to end with a flow-enhanced prior and adversarial waveform losses. MAS is redefined over the latent prior instead of the data likelihood. The stochastic duration predictor is new for parallel TTS, which the paper notes had relied on deterministic duration prediction (§5.3). Its variational dequantization and data augmentation come from Flow++ and VFlow, applied here to phoneme durations. Choosing a linear-spectrogram posterior and a mel-domain reconstruction loss are smaller design decisions, and the ablation supports both. The evidence for the main claim, end-to-end quality above two-stage systems, rests on MOS tests on two English corpora. The ablation shows that the flow prior is essential.

## Field Significance

> [!important]
> Foundational: VITS shows that a single parallel model trained end to end can match or exceed the naturalness of the best public two-stage acoustic-model-plus-vocoder systems on LJSpeech and VCTK. It introduces the combination of a conditional VAE, a flow prior and a GAN waveform decoder, together with a stochastic flow-based duration predictor. This gives parallel TTS a way to sample varied rhythms instead of a single deterministic duration.

## Claims

- **supports:** End-to-end training of text-to-waveform synthesis through a shared latent variable can match or exceed the naturalness of two-stage acoustic-model-plus-vocoder pipelines, even when the vocoder is fine-tuned on acoustic-model outputs.
  > *Evidence:* VITS reaches MOS 4.43 on LJSpeech versus 4.32 for Glow-TTS with fine-tuned HiFi-GAN (ground truth 4.46), and 4.38 on VCTK versus 3.82 (ground truth 4.38); with the same deterministic duration predictor as Glow-TTS, VITS still scores 4.39. *(§4.1, Table 1; §4.2, Table 3)*
- **supports:** The expressiveness of the prior distribution is a major determinant of sample quality in latent-variable TTS models.
  > *Evidence:* Removing the normalizing flow from the conditional prior lowers MOS from 4.50 to 2.98 in the ablation, the largest effect in the study. *(§4.1, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Modeling phoneme duration as a learned distribution rather than a deterministic prediction yields more natural and more varied rhythm in parallel TTS, at some cost in synthesis speed.
  > *Evidence:* The stochastic duration predictor raises MOS from 4.39 (DDP) to 4.43 and produces a length distribution over 100 samples comparable to autoregressive Tacotron 2, where Glow-TTS produces one fixed length; synthesis speed falls from 2005.03 kHz (DDP) to 1480.15 kHz. *(§4.1, Table 1; §4.3, Figure 2; §4.4, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Matching ground-truth MOS does not mean that listeners cannot tell synthetic speech from recordings; side-by-side preference tests can still show a gap.
  > *Evidence:* VITS ties ground truth MOS on VCTK (4.38) yet scores CMOS −0.262 against ground truth on VCTK and −0.106 on LJSpeech. *(§Appendix C, Table 5)*
- **refines:** Higher-resolution spectral input to the posterior encoder improves end-to-end VAE TTS quality, even when the reconstruction target is a mel-spectrogram.
  > *Evidence:* Replacing the linear-scale spectrogram with a mel-spectrogram as posterior input lowers MOS from 4.50 to 4.31. *(§2.1.3; §4.1, Table 2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The evaluation is narrow: two English read-speech corpora, naturalness MOS and CMOS only, no rater counts, and no intelligibility or speaker-similarity metrics. The LJSpeech baselines are public checkpoints, and the paper does not confirm that they exclude the authors' random test split.

- **Text preprocessing remains.** The model still needs an external phonemizer to produce IPA sequences. The authors name self-supervised language representations as a possible way to remove this step (§6).
- **Multi-speaker scope.** Speakers are learned embedding IDs over the VCTK training speakers. The paper does not test unseen speakers or adaptation to new voices.
- **Voice conversion is only demonstrated.** Appendix D describes conversion through the speaker-independent flow output, but shows only pitch tracks (Figure 7), with no listening test or VC metrics.
- **Residual gap to real speech.** CMOS still favors ground truth, more strongly on VCTK. The VCTK CMOS value differs between the appendix text (−0.270) and Table 5 (−0.262).
- **Sampling hyperparameters.** Quality and diversity depend on fixed noise scales (0.8 for durations, 0.667 for the prior). The paper does not study how these trade diversity against quality for VITS.
- **Model size and training cost.** The paper reports no parameter count. Training takes 800k steps on 4 V100s.
