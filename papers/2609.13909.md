> Adds two parameter-free changes to the local diffusion decoder of DiTAR, dilated sampling of distant history patches and attention masking of acoustic history in shallow layers, to cut speaker drift on long utterances and word errors on hard sentences.

## Problem

Continuous-latent autoregressive diffusion Transformers (AR-DiT), with DiTAR as the reference design, pair a causal LM that predicts one semantic condition vector per latent patch with a local diffusion Transformer (LocDiT) that renders each patch. The authors identify two failure sources in this design. First, LocDiT sees only the immediately preceding patch as acoustic history, so distant speaker and prosody cues are lost and speaker similarity drifts over long utterances. Second, LocDiT develops what the authors call "acoustic inertia": its shallow layers copy or extend the previous acoustic pattern instead of following the LM's semantic condition. On hard text this produces repetition, omission, mispronunciation and runaway looping. Simply giving the decoder more history makes the second problem worse, and reordering patches to widen the receptive field (Skip-Patchify) breaks temporal continuity.

## Method

DiTAR+ keeps the DiTAR pipeline unchanged at the macro level. A VAE compresses the waveform into continuous latents, latents are grouped into non-overlapping patches with an aggregation encoder, a causal autoregressive Transformer conditioned on transcript and reference-audio prefix predicts a semantic vector h_i per patch, and a bidirectional LocDiT denoises patch i conditioned on h_i, the timestep and the acoustic pre-context. The two changes both sit inside LocDiT's conditioning and add no parameters.

**Dilated Context Sampling (DCS).** The pre-context for patch i becomes a dense local window of the last c patches (example c = 3) plus a sparse set of m earlier patches sampled with a fixed stride j > c, concatenated in their original temporal order (Eq. 1, Algorithm 1 Phase 1). The decoder thus gets a wider "macro" view of the speaker's earlier acoustics while adjacent patches stay adjacent, in contrast to reordering schemes.

**Hierarchical Acoustic Masking (HAM).** The LocDiT input is the sequence [h_i, historical patches, noisy current patch]. In the first N1 Transformer blocks an additive mask sets attention from current-patch queries to historical-patch keys to minus infinity, so shallow layers can only use the semantic condition; the remaining N2 blocks see the history for timbre and boundary smoothness (Eqs. 2-3, Algorithm 1 Phase 2). The authors frame this as decoupling semantic alignment (shallow) from acoustic detail rendering (deep).

(b) Hierarchical Acoustic Masking (HAM), which masks historical acoustic context in shallow layers to decouple semantic alignment and acoustic rendering. (c) Dilated Context Sampling (DCS), which expands the macro-receptive field via dilated sampling while preserving temporal continuity.](assets/2609.13909/figure-1.png)

All systems the authors train are trained from scratch on the multilingual Emilia corpus on 32 H20 GPUs, batch size 8 per GPU. Inference uses a 10-step Euler ODE sampler with classifier-free guidance scale 2.0, a "flow-matching scale" of 1.0 and sway sampling, with prompts truncated to 75 (units not stated). Model size, the VAE configuration, patch size and the values of j, m and N1 used in the experiments are not reported.

## Key Results

**Objective evaluation (Table 1, seed-tts-eval protocol).** DiTAR+ has the lowest WER of all compared systems on all four sets: 1.672% on Seed-EN (DiTAR 1.774, F5-TTS 1.982), 0.985% on Seed-ZH (DiTAR 1.287, CosyVoice 2 1.45), 9.893% on ZH-Hard (F5-TTS 11.209, DiTAR 12.478, CosyVoice 2 13.415, MaskGCT 14.085) and 2.173% on ZH-Long (CosyVoice 2 2.776, DiTAR 2.778). Speaker similarity is highest on ZH-Hard (0.695 vs MaskGCT 0.688) and ZH-Long (0.759 vs CosyVoice 2 0.748). It is not highest on the standard sets: on Seed-EN DiTAR scores 0.737 vs 0.735, and on Seed-ZH MaskGCT scores 0.774 vs 0.762.

**Subjective evaluation (Table 2).** Ten professional raters scored 50 random utterances per system on the SeedTTS test set (the subset is not specified), comparing DiTAR+, DiTAR, F5-TTS and E2 TTS. DiTAR+ has the highest N-MOS among the synthetic systems, but only by 0.01 over DiTAR (3.71 vs 3.70); ground truth scores 3.88. Its S-MOS of 3.58 is the highest in the table, 0.06 above DiTAR and 0.01 above ground truth (3.57). DiTAR has the higher Q-MOS (3.88 vs 3.82). In C-MOS, with DiTAR+ anchored at 0, DiTAR scores -0.06, F5-TTS -0.02 and E2 TTS -0.28, so raters slightly prefer DiTAR+ over the other systems; ground truth at +0.22 is preferred over DiTAR+. No confidence intervals or significance tests are reported, and CosyVoice 2 and MaskGCT are not in the listening test.

**Ablation (Table 3, Figure 2).** The two modules act on different failure modes. DCS alone mainly helps long-form generation (ZH-Long WER 2.165%, SIM 0.757) and gives a smaller ZH-Hard gain (10.671%). HAM alone gives the lowest ZH-Hard WER (9.889%) and highest ZH-Hard SIM (0.700) of any configuration, but raises ZH-Long WER above the baseline (3.056% vs 2.778%). The combined DiTAR+ is near the best single module on each axis rather than above both: ZH-Hard WER 9.893% (vs HAM 9.889%) and ZH-Long WER 2.173% (vs DCS 2.165%). It has the highest ZH-Long SIM (0.759, vs DCS 0.757). A naive patch-reordering control (DiTAR + Skip) degrades every metric (ZH-Hard WER 16.231%, ZH-Long WER 5.105%). In the chunk-level similarity plot over 3-second chunks (Figure 2), the baseline falls to about 0.656 by chunks 9-11. DiTAR+ stays above about 0.70 through chunk 12, and DCS-only and HAM-only both end near 0.67 at the final chunk.

A single qualitative case (Figure 3) shows DiTAR looping to 25.5 s with 52% WER, while DiTAR+ ends at 14.0 s with 6% WER.

## Novelty Assessment

Both mechanisms are small, targeted changes to how DiTAR's local decoder sees its acoustic history. They add no parameters and leave the LM, the VAE and the training objective unchanged. HAM is the more interesting idea: a layer-dependent attention mask that forces the decoder to rely on the semantic condition before it can copy acoustic context, presented as a fix for a named shortcut-learning failure. DCS is a dilated-context variant of the standard prefix conditioning. The paper's comparison against patch reordering shows why the temporal order matters. The diagnosis is plausible and the ablation separates the two effects cleanly, but the evidence is narrow. Robustness gains are shown only on Chinese stress sets, ZH-Long is the authors' own curated subset, and key hyperparameters (dilation stride, number of sampled patches, masked depth N1) are neither reported nor ablated.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper provides controlled evidence that, in continuous-latent AR-diffusion TTS, how much acoustic history the local decoder can see trades off against how closely it follows text. Restricting history in shallow layers helps on hard sentences, while sparse long-range history helps on long utterances. This framing of decoder shortcut learning is relevant to other hybrid AR-diffusion designs, but the evidence comes from one base model and Chinese stress sets.

## Claims

- **supports:** In autoregressive diffusion TTS, a local diffusion decoder with unrestricted access to preceding acoustic context can learn to extrapolate prior acoustics instead of following the semantic condition, which causes repetition and looping on hard text.
  > *Evidence:* Masking historical acoustic patches from the current patch in shallow LocDiT layers (HAM) cuts ZH-Hard WER from 12.478% to 9.889% and raises SIM from 0.672 to 0.700. In a qualitative case, the baseline loops to 25.5 s with 52% WER, while DiTAR+ stops at 14.0 s with 6% WER. *(§2.3, §3.5, Table 3; §3.6, Figure 3)*
- **supports:** Giving the acoustic decoder sparse long-range history in its original temporal order reduces speaker drift in long-form autoregressive speech generation.
  > *Evidence:* Dilated Context Sampling raises ZH-Long (25-35 s) SIM from 0.741 to 0.757 and lowers WER from 2.778% to 2.165%. In chunk-level similarity, the baseline falls to about 0.656 late in the utterance, while the full model stays above about 0.70. *(§2.2, §3.5, Table 3, Figure 2)*
- **complicates:** Widening a speech generator's context by reordering latent patches can hurt more than it helps, because the reordering breaks the temporal prior of continuous latents.
  > *Evidence:* The DiTAR + Skip reordering control degrades every metric relative to the baseline: ZH-Hard WER 16.231% vs 12.478%, ZH-Long WER 5.105% vs 2.778%, and ZH-Long SIM 0.685 vs 0.741. *(§3.5, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Robustness fixes for long-form and hard-text TTS do not compose cleanly, because limiting acoustic context for text fidelity can cost long-form stability.
  > *Evidence:* HAM alone raises ZH-Long WER above the baseline (3.056% vs 2.778%). The combined model does not beat the best single module on either WER axis: ZH-Hard 9.893% vs HAM 9.889%, and ZH-Long 2.173% vs DCS 2.165%. *(§3.5, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Robustness interventions that improve intelligibility and speaker consistency can slightly lower perceived local audio quality.
  > *Evidence:* DiTAR+ has a lower Q-MOS than DiTAR (3.82 vs 3.88), while its N-MOS is nearly equal (3.71 vs 3.70) and its S-MOS is higher (3.58 vs 3.52). The authors attribute the Q-MOS gap to the baseline's smoother local acoustic texture. *(§3.4, Table 2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] Missing settings for reproduction
> The paper does not report model size, VAE and patch configuration, or the dilation stride, number of sampled distant patches and number of masked layers (N1) used in the experiments. None of these choices is ablated, and no code or demo link is given, so the method cannot be reproduced as described.

- The robustness evidence (ZH-Hard, ZH-Long, ablation, chunk-level plot) is Chinese only. English is covered only by the standard Seed-EN set. ZH-Long is a 400-sentence subset curated by the authors, and the paper does not describe how it was selected.
- The paper does not say whether CosyVoice 2, MaskGCT and F5-TTS were retrained on Emilia or run from released checkpoints, so the cross-system comparison may mix training-data conditions. Only the DiTAR baseline is a clearly matched comparison.
- The listening test uses 10 raters and does not specify which SeedTTS subset it uses. It reports no confidence intervals, and most MOS gaps between DiTAR+ and DiTAR are 0.01-0.06. Ground truth outscores DiTAR+ in C-MOS.
- Figure 2 shows DCS-only and HAM-only ending close to the baseline at the final chunk (all near 0.67). Only the combined model keeps a clear tail advantage, so the per-module long-tail effect is less clear-cut than the per-set averages suggest.
- The ablation runs each module alone and both together, but does not test intermediate masking depths or dilation strides. Whether the semantic-versus-acoustic split depends on layer count is left open.
