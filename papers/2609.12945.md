> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Bin Lin et al.** (StepFun) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12945) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ?
>
> StepAudio 3 Gen extends a pretrained text LLM into one discrete autoregressive generator for speech, voice design, singing, music and sound. Generation runs over a shared 12.5 Hz, 16-codebook RVQ token space, with no diffusion or flow-matching renderer. A zero-initialized RVQ Adaptor and a four-stage pretraining curriculum limit damage to the LLM's text abilities.

## Problem

Unified audio generators mostly use continuous latents with diffusion or flow-matching renderers. Discrete-token generators can share a vocabulary and causal objective with a text LLM, but high-fidelity RVQ codes are hard to model. Flattening the codebooks over time makes sequences too long. Keeping only a coarse semantic token loses acoustic detail. Adding audio to a pretrained LLM also causes interference through two channels, according to the authors. First, the summed embeddings of new RVQ codebooks do not match the statistics of pretrained text embeddings. Second, a randomly initialized residual-codebook loss sends large gradients into the backbone. Both effects can trade the LLM's language ability for audio capability *(§1)*.

## Method

**Tokenizer.** The StepAudio Tokenizer, following the X-Codec line of work, fuses two feature streams. A frozen self-supervised encoder (not named in the report) supplies semantic features. A convolutional encoder with SnakeBeta activations extracts 50 Hz acoustic features from the waveform. A strided convolution compresses the fused features to 12.5 Hz. A single 16 × 2048 residual vector quantizer, with factorized cosine-similarity lookup, then discretizes them, so every code layer carries both semantic and acoustic information. Quantizer dropout of 0.5 and a distillation branch that regresses the SSL features push semantic information into the earliest codebooks. The decoder is fully causal: a Vocos-style Transformer with RoPE and 25-frame sliding-window attention feeds an ISTFT head that outputs 24 kHz audio, which supports streaming. Training is GAN-based, with a multi-period waveform discriminator, a multi-resolution spectral discriminator, and feature-matching, multi-scale mel and commitment losses. The tokenizer is pretrained on about 700k hours of speech, music and sound events. A second stage then refines only the decoder, which leaves the token space unchanged *(§2.1, §4.1)*.

**Backbone and time-depth generation.** A pretrained decoder-only text LLM (not named, size not reported) adds the 2,048 entries of codebook 0 to its vocabulary, so one LM head predicts both text tokens and the first audio code. On the input side, each of the 16 codebooks has its own embedding table. The 16 vectors are summed and passed through the **RVQ Adaptor**, a token-wise stack of pre-norm SwiGLU residual blocks with zero-initialized down projections. Its output is added to the codebook-0 token embedding at audio positions only, so text positions are unchanged. A four-layer causal **RVQ Code Predictor** generates codebooks 1 to 15 along the depth axis. It is conditioned on the projected backbone hidden state and on codebook 0. The full 16-code frame is decoded by the tokenizer. The training loss sums a token-level term (text and codebook 0) and a speech term over the 15 residual codebooks, weighted by λ. The speech term is summed over depth rather than averaged *(§2.2)*.

**Interference-aware progressive pretraining (about 2.7T tokens).** The curriculum has four stages *(§4.2, Table 1)*:
1. Modality alignment on ASR and speech-to-text translation with a frozen backbone. Only the audio embeddings and the adaptor train. Because the adaptor starts as an identity map, text ability cannot drift in this stage.
2. Audio understanding with the full model unfrozen and a 1:1 text replay.
3. Generation with a text : TTS : interleaved-dialogue mixture of 3 : 1 : 2 and λ = 1.0. The predictor's hidden-state input is detached, so its 15-codebook loss trains only the predictor.
4. Long-context joint cool-down. Detachment is removed, λ drops to 0.1, and the context grows from 16,384 to 32,768 tokens.

**Post-training.** Full-parameter SFT uses about 5,000 hours spanning speech, voice design, singing, music and sound. For TTS, 1,500 hours of natural conversational speech were selected with Step-Audio-R1.5 captioning and a paralinguistic-aware ASR. The music and text-conditioned singing data come from an in-house StepAudio Music Model. Speech-to-vocal pairs are pseudo-paired: TTS cloning from singing references, plus singing voice conversion. Requests use a ROLE / DIRECTOR / SCRIPT instruction format that sets speaker identity, scene and a time-ordered script of speech and sound events. GRPO then runs with G = 16 samples. The reward multiplies an instruction-consistency score by an ASR error term, exp(-3e), set to zero when e > 0.5. The consistency score comes from a text LLM that compares a generated-audio caption with the instruction, averaged over four scorings. Groups with low reward variance are dropped (DAPO-style). The policy ratio is computed per codebook token across all 16 codebooks *(§3.2, §4.3)*.

## Key Results

**TTS human-likeness (Chinese, human raters).** Raters compare pairs and pick the sample that sounds more like a real person; ties are allowed. Over 1,500 comparisons among six systems, StepAudio 3 Gen has the highest Elo (1,755.3). The other five are Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS-Plus (1,544.3), StepAudio 2.5 TTS (1,504.0), Doubao-App (1,490.3), Inworld-TTS-2 (1,209.6) and MiniMax-Speech-2.8-HD (1,181.4). In head-to-head tests with 100 trials per opponent, its win rate (wins over all trials, ties included) is 73.0% to 90.0% per opponent and 82.0% overall (410 wins, 39 ties, 51 losses) *(§5.3, Figure 2)*. Each competitor contributes one voice. The report gives no CER or speaker similarity, arguing that these metrics miss expressive delivery.

**Voice design.** On the full InstructTTSEval benchmark, judged by Gemini 3.1 Pro rather than the benchmark's original setup, StepAudio 3 Gen scores highest on every condition in both languages. In Chinese it scores 88.5 / 91.5 / 75.5 on APS / DSD / RP (average 85.2). The strongest baseline is Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign at 79.0 / 86.7 / 56.2 (average 74.0). In English it scores 82.4 / 85.5 / 65.2 (average 77.7), against 74.7 / 83.8 / 61.9 (average 73.5) for Qwen3-TTS. VoiceSculptor reports Chinese only. In a blind human arena with 490 comparisons across Chinese and English, StepAudio 3 Gen again has the highest Elo (1,668.5). Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign is next at 1,571.5. The head-to-head win rate is 65.3% to 85.7% per opponent and 75.5% overall (148 wins, 9 ties, 39 losses of 196) *(§5.4, Table 4, Figure 3)*.

**RVQ Adaptor ablation (after pretraining).** Adding the adaptor improves every understanding benchmark. AISHELL-1 CER drops from 5.25% to 3.00%, and LibriSpeech WER from 6.00% to 3.41%. MMAU accuracy rises from 40.7% to 51.7%. CoVoST BLEU rises from 12.05 to 30.56 (En→Zh) and from 5.99 to 18.59 (Zh→En). SpeechMMLU (T2S) accuracy rises from 12.13% to 58.76% *(§5.1, Table 2)*.

**Text retention.** The baseline here is a three-stage recipe without the adaptor. Against it, the progressive recipe scores higher on all eight text benchmarks, by +4.2 to +8.5 points: for example MMLU 64.99 → 69.20, MATH 36.62 → 45.07, and HumanEval 47.56 → 54.27 *(§5.2, Table 3)*. The baseline differs in two ways at once: it has no adaptor, and its curriculum is different. Neither comparison reports the original text LLM's scores, so the absolute amount of forgetting is unknown.

## Novelty Assessment

The time-depth factorization, a temporal LLM for codebook 0 plus a small depth Transformer for the residual codes, follows existing time-depth designs that the report cites (RQ-Transformer, Moshi, UniAudio). The X-Codec-style semantic-acoustic tokenizer is also an established recipe. The new parts are three. The first is the zero-initialized, position-gated RVQ Adaptor. The second is a training procedure that detaches the residual-code predictor during main generation pretraining and reconnects it only in a low-learning-rate cool-down with a reweighted loss. The third is running a single 16-codebook RVQ space across speech, singing, music and sound with no continuous renderer. The adaptor has a clean ablation. The curriculum comparison mixes the adaptor and the staging into one baseline, so the detachment step is not isolated. Data scale, in-house SFT curation and GRPO likely drive much of the TTS and voice-design gains, but none of them is ablated.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The report shows that a fully discrete autoregressive RVQ generator, built on a pretrained text LLM, can match or beat commercial systems in human-likeness and voice-design preference tests. It also provides ablation evidence that a zero-initialized input adaptor and a staged, gradient-detached curriculum reduce interference with the LLM's text abilities. Its claims about music, sound and singing are demonstrations only, and its TTS evaluation uses in-house preference arenas without objective intelligibility or similarity metrics.

## Claims

- **supports:** Adding audio to a pretrained text LLM through a zero-initialized, modality-gated input adaptor improves speech understanding over feeding summed codec embeddings directly.
  > *Evidence:* After pretraining, the RVQ Adaptor lowers AISHELL-1 CER from 5.25% to 3.00% and LibriSpeech WER from 6.00% to 3.41%. It also raises MMAU accuracy from 40.7% to 51.7% and SpeechMMLU (T2S) from 12.13% to 58.76%. *(§5.1, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Staged audio integration with text replay and delayed backpropagation of the residual-codebook loss preserves more of an LLM's text ability than joint audio training.
  > *Evidence:* The four-stage recipe uses a frozen-backbone alignment stage, 50% text replay and a detached code predictor. It beats a three-stage baseline without the adaptor on all eight text benchmarks (for example MMLU 69.20 vs. 64.99, MATH 45.07 vs. 36.62). The baseline differs in both adaptor and curriculum, and neither result is compared with the original LLM. *(§4.2, §5.2, Table 3)*
- **supports:** Discrete residual-codebook autoregression without a continuous acoustic renderer can reach top human preference in expressive TTS and voice design.
  > *Evidence:* StepAudio 3 Gen has the highest Elo in a Chinese human-likeness arena (1,755.3 against five commercial systems) and in a voice design arena (1,668.5 against four open systems). Its head-to-head win rates are 82.0% and 75.5%. *(§5.3, §5.4, Figure 2, Figure 3)*
- **complicates:** Character error rate and speaker similarity do not capture the expressive, human-like delivery that recent TTS systems optimize for, so preference-based evaluation is needed alongside them.
  > *Evidence:* The authors drop CER and speaker similarity for TTS, arguing that a flat-prosody model can still score a low CER. They report only pairwise human human-likeness judgments, so intelligibility and cloning fidelity remain unmeasured. *(§5.3)*
- **refines:** RL rewards for instruction-following speech generation need a content-accuracy gate, so that style matching cannot hide wrong linguistic content.
  > *Evidence:* The GRPO reward multiplies a caption-based instruction-consistency score by exp(-3e) of the ASR error and is zero when error exceeds 0.5. The authors design this so that wrong content cannot score high on style match alone; the reward design is not ablated. *(§4.3)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The TTS evaluation is one in-house Chinese preference arena with one voice per competitor. It reports no CER/WER, speaker similarity or zero-shot cloning test, even though the report lists zero-shot TTS as a capability. Singing, music, sound effects and "vibe speech" have no metrics at all (§6 is descriptive only).

- The backbone LLM, its parameter count and the code predictor width are not disclosed. All training data is in-house, and no code release is stated, so the work cannot be reproduced.
- The StepAudio Tokenizer has no reconstruction metrics (PESQ, STOI, MOS or similar), so its quality as a codec is not measured directly.
- InstructTTSEval scores come from a single LLM judge (Gemini 3.1 Pro) giving binary decisions, not the benchmark's reference protocol. This limits comparison with numbers reported elsewhere.
- The arenas do not report the number of raters, rater qualifications or confidence intervals. The voice design arena uses 49 completed trials per opponent.
- Neither the progressive-pretraining comparison nor the RL stage is ablated in isolation (detachment alone, text replay alone, SFT vs. SFT+GRPO).
- Several training corpora (music, text-conditioned singing, some speech) are generated by the authors' own models. The effect of this synthetic data is not analyzed.
