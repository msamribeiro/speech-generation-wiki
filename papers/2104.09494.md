> NISQA is a single-ended (reference-free) neural model that predicts overall speech quality MOS together with four perceptual quality dimensions (Noisiness, Coloration, Discontinuity, Loudness) from the degraded signal alone, released with open code, weights and crowdsourced training datasets.

> [!info] Citation Stub

## Context in Speech Generation

NISQA computes 48-band Mel-spectrogram segments, encodes each with a CNN, models time dependencies with a small single-head Self-Attention network, and pools over time with separate attention-pooling heads that output overall MOS and the four quality dimensions in one multitask network *(§2)*. It is trained end-to-end on subjective ratings only, across 59 training and 18 validation datasets, including new crowdsourced ITU-T P.808 datasets with simulated and live telephony distortions, and it accepts speech of any duration or sample rate without preprocessing *(§1, §3)*. Because it needs no clean reference and its predictions are validated against human MOS ratings (reported as PCC and RMSE against P.563, ANIQUE+, WAWEnets, POLQA, DIAL and ViSQOL, *Table 4*), it can serve as an automatic naturalness or quality proxy for synthesized speech where no ground-truth recording exists. The authors state that the model targets distortions in communication networks and that predicting the quality of enhanced or synthesized speech is a related task for which the released weights can be fine-tuned *(§6)*, so scores on TTS or VC output fall outside its original training domain.
