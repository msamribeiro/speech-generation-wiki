> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Asaria et al.** (Transformer Lab) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18323) · Demo: ? · Code: ?
>
> Shows that best-of-N sampling with an ASR round-trip check removes catastrophic failures (silence, early stop, repetition, hallucinated content) from four open autoregressive codec TTS models, and that LoRA distillation on the self-verified samples recovers roughly half of that robustness in single-shot decoding on hard inputs.

## Problem

Open autoregressive TTS models that generate discrete codec tokens produce good speech on most inputs but fail stochastically and catastrophically on a fraction of utterances: they emit silence, terminate early, loop, or produce unrelated content. On a broad evaluation of Llasa-1B that includes adversarial rare-word and number/date prompts, the authors measure a catastrophic-failure rate of about 0.36, and about 0.27 single-shot on their hand-written hard set *(§1)*. Prior robustness post-training work typically applies one preference method to one model, so it is unclear whether a simple test-time verification baseline, and its distilled form, generalises across models and codecs *(§2)*.

## Method

**Metric.** Each generation is transcribed with Whisper (model size not reported). A generation counts as a catastrophic failure if it is a dropout (fewer than 25 decoded speech tokens, or an ASR transcript of at most one word) or a content failure (WER above 0.5 against the reference text). For best-of-N, a prompt fails only when all N candidates fail, giving a catastrophic-failure rate CFR_N that is non-increasing in N; CFR_1 is the single-shot rate *(§3)*. The authors adopt this dropout/collapse rate after finding that number/date WER is dominated by digit-versus-word formatting noise *(§3, §6)*. Zero-failure cells are reported with a rule-of-three 95% upper bound (3/n), and proportions carrying a claim get Wilson 95% intervals.

**Test-time self-verification.** Sample N candidates, transcribe each, and return the lowest-WER non-dropout candidate. Because the selector uses the target transcript, best-of-N is a reference-aware oracle: an upper bound on what verification can buy, not a deployable number *(§4, §5.1)*.

**Distillation.** The model is fine-tuned with LoRA on the best self-verified sample per training prompt so that single-shot decoding imitates the verified behaviour. The authors compare plain supervised distillation (SFT on the chosen sample) with preference optimisation using a rejected sample: DPO (reference policy is the LoRA-disabled base), IPO, a failure-targeted pairing (FTPO, where the rejected sample is specifically a dropout or collapse), and an online iterative variant that re-samples from the current policy, re-scores by ASR and rebuilds pairs for three rounds *(§4)*.

**Models: which are trained and which are only sampled.** All four systems are LLaMA-style or transformer autoregressive LMs over codec tokens; no codec is trained or modified.
- *Fine-tuned (LoRA distillation):* Llasa-1B (XCodec2), the primary model and the only one used for the SFT-versus-preference comparisons; Llasa-3B (XCodec2) and Orpheus-3B (SNAC), which receive a distillation pass evaluated on LibriSpeech only (Table 1).
- *Sampled unchanged:* CSM-1B (Sesame, Mimi RVQ codec), used only for base versus best-of-N, because its two-transformer design does not expose the single token stream the distillation path trains on *(§5.1)*.

The three codecs (single-stream XCodec2, flattened-RVQ SNAC, hierarchical-RVQ Mimi) vary together with the model family, so codec choice is not isolated as an experimental variable. Evaluation uses LibriSpeech test-clean (120 transcripts x 3 generations, training prompts from the disjoint dev-clean split) and a hand-written hard set of normal, long and tongue-twister prompts (26 prompts x 6 generations), with rare-word and number/date buckets analysed separately *(§3)*. Total compute is about 45 GPU-hours *(§7)*.

## Key Results

**Best-of-N on the primary model (Table 2).** On the hard set, Llasa-1B falls from 0.269 single-shot (95% CI [0.205, 0.343]) to 0.154 at N=2, 0.038 at N=3, and no observed failures at N=4, 5 and 6 (upper bound 3/156, about 0.019). On LibriSpeech it falls from 0.058 to no observed failures at N=2 and N=3 (upper bound 0.008) *(§5.1)*.

**Across four models (Table 1, LibriSpeech test-clean).**

| Model | Codec | Base | Best-of-2 | Best-of-3 | Distilled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Llasa-1B | XCodec2 | 0.058 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.058 |
| Orpheus-3B | SNAC | 0.008 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.033 |
| CSM-1B | Mimi | 0.017 | 0.000 | 0.000 | not run |
| Llasa-3B | XCodec2 | 0.108 | 0.042 | 0.033 | 0.142 |

Best-of-N reduces failures on all four models; three of four reach no observed failures by N=2, while Llasa-3B plateaus at 0.033 at N=3. The authors caution that the Llasa-3B run used a tighter generation budget (max_new_tokens = 1024) than the 1B run, which can truncate long generations into failures, so they do not read the 1B-versus-3B gap as a scale effect *(§5.1)*.

**Distillation (Table 3).** On the hard set, single-shot failure drops from 0.199 [0.143, 0.270] to 0.096 [0.059, 0.152] with SFT distillation and 0.083 [0.049, 0.137] with DPO distillation, closing about 52% (SFT) to 58% (DPO) of the failure mass, with residual failures remaining. On LibriSpeech there is no detectable change for Llasa-1B (0.058 to 0.058), and where the base is already near the floor distillation regresses slightly (Orpheus-3B 0.008 to 0.033; Llasa-3B 0.108 to 0.142) *(§4, §5.2, Table 1)*. Because best-of-N is reference-aware, the distilled single-shot column is the deployable number.

**Preference optimisation versus SFT (§5.3).** In an offline sweep on the hard set (about 108 generations), DPO (0.292), FTPO (0.292) and IPO (0.319) did not improve on one round of SFT-on-best (0.264); the intervals overlap heavily, so the authors read this as "no method beat SFT", not a ranking. In the matched distillation comparison, SFT (0.096) and DPO (0.083) differ by 0.013, inside the standard error of the difference (about 0.032), which the authors call a tie. The online iterative variant gives the lowest single-shot (trained-model) failure rates in the paper, 0.013 (DPO, 1/78) and 0.026 (SFT, 2/78), but on a smaller evaluation, with 95% intervals (e.g. [0.002, 0.069]) overlapping the single-pass numbers, and as a post-hoc minimum over many conditions; the authors call it promising, not confirmed.

**Analysis (§6).** Base-generation silence/dropout is about 4.6%. Rare words are a capability ceiling: only 2 of 10 rare-word prompts ever yield a faithful sample within N=3, so neither best-of-N nor distillation can recover them. For numbers/dates, a raw-versus-normalised A/B leaves WER essentially unchanged (0.155 vs. 0.143), which the authors read as artifact-dominated measurement. No human listening test, MOS or speaker-similarity results are reported.

## Novelty Assessment

The components are established: ASR-filtered best-of-N sampling, SFT on self-selected samples, and DPO/IPO with ASR-derived pairs. The contribution is empirical: applying one protocol uniformly across four open codec TTS models with three different codecs, reporting zero-failure cells with explicit upper bounds, and running a controlled SFT-versus-preference comparison that comes out as a tie. The failure-rate metric is a thresholded ASR round-trip rather than a new measurement method, though the observation that WER cannot track number/date robustness because of formatting noise is a useful measurement note. The distillation result rests on Llasa-1B for the hard set, and the generalisation study covers the test-time procedure, not distillation, on CSM-1B.

## Field Significance

Low. The paper gives a carefully caveated, multi-model data point that catastrophic failures in autoregressive codec TTS are largely a sampling problem that oracle ASR verification removes, and that one supervised distillation pass recovers part of that robustness on hard inputs. Its evidence is a single-language, automated-metric study with single training runs per condition and no human evaluation, so it serves mainly as confirmatory evidence for ASR-guided post-training and as a cautionary data point that offline DPO did not beat SFT at this scale.

## Claims

- **supports:** Catastrophic failures in autoregressive codec-token TTS are largely stochastic rather than systematic, so selecting among a few samples with an ASR check removes most of them across model families and codec token formats.
  > *Evidence:* Best-of-2 reaches no observed failures on LibriSpeech for Llasa-1B (0.058 base), Orpheus-3B (0.008) and CSM-1B (0.017), and Llasa-1B's hard-set rate falls from 0.269 to none at N=4; Llasa-3B falls from 0.108 to 0.033 but does not reach zero. *(§5.1, Table 1, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Supervised fine-tuning on self-verified samples transfers part of the robustness of best-of-N selection into single-shot decoding, with the gain concentrated on inputs where the base model fails often.
  > *Evidence:* Hard-set single-shot failure falls from 0.199 to 0.096 (SFT) and 0.083 (DPO) after one distillation pass, while LibriSpeech stays at 0.058 for Llasa-1B and regresses for near-floor models (Orpheus-3B 0.008 to 0.033, Llasa-3B 0.108 to 0.142). *(§5.2, Table 1, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Offline preference optimisation with ASR-derived chosen/rejected pairs does not necessarily improve over plain supervised distillation on the selected samples for TTS robustness.
  > *Evidence:* Offline DPO (0.292), FTPO (0.292) and IPO (0.319) did not beat SFT-on-best (0.264) on the hard set, and matched SFT (0.096) versus DPO (0.083) differ by 0.013 against a standard error of about 0.032; an online iterative variant reaches 0.013/0.026 but on 78 generations with overlapping intervals. *(§5.3)*
- **complicates:** Sampling-based verification and self-distillation cannot repair failures that reflect a missing capability of the base model.
  > *Evidence:* Only 2 of 10 rare-word prompts yield a faithful sample within N=3, so both best-of-N and distillation plateau high on this bucket. *(§6)*
- **complicates:** WER is an unreliable measure of number and date robustness in TTS because digit-versus-word formatting differences dominate the error.
  > *Evidence:* Number/date WER sits around 0.14 to 0.15 from canonicalisation noise, and a raw-versus-normalised A/B leaves it essentially unchanged (0.155 vs. 0.143), leading the authors to centre the metric on dropout/collapse instead. *(§3, §6)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The headline best-of-N numbers are oracle upper bounds: the verifier scores candidates against the reference transcript, which is unavailable at deployment. The deployable result is the distilled single-shot rate, which closes only about 52-58% of hard-set failures and does not improve, or slightly regresses, on LibriSpeech *(§5.1, §5.2, Table 1)*.

- The claim that distillation gains depend on difficulty rests on a two-regime comparison (hand-written hard set vs. LibriSpeech) that also differs in source, length, word frequency and speaker reference; the within-corpus difficulty sweep that would isolate it was not run *(§7)*.
- Distillation is evaluated on Llasa-1B/3B and Orpheus-3B only; CSM-1B gets no distillation, and the SFT-versus-preference comparisons are on Llasa-1B only *(§5.1, §7)*.
- Every distilled number is a single training run; the hard set is small (156 generations) and the online iterative evaluation smaller (78) *(§3, §5.3)*.
- The metric inherits Whisper's errors, and its false-positive floor on ground-truth audio was not measured *(§7)*.
- Single language (English), two corpora; no human listening tests, naturalness or speaker-similarity measures, so possible quality or similarity side effects of distillation are not assessed.
- The Llasa-3B results carry a generation-budget confound *(§5.1)*.
- Code and demo availability are not stated.
