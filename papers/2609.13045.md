> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Hayato Futami et al.** (Sony Group Corporation / Sony Europe Limited) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13045) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✗
>
> Kraken shows that a speech LLM for speech-to-speech translation can emit a single stream of very low-bitrate (325 bps) VQ tokens, and that conditioning both the LLM and a separate GAN vocoder on the source speech transfers speaker identity and prosody even when the training pairs are not speaker-matched.

## Problem

End-to-end speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) with speech LLMs promises joint optimization and the preservation of speaker identity and prosody. Two obstacles limit it. First, recent systems predict high-bitrate, multi-layer tokens (RVQ streams of about 2.2 kbps in Hibiki and Qwen3-Omni), which forces hierarchical or multi-token prediction modules on top of the LLM. Second, a single model that learns voice preservation end to end needs bilingual recordings with matched speaker and prosody, which are rare. The paper asks whether a compact single-stream token plus a source-conditioned decoder can avoid both constraints.

## Method

Kraken has three parts: a speech encoder, an LLM, and a speech decoder. The encoder is a W2v-BERT 2.0 model that was fine-tuned for multilingual ASR on 250k hours (the authors' earlier Whale model). It stays frozen. A trained adapter (conv2d downsampling and a linear layer) turns its 50 Hz continuous features into 12.5 Hz inputs for the LLM. The input side therefore uses continuous features, not discrete units.

The output side uses a learned low-bitrate tokenizer. A VQ-VAE-style quantizer reconstructs the 20th-layer embeddings of a pre-trained (self-supervised) W2v-BERT 2.0, the layer the authors found to carry the most semantic information. A strided convolution halves the frame rate to 25 Hz. A 3-layer Conformer encoder feeds a single 8192-entry codebook in a 512-dimensional space, and a symmetric transposed-convolution and Conformer decoder reconstructs the features. Training combines a cosine reconstruction loss with a commitment loss. The result is 13 bits at 25 Hz, or 325 bps, against 664 bps for SeamlessM4T-Large v2 units. Because there is only one codebook, the LLM predicts it with a standard next-token head; Qwen3's 156k vocabulary is extended with the 8192 VQ entries.

The LLM is Qwen3-8B-Base, fully fine-tuned on 150k hours of multitask data (ASR, speech-to-text translation (S2TT), S2ST, MT and TTS) in 11 languages, with translation limited to X-to-English. Tasks are selected by natural-language instructions. S2ST uses chain-of-thought decoding: the model first transcribes the source, then translates to target text, then emits target speech tokens, while still attending to the source speech features. Text uses beam search (width 3); speech tokens are sampled at temperature 1.0. S2ST training data mixes CoVoST2/CVSS-C and VoxPopuli with synthetic pairs made by an internal TTS model on both sides of NLLB MT corpora (filtered by speaker similarity, ASR and hallucination checks) and with speech pairs aligned by Speech Vecalign.

The decoder is the second conditioning path. Autowave-X is a GAN vocoder adapted from UniCATS (CTX-vec2wav) with a Vocos generator. It maps the VQ tokens to continuous features through a Q-decoder and conditions on 8th-layer W2v-BERT 2.0 embeddings of the source speech, since lower layers carry more speaker information. It trains with multi-period and multi-band discriminators, mel and feature-matching losses, and a multi-resolution spectral loss. An optional 16.5M-parameter iterative refiner, built on WaveFit and also conditioned on layer-8 source embeddings, trades extra steps for audio quality. The full system has 9.4B parameters.

## Key Results

Translation quality is close to the strongest comparable baselines on FLEURS and best among the compared systems on in-domain CVSS:

- **FLEURS X-En S2ST (Table 1):** Kraken averages 32.3 ASR-BLEU, against 32.1 for SeamlessM4T-Large v2 (2.3B) and 25.1 for Qwen2.5-Omni (7B). The margin over SeamlessM4T is 0.2 BLEU, and the two systems split the languages five to five (SeamlessM4T is higher on Ar, De, It, Ko and Pt). Qwen3-Omni (30B) is higher on average (33.6); Kraken leads it only on Hindi (33.6 vs 31.5) and Japanese (26.4 vs 25.6).
- **CVSS X-En S2ST (Table 5):** Kraken has the highest average, 43.5 ASR-BLEU, against 41.3 (SeamlessM4T), 40.9 (Qwen3-Omni) and 33.4 (Qwen2.5-Omni). It leads on every language except Portuguese, where SeamlessM4T scores 61.6 against 59.8. CVSS is in-domain for Kraken (CVSS-C is in its training data), so this comparison favours it.
- **S2TT (Table 2):** the intermediate text translation averages 33.1 BLEU, above Whisper-large-v3 (25.6), SeamlessM4T (32.3) and Qwen2.5-Omni (24.9), and below Qwen3-Omni (34.6).

Voice and prosody transfer is where the source conditioning shows. On FLEURS (Table 3), Kraken has the highest speaker similarity to the source (0.75 vs 0.66, 0.49 and 0.61), the highest emotion similarity (0.77 vs 0.76, 0.53 and 0.75) and the highest AutoPCP (2.48 vs 2.05, 2.40 and 2.19). Its UTMOS (3.13) is below both Qwen-Omni models (3.48 and 3.59), which speak in a clean default voice; the authors attribute part of this to FLEURS source audio quality being carried over. On CVSS (Table 7), Kraken keeps the highest speaker similarity (0.68) but ties SeamlessM4T on emotion similarity (0.70) and trails Qwen2.5-Omni on AutoPCP (2.32 vs 2.42). The baselines use a default speaker, so the similarity comparison measures a capability they are not attempting.

The ablations (Table 4, FLEURS) separate the two conditioning paths. Removing decoder conditioning raises UTMOS from 3.13 to 3.62 (above Qwen3-Omni) but lowers speaker similarity from 0.75 to 0.65. Removing LLM conditioning lowers speaker similarity to 0.73 and AutoPCP to 2.38, with UTMOS unchanged (3.14). Removing the refiner drops UTMOS to 2.70 with small similarity changes (AutoPCP rises slightly to 2.54).

The human evaluation (Table 6) uses 25 Japanese-to-English dubbing clips from MELD-ST, unseen in training, rated by three professional bilingual annotators on 5-point scales (Krippendorff's α 0.772 to 0.878). Kraken scores highest of the three systems on prosody naturalness (3.92), speaker match (2.82) and emotion match (3.13). Qwen3-Omni scores higher on audio quality (3.11 vs 1.84), translation quality (3.07 vs 2.91) and overall quality (2.40 vs 2.23). Kraken's audio quality is also below SeamlessM4T's (2.08). The sample is small: 25 clips and three raters, without significance tests.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is primarily engineering integration. Each component has a close precedent: VQ-VAE quantization of SSL features, a two-stage S2ST design whose second stage is conditioned on source speech (AudioPaLM with AudioLM or SoundStorm), a UniCATS vocoder with a Vocos generator, a WaveFit refiner, and chain-of-thought ASR-then-translate prompting from the authors' earlier work. What is new is the specific combination: a single-codebook 325 bps output that fits a plain LLM head, and a GAN vocoder in place of a codec LM as the source-conditioned second stage. The ablation that conditions source speech at both the LLM and the decoder is useful evidence. Training uses internal TTS-synthesized and internal ASR/TTS data, and no code, models or demos are released, so the results cannot be reproduced.

## Field Significance

Moderate — Kraken provides evidence that S2ST speech LLMs do not need multi-stream RVQ outputs to reach competitive translation quality, and that moving voice and prosody transfer into a source-conditioned vocoder can loosen the requirement for speaker-matched parallel data. Its gains are concentrated in speaker and emotion similarity; translation quality is on par with SeamlessM4T on out-of-domain data, and audio quality trails general-purpose omni models.

## Claims

- **supports:** A single-codebook, low-bitrate token stream derived from self-supervised speech features is sufficient for an LLM to generate intelligible translated speech, without hierarchical or multi-token prediction modules.
  > *Evidence:* Kraken predicts one 8192-entry VQ stream at 25 Hz (325 bps) from a standard LLM head and reaches 32.3 average ASR-BLEU on FLEURS X-En (SeamlessM4T-Large v2: 32.1) and 43.5 on CVSS X-En, above Qwen3-Omni (40.9), which uses about 2.2 kbps RVQ outputs. *(§2.2, §3.2, §4.2, Tables 1 and 5)*
- **supports:** In two-stage speech-to-speech generation, conditioning the token-to-waveform decoder on source speech transfers speaker identity that the token stream alone does not carry.
  > *Evidence:* Removing source conditioning from the Autowave-X decoder lowers speaker similarity from 0.75 to 0.65 on FLEURS; removing it from the LLM lowers it to 0.73 and AutoPCP from 2.48 to 2.38. *(§4.2, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Source-speech conditioning that improves speaker and prosody transfer also carries source recording quality into the output, lowering predicted naturalness.
  > *Evidence:* Without decoder conditioning, UTMOS rises from 3.13 to 3.62 while speaker similarity falls; with conditioning, Kraken's UTMOS stays below default-voice Qwen-Omni models on FLEURS (3.48 and 3.59), and the gap narrows on cleaner CVSS audio (3.35 vs 3.41 and 3.49). *(§4.2, Tables 3, 4 and 7)*
- **complicates:** Expressive speech translation systems can lead on speaker and emotion match in human dubbing evaluations while still trailing on perceived audio quality and overall quality.
  > *Evidence:* On 25 MELD-ST Ja-En clips, Kraken scores highest on prosody naturalness (3.92), speaker match (2.82) and emotion match (3.13), but lowest on audio quality (1.84) and below Qwen3-Omni on overall quality (2.23 vs 2.40). *(§4.2.1, Table 6)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

Translation covers only X-to-English for 10 languages; English-to-X and non-English pairs are not supported, so the output is always English speech. The model is offline; the authors argue the low-bitrate tokens and GAN vocoder suit streaming, but simultaneous S2ST is not tested. Full fine-tuning on fixed task templates removes the base LLM's general instruction following, and the authors expect it to fail on S2ST requests with advanced instructions.

The evaluation has gaps. The quantizer's reconstruction quality is never measured on its own, so the cost of the 325 bps bottleneck is visible only through the end-to-end scores. The FLEURS translation margin over SeamlessM4T is 0.2 ASR-BLEU, with no significance test, and each system leads on five of the ten languages. The baselines speak in default voices, so speaker and emotion similarity compare a voice-preserving system with systems that do not attempt preservation. The human study covers one direction (Ja-En), 25 clips and three raters. No code, model or demo is released, by design for misuse reasons, and much of the training data is internal or internally synthesized.
