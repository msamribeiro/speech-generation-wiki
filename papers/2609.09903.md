> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Haoyu Zhang et al.** (Northwestern Polytechnical University / NetEase) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09903) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✗
>
> Constrains a speech VAE's continuous latents to the unit hypersphere with a Power Spherical posterior, trading reconstruction quality for latents that a continuous autoregressive TTS model predicts with lower content error and less long-form speaker drift.

## Problem

Continuous latents (VAE outputs) avoid the quantization bottleneck of discrete codec tokens, which loses fine prosody, timbre and frame-to-frame continuity. But when an autoregressive model predicts continuous latents step by step, each prediction error is a shift in vector space that becomes the next step's condition, so errors can accumulate and push the generated sequence off the training distribution (the authors call this latent drift). In a Euclidean Gaussian VAE, both the norm and the direction of a latent carry information, so drift can grow along either. Prior work on continuous autoregressive TTS improves the generator, alignment or diffusion head; the authors argue that the geometry of the representation itself has not been targeted, and that reconstruction metrics alone do not tell whether a latent space is easy to predict over long horizons.

## Method

SphereVAE replaces the Gaussian posterior of a speech VAE with a Power Spherical distribution on the unit hypersphere S^(d-1). The encoder output is split into a direction branch, L2-normalized to give the mean direction µ, and a concentration branch giving κ = softplus(h_κ(x)) + 1 (so κ ≥ 1, which the authors use to avoid an under-concentrated posterior early in training). A latent is sampled by reparameterization, rescaled by √d, and decoded to a waveform. The KL term regularizes the posterior toward the uniform distribution on the hypersphere, so information must be carried by direction rather than norm (§3.1).

![Fig. 1. Overall architecture of SphereVAE. The encoder maps input speech into latent parameters; the direction branch is L2-normalized to lie on the unit hypersphere, and the Power Spherical posterior is regularized toward the uniform hyperspherical prior. The decoder reconstructs speech from the sampled latent representation.](assets/2609.09903/figure-1.png)

Training combines a DAC-style multi-scale mel-spectrogram L1 reconstruction loss, the hyperspherical KL term, and an adversarial loss plus feature matching from an EnCodec-style multi-scale STFT discriminator, with weights λ_adv = 1.0, λ_msspec = 15, λ_fm = 1.5 and λ_KL = 0.01 (§3.2, §4.1). The autoencoder follows the Mimi architecture with a SEANet encoder-decoder: 24 kHz input, downsampling rates [8, 6, 5, 4] (factor 960), giving a 25 Hz sequence of 64-dimensional latents. It is trained for 500k steps on 12-second segments (batch 32). Standard VAE, SemanticVAE and σ-VAE baselines use the same architecture and hyperparameters, so the comparison isolates the latent geometry (§4.1).

For downstream evaluation, each representation is used as the prediction target of a VoxCPM autoregressive backbone trained from scratch (500k iterations on four GPUs, with diffusion and stop-token losses weighted 1.0) in a zero-shot TTS setting. Both the autoencoders and the TTS models are trained on the Emilia Chinese and English subsets (§4.1). Model parameter counts are not reported.

## Key Results

**Reconstruction (Table 1, LibriSpeech-PC test-clean).** SphereVAE is the weakest of the four autoencoders on every reconstruction metric: STOI 0.9574 (VAE 0.9707), PESQ-WB 3.009 (VAE 3.47), MCD 2.443 (VAE 1.996), UTMOS 3.58 (VAE 3.966), SIM 0.673 (SemanticVAE 0.780, VAE 0.772) and WER 0.042 (VAE and SemanticVAE tied at 0.038). The authors attribute this to the reduced freedom of a fixed-norm latent and present it as an accepted cost.

**Zero-shot TTS (Table 2, SeedTTS-eval).** With SphereVAE latents, the VoxCPM-style model reaches the lowest content error of the four representations on both subsets: EN WER 5.305 (VAE 5.591, SemanticVAE 7.702, σ-VAE 9.442) and ZH CER 1.141 (SemanticVAE 1.241, VAE 1.327, σ-VAE 4.621). Speaker similarity is essentially tied among SphereVAE, VAE and SemanticVAE (EN 0.654 / 0.655 / 0.654; ZH 0.733 / 0.731 / 0.731), while σ-VAE is clearly lower (0.534 EN, 0.675 ZH).

**Long-text speaker retention (Table 3, Long-TTS-Eval).** Generated speech is cut into 3-second segments and each is compared with the prompt over the common 0-27 s window. SphereVAE has the highest first-segment SIM (0.667), last-segment SIM (0.447), mean SIM (0.591, vs VAE 0.567, SemanticVAE 0.562, σ-VAE 0.533) and late-segment SIM over 18-27 s (0.524, vs VAE 0.494, SemanticVAE 0.480, σ-VAE 0.478). ∆SIM is defined as first-segment minus last-segment SIM (lower is better): SphereVAE's drop (0.220) is smaller than VAE (0.243) and SemanticVAE (0.251), but σ-VAE has the smallest drop (0.179), which the authors read as a smaller decline from a lower starting level rather than better stability. Segments beyond 27 s were excluded because too few valid utterances remained for some models (§4.4).

**Latent analysis (§4.5, Figures 3-4).** On mean-pooled utterance latents from 40 LibriSpeech-PC speakers, SphereVAE gives better Silhouette and Davies-Bouldin scores than the standard VAE, and its PCA cumulative explained variance is higher for the same number of components, which the authors read as information concentrated in fewer dominant directions.

The comparisons are controlled (same autoencoder architecture, data and AR backbone), but there is no comparison against discrete-token systems or published VoxCPM checkpoints, no human listening test, and no confidence intervals in the tables.

## Novelty Assessment

The hyperspherical VAE and the Power Spherical distribution are existing tools, and the authors cite prior evidence that hyperspherical latents help continuous-token autoregressive generation in vision. The contribution is bringing this latent geometry to speech autoencoders and testing it specifically as a target for continuous autoregressive TTS, with a controlled comparison against three VAE variants and a long-form speaker-drift measurement. It is a change of training objective and latent parameterization on an unchanged Mimi/SEANet architecture, not a new generator. The evidence is a single AR backbone, one data mix and automatic metrics only, so the result is a promising, narrow data point rather than a general demonstration.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper isolates latent geometry as a design variable for continuous-latent autoregressive TTS and shows, under a controlled setup, that a representation which reconstructs worse can still be easier to predict, giving lower content error and better long-form speaker retention. It provides a concrete argument that continuous speech tokenizers for autoregressive generation should be judged on downstream predictability, not reconstruction alone. The authors state that the paper is accepted to NCMMSC 2026; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** Constraining continuous speech latents to a bounded geometry can make them easier for an autoregressive TTS model to predict, lowering content error rates.
  > *Evidence:* With the same VoxCPM-style backbone and training data, hyperspherical latents give EN WER 5.305 and ZH CER 1.141 on SeedTTS-eval, the lowest of four VAE variants (standard VAE 5.591 / 1.327), at near-identical speaker similarity. *(§4.3, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** Reconstruction fidelity of a speech tokenizer is a poor proxy for how well an autoregressive generator performs on its representation.
  > *Evidence:* The hyperspherical autoencoder is worst of the four on every reconstruction metric (e.g. PESQ-WB 3.009 vs 3.47, UTMOS 3.58 vs 3.966 for the standard VAE), yet yields the lowest downstream TTS content error. *(§4.2, Table 1; §4.3, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Error accumulation in continuous-latent autoregressive TTS shows up as speaker-similarity decay over long generations, and latent-space constraints can reduce it.
  > *Evidence:* On Long-TTS-Eval with 3 s segments compared to the prompt, the hyperspherical latent model keeps the highest mean SIM (0.591) and 18-27 s SIM (0.524), and a smaller first-to-last drop (0.220) than the standard VAE (0.243) and SemanticVAE (0.251). *(§4.4, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** A small first-to-last drop in speaker similarity is not on its own evidence of long-form stability, because a system that starts lower has less to lose.
  > *Evidence:* σ-VAE has the smallest drop (0.179) but also the lowest first-segment (0.571) and mean (0.533) SIM, so the authors read the drift metrics jointly rather than ranking on the drop alone. *(§4.4, Table 3)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

- All evaluation is automatic (WER/CER, WeSpeaker similarity, UTMOS); there is no human listening test of the generated speech, so the effect on perceived naturalness of TTS output is unknown.
- Only one autoregressive backbone (VoxCPM, retrained from scratch on Emilia ZH/EN) is tested. Whether the benefit holds for other continuous AR designs or larger data is open.
- The reconstruction cost is substantial (PESQ-WB about 0.46 lower and UTMOS about 0.39 lower than the standard VAE), which bounds the acoustic quality the decoder can deliver.
- The long-text analysis covers only 0-27 s because valid generated utterances drop sharply after that for some models; the setting the method targets (very long generations) is therefore only partly measured. Long-TTS-Eval texts are Chinese, so long-form evidence is single-language.
- The geometric analysis (t-SNE, PCA) is qualitative; the authors note that linking latent geometry quantitatively to drift metrics remains future work.
- Code is announced as forthcoming (a GitHub repository is linked) but not stated as released.
