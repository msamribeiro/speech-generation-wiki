> [!abstract] ACL · 2026 · Conference
> **Yifu Chen et al.** (Zhejiang University / Alibaba Group / Beijing University of Technology) · [→ Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.6) · [→ Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14920) · Demo: ? · Code: ?
>
> Trains an audio LLM to judge full-duplex spoken dialogue from dual-track audio, producing separate reasoning on semantic coherence and on turn-taking timing before a single binary reward, and shows it matches human labels more closely than zero-shot audio LLMs and commercial models on most test sets.

## Problem

Reinforcement learning for full-duplex spoken dialogue models needs a reward that judges both what the system says and when it says it. Existing automatic proxies cover only one side: oracle-aligned timing metrics are largely blind to content, behavioural statistics (overlap counts, gap lengths, as used with [[2410.00037|Moshi]]) count events without judging whether they were appropriate, and metric suites such as [[2503.04721|Full-Duplex-Bench]] give fragmented, high-latency signals that are hard to fuse into one online reward. LLM-as-judge evaluators, including the earlier [[2505.09558|WavReward]], were built for turn-based, single-channel dialogue and cannot perceive overlaps, gaps, and interruptions. Human evaluation is reliable but too slow and costly for an RL loop.

## Method

The paper first defines a taxonomy of dyadic interaction (§3.1). Each speaker's track is segmented into speech and silence; from these it derives pauses (intra-speaker silence), gaps (inter-speaker silence), and overlaps, and four functional events: smooth turn transition, successful interruption, backchannel, and failed interruption. Failures are organised on two axes: semantic errors (contextual incoherence, including "interruption amnesia", where the system loses the discourse state after being interrupted) and timing errors, split into over-reactive (inappropriate barge-in, overly deferential ceding to a backchannel) and under-reactive (delayed turn transition, ignored interruption). The taxonomy is grounded in public conversation corpora and about 10 hours of in-house human-machine dialogue annotation.

The reward model maps a dual-track dialogue to two parallel chain-of-thought analyses, one on semantic coherence and one on turn management, followed by a binary score that is 1 only if both axes are acceptable (§3.2). The authors argue a binary score matches user judgments and yields higher annotator agreement than Likert scales.

Training data is mostly synthetic: text dialogues from SODA, DialogSum, MultiWOZ 2.2 and UltraChat are rewritten by LLMs (Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1) to script specific events and errors, then rendered into dual-track audio with GPT-4o-mini-tts and Gemini TTS, giving programmatic timestamps for every overlap and silence (§3.2, Appendix A-B). The synthetic set has 6,361 samples (about 146 h), roughly 60% successful and 40% failure cases. Real data adds 100 human-human segments from Seamless Interaction, filtered with pyannote diarization and speaker-embedding checks and then manually annotated, and 289 human-machine dialogues collected by seven trained testers who deliberately provoked good and bad behaviour from GPT-4o voice mode, Gemini Live, and Doubao (Appendix C).

The backbone is [[2503.20215|Qwen2.5-Omni]]-7B, trained in three stages (§3.3, Table 5):

1. **SFT-1, interaction grounding** (4,904 samples, full model): multi-task supervised fine-tuning on event detection with timestamps, speaker diarization, and timestamped diarized transcription, so the model learns to perceive dual-track structure.
2. **SFT-2, reasoning distillation** (2,670 samples, full model): Gemini-2.5-Pro is given the text metadata (timestamps, transcripts, speakers) and generates structured CoT analyses and scores, which the model learns to produce from audio.
3. **GRPO** (3,513 synthetic scenarios plus the real-world data; LLM only, audio encoder frozen; K=4, about 400 steps): the reward is an equal-weighted sum of a format indicator (required think and score blocks present) and an accuracy indicator (score matches the ground-truth label).

## Key Results

All models predict the binary interaction-quality label zero-shot; metrics are accuracy and macro-F1 (§4.1, Table 1).

| Test set | Proposed (Acc / F1) | Best baseline (Acc / F1) | Base Qwen2.5-Omni (Acc / F1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-Distribution | 0.9853 / 0.9852 | Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.7473 / 0.7423 | 0.0733 / 0.0811 |
| Fine-Grained | 0.8500 / 0.8476 | Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.6750 / 0.6791 | 0.2500 / 0.2500 |
| Out-of-Distribution | 0.9614 / 0.9610 | Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.6781 / 0.6715 | 0.0858 / 0.0837 |
| RW-HH | 0.8679 / 0.6931 | GPT-4o 0.9245 / 0.6467 | 0.4906 / 0.4851 |
| RW-HM | 0.7727 / 0.7647 | GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Flash 0.6818 / 0.6818 | 0.5227 / 0.5227 |

The proposed model has the highest accuracy and F1 on the three synthetic sets and on RW-HM, and the highest macro-F1 on RW-HH. On RW-HH, however, GPT-4o has higher accuracy (0.9245 vs 0.8679). The paper notes that the human-human set is heavily imbalanced towards fluent conversations (Appendix C.2), so accuracy there rewards a judge that rates most samples as fine; macro-F1 is the more informative column, and the proposed model's lead on it is modest (0.6931 vs 0.6467). The confusion matrices in Figure 4 show GPT-4o labelling most errors as correct, while the proposed model separates semantic, over-reactive, and under-reactive errors. The real-world test sets are small (53 and 44 samples), so the gaps there are sensitive to a handful of items.

In a blind human-expert review of CoT outputs on 40 snippets (Table 2), the proposed model has the highest consistency with a reference rationale on a 5-point scale (4.125 vs 3.95 for Gemini-2.5-Pro and 2.5 for GPT-4o) and is picked as the best reasoning in 55% of cases (Gemini-2.5-Pro 40%, GPT-4o 5%). Only two baselines were included in this comparison.

The ablation (Table 3) shows each stage matters, and in a non-monotonic way:

- SFT-2 alone gives 64.96% ID, 45.28% RW-HH, 63.64% RW-HM.
- Adding SFT-1 before it raises RW-HH to 84.91% and RW-HM to 72.73%, but ID is only 76.64%.
- Adding GRPO on synthetic data only raises ID to 97.45% but lowers RW-HH to 83.02% and RW-HM to 56.82%, which the authors attribute to overfitting to synthetic patterns.
- Skipping SFT-1 (SFT-2 + GRPO) collapses RW-HM to 38.64%.
- Adding the small real-world set to GRPO gives the full model's 98.54% ID, 86.79% RW-HH, and 77.27% RW-HM.

## Novelty Assessment

The model and RL algorithm are existing components (Qwen2.5-Omni, SFT, CoT distillation from Gemini, GRPO with a rule-based accuracy reward). What is new is the problem framing and data: a taxonomy that pairs full-duplex timing events with content errors, a synthetic pipeline that injects each failure type with exact timestamps, and a judge whose reasoning is split into a semantic axis and a timing axis. Its predecessor [[2505.09558|WavReward]] judged turn-based dialogue; this paper's contribution is extending generative reward modelling to dual-track, overlapping speech. Despite the framing as a reward model for RL, the paper never uses it to train a dialogue model, so its value as a reward signal is untested. The comparison is also lopsided: the proposed model is trained on the same taxonomy and label definition it is scored on, while all baselines are zero-shot with a prompt.

## Field Significance

Moderate. The paper gives a concrete taxonomy and labelled dual-track test sets for judging full-duplex interaction quality, and shows that general audio LLMs, including commercial ones, perceive turn-taking violations poorly without targeted training. It is an evaluation and data contribution; its central motivation (an online RL reward for full-duplex models) is still unvalidated.

## Claims

- **supports:** Zero-shot audio LLM judges fail to perceive turn-taking violations in full-duplex dialogue and tend to rate flawed interactions as acceptable.
  > *Evidence:* Zero-shot open-source audio LLMs, including the untrained base model, score 0.07-0.44 accuracy on the synthetic in-distribution set; GPT-4o misclassifies most error types as correct in the fine-grained confusion matrix, and in a case study GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro both describe an inappropriate barge-in as smooth or natural. *(§4.2, Table 1, Figure 4, Figure 5)*
- **supports:** Explicit training on dual-track interaction structure (event detection, diarization, timestamped transcription) is necessary for an audio LLM to judge real conversational timing.
  > *Evidence:* Removing the SFT-1 grounding stage drops RW-HM accuracy from 77.27% to 38.64% and RW-HH from 86.79% to 77.36% (SFT-2 + GRPO), and SFT-2 alone reaches only 45.28% on RW-HH. *(§4.2, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Interaction-quality judges trained mainly on TTS-synthesised dialogue overfit to synthetic patterns and need some real recorded interactions to generalise.
  > *Evidence:* GRPO on synthetic data alone raises in-distribution accuracy to 97.45% but cuts RW-HM accuracy to 56.82%, below the 72.73% of the SFT-only model; adding 389 real samples to GRPO restores RW-HM to 77.27%. *(§4.2, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Accuracy on naturally imbalanced human-human conversation data can favour weak judges that rate most interactions as fluent, so balanced metrics are needed to compare dialogue evaluators.
  > *Evidence:* On RW-HH, GPT-4o reaches 0.9245 accuracy but only 0.6467 macro-F1, while the trained model scores 0.8679 accuracy and 0.6931 macro-F1; the paper notes the human-human data is highly imbalanced towards fluent conversations. *(§4.2, Table 1; §Appendix C.2)*
- **complicates:** Interaction-quality reward models for full-duplex speech LMs are often validated only as offline classifiers, leaving their benefit as an RL training signal undemonstrated.
  > *Evidence:* The authors state they could not integrate the model into an online RL loop, so no dialogue model is optimised with the proposed reward. *(§Limitations)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The model is presented as a reward for online RL of full-duplex dialogue models, but it is never used to train one (§Limitations). All evidence is offline agreement with labels, so whether it improves a policy, or is exploitable by one, is unknown.

- The real-world test sets are small (53 human-human and 44 human-machine samples), so differences of a few points there correspond to a few samples.
- Training and in-distribution test data are generated with the same LLM-rewrite and commercial-TTS pipeline, and the CoT targets are distilled from Gemini-2.5-Pro working from text metadata, so judgments may be tied to these synthesis artefacts and to the teacher's reasoning.
- The binary score is coarse; the authors suggest separate semantic and timing scores or graded scores as future work, but neither is evaluated.
- The CoT quality study compares only against GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro and does not report inter-rater agreement or the number of experts.
- The real-world human-machine data covers three commercial systems and English speakers only.
