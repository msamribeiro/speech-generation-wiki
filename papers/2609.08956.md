> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Yi-Chang Chen et al.** (MediaTek Research / National Taiwan University / NVIDIA) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08956) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> Turns the utterance-level TASTE text-aligned speech language model into an incremental full-duplex dialogue stack, in which every text token carries exactly one continuous audio latent, so the speech LM's sequence never grows beyond the text length while it still emits acoustic information at every step.

## Problem

Full-duplex spoken conversational agents must process speech as it arrives, decide when to take or yield the floor, and stop when interrupted, while keeping the linguistic competence of the pretrained text LLM. Systems that place acoustic tokens in their own sequence positions (parallel streams in Mini-Omni, multi-codebook streams in Moshi, word-level interleaving in SpiritLM) dilute the text regularities the backbone learned, and accuracy retention drops accordingly. The predecessor TASTE (Text-Aligned Speech Tokenization and Embedding, Tseng et al., ICLR 2026) avoided the length mismatch by aggregating one speech representation per text token, but it was not built for dialogue: its Whisper-based tokenizer and LLaMA backbone used different vocabularies bridged by word-level averaging, its CosyVoice synthesis stack only ran on complete utterances, and it was never tested on turn-taking, interruption handling or explicit paralinguistic control (§1, §3.1.1).

## Method

TASTE2 keeps the TASTE three-component design but rebuilds each part around a single shared text-token vocabulary (§3.1.2). The **Speech Tokenizer** uses a frozen Distil-Whisper encoder; its decoder (an attention-based aggregator initialised from the Whisper decoder) has its input embedding replaced by the Qwen2.5 vocabulary, its LM head removed, and outputs one continuous embedding per text token, which passes through a randomly initialised Residual Vector Quantizer (RVQ). Because the tokenizer, speech LM and detokenizer now share one vocabulary, word-level averaging and language-dependent word segmentation disappear. The **Spoken LM** (Qwen2.5-7B or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone, LoRA) adds each text-token embedding and its aligned audio-latent embedding by a weighted sum at the same position, and predicts the next text token and then (with a one-position shift) its audio latent; the audio target is the final RVQ-synthesised latent rather than discrete indices (§3.3.1). The **Speech Detokenizer**, adapted from the CosyVoice2 Qwen2.5-0.5B text-to-S3 model, autoregressively maps text-token/audio-latent pairs to 25 Hz S3 units in chunks (15 S3 units per 5 input pairs), which the frozen CosyVoice2 chunk-aware causal flow-matching model and vocoder turn into audio, enabling streaming synthesis (§3.1.3).

Training has three stages (§3.3). Stage 1 jointly trains the tokenizer and detokenizer on Emilia (40,000 h English) and LibriTTS (600 h); Stage 2 adapts the text LLM into the Spoken LM with LoRA on the same data; Stage SFT fine-tunes it on 1,493 h of dialogue: DeepDialogue (466 h), 778 h of synthetic general conversations with interruptions and disfluencies (GPT-4o and Llama-3.3-70B scripts, CosyVoice2 synthesis) and 249 h of synthetic paralinguistic-control dialogues (IndexTTS emotion-vector synthesis) (§3.2, Table 1). Two instruction-following initialisation paths are compared: **TASTE2 (Merge)** pretrains from the base Qwen2.5-7B and then adds a TIES-sparsified instruct task vector (top 30% of parameters, scale 0.5) before SFT; **TASTE2 (Direct)** pretrains from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with no merging (§3.3.2).

SFT uses the native Qwen ChatML template: system segments carry zero-filled audio latents, user segments pair ASR transcripts with tokenizer latents, assistant segments pair response text with ground-truth latents (§3.3.3). An **interruption-aware loss** sets the loss weight of the terminal `<|im_end|>` token to zero on assistant turns truncated at a user barge-in, while complete turns keep it. Turn-taking is **model-gated**: the learned probability of `<|im_end|>` after the user's tokens serves as a semantic end-of-turn signal, with no separate classifier.

**TASTE2 VoiceBot** (§4) deploys this as four WebSocket services: a User Speech Processor (VAD on 100 ms chunks, streaming ASR, incremental TASTE tokenization in 600 ms batches, barge-in detection on silence-to-voice transitions), an Agent Response Generator (runs the `<|im_end|>` gate only after a 100 ms VAD silence, using top-k (k = 800) or probability-threshold (b = 0.3) tests plus one-token lookahead after punctuation), an Agent Speech Generator (streaming detokenization and vocoding), and an Orchestrator. Audio is delivered by client pull, and response tokens enter the dialogue history only after their audio has been delivered, so after an interruption the context contains only what the user actually heard.

## Key Results

**Semantic retention (§5.1, Table 2).** On 300 LLaMA-Questions items with spoken input, TASTE2 (Merge) reaches 56.3% answer-containment accuracy against 57.3% for its text-only Qwen2.5-7B Instruct reference (98.2% retention, defined as spoken accuracy divided by text accuracy; higher is better), and TASTE2 (Direct) reaches 53.0% (92.4%). Retention figures quoted for other systems (Mini-Omni 29.7%, Moshi 83.1%, Llama-Omni 93.9%) come from published setups with different backbones and prompting protocols, which the authors state are not directly comparable. The absolute accuracy of TASTE2 is below Moshi (62.3%) and Llama-Omni (67.3%), because its text reference is itself weaker.

**RVQ ablation (§5.2, Figure 5).** Removing the RVQ bottleneck raises Stage 1 S3 reconstruction accuracy (46.83% vs 23.56%) but makes the latent space unlearnable in Stage 2: latent L1 distance stays at 14,573 versus 1.35, and text accuracy collapses to 9.16% versus 38.67%, because the text and latent targets share one forward pass.

**Full-Duplex-Bench v1.0 (§5.3, Table 3).** TASTE2 (Merge) has the lowest synthetic pause-handling takeover rate (TOR 0.146; next best is TASTE2 (Direct) at 0.161, Gemini Live 0.255) and the lowest user-interruption latency (0.060 s; TASTE2 (Direct) 0.134 s, Moshi 0.257 s), with an interruption TOR of 1.000, tied with Moshi. Its post-interruption GPT-4o coherence score (3.275) is below Freeze-Omni (3.615) and Gemini Live (3.376) but far above Moshi (0.765) and dGSLM (0.201). On natural CANDOR pauses, Gemini Live (0.310) is best, with TASTE2 (Direct) at 0.319 and TASTE2 (Merge) at 0.389. Smooth turn-taking TOR is 0.908 for Merge (dGSLM 0.975 and Moshi 0.941 are higher), but smooth turn-taking latency is 1.207 s against 0.265 s for Moshi, the direct cost of waiting for VAD speech end before querying the gate. Backchannel metrics are not reported, because the deployed gate produces no listener feedback.

**Deployed latency (§5.4, Table 4).** Averaged over single measurements at five input lengths, TASTE2 VoiceBot's time to first audio (TTFA) is 2,701 ms with TensorRT (3,469 ms without, a 22% reduction) on two RTX A6000 GPUs, compared with 1,370 ms for ChatGPT Voice, 2,310 ms for Grok and 4,032 ms for Gemini Live measured through their mobile apps. The Agent Speech Generator is the main bottleneck.

**Paralinguistic control (§5.5, Table 5).** Three human raters scored appropriateness on a 1-5 scale. In a Stage 2 continuation probe, fast (4.87) and slow (4.27) rate, happy (3.80), sad (3.56), surprise (3.33) and whisper (3.27) clear the 3-point threshold, while fear (2.40) and angry (2.00) do not. After SFT, on a 41-item VStyle acoustic-attribute subset, only fast rate exceeds 3 for both variants (Merge 4.15, Direct 3.53); slow exceeds it for Direct (3.77) and reaches exactly 3.00 for Merge; angry (3.33) and surprise (3.42) clear it only for Direct; happy, sad, fear and whisper fall below 3 under both strategies, with whisper at 1.00.

## Novelty Assessment

The core contribution is an engineering redesign of TASTE rather than a new paradigm: the text-aligned one-latent-per-token idea, the three-component structure and the two-stage pretraining recipe are inherited. What is new is the shared-vocabulary tokenizer that removes word-level averaging, the interruption-aware `<|im_end|>` loss masking and its reuse as a classifier-free turn gate, the chunked streaming detokenizer into CosyVoice2's S3 space, and the delivery-synchronised dialogue history in the deployed system. The RVQ ablation is a useful, clearly reported finding (quantization matters for predictability, not fidelity). The model-merging versus direct-instruct comparison is a controlled two-way contrast. Synthesis reuses frozen CosyVoice2 components, and the paralinguistic evaluation is small (41 items, three raters, single runs).

## Field Significance

Moderate: TASTE2 provides a working, open full-duplex system built on text-aligned speech modeling. It demonstrates that keeping the speech LM's sequence at text length while emitting a continuous acoustic latent per token can retain most of the text backbone's question-answering accuracy, and that a text-token dialogue state allows exact truncation at interruption points with very fast stopping. It is equally useful as a cautionary data point: model-gated turn-taking costs over a second of latency, the deployed time to first audio is well behind a commercial assistant, and explicit paralinguistic control does not carry over reliably from pretraining to dialogue fine-tuning.

## Claims

- **supports:** Pairing each text token with a single aligned acoustic latent, rather than inserting acoustic tokens as additional sequence positions, lets a speech LM generate acoustic information at every step while retaining most of its text backbone's reasoning accuracy.
  > *Evidence:* TASTE2 (Merge) keeps the sequence at text length and reaches 56.3% on LLaMA-Questions against 57.3% for its Qwen2.5-7B Instruct reference (98.2% retention), while published interleaved or parallel-stream systems retain 29.7% (Mini-Omni) and 83.1% (Moshi) under non-identical protocols. *(§5.1, Table 2)*
- **refines:** When a speech LM regresses continuous acoustic latents, a quantization bottleneck on the target space matters for learnability rather than reconstruction fidelity, and an unlearnable acoustic target also degrades the jointly predicted text.
  > *Evidence:* Removing RVQ doubles Stage 1 S3 reconstruction accuracy (46.83% vs 23.56%) but leaves Stage 2 latent L1 distance at 14,573 versus 1.35 and cuts text accuracy to 9.16% versus 38.67%. *(§5.2, Figure 5)*
- **refines:** Part of the semantic loss incurred when adapting a text LLM into a speech LM can come from how instruction-following ability is introduced, not from the speech representation itself.
  > *Evidence:* With identical tokenizer, speech objectives, dialogue data and SFT, merging an instruct task vector into a base-pretrained speech LM retains 98.2% of text accuracy, while pretraining directly from the instruct model retains 92.4%. *(§3.3.2, §5.1, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** Deriving turn-end decisions from a dialogue LM's end-of-turn token probability, gated by voice-activity detection, gives accurate turn and interruption handling but adds substantial turn-transition latency.
  > *Evidence:* TASTE2 (Merge) stops on every interruption within 0.060 s and reaches a smooth turn-taking TOR of 0.908, but its smooth turn-taking latency is 1.207 s versus 0.265 s for Moshi, and deployed TTFA averages 2,701 ms against 1,370 ms for ChatGPT Voice. *(§5.3, Table 3; §5.4, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Paralinguistic attributes a speech LM can continue after speech pretraining do not reliably remain controllable by explicit instruction after dialogue fine-tuning, and emotion control can depend on the fine-tuning initialisation.
  > *Evidence:* Whisper scores 3.27 in the Stage 2 continuation probe but 1.00 on request after SFT for both variants; happy and sad fall from 3.80 and 3.56 to at most 2.00; angry reaches 3.33 under TASTE2 (Direct) but 1.33 under TASTE2 (Merge); only fast speaking rate exceeds 3 under both strategies (slow reaches exactly 3.00 under TASTE2 (Merge)). *(§5.5, Table 5)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The deployed system is not yet full-duplex in the strong sense: the turn gate runs only after VAD detects speech end, so VoiceBot never speaks during user speech, produces no backchannels (backchannel metrics are absent from Table 3), and active overlap behaviours are not deployed. Smooth turn-taking takes more than one second, and average time to first audio (2.7 s even with TensorRT) remains well above ChatGPT Voice; the authors locate the bottleneck in the Agent Speech Generator.

The semantic-retention comparison uses a weaker text reference (57.3%) than the baselines it is set against, and the other systems' retention values come from published setups with different protocols, so the cross-system ranking is indicative only. Only one spoken QA benchmark is used, and English is the only pretraining language.

The paralinguistic study is small: 41 VStyle items (one whisper item, four items per emotion), five openings per feature in the continuation probe, three raters, no inter-rater agreement reported, and scores averaged only over valid outputs; it cannot be compared with published VStyle results. Implicit paralinguistic adaptation (inferring a suitable style from the user's voice without instruction) is not evaluated. TTFA comparisons rely on one sample per input length, and commercial systems were measured through mobile apps on unknown hardware. Synthesis quality of the generated speech (naturalness, intelligibility, speaker consistency) is not measured. Much of the SFT data is synthetic (CosyVoice2 and IndexTTS outputs from LLM-written scripts), which may limit robustness on natural conversation, consistent with the weaker CANDOR results.
