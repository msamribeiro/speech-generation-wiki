> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Xiangyu Zhang et al.** (The University of New South Wales / Nanyang Technological University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29209) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✗
>
> Argues that a low character or word error rate is a misleading proxy for speech-token quality: a 5.0Hz semantic tokenizer that keeps low CER and strong audio-QA accuracy still yields badly blurred spectrogram reconstructions, even when the decoder is given oracle durations.

## Problem

Speech LMs increasingly pursue a single "unified" discrete token stream for both understanding and generation, and push it toward low frame rates to save LLM context. Semantic tokenizers derived from Whisper-style encoders are mainly validated by WER, and the authors argue the field implicitly assumes that a low-WER token also carries what a continuous generative decoder needs. Testing this at extreme compression is hard because fixed-stride downsampling (strided convolution or pooling) cuts across phonetic boundaries: WER rises sharply at very low frame rates, so any generation failure can be blamed on corrupted alignment rather than on what the tokens fundamentally lack.

## Method

The authors build a dynamic compression tokenizer to obtain ultra-low-rate tokens whose CER stays low, then probe the same tokens for understanding and for generation (the "Dual-Probing Protocol").

**Tokenizer.** A pre-trained Whisper-style Transformer encoder (32 layers, 1280 hidden dim, 128-bin log-Mel input at 16kHz, 50Hz features) feeds a Dynamic Merge Module modelled on continuous integrate-and-fire (CIF). A 1D convolution, linear projection, and sigmoid predict a per-frame information weight; frames are soft-accumulated and a token is emitted each time the running sum crosses 1.0. To pin the frame rate, the weights are globally rescaled so that they sum exactly to N = round(T/R) for a fixed compression ratio R ("macroscopic fixed-ratio, microscopic dynamic alignment"), and a quantity loss (L1 between the unscaled weight sum and N) guides the predictor. With R = 10 the merged tokens run at 5.0Hz. They are quantised with finite scalar quantization (FSQ, 7 dimensions with 4 levels, 16,384 codes). Training is end-to-end with a CTC loss and an attention cross-entropy loss through a 32-layer Whisper-style decoder, both supervised by transcripts, plus an optional mel-reconstruction MSE loss from a lightweight Recon Decoder (3-layer 1D ResNet with Snake activations, Appendix A). Training data is LibriSpeech, GigaSpeech, AISHELL, and other unspecified open-source English and Mandarin corpora. Parameter count is not reported.

**Oracle upsampling.** For generation, the cumulative scaled weights map each of the T original frames back to its source token, so the decoder receives a token sequence exactly aligned to the original frame timing. This is meant to remove duration and boundary errors as an explanation for poor reconstruction.

**Probes.** The discriminative probe projects FSQ codes into a frozen Llama 3.1 8B through a trainable audio projector and classification head, and scores multiple-choice audio QA (AVQA). The generative probe is described in §5 and Appendix B.2 as a flow-matching decoder conditioned on the oracle-upsampled tokens, predicting log-Mel spectrograms from a Gaussian prior. No speaker embedding, reference prompt, or vocoder is described.

## Key Results

**CER (Table 1, WenetSpeech Test_Net / Test_Meeting).** Fixed-stride Whisper + FSQ degrades from 18.66 / 20.46 at 12.5Hz to 29.80 / 31.50 at 5.5Hz. The dynamic tokenizer stays much lower: 14.47 / 16.14 at 6.0Hz, 14.32 / 15.94 at 5.0Hz with the reconstruction loss, 11.98 / 12.50 at 5.0Hz without it ("Pure Semantic"), and 15.61 / 17.44 at 4.0Hz. The Pure Semantic variant has the lowest CER of all compressed rows on both sets. Against the uncompressed Whisper v3 reference (9.68 / 18.54), it is worse on Test_Net and better on Test_Meeting; how the reference was trained or fine-tuned is not described. The 6.0Hz variant has slightly higher CER than the 5.0Hz one, so error does not fall monotonically with frame rate.

**AVQA accuracy (Table 2, frozen Llama 3.1 8B).** The Pure Semantic 5.0Hz variant scores 0.7246, the highest in the table, and the 5.0Hz variant with reconstruction scores 0.7139. Both exceed the high-rate baselines ([[2408.16532|WavTokenizer]] 0.6732, DAC 0.6561, [[2308.16692|SpeechTokenizer]] 0.5839), the syllable-unit SyllableLM (0.5526), and fixed-stride FSQ at about 5.5Hz (0.5777). Against that framing, the 4.0Hz dynamic variant (0.6526) falls below WavTokenizer and DAC. The baselines are codec tokenizers whose configuration inside this probe (codebooks used, projector capacity) is not described, and WavTokenizer, DAC, and SpeechTokenizer are not in the reference list.

**Generative probe (Figure 4, Table 3).** For one example utterance, the reconstruction matches the source duration (ratio 0.9995) and keeps a rough spectral envelope (mel correlation 0.6285), but shows large frame-wise and temporal-difference errors (mel MAE 11.11, delta-mel MAE 7.90, spectral-flux MAE 4.04). Over 495 utterances (Table 3) the medians are mel MAE 14.99 (IQR 13.59 to 16.68), mel correlation 0.471 (0.390 to 0.543), delta-mel MAE 6.41, flux MAE 3.28, and duration ratio 0.9978 (0.9965 to 0.9989). The authors describe the output as "effectively unintelligible", but they report no ASR-based WER/CER, MOS, or other perceptual score for the reconstructed speech, stating that the mel-domain errors make MOS unnecessary.

## Novelty Assessment

The main contribution is diagnostic: it puts forward a negative claim (low WER does not mean a token is enough for generation) and a probing protocol (the same tokens evaluated by a frozen-LLM understanding probe and an oracle-aligned generative probe). The tokenizer itself mostly adapts existing parts (Whisper encoder and decoder, CIF-style integrate-and-fire merging, FSQ, hybrid CTC/attention training). Its specific change is to rescale weights globally so the frame rate stays fixed, which is close to CIF's own length-scaling strategy. The result that semantic-only tokens at very low rates lose fine acoustic detail fits the motivation behind existing semantic-acoustic decoupled tokenizers such as [[2506.23325|XY-Tokenizer]], which the paper cites. What is new is the attempt to remove alignment and semantic scarcity as confounds before attributing the failure to the representation. The evidence for that attribution is limited (see Limitations).

## Field Significance

moderate — The paper gives a clear warning against treating WER/CER as the main quality signal for speech tokens meant to drive synthesis, and a reusable two-probe recipe for testing understanding and generation adequacy on the same tokens. It also shows that CIF-style dynamic merging with a fixed global ratio keeps CER far lower than fixed-stride pooling at about 5Hz. The generation-side evidence lacks both a high-rate control and any intelligibility measurement, so the paper is better read as a cautionary data point than as a demonstration that unified tokens cannot work.

## Claims

- **complicates:** Low ASR error rate on discrete speech tokens is not sufficient evidence that the tokens can condition intelligible continuous acoustic generation; understanding adequacy and generation adequacy need separate measurements.
  > *Evidence:* 5.0Hz dynamic FSQ tokens reach 14.32 / 15.94 CER on WenetSpeech Test_Net / Test_Meeting and 0.7139 AVQA accuracy, yet oracle-aligned spectrogram reconstruction from them shows a median mel correlation of 0.471 and median delta-mel MAE of 6.41 over 495 utterances. *(§6.2–§6.3, Tables 1–3)*
- **supports:** At very low token rates, content-adaptive (integrate-and-fire style) merging preserves linguistic content much better than fixed-stride downsampling.
  > *Evidence:* Fixed-stride Whisper + FSQ at 5.5Hz gives 29.80 / 31.50 CER and 0.5777 AVQA accuracy, while dynamic merging at 5.0Hz gives 14.32 / 15.94 CER and 0.7139 AVQA. *(§6.2, Tables 1–2)*
- **refines:** Ultra-low-rate semantic tokens keep utterance-level timing and coarse spectral envelope but lose the frame-to-frame spectral transitions tied to articulation, so the reconstruction error concentrates in temporal-difference measures rather than in duration.
  > *Evidence:* Under oracle upsampling, the median duration ratio is 0.9978 (IQR 0.9965 to 0.9989), while the reconstructions keep substantial delta-mel (median 6.41) and spectral-flux (median 3.28) errors; error maps show the deviations at rapid spectral transitions across short, medium, and long utterances. *(Appendix C–D, Figure 5, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Training a semantic tokenizer with an added acoustic reconstruction objective can cost linguistic accuracy, which suggests a tension between the two objectives within one token stream.
  > *Evidence:* At 5.0Hz, removing the mel-reconstruction loss lowers CER from 14.32 to 11.98 (Test_Net) and from 15.94 to 12.50 (Test_Meeting) and raises AVQA accuracy from 0.7139 to 0.7246. *(§6.2, Tables 1–2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The generative probe has no positive control: no higher-rate token sequence (for example 25Hz or 50Hz from the same encoder) is reconstructed with the same decoder. The collapse therefore cannot be attributed specifically to low frame rate or to the semantic objective rather than to the probe decoder's capacity. Intelligibility is also never measured on the generated speech (no ASR WER/CER, MOS, or listening test). The "unintelligible" verdict rests on mel-domain error statistics.

- The paper describes two generation paths: a flow-matching decoder (§5, Appendix B.2) and a jointly trained 3-layer ResNet Recon Decoder with MSE loss (§4.3–4.4, Figure 2, Appendix A). It does not say clearly which one produced Figure 4, Figure 5, and Table 3.
- The generative decoder gets no speaker embedding, reference prompt, or other acoustic side information. Semantic-token synthesis pipelines often condition the decoder on such information, so the probe may test a stricter condition than the unified-token systems the paper critiques.
- Only mel spectrograms are reconstructed; no vocoder or waveform output is described.
- All evaluation is in Mandarin (WenetSpeech) even though English data is used in training. The authors argue that a tonal language is a demanding test case, but they do not verify this.
- The AVQA setup is only loosely specified: the source of the AVQA data is not identified, and neither is how the codec baselines (DAC, WavTokenizer, SpeechTokenizer) were fed to the projector. The 4.0Hz dynamic variant scores below two of these baselines.
- No code, checkpoints, or audio samples are released. The paper is diagnostic and proposes no decoupled architecture of its own.
