> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Xianhao Zhou et al.** (UESTC / Monash University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00545) · Demo: ? · Code: ✓
>
> A paired audit of natural-language voice control in three reference-conditioned TTS systems shows that outputs which move in the requested direction usually also change pitch, timing, energy or spectral measures outside the request, and a training-free candidate selector (VoDER-Cal) reduces that collateral change within a fixed three-sample budget.

## Problem

Instruction-conditioned TTS (natural-language voice descriptions such as "deep", "bright" or "rough") is usually evaluated by asking whether the output matches the description: style preference, naturalness, intelligibility, speaker similarity, or instruction-following benchmarks such as [[2506.16381|InstructTTSEval]] and the prompt-adherence metric [[2601.05554|SPAM]]. These measures say whether a system responds to a prompt. They do not say whether it changes only the requested attribute while the rest of the voice stays stable. A "deep" request can lower F0 but also slow the speech, make it quieter, darken the spectrum, or move it away from the reference speaker. The paper treats prompt adherence and attribute-specific control as distinct capabilities and asks what changes outside the requested attribute.

## Method

**Paired audit.** Three reference-conditioned systems are audited through their native control interfaces: [[2505.17589|CosyVoice 3]] (Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B, instruction plus reference speech), [[2606.06928|VoxCPM2]] (inline instruction plus reference) and [[2603.08823|Fish Audio S2]] (the `s2-pro` checkpoint, called Fish-Speech-S2 in the paper, driven by its native inline voice tag rather than a free-form instruction channel) *(§3.1, Table 3)*. Each system runs the same factorial matrix: 6 reference speakers, 10 English texts, 3 random seeds and 11 conditions (a neutral baseline, six descriptors, four controls), giving 1,980 outputs per system and 5,940 overall. Every descriptor-conditioned output is paired with a neutral output from the same system, speaker, text and nominal seed. The primary claims cover only deep, bright and rough. Warm, young, professional and the identity-preservation control are supplementary.

**Measurements.** Eight signal-level features are extracted from each output: F0 (in semitones), speaking rate, duration, RMS energy, spectral centroid, 85% roll-off, spectral flatness and zero-crossing rate. Each descriptor gets a pre-registered target set with an expected direction: deep = {F0 down, roll-off down}, bright = {centroid up, roll-off up}, rough = {flatness up, ZCR up} *(§3.2)*. Everything outside the target set counts as off-target. Paired changes are normalized by fixed physical scales shared across systems (for example 3 semitones for F0, 0.8 words/s for rate, 220 Hz for centroid) *(Table 4)*. Content validity comes from normalized ASR transcripts ([[2212.04356|Whisper]] is the cited recognizer), and speaker preservation from [[2005.07143|ECAPA-TDNN]] cosine similarity to the reference, with x-vector similarity as a cross-check. Both thresholds are fixed on a calibration split *(§3.3)*.

**Statistics and controls.** Inference is at the request level (system, descriptor, speaker, text). The three seed deltas are averaged within a request, and request blocks are resampled with 10,000 bootstrap replicates. A conditional analysis keeps only outputs whose target score exceeds a one-sided noise threshold derived from baseline seed variation. It then counts outputs with at least one off-target change of at least half its normalization scale *(§3.4, §11.3)*. Four controls are run: a neutral reading instruction, a nonsense descriptor, an identity-preservation instruction and an explicit lower-pitch instruction (a positive control).

**VoDER-Cal.** For each request, B descriptor-conditioned candidates are compared with the neutral baseline. A direction-aligned target score T averages the normalized target-feature changes. A candidate is feasible if T exceeds both the noise threshold and a fraction ρ of the strongest target score in the pool, and if it passes the content and speaker checks. Among feasible candidates, VoDER-Cal picks the one with the smallest mean absolute normalized change on a selector-side set of non-target features. If no candidate is feasible, it abstains *(§5.1)*. Evaluation uses a separate, non-overlapping set of held-out off-target features. The main operating point is B = 3, ρ = 0.75. The method needs no retraining and no access to model internals.

## Key Results

**Responses are directional but not isolated.** For deep, outputs move in the expected target direction in 84.4% (CosyVoice3), 77.2% (VoxCPM2) and 71.1% (Fish-Speech-S2) of pairs. Bright and rough are weaker and less consistent (44.4% to 71.1%) *(§4.1, Table 1)*. Content is preserved in at least 99.4% of outputs, and mean speaker similarity is 0.821 to 0.902. The off-target movement therefore occurs mostly in outputs that keep both content and speaker identity. In most system-descriptor settings, several non-target features have request-level confidence intervals that exclude zero (for example 5/6 or 6/6 for CosyVoice3, versus 1/6 to 4/6 for VoxCPM2 and Fish-Speech-S2) *(Table 1)*. Systems realize the same descriptor differently. CosyVoice3's deep response also changes rate, duration, energy, centroid and roll-off. VoxCPM2's bright response is carried more by energy than by the spectral targets *(§4.1, Figure 2)*.

**Conditioning on a clear response does not remove collateral change.** In the eight settings with at least ten above-noise responsive outputs, 54.5% (Fish-Speech-S2 bright, the lowest) to 95.8% (VoxCPM2 deep, the highest) of responsive outputs show at least one substantial off-target deviation *(§4.2, Table 5)*. Fish-Speech-S2 deep has only two responsive outputs under the composite threshold, so the authors treat it as descriptive only.

**Controls.** The explicit lower-pitch instruction lowers mean F0 in all three systems (−7.33 Hz CosyVoice3, −7.17 Hz VoxCPM2, −4.80 Hz Fish-Speech-S2, all intervals below zero). Neutral and nonsense instructions alone shift 5/8 to 7/8 measurements in CosyVoice3 and VoxCPM2, against 0/8 and 2/8 in Fish-Speech-S2 *(§4.3, Table 6)*. Extra natural-language conditioning alone can therefore perturb the voice. This supports comparing against a matched baseline rather than judging conditioned outputs in isolation.

**VoDER-Cal.** Macro-averaged over nine system-descriptor settings *(§5.2, Table 2)*:

| Policy | Eligible | All checks | Held-out dev. ↓ | Calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | 11.5% | 4.8% | 0.325 | 1 |
| Random | 11.2% | 4.7% | 0.322 | 1 |
| Target-only | 31.3% | 14.1% | 0.344 | 3 |
| VoDER-Cal | 31.3% | 14.3% | 0.276 | 3 |
| Oracle | 31.3% | 14.6% | 0.225 | 3 |

Most of the joint-success gain comes from having three candidates, not from the ranking rule. VoDER-Cal minus Target-only is +0.19 points (95% CI [−0.56, +0.93]), while VoDER-Cal minus Direct is +9.44 points ([+6.85, +12.04]) *(Table 7)*. The matched-budget benefit appears in the continuous measure. Held-out off-target deviation falls from 0.344 (Target-only) to 0.276 (VoDER-Cal), against 0.225 for the Oracle, which has the lowest deviation of the five policies. Target-only selection has *higher* held-out deviation than single-sample Direct (0.344 vs. 0.325), so picking the strongest target response also picks more collateral change. The ordering of Target-only, VoDER-Cal and Oracle holds for unseen speakers, unseen texts and both together (VoDER-Cal 0.276 to 0.277 vs. Target-only 0.343 to 0.344) *(Table 8)*. Lowering ρ trades target retention for preservation and eligibility. At ρ = 0, joint success is 17.4% and deviation 0.269 (the best values across the four ρ settings), but target retention drops to 87.9% *(Table 9)*.

**Listening studies.** Ten adult listeners took part in two blinded studies of 45 samples each *(§6, §14)*. In H1, listeners judged the requested attribute changed in 51.1% of pairs and reported a noticeable non-target change in 51.1%, with both in 22.2%. In H2 (VoDER-Cal vs. Target-only, with the neutral output as reference), 63.3% of off-target judgments favored VoDER-Cal as closer to baseline, 9.9% favored Target-only and 26.8% were ties. That is 86.4% of non-tied judgments, consistent across rate, loudness, pitch and voice quality, and robust to A/B position and trial order *(Table 10, Table 12)*. Target expression goes the other way: 29.6% favored Target-only, 20.7% VoDER-Cal and 49.6% were ties. Speaker similarity and naturalness were roughly even. Pairwise exact agreement is low for H1 five-point judgments (22.2% to 40.7%) and moderate for H2 (31.9% to 54.1%) *(Table 11)*.

## Novelty Assessment

The main contribution is the evaluation design. It pairs each descriptor-conditioned output with a matched neutral output, uses pre-registered target and off-target feature sets, applies request-clustered bootstrap inference, and adds a conditional analysis that separates "failed to respond" from "responded with collateral change". The individual measurements are standard librosa/pYIN-style signal features, and the content and speaker checks reuse ASR and ECAPA-TDNN. The novelty is in the paired, preservation-sensitive framing, not in any new measurement model.

VoDER-Cal is a new selection criterion inside the existing best-of-N reranking paradigm, which the paper itself places alongside ASR-verifier reranking such as [[2606.18323|ASR self-verification]]. It is a cheap, training-free heuristic. Its binary success gain over Target-only is not significant, and its selector and evaluator features come from the same correlated family, a caveat the authors acknowledge *(§7.2)*. It is therefore not tagged as an architectural contribution. The audit covers three systems, three primary descriptors and English only, and it measures roughness with weak proxies (flatness, ZCR).

## Field Significance

Low — The paper gives a concrete, statistically careful demonstration that prompt adherence and attribute-specific control can diverge in current instruction-conditioned TTS. It also offers a reusable paired protocol for measuring off-target change against a matched baseline. The evidence is narrow (three systems, three signal-level descriptors, one language, ten listeners), and the proposed selector gives a modest, inference-time improvement rather than a new modelling direction.

## Claims

- **complicates:** Instruction-conditioned TTS outputs that move in the requested attribute direction frequently also change prosodic, energy and spectral characteristics outside the requested attribute, even when content and speaker identity are preserved.
  > *Evidence:* Among outputs whose target score exceeds baseline seed variation, 54.5% to 95.8% show at least one substantial off-target deviation across eight system-descriptor settings in CosyVoice3, VoxCPM2 and Fish-Speech-S2, while content is preserved in at least 99.4% of outputs and speaker similarity stays at 0.821 to 0.902. *(§4.1, §4.2, Table 1, Table 5)*
- **refines:** The same natural-language voice descriptor maps to different combinations of acoustic changes in different TTS systems, so descriptor adherence does not correspond to a consistent intervention across models.
  > *Evidence:* For "deep", CosyVoice3 lowers F0 while also changing rate, duration, energy, centroid and roll-off. VoxCPM2's "bright" response is carried mainly by RMS energy rather than the target spectral measures, and non-target features with intervals excluding zero range from 1/6 to 6/6 across systems. *(§4.1, Figure 2, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** Adding natural-language conditioning without a meaningful voice target can by itself shift the acoustic properties of generated speech, so instruction-conditioned outputs need a matched neutral baseline for evaluation.
  > *Evidence:* Neutral-reading and nonsense-descriptor instructions shift 5/8 to 7/8 measured features in CosyVoice3 and VoxCPM2 (0/8 and 2/8 in Fish-Speech-S2), while an explicit lower-pitch instruction lowers mean F0 in all three systems. *(§4.3, §12.2, Table 6)*
- **supports:** Inference-time reranking of multiple sampled candidates can improve attribute preservation in instruction-conditioned TTS without retraining, but most of the gain in binary success comes from having candidates at all rather than from the ranking rule.
  > *Evidence:* With three candidates, joint success rises from 4.8% (Direct) to 14.1% (Target-only), 14.3% (VoDER-Cal) and 14.6% (Oracle). VoDER-Cal minus Target-only is +0.19 points (CI [−0.56, +0.93]), while held-out off-target deviation falls from 0.344 to 0.276, and listeners favor VoDER-Cal's off-target preservation in 86.4% of non-tied judgments. *(§5.2, §6, Table 2, Table 7, Table 10)*
- **complicates:** Selecting the candidate with the strongest target expression tends to increase collateral change, and favoring preservation costs some perceived target strength.
  > *Evidence:* Target-only selection has higher held-out off-target deviation (0.344) than single-sample Direct generation (0.325). In H2, target-expression judgments favor Target-only over VoDER-Cal 29.6% to 20.7% with 49.6% ties, despite 99.1% automatic target-score retention. *(§5.2, §6, Table 2, Table 10)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

- **Narrow scope.** Three systems, three primary descriptors, English texts and six reference speakers. The reference-speech source and the ten texts are not described in the paper itself; they are deferred to the code repository. Higher-level descriptors (warm, professional, emotional) are supplementary and may need richer perceptual definitions *(§7.3)*.
- **Signal-level proxies.** Target and off-target sets are operational choices, not perceptual decompositions. Several features are correlated (rate with duration, centroid with roll-off), so shifted-feature counts measure breadth, not independent attributes. Spectral flatness and ZCR are weak proxies for roughness *(§7.3, §11.1)*.
- **Heterogeneous interfaces.** Fish-Speech-S2 is driven through inline voice tags rather than free-form instructions, so cross-system differences mix model behaviour with interface differences *(§3.1, §10.2)*.
- **Small listening studies.** Ten listeners, 45 samples per study, with low exact agreement on H1 five-point scales. H2 compares selectors only when both return a candidate, not the full abstaining procedure *(§7.3, Table 11)*.
- **Selector limits.** VoDER-Cal relies on suitable candidates appearing in a small pool and on hand-defined feature sets. Its selector and evaluator features belong to the same correlated measurement family, so the gain in held-out deviation is not evidence of a perceptually optimal ranking. The authors argue that removing systematic coupling requires changes to training objectives or representations *(§7.2, §7.3)*.
