# Recent Advances in Speech Language Models: A Survey

**Paper:** [https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.682](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.682)

**One-sentence contribution:** A comprehensive survey of Speech Language Models (SpeechLMs), systematically covering their three-component architecture (speech tokenizer, language model, vocoder), training recipes (pretraining, instruction tuning, post-alignment, RLHF), evaluation methods, and future challenges including safety and low-resource languages.

## Problem

The rapid emergence of Speech Language Models — end-to-end foundation models for speech interaction — has outpaced systematic documentation. The naive "ASR + LLM + TTS" pipeline suffers from information loss (paralinguistic features stripped by text-only LLMs), significant latency, and cumulative error across modules. SpeechLMs address these limitations but span diverse architectural choices, training strategies, and evaluation protocols, making it hard to orient new researchers or compare systems. This survey provides the first thorough taxonomy.

## Method

This is a survey paper, not a system contribution. The taxonomy organizes SpeechLMs along three axes:

**Architecture (Section 3)**:
1. *Speech Tokenizer*: Three objective families — semantic understanding (Wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, WavLM, USM), acoustic generation (neural codecs: SoundStream, EnCodec, DAC), and mixed objectives (SpeechTokenizer, Mimi). Discrete vs. continuous token representations.
2. *Language Model*: Decoder-only transformers adapted for speech via expanded vocabulary (text + speech embeddings) or replacement with speech-only embedding; joint text-speech vs. speech-only modeling.
3. *Vocoder (Token-to-Speech Synthesizer)*: Direct synthesis (HiFi-GAN, adapted for speech tokens) vs. input-enhanced synthesis (flow-matching → mel-spectrogram → HiFi-GAN, as in CosyVoice); GAN-based vocoders (MelGAN, HiFi-GAN, Fre-GAN, BigVGAN) detailed.

**Training (Section 4)**:
- Features modeled: discrete semantic tokens (HuBERT), acoustic tokens (codec), paralinguistic tokens (prosody, pitch), continuous features.
- Pretraining: cold initialization (GSLM) vs. continued pretraining from TextLMs (TWIST, AudioPaLM); single-sequence vs. multi-sequence alignment of text and speech (SPIRIT-LM, Mini-Omni, Moshi).
- Instruction tuning: cross-modal fine-tuning and chain-of-modality tuning (SpeechGPT, LLaMA-Omni).
- Post-alignment: DPO for semantic consistency (AlignSLM), SpeechAlign for token distribution correction.

**Speech Interaction Paradigm**: Traditional (complete input → complete output) vs. real-time/full-duplex (streaming tokenizers, simultaneous bidirectional communication). Full-duplex systems: dGSLM (dual-stream transformer), Parrot (next-token-pair), Moshi (RQ-Transformer), LSLM (streaming SSL encoder).

**Evaluation (Section 5)**: Categorized into: representation evaluation (ABX score, resynthesis WER/CER), linguistic evaluation (sWUGGY, sBLIMP, Spoken StoryCloze), paralinguistic evaluation (prosody accuracy, STSP benchmark), generation quality/diversity (perplexity AUC, VERT, ChatGPT score), real-time interaction evaluation (turn-taking statistics, Full-Duplex-Bench), and downstream benchmarks (SUPERB, DynamicSUPERB, VoiceBench, MMAU, OpenAudioBench, VoxEval).

**Challenges (Section 6)**: Component choice comparisons lack depth; end-to-end training (backprop through all three components) is unexplored; safety (toxicity, privacy) for SpeechLMs is underexplored relative to TextLMs; low-resource languages.

## Key Results

This is a survey; no original experimental results are reported. Key empirical findings synthesized from cited work:
- HuBERT consistently outperforms CPC and wav2vec 2.0 as speech tokenizer (GSLM).
- Continued pretraining from TextLMs (TWIST) outperforms cold initialization for SpeechLMs.
- Interleaving text and speech tokens during pretraining (SPIRIT-LM) improves speech task performance.
- Post-alignment via DPO improves semantic consistency (AlignSLM).
- Full-duplex modeling requires joint modeling of both speaker streams simultaneously.

## Novelty Assessment

The survey's value is organizational rather than empirical. The taxonomy is thorough and the coverage of full-duplex systems, post-alignment, and evaluation benchmarks is timely. The paper does not introduce new methods, datasets, or metrics. The companion GitHub repository (Awesome-SpeechLM-Survey) provides ongoing curation value. The positioning at ACL 2025 Main reflects the maturity and centrality of SpeechLMs to the NLP community.

## Limitations and Open Questions

- Coverage is necessarily selective; some recent systems (e.g., those post-2024) may be underrepresented given the publication timeline.
- The survey's taxonomy is biased toward English-centric, resource-rich systems; low-resource and multilingual SpeechLMs are acknowledged as a gap but not deeply covered.
- Safety risks specific to SpeechLMs (voice cloning, speaker privacy) are identified but not analyzed in depth.
- End-to-end training (jointly optimizing tokenizer, LM, and vocoder) is flagged as an open research direction.
