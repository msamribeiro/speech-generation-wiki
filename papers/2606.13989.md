> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Ferreira et al.** (Federal University of Goiás / Federal University of Mato Grosso / Elsa Speak) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13989) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> A training-free inference stack for discrete flow matching TTS over codec tokens combines CTMC guidance, prompt-matched coupling and token-to-mask remasking, so that early token decisions can be revised during sampling and low-step prompted infilling no longer collapses.

## Problem

Alignment-free non-autoregressive TTS treats synthesis as infilling: the text is padded with filler tokens to the target length and the model fills the masked acoustic region, with no duration predictor or aligner. This setup is brittle at inference. Conditional control weakens at small sampling budgets, and early errors persist as deletions, substitutions or speaker drift. When speech is a sequence of discrete codec tokens, discrete flow matching (DFM), a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) formulation, fits the infilling task naturally. Prior DFM-TTS work focused on representations, codec hierarchy, probability paths or solver correction. It did not ask which sampling-time controls make prompted, mask-source infilling stable.

## Method

The system, G-DFlow-TTS, keeps the model fixed and studies the sampler. A 232M-parameter DiT (12 layers, 12 heads, hidden size 768, RoPE) is trained with the DFM convex-mixture path from a fully masked source to the target token sequence, using a linear schedule (κ_t = t) and cross-entropy on the target tokens. Speech is tokenized with NeuCodec, an FSQ-based single-codebook codec derived from XCodec2, so speech stays a single discrete stream with no residual-codebook hierarchy. Text uses a GPT-2 BPE tokenizer with E2-style text-filler padding. Training runs for 1M iterations on one B200 GPU over the English portion of Emilia-YODAS, which the authors chose for its CC BY 4.0 licence.

The "Mask, Sample, Revise" stack adds three components, none of which requires post-hoc fine-tuning:

- **Predictor-free guidance (PFG):** a geometric mixture of conditional and unconditional CTMC transition rates, R = R_c^γ · R_u^(1−γ), following CTMC discrete guidance. One checkpoint produces both rates because the text condition is replaced by filler for 10% of training examples.
- **Prompt-matched conditional coupling (C-coupling):** during training, the source sequence copies a random-length prefix (0.25 to 12 s) of the target and masks the rest. The probability path therefore matches the prompted infilling task used at inference. This is a training-side change to the coupling, not to the architecture.
- **SC-ReMask (schedule-constrained CTMC remasking):** at each tau-leaping step a per-step remask probability is computed, bounded by the noise schedule, capped and rescaled (η_cap, η_rescale) and gated by a switch time. It is then converted to a CTMC rate, r_rm = −log(1−σ)/Δt, on eligible generated suffix tokens. Generated tokens can return to the mask state and be regenerated later. The acoustic prompt stays pinned. The idea adapts ReMDM-style remasking from masked discrete diffusion, here implemented as an explicit CTMC transition that contributes to the total hazard of the tau-leaping jump.

Main experiments use guidance γ = 1.5, chosen from a γ × NFE grid (Figure 2), and always-on remasking (t_switch = 0, η_rescale = η_cap = 0.5), chosen on LibriSpeech dev-clean (Figure 4).

## Key Results

Evaluation follows the VALL-E voice-prompted protocol on a 2.2-hour LibriSpeech test-clean subset (4 to 10 s utterances), with a different utterance from the same speaker as prompt. Metrics are WER/CER from a CTC HuBERT ASR model, SIM-o with WavLM-TDCNN, UTMOS, and a 5-point MOS test with 19 listeners on 20 clips per system. Per-utterance differences carry bootstrap confidence intervals and paired sign-flip permutation tests.

Controlled ablation at 32 NFE (Table I):

| Variant | WER (%) | CER (%) | SIM-o | UTMOS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U-coupling baseline (unguided) | 75.44 | 47.25 | 0.17 | 2.12 |
| C-coupling only | 90.12 | 57.79 | 0.17 | 1.80 |
| U-coupling + PFG | 28.61 | 16.66 | 0.33 | 2.97 |
| C-coupling + PFG | 18.38 | 8.96 | 0.35 | 3.17 |
| C-coupling + PFG + SC-ReMask | 8.39 | 3.56 | 0.42 | 3.77 |

- The unguided sampler fails in the prompted setting, and C-coupling alone makes it worse (WER 75.44 to 90.12). C-coupling helps only when paired with PFG.
- Each added component gives a statistically significant paired gain (all p < 10⁻⁴, Table II). Adding SC-ReMask to C-coupling + PFG changes WER by −9.99 points, CER by −5.40, SIM-o by +0.07 and UTMOS by +0.60. These are mean paired differences, variant minus baseline, so negative is better for WER/CER.
- Within the G-DFlow-TTS variants, the full stack has the lowest WER and CER and the highest SIM-o and UTMOS in Table I.
- Sampling steps matter less than inference control (Table III). Raising the unguided baseline from 32 to 128 NFE only lowers CER from 47.25% to 40.39%. The full stack at 8 NFE reaches 15.92% CER, and PFG + C-coupling at 8 NFE reaches 30.42%. Both are below the baseline at 128 NFE. The full stack keeps improving with more steps (CER 5.69% at 16, 3.56% at 32, 3.00% at 128 NFE). SIM-o is 0.42 at 32 NFE and 0.41 at 64 and 128.
- In this table, remasking does not add runtime: at 32 NFE the full stack's RTF is 0.10 against 0.13 for PFG + C-coupling. Both guided variants cost more than the unguided baseline (0.05), because PFG needs two model passes per step.

The external systems (official checkpoints, reported "for contextual reference") are better than the full stack on every metric. F5-TTS at 32 NFE has WER 2.97%, SIM-o 0.78, UTMOS 3.90 and MOS 3.82. CosyVoice 2 has WER 3.86% and MOS 4.27, and MaskGCT has WER 4.89% and MOS 3.68. The full stack has MOS 3.46 ± 0.34. These systems use more and partly multilingual data (100K to 166K hours, against 60K English hours) and different architectures, so the comparison does not isolate the sampler.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is an inference procedure, with one training-side change (prompt-matched coupling plus text dropout for guidance). PFG is taken directly from CTMC discrete guidance, and remasking is adapted from ReMDM. The new parts are implementing remasking as an explicit, schedule-bounded CTMC rate inside tau-leaping, and combining all three controls for prompted codec-token TTS. The authors say this is the first use of CTMC discrete guidance and of revisable remasking in alignment-free TTS. The ablation design is clean: fixed backbone, data and evaluation pipeline, with paired significance tests. However, the reference point is an unguided baseline with 75% WER. Large relative gains from such a broken starting point do not show how much SC-ReMask would add to a DFM-TTS system that already works well.

## Field Significance

Moderate. The paper gives controlled evidence that, for discrete flow matching TTS, sampling-time controls (rate-space guidance and token-to-mask revision) matter more for content accuracy than extra CTMC steps. It also shows a property continuous flow-matching infillers lack: discrete generation can be made explicitly revisable at inference. The demonstration is limited to one small English model whose absolute quality is well below current zero-shot systems. The authors state that the paper is accepted to SLT 2026; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** In discrete flow matching TTS, inference-time control of the CTMC sampler is a stronger lever on intelligibility than increasing the number of sampling steps.
  > *Evidence:* Raising the unguided baseline from 32 to 128 NFE lowers CER only from 47.25% to 40.39%. The guided, remasking stack reaches 15.92% CER at 8 NFE and 3.56% at 32 NFE with the same backbone. *(§V, Table III)*
- **supports:** Allowing already generated discrete tokens to be remasked and regenerated during sampling improves content accuracy and speaker similarity in masked codec-token TTS.
  > *Evidence:* Adding SC-ReMask to C-coupling + PFG at 32 NFE changes WER by −9.99 points, CER by −5.40, SIM-o by +0.07 and UTMOS by +0.60 (paired, p < 10⁻⁴). Always-on remasking with a capped schedule gives the best low-NFE CER on dev-clean. *(§V, Table II, Figure 4)*
- **refines:** Classifier-free-style guidance is required for usable prompted, alignment-free infilling at low step counts in discrete flow matching TTS, and too much guidance strength hurts intelligibility.
  > *Evidence:* Guidance alone reduces WER from 75.44% to 28.61% at 32 NFE. The γ × NFE grid shows WER worsening at overly large γ, especially at small NFE, with the best setting at γ = 1.5. *(§V, Table I, Figure 2)*
- **complicates:** Training on prompt-matched probability paths does not by itself improve prompted infilling; its benefit depends on sampling-time conditioning control.
  > *Evidence:* C-coupling without guidance raises WER from 75.44% to 90.12% and lowers UTMOS from 2.12 to 1.80. Combined with PFG it improves WER by −10.23 points over U-coupling + PFG. *(§V, Table I, Table II, Figure 3)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The full system stays far behind every external baseline: WER 8.39% against 2.97% (F5-TTS), SIM-o 0.42 against 0.74 to 0.78, and MOS 3.46 against 3.68 to 4.27. The gains are measured against an unguided baseline with 75% WER, so it is untested whether the stack helps a DFM-TTS model that is already strong.

- Speaker similarity is low (0.42 against 0.76 for ground truth). The authors attribute this to relying only on the acoustic prefix with no speaker objective, which lets timbre drift when early errors propagate.
- Training uses unfiltered Emilia-YODAS English without the transcript-quality or language filters used by stronger systems. Data and model scale both differ from the external references, so their gap cannot be split between sampler, model and data.
- MOS is reported only for the full stack and external systems, not for the ablation variants, so the subjective effect of each component is unknown.
- The remasking schedule and guidance strength are tuned on a single English test protocol. Generalization to other languages, longer utterances or multi-codebook codecs is not evaluated.
- SIM-o is an embedding proxy, and the authors note that codec artifacts may bias speaker embeddings.
