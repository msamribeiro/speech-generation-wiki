> Reinforcement learning on a full-duplex speech LM is restricted to a binary speak-or-stay-silent policy, obtained by summing padding and non-padding text logits, so that turn-taking can be optimized with GRPO without the repetition collapse that raw-token GRPO causes.

## Problem

Full-duplex speech LMs such as [[2410.00037|Moshi]] must decide at every frame whether to speak, stay silent, backchannel, or yield to a user who barges in. Recent work applies reinforcement learning (typically GRPO) with timing rewards to tune these behaviors, but the reward is applied to the full text-token policy. The authors argue that this forces one optimization signal to shape both conversational timing and lexical content: under latency rewards, standard GRPO becomes over-eager, talks over pauses and backchannels, and degrades into repetitive, semantically empty speech. Plain supervised fine-tuning on conversational data does not fix timing either and also damages content quality.

## Method

ASPIRin (Action Space Projection for Interactivity-Optimized Reinforcement Learning) keeps the base model's architecture and changes only the space in which the RL objective is defined. In Moshi-style models the inner monologue text stream signals timing: padding tokens mean silence, non-padding tokens mean speech. The text vocabulary is partitioned into a padding set and a non-padding set, and each generated token is mapped to a binary action state (inactive silence vs. active speech). The projected state logit for each class is the sum of the raw logits of the tokens in that set, and a two-way softmax over these state logits defines a coarse state policy. This projected policy replaces the token policy inside the GRPO objective (importance ratio, KL penalty to a reference model's projected policy), so gradients push probability mass between "speak" and "do not speak" without directly rewarding or penalizing specific words *(§2.1)*.

![Overview of the ASPIRin framework. (a) Action Space Projection & State Policy Optimization: The fine-grained text vocabulary is decoupled into a coarse-grained binary state (Active Speech vs. Inactive Silence) by grouping and summing non-padding and padding logits. This projected state policy is then explicitly optimized. (b) Rule-Based Rewards: The state policy is guided by continuous temporal constraints that penalize user interruption and excessive response latency. This explicit decoupling allows ASPIRin to master conversational timing without compromising semantic generation.](assets/2604.10065/figure-1.png)

Rewards are rule-based rather than learned. User voice activity intervals come from ASR timestamps (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3), and the model's state sequence is segmented into utterances. An Interruption Score gives the fraction of model utterances whose overlap with the user is below a tolerance (1.0 s), and a Response Score gives the fraction whose latency after the last user utterance is below a limit (1.0 s). The sequence reward is the product of the two, normalized across the group to give advantages *(§2.2)*.

The base model is Moshi, used with a 3-second prompt delay heuristic in all experiments. Training uses a 43-hour in-house corpus of consented, anonymized dual-channel conversations (about 1,300 two-minute clips, filtered to at least 50% active speech), LoRA (r = 256) on all linear layers with fully trained temporal transformer embeddings, 8 V100 GPUs, 3 epochs, learning rate 1e-5, GRPO group size G = 2, and KL weight 0.001 *(§3.1)*. Model size and codec are not restated in the paper.

## Key Results

All results are on Full-Duplex-Bench (pause handling, backchanneling, smooth turn-taking, user interruption) against three baselines: Moshi with a 3-second prompt delay, standard SFT on the same data, and standard GRPO on the raw token policy *(Table 1)*.

- **Prompt-delay heuristic:** adding a 3-second prompt delay to Moshi cuts pause-handling and backchannel takeover rates (TOR) from about 0.98 to 1.0 down to 0.44 to 0.50 and raises the user-interruption GPT-4o score from 0.765 to 3.894, at the cost of higher interruption latency (0.257 s to 1.159 s). This delayed Moshi is the main baseline.
- **Standard SFT** worsens nearly every dimension: backchannel TOR rises to 0.927, interruption TOR falls to 0.625, and the GPT-4o score collapses to 0.44.
- **Standard GRPO** is the most eager system: best turn-taking TOR (0.857) and lowest turn-taking and interruption latencies (0.153 s, 0.614 s), but pause-handling TOR rises to 0.642/0.704 and backchannel TOR to 0.709, and the GPT-4o score drops to 3.247.
- **ASPIRin** lowers backchannel TOR to 0.364 (best of all systems) and Candor pause TOR to 0.486, raises turn-taking TOR to 0.765 and interruption TOR to 0.941, and cuts interruption latency to 0.992 s relative to delayed Moshi, with a GPT-4o score of 3.734 (0.16 below delayed Moshi). It is not uniformly better than the delayed Moshi baseline: synthetic pause-handling TOR is slightly worse (0.482 vs. 0.467), backchannel JSD is worse (0.752 vs. 0.705), and turn-taking latency is higher (0.273 s vs. 0.161 s).
- **Repetition:** relative to standard GRPO, ASPIRin reduces seq-rep-2 from 0.117 to 0.054 and seq-rep-3 from 0.072 to 0.029 (both over 50%), seq-rep-1 from 0.303 to 0.202, and Self-BLEU from 0.369 to 0.343 *(Table 3)*. Qualitative interruption examples show standard GRPO looping on fillers ("you know") and SFT producing word salad *(Table 2)*.
- **Reward dynamics:** both RL variants increase total reward, but the interruption component of standard GRPO oscillates and trends downward while ASPIRin's stays stable, so total reward alone is a misleading stopping criterion for raw-token GRPO *(§4.2, Figure 2)*.

Semantic quality is judged by GPT-4o and n-gram statistics on ASR transcripts; there is no human listening test, and Table 3 does not include the Moshi or SFT baselines.

## Novelty Assessment

The architectural contribution is narrow but genuine: redefining the GRPO policy over a projected binary action space (summed logits over padding vs. non-padding tokens) is a new training objective for full-duplex speech LMs, and it neatly exploits the fact that Moshi-style inner-monologue padding already encodes timing. The reward design (overlap and latency thresholds from ASR timestamps) is a straightforward rule set, and the RL algorithm is standard GRPO. The empirical case is mainly that ASPIRin avoids the failure modes of raw-token GRPO and SFT; against the strong delayed-Moshi baseline the gains are modest and mixed. Evidence comes from a single base model, a small in-house dataset, a group size of two, and no variance or significance reporting.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper gives a clear, mechanistic demonstration that applying timing rewards to the full token policy of a full-duplex speech LM can trade semantic quality for eagerness, and that restricting the RL action space to the speak/silence decision keeps content quality close to the base model. It offers a simple, reusable recipe for interaction-timing alignment in models whose text stream uses padding to mark silence, although its gains over a well-tuned heuristic baseline are small.

## Claims

- **supports:** Optimizing turn-taking rewards directly on the full token policy of a full-duplex speech LM degrades semantic coherence and induces repetitive generation.
  > *Evidence:* Raw-token GRPO with latency/interruption rewards on Moshi drops the user-interruption GPT-4o score from 3.894 to 3.247 and yields seq-rep-2 of 0.117 and Self-BLEU of 0.369, with qualitative outputs looping on fillers. *(§4.1, §4.3, Table 1, Table 2, Table 3)*
- **supports:** Decoupling when to speak from what to say, by restricting reinforcement learning to a binary speak/silence action space, preserves content quality while improving interaction timing in full-duplex speech LMs.
  > *Evidence:* GRPO over a projected state policy (summed padding vs. non-padding logits) reduces seq-rep-2 to 0.054 and seq-rep-3 to 0.029 versus standard GRPO, keeps the GPT-4o score at 3.734 (vs. 3.894 for the base), and lowers backchannel TOR to 0.364 while raising interruption TOR to 0.941. *(§2.1, §4.1, §4.3, Table 1, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Supervised fine-tuning on natural conversational data does not by itself teach full-duplex speech LMs appropriate turn-taking behavior and can damage response quality.
  > *Evidence:* SFT of Moshi on 43 hours of dual-channel conversation raises backchannel TOR from 0.436 to 0.927, lowers interruption TOR from 0.901 to 0.625, and collapses the GPT-4o score to 0.44. *(§4.1, Table 1, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** In full-duplex dialogue, reward schemes that favor responsiveness trade off against yielding behavior, so gains in turn-taking latency often come with more talking over pauses and backchannels.
  > *Evidence:* Standard GRPO achieves the lowest turn-taking and interruption latencies but the worst pause-handling and backchannel TOR among the RL systems; its interruption score oscillates and declines during training even as total reward rises. ASPIRin's balanced profile comes with higher turn-taking latency than delayed Moshi (0.273 s vs. 0.161 s). *(§4.1, §4.2, Table 1, Figure 2)*
- **refines:** Simple inference-time delays can be a strong baseline for full-duplex interaction timing, against which trained interaction policies show only modest additional gains.
  > *Evidence:* A 3-second prompt delay alone reduces Moshi's pause and backchannel TOR by roughly 0.5 and raises the interruption GPT-4o score from 0.765 to 3.894; ASPIRin's further changes over this baseline are within about 0.02 to 0.07 TOR on most dimensions and slightly worse on synthetic pause handling. *(§4.1, Table 1)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> All results come from a single base model (Moshi), a 43-hour in-house training set that is not released, and a GRPO group size of two, with no variance, seeds, or significance tests reported; semantic quality is judged only by GPT-4o and n-gram statistics, with no human evaluation.

- Gains over the delayed-Moshi baseline are small and not uniform: synthetic pause-handling TOR, backchannel JSD, and turn-taking latency are worse than the baseline.
- The repetition analysis (Table 3) compares only ASPIRin and standard GRPO, so it does not show whether ASPIRin matches the base model's repetition level.
- The projection relies on padding tokens in a text stream marking silence; applicability to full-duplex models without such a stream is not discussed.
- Rewards depend on ASR-derived timestamps and fixed 1-second thresholds; sensitivity to these choices is not studied.
- The binary action space cannot distinguish backchannels from full responses; the authors propose multi-class or hierarchical action spaces as future work *(§5)*.
