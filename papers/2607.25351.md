>
> Recovers a usable style vector for a new voice from a frozen, inference-only TTS release whose reference encoder is withheld, by gradient descent on the style input alone against time-pooled WavLM statistics of one untranscribed recording.

## Problem

Some TTS releases ship the synthesis model and a handful of preset style vectors but keep the reference encoder (the module that maps a recording to a style vector) private. The model still accepts any style vector, but a user has no way to produce one for a new voice. Earlier work that fits a conditioning vector with the generator held fixed (VoiceLoop, the embedding-only variant of Arik et al., SEA-EMB) assumes the authors trained the generator themselves and supervises the fit by reconstructing the target's own transcribed utterance with a frame-level loss. That needs a transcript, an alignment, and a trainable graph, none of which a bare recording and an inference-only release provide.

## Method

The target is the public SupertonicTTS 2 release (internal version v1.6.0), which ships ten preset styles and no reference encoder. All weights of the TTS pipeline and of WavLM stay frozen; only the style input is optimized. SupertonicTTS exposes two style tensors: a timbre style s_ttl (50 x 256, 12,800 parameters) that enters the acoustic modules as cross-attention keys and values, and a duration style s_dp (8 x 16, 128 parameters) that reaches only the duration predictor and sets the utterance length. The four released ONNX inference modules are simplified with onnxslim, lowered from opset 19 to 17, cleaned of trailing empty Clip inputs, and converted with onnx2torch into a differentiable PyTorch graph.

The objective ignores content so that the synthesized text can differ from the target's words. It takes the WavLM-Large layer-4 hidden states (chosen because a layer-wise probe reports peak speaker-identification accuracy there), pools them over time into per-dimension mean and standard deviation, and minimizes the mean squared error between these statistics for the synthesized and target audio. No transcript, forced aligner, speaker-verification model, or reference encoder is needed.

The presets supply the two missing pieces of information. Every row of all ten released s_ttl vectors has unit L2 norm, so after each Adam step every row is projected back onto the unit sphere. The presets also set the stopping point: 100 same-preset pairs with different sentences give a content-only loss band of 0.148 to 0.413 (mean 0.242), and optimization stops when the loss reaches 0.24. Optimization starts from the nearest of the ten presets under the same loss, rotates five English prompts across steps, and uses a fixed noise latent per prompt (Adam, learning rate 2e-4, gradient clipping at 1.0, ReduceLROnPlateau). Batches of 16 speakers run on one RTX 3090 at about half a minute per speaker.

![Fig. 1. The timbre style s_ttl (dark) receives the gradient of the time-pooled WavLM loss and is projected back onto the unit sphere after every step. The duration style s_dp sets the latent length and is fitted so that the speaking rate of the synthesis matches the target. All modules of the TTS pipeline and of WavLM remain frozen; generated and target audio may carry different text.](assets/2607.25351/figure-1.png)

A time-pooled loss cannot see speaking rate, so s_dp is fitted in a second stage. Speaking rate is measured as peaks of the smoothed RMS envelope per second of trimmed speech (a crude syllable-nucleus count). Starting from the preset's s_dp, each iteration inverts the frozen duration predictor by gradient descent toward the current target durations (rows kept on the unit sphere, with a small penalty toward the start), synthesizes the five prompts, measures their rate, and takes a secant step in log-duration. This stage takes about a minute per speaker, for roughly ninety seconds of GPU time per voice in total.

## Key Results

The evaluation covers 147 speakers (103 VCTK speakers after a reference-quality filter removed 7, plus 44 Common Voice speakers from Seed-TTS Eval), each synthesized on 100 Harvard sentences that were never used during optimization. The comparison is paired: each extracted style against the preset it started from, on the same sentences. There is no comparison against another cloning or adaptation system.

- **Speaker similarity** (Table 1): ECAPA-TDNN similarity rises from 0.129 to 0.419 and ResNet similarity from 0.095 to 0.409. All 147 speakers end closer to their target than their starting preset under both verifiers, and 141 of 147 under WavLM-base-plus-sv (0.703 to 0.837). For scale, impostor pairs of these references average 0.100 ECAPA similarity (95th percentile 0.302), and a second real recording of the same VCTK speaker has a median of 0.680. Held-out sentences cost about a hundredth of ECAPA similarity relative to the five fitted prompts (0.475 fitted, 0.467 on similar held-out prompts).
- **Verifier acceptance** (Table 2): with an ECAPA verifier at its equal-error point (EER 1.0%, threshold 0.410), 51.7% of extracted voices are accepted as the target against 1.4% of the presets; at a 5% false-accept rate, 89.1% against 2.7%; at 0.1%, 11.6% against 0.0%.
- **Intelligibility** (Table 1): pooled adjudicated WER is 5.71% for extracted styles against 6.00% for presets. The overall gain hides a split: WER falls on VCTK (5.73% to 4.37%) but rises on the Seed-TTS subset (6.65% to 8.86%), where the fitted duration styles move furthest from the presets. The paper attributes a WER floor of about 6% to the frozen model, which sometimes drops or repeats words.
- **Naturalness** (§4.2): UTMOS is 4.03 for extracted voices against 4.27 for presets (94% of the preset score, a 0.24 drop), and 3.92 for the real references. VCTK speakers keep 98% of the preset score, Seed-TTS speakers 86%.
- **Duration style** (§4.2): on the fitted prompts, speaking-rate error falls from 9.4% to 2.0%; on held-out prompts, from 11.9% to 8.1%.

All quality numbers are automatic; there is no listening test.

## Novelty Assessment

Fitting a conditioning vector through a fixed generator is old: the paper itself traces it to 2018 speaker-adaptation work. What is new is the setting and the objective. The generator is someone else's inference-only ONNX release, and the fit uses a transcript-free, time-pooled SSL statistic rather than frame-level reconstruction, with the constraints (unit-sphere rows, stopping threshold, duration start) read off the released presets. The engineering of making a deployed graph differentiable is practical rather than conceptual. The evaluation is careful within its scope (paired comparisons, bootstrap intervals, signed-rank tests, an impostor baseline), but it covers one TTS model and reports no comparison against zero-shot cloning systems or against an encoder-based style.

## Field Significance

Low — The paper gives a reproducible, single-model demonstration that withholding a reference encoder does not prevent cloning when the conditioning space is continuous, differentiable, and shipped with examples. Its value is mainly as a release-policy data point: a partial release should be judged by what its released components already determine.

## Claims

- **supports:** The conditioning space of a frozen TTS model can be reached for new speakers by optimizing the conditioning input through the frozen generator, without the encoder that normally produces it.
  > *Evidence:* Optimizing only the SupertonicTTS style vector raises ECAPA similarity from 0.129 to 0.419 and ResNet similarity from 0.095 to 0.409 on held-out sentences, with all 147 speakers ending closer to their target than their starting preset. *(§4.2, Table 1)*
- **supports:** Time-pooled statistics of early self-supervised speech layers act as a largely content-independent speaker-matching objective, removing the need for transcripts or alignment when fitting a voice.
  > *Evidence:* Matching mean and standard deviation of WavLM-Large layer-4 states, with the synthesized text unrelated to the target's words, transfers to two verifiers the objective never sees; the residual content-only loss between two sentences from the same preset is 0.148 to 0.413. *(§3.3, §3.4, §4.2)*
- **complicates:** Objectives that pool over time cannot recover timing attributes such as speaking rate, which must be fitted separately and transfer only partly to new text.
  > *Evidence:* The duration style is invisible to the pooled loss; a separate rate fit cuts rate error from 9.4% to 2.0% on fitted prompts but only from 11.9% to 8.1% on held-out prompts, because syllable density varies by sentence. *(§3.6, §4.2, §5)*
- **complicates:** Pushing a voice away from a model's preset styles can cost intelligibility and predicted naturalness, concentrated on speakers far from the presets.
  > *Evidence:* WER rises from 6.65% to 8.86% on the crowdsourced Seed-TTS subset (while falling on VCTK), and UTMOS falls from 4.27 to 4.03 overall, with Seed-TTS speakers keeping 86% of the preset score against 98% for VCTK. *(§4.2, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** Speaker-verifier acceptance of synthetic voices measures how much identity a conditioning space carries, not whether the speech would pass a spoofing countermeasure.
  > *Evidence:* An ECAPA verifier at its 1.0% equal-error point accepts 51.7% of extracted voices, but the paper evaluates no countermeasure and notes the speech may remain detectable. *(§4.3, Table 2, §5)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> All results come from a single TTS model (SupertonicTTS 2) with automatic metrics only; naturalness is a UTMOS prediction rather than a listening test, and no other cloning or adaptation method is compared.

- The method applies only to releases with a continuous, differentiable style input and preset examples; the paper notes that few systems fit this pattern (CosyVoice ships its encoder, F5-TTS prompts on audio, VALL-E conditions on codec tokens).
- Layer 4 is adopted from a probing study; other layers would each need their own content floor and stopping point, which the paper does not test.
- Results are weakest on the crowdsourced Seed-TTS subset, which loses intelligibility and predicted naturalness.
- The reference filter removed 7 of 110 VCTK speakers for low speaking rate or low signal-to-noise; performance on such recordings is not reported.
- The work is dual-use: the paper discusses mitigations (perturbing or quantizing presets, watermarking, hiding gradients) but measures none of them.
