>
> A non-autoregressive end-to-end streaming TTS system that replaces continuous Mel-spectrogram regression and neural vocoders with a depth-wise sequential decoding strategy over 32 Mimi codec RVQ layers, achieving a real-time factor of ~0.0033 and 48.99 ms average time-to-first-byte latency.

## Problem

Conventional two-stage TTS pipelines — a non-autoregressive acoustic model generating Mel-spectrograms followed by a neural vocoder — face two interlocking bottlenecks for streaming deployment. First, the vocoder (e.g., HiFi-GAN) requires dense transposed convolution over overlapping frames to reconstruct phase information, creating an execution bottleneck that is incompatible with strict chunk-level streaming. Second, continuous regression targets trained with MSE loss produce spectral over-smoothing: the model learns the mean of a multi-modal distribution, losing high-frequency textures and transient details.

Autoregressive codec language models (e.g., VALL-E) eliminate the vocoder but introduce prohibitive temporal latency by flattening the multi-layer RVQ codes into a single long sequence. Masked iterative models (SoundStorm, MaskGCT) still incur multiple refinement passes. A fully feed-forward path from text to discrete codec tokens, without temporal autoregression or iterative sampling, did not exist in usable form for real-time streaming.

## Method

The system is a modified FastSpeech 2 backbone coupled with the pre-trained Mimi neural audio codec (operating at 12.5 Hz, 32 RVQ codebooks with 2048 codes each).

**Architecture overview (three modules):**

1. **Phoneme encoder and variance adaptor (Module 1).** Standard FastSpeech 2 phoneme encoder processes phoneme sequences through feed-forward transformer blocks. The variance adaptor predicts log-duration, log-F0, and frame-wise energy via 1-D convolutional predictors. A length regulator expands hidden states to match the 12.5 Hz Mimi frame rate. A dummy token mechanism injects placeholder frames for sub-frame transient phonemes (those spanning fewer than 80 ms), preventing alignment collapse in the length regulator.

2. **Depth-wise cascaded discrete decoder (Module 2).** Rather than predicting all 32 quantizer layers naively in parallel, the decoder proceeds sequentially across quantization depth within each individual temporal frame. At frame $t$, the conditioned hidden state for the $i$-th quantizer accumulates learned embeddings of all previously predicted layers: $h_{t,i} = h_t + \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} E_j(y_{t,j})$. Each layer's prediction is a softmax classification over the 2048-code vocabulary. Because this recurrence is confined to the depth dimension of a single frame — not across the time axis — it introduces no temporal autoregressive delay.

3. **Mimi decoder (Module 3).** The predicted discrete indices are fed directly into the frozen pre-trained Mimi neural decoder to reconstruct 24 kHz waveforms. No additional vocoder is used.

**Training objective:** A hybrid multi-task loss combines cross-entropy over all 32 codebook heads (with staged weighting: 1.0 for layers 1–4, 0.5 for 5–16, 0.1 for 17–32), MSE for duration/pitch/energy predictors, and an auxiliary Mel-spectrogram L1 reconstruction loss (weight 10.0) via a parallel PostNet branch. The auxiliary Mel branch is discarded at inference.

**Data alignment:** Mel-spectrograms at ~10 ms hop rate are average-pooled by factor $K=8$ to synchronize with the 80 ms Mimi hop. Montreal Forced Aligner provides phoneme boundaries; durations are converted to integer frame counts at 12.5 Hz.

**Training:** English model trained 200,000 steps; Malay model 90,000 steps. Both on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with batch size 16, Adam optimizer with 4,000-step linear warmup.

**Inference:** Fully feed-forward. Duration predictor determines sequence length without future context buffering. Depth-wise decoding over 32 layers then Mimi reconstruction. End-to-end latency: ~19.63 ms (English), ~21.12 ms (Malay).

## Key Results

**Inference speed (Table 7, LJSpeech test set):**
- Proposed (FS2+Mimi): RTF ~0.0033 (303× real-time)
- VITS: RTF ~0.020 — proposed is 6× faster
- FS2+HiFi-GAN: RTF ~0.025 — proposed is 7.6× faster
- FS2+Parallel WaveGAN: RTF ~0.035 — proposed is 10.6× faster
- Average time-to-first-byte: 48.99 ms (well below 200 ms perceptual threshold)

**Acoustic fidelity (Table 4/5, LJSpeech):**
- MCD: 10.20 dB (vs. 7.31 dB VITS, 8.24 dB FS2+HiFi-GAN) — notably higher, reflecting aggressive quantization
- V/UV Error: 2.67% (outperforms FS2 3.62%; matches VITS 2.82%) — the strongest metric for the proposed system
- WER (Whisper): 8.89% (vs. 1.64% VITS, 5.19% FS2+HiFi-GAN, 1.34% topline)

**Subjective evaluation (Table 8, 11 listeners):**
- English MOS: 2.51 ± 0.11 (vs. 2.81 ± 0.13 FS2+PWG; GT 4.51 ± 0.08) — below baseline
- Malay MOS: 4.31 ± 0.11 (vs. GT 4.65 ± 0.08) — near ground truth, no baseline reported

**Ablation (Tables 9–10):**
- 16 vs. 32 codebooks: 32 layers lower WER (8.89 vs. 9.07%) and MCD (10.20 vs. 10.38 dB)
- Depth-wise sequential vs. naive parallel: removing inter-layer conditioning raises WER from 8.89% to 14.37% and MCD from 10.20 to 12.49 dB — confirming that depth-wise conditioning is the key design element
- Subword aggregation via BPE caused catastrophic failure (unintelligible output), establishing that phoneme-level granularity is mandatory

## Novelty Assessment

The primary architectural novelty is the **depth-wise sequential decoding** over multi-layer RVQ codes in a non-autoregressive framework. Conditioning each quantizer layer on the accumulated embeddings of prior layers — strictly within a single frame, not across time — is a focused solution to a well-known problem: naive parallel prediction of deep RVQ codes collapses because semantic and acoustic layers are interdependent. This sits between fully autoregressive prediction (too slow) and naive parallel prediction (too inaccurate).

The Mimi codec choice at 12.5 Hz is also architecturally motivated: the 8× lower frame rate compared to standard 100 Hz Mel-spectrograms substantially reduces transformer attention cost, making the backbone lightweight enough for streaming.

The contribution is primarily an **engineering integration** — combining an existing NAR backbone (FastSpeech 2), an existing codec (Mimi), and a novel inter-layer conditioning mechanism. There is no new training objective beyond the staged-weight cross-entropy plus auxiliary Mel supervision, which is relatively standard. The paper's honest acknowledgement of an MOS gap on English (2.51 vs. baseline 2.81) reflects accurate reporting of the quality-latency trade-off rather than overclaiming.

The Malay result (MOS 4.31) is impressive but lacks a baseline comparison, and the evaluation uses only 11 listeners, limiting statistical power.

## Limitations and Open Questions

- **English MOS below baseline:** The proposed system scores lower than the continuous FS2+vocoder baseline on English subjective evaluation, indicating that aggressive 12.5 Hz quantization sacrifices perceptual quality in exchange for speed. The Malay result may not generalize, partly due to evaluator differences (native vs. non-native listeners).
- **Single-speaker only:** Both models are single-speaker; multi-speaker and zero-shot capabilities are left to future work. This is a significant gap for practical deployment.
- **Small listener pool:** 11 listeners in the MOS study is below the typical minimum for reliable confidence intervals; the 95% CI values must be interpreted cautiously.
- **No streaming baseline under identical conditions:** Because vocoders require buffering ahead-of-time, a direct millisecond TTFB comparison to continuous systems is not made. The 10.6× RTF speedup is a system-level figure, not a head-to-head streaming protocol comparison.
- **Quantization artifacts in expressive speech:** The authors acknowledge minor artifacts at high prosodic variance; future work proposes lightweight flow-matching within the discrete latent space.
- **Code not public:** Models and code are available only on request.
