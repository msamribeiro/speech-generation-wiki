> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Yizhong Geng et al.** (Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications / Li Auto Inc. / Tsinghua University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03253) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✗
>
> A continuous-latent autoregressive singing model that changes the lyrics of a reference recording while keeping its melody, timing and singer, trained only on same-recording reconstruction, with a routing scheme that keeps generated acoustic patches out of the global lyric planner.

## Problem

Reference-conditioned lyric editing takes a singing recording and revised lyrics, and must produce the same performance with new words, without note, duration or phoneme scores. Paired data for this task does not exist in practice: resinging different words also changes timing and phrasing. Models therefore train on reconstruction tuples where reference, transcript and target all come from one recording. At inference the revised lyrics conflict with source-lyric cues that leak through the reference, a conflict never seen in training. In an autoregressive (AR) model, one generated patch that follows the source lyrics can then bias every later step through the AR history. Prior continuous AR speech systems ([[2502.03930|DiTAR]], [[2605.16964|SemaVoice]]) target content-consistent synthesis and do not address this mismatch; discrete-token AR singing editors such as [[2508.16332|Vevo2]] are bounded by finite codebooks, and continuous non-autoregressive editors such as [[2603.24589|YingMusic-Singer-Plus]] give up stepwise state and learned stopping.

## Method

CLASVS generates 64-D continuous latents from a frozen Ming-UniAudio AudioVAE ([[2511.05516|Ming-UniAudio]]; 16 kHz audio, 50 Hz frames), grouped into 5-frame patches so the AR recurrence runs at 10 Hz (100 ms per patch). Inputs are target lyric tokens, melody tokens from a frozen chroma-based melody tokenizer (about 6.25 Hz, 512-entry codebook) applied to the reference, and a 192-D singer vector from a frozen CAM++ encoder. Generation stops through a learned stop head; a rollout bound of min(K_ref + 8, 256) patches serves only as a safeguard.

The State-Control-Transition (SCT) routing splits the recurrent context into three paths. **Control:** lyric and melody tokens prefill a Qwen3-0.6B causal planner ([[2505.09388|Qwen3]]) as separate cached spans and stay visible to every step. **State:** each generated patch passes through a frozen Whisper-style 32-layer causal audio encoder (the "semantic encoder"), whose five frame states are projected to 1024-D and pooled into one semantic-feedback vector; only this vector is appended to the planner cache. **Transition:** the raw previous latent patch conditions only the next step of an 8-block, parameter-shared Flow-DiT that predicts the current patch by conditional flow matching from one flow-time/planner token, five predecessor tokens and five noisy current-patch tokens. Singer identity bypasses the planner and enters each Flow-DiT block through adaptive layer normalization.

Each patch updates semantic feedback and becomes the previous latent patch for the next step; the concatenated sequence is decoded once. Repeated Flow-DiT blocks share parameters.](assets/2608.03253/figure-1.png)

What is new relative to a standard continuous AR backbone of the DiTAR type is the routing, not the components. In that family the generated continuous patches themselves feed back into the language model as history. In CLASVS the latent patches never re-enter the causal planner: the planner sees only a content-oriented summary from a frozen recognizer-style encoder, while acoustic continuity is confined to a one-step local path. The paper's matched "unified-context AR" ablation, which merges semantic feedback and the previous patch into one shared route visible to both planner and generator, is the closest within-stack stand-in for the conventional design.

Progressive State-Control Grounding (PSCG) trains this routing in three stages. State Grounding pretrains the semantic encoder with an ASR loss plus a masked mel loss, then freezes it. Control Grounding applies history dropout (p = 0.10) to the teacher-forced previous patch and masks reference-melody spans (p = 0.15). Task Grounding schedules 72K broad-singing, 24K speech-assisted and 24K curated-singing updates. The full objective is L_text + 10 L_FM + L_stop, where L_text is a planner-side cross-entropy on target-lyric positions. Joint lyric-melody-singer dropout (p = 0.10) enables classifier-free guidance ([[2207.12598|CFG]]); inference uses 24 Euler steps per patch with guidance 2.0. Training uses about 8,000 hours of Mandarin singing, 2,000 hours of Mandarin [[2407.05361|Emilia]] speech and 300 hours of curated open-source singing, all Mandarin; no paired edit recordings are used. The model has 0.765B trainable and 2.115B total parameters.

## Key Results

Evaluation uses a new 320-edit Mandarin test set (CLA-LyricEdit-320; 80 songs, 40 singers, balanced across partial-line substitution, full-line substitution, deletion and insertion) and the four Mandarin-only operations of LyricEditBench. Comparators are released checkpoints of [[2508.16332|Vevo2]] (discrete AR) and [[2603.24589|YingMusic-Singer-Plus]] (continuous NAR), all under one automatic-input protocol (reference audio plus target lyrics). Phone error rate (PER) comes from FireRedASR-AED-L transcripts.

- **Lyric accuracy (Table 4):** CLASVS reaches the lowest macro-PER, 0.0376, against 0.0411 for YingMusic-Singer-Plus and 0.0699 for Vevo2 (the 46.2% relative reduction is against Vevo2). CLASVS beats Vevo2 on all four operations. Against YingMusic-Singer-Plus it wins deletion (0.0260 vs 0.0847) and insertion (0.0298 vs 0.0382) but loses both substitution operations (PSub 0.0505 vs 0.0171; FSub 0.0442 vs 0.0245).
- **Public benchmark (Table 3):** the same profile holds on LyricEditBench: macro-PER 0.0425 vs 0.0443 (YingMusic-Singer-Plus) and 0.0761 (Vevo2), with YingMusic-Singer-Plus still stronger on PSub and FSub.
- **Preservation (Table 4):** CLASVS has the highest FPC (0.941 vs 0.934 and 0.744), the highest singer similarity (0.914 vs 0.906 and 0.892) and the lowest voiced-onset MAE (7.68 ms vs 18.80 and 69.58 ms). YingMusic-Singer-Plus has the lowest global duration deviation (0.1464% vs 0.9923% for CLASVS).
- **Listening test (Table 6):** 20 native Mandarin listeners rated 80 blinded outputs. CLASVS scores N-MOS 4.13, M-MOS 4.28 and L-MOS 4.42; against YingMusic-Singer-Plus the naturalness (+0.48) and intelligibility (+0.32) gains are significant after Holm correction, while melody is a non-detectable +0.01 (4.28 vs 4.27).
- **Speed and memory (Table 4):** CLASVS is the slowest system (cold-start/steady-tail RTF 3.840/1.395 vs 2.649/0.744 for Vevo2 and 3.392/0.241 for YingMusic-Singer-Plus) but uses the least incremental peak memory (5.82 GiB).
- **Content audit (Table 2):** output-to-target phone NED is 0.0365 overall, while output-to-source NED (0.4050) tracks the required lexical change (0.3965). The target-preference margin (output-source NED minus output-target NED, so positive means the output is closer to the target) is positive for every operation, 0.2248 to 0.7209.
- **Ablations (Table 5, three seeds):** the matched unified-context AR raises PER from 0.0376 to 0.0447 and source-lyric reversion (SrcRev) from 0.081 to 0.118. Removing the semantic-feedback route hurts lyric accuracy most (PER 0.0564, SrcRev 0.154); removing the latent-patch route hurts melody most (FPC 0.8896). Removing State Grounding gives PER 0.0511; a randomly initialized and then frozen semantic encoder gives 0.0918.

Comparisons are against released checkpoints with their official recipes rather than retrained systems, and Vevo2 and YingMusic-Singer-Plus are general systems not trained on the authors' 8,000-hour singing set, so the system-level gaps mix architecture with data. The matched ablations are the cleaner evidence for the routing itself.

## Novelty Assessment

The components are established: a Qwen3 causal planner, a flow-matching DiT patch head, a frozen pretrained AudioVAE and a Whisper-style encoder. The contribution is the SCT routing (separating a persistent control span, a content-only recurrent feedback path and a one-step acoustic path) plus the PSCG recipe that makes it learnable from reconstruction alone. This is a genuine change to the inference-time information flow of continuous AR generation, and the matched unified-context ablation supports that it matters for target-lyric following. The new test set and the target-preference margin and SrcRev audits are useful secondary evaluation contributions. The scope is narrow: Mandarin only, short 2-6 syllable edits and one ASR recognizer.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper shows that continuous-latent AR can handle reference-conditioned singing lyric editing better than a discrete-token AR editor, and gives matched ablation evidence that keeping generated acoustics out of the global planner reduces source-lyric leakage. Its routing idea may transfer to speech editing, but the evidence is limited to Mandarin singing and released-checkpoint comparisons.

## Claims

- **supports:** In continuous-latent autoregressive generation, feeding the planner a content-oriented summary of each generated chunk instead of the raw acoustic latents improves adherence to the target text when conditioning cues conflict with it.
  > *Evidence:* A matched unified-context AR that routes semantic feedback and the previous latent patch through one shared path raises macro-PER from 0.0376 to 0.0447 and source-lyric reversion from 0.081 to 0.118, with identical frontends, planner, generator, data and parameter count. *(§PSCG and Inference Diagnostics, Table 5)*
- **refines:** Separate recurrent paths in an AR acoustic model can carry distinct roles, with a content-feedback path driving lexical progress and a short acoustic-history path driving local melodic continuity.
  > *Evidence:* Removing semantic feedback degrades lyric accuracy most (PER 0.0564, SrcRev 0.154), while removing the previous latent patch degrades F0 correlation most (FPC 0.8896 vs 0.941); shuffle tests in Figure 4 show the same split between target-lyric and reference-melody control margins. *(§PSCG and Inference Diagnostics, Table 5, Figure 4)*
- **supports:** Reference-conditioned singing lyric editing can be learned from same-recording reconstruction alone, without paired counterfactual edit recordings, when training perturbs the reference cues and acoustic history.
  > *Evidence:* Trained only on content-consistent tuples, CLASVS produces outputs whose phone distance to the target lyrics (0.0365) is far below their distance to the source lyrics (0.4050), with positive target-preference margins in all four edit operations; removing Control Grounding raises PER to 0.0468. *(§Training Tuples and the Content Conflict, §SCT Control Audit, Table 2, Table 5)*
- **complicates:** Continuous autoregressive and continuous non-autoregressive singing editors show operation-specific trade-offs rather than one dominating the other.
  > *Evidence:* CLASVS leads the continuous-NAR YingMusic-Singer-Plus on deletion and insertion and on macro-PER, but trails it on partial- and full-line substitution, global duration deviation and steady-tail RTF on both CLA-LyricEdit-320 and LyricEditBench. *(§Editing Accuracy, Quality, and Identity, §Public-Benchmark Replication, Table 3, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Stepwise continuous-latent autoregression with a per-step flow-matching head carries a substantial inference-speed cost.
  > *Evidence:* With 24 Euler steps per 100 ms patch, CLASVS runs at cold-start/steady-tail RTF 3.840/1.395, slower than both the discrete-AR (2.649/0.744) and continuous-NAR (3.392/0.241) comparators; the authors describe it as better suited to offline use. *(§Analysis and Discussion, Table 4)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

- All training and evaluation is Mandarin, with 2-6 syllable edits; cross-lingual editing and long edits are untested, and LyricEditBench's cross-lingual categories are excluded.
- Lyric accuracy relies on a single recognizer (FireRedASR-AED-L), and melody metrics follow reference timing.
- Baselines are released checkpoints under their official recipes, while CLASVS trains on about 8,000 hours of in-house singing that cannot be redistributed; system-level gaps therefore mix architecture and data.
- Code, configurations and the CLA-LyricEdit-320 annotations are promised upon publication; they are not public at the time of the fetched version. The full ablation tables are in a supplement not included in the main text.
- The system is slower than real time at cold start (RTF 3.840), and cache growth is linear in rollout length, so long inputs would need future compression.
- The melody MOS difference against YingMusic-Singer-Plus is not detectable, and the paper does not claim equivalence.
