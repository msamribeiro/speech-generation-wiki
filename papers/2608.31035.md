> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Park et al.** (Spellbrush) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31035) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> A controlled study of GRPO post-training of a codec-based TTS model with learned perceptual rewards (anime-likeness, UTMOS naturalness, likability, arousal) shows that predictor gains transfer to listeners unevenly, that Best-of-8 reranking under the same reward is not clearly worse perceptually, and that signed reward gaps, not residual CER gaps, predict listener choices.

## Problem

RL post-training of codec-based TTS has so far relied mostly on verifiable or automatically computed rewards: CER or WER, target-text likelihood, speaker similarity, duration and rule-based prosody terms. Many useful targets (naturalness, likability, affective intensity, a domain-specific speaking style) are defined by listeners, so the only practical reward is a learned perceptual predictor. Such predictors are proxies. A policy can raise the predictor score through blind spots or transcript drift and still lose listener preference. The paper asks when optimizing a learned subjective predictor moves a codec speech LM in a direction that listeners also perceive, and how to screen a reward before using it in multi-reward post-training.

## Method

The base policy is the public Llasa-1B-Multilingual checkpoint, an autoregressive LM over XCodec2 tokens (trained by its authors on Emilia and Multilingual LibriSpeech). The policy is conditioned on text only; all main runs update the full actor, and the KL reference is a frozen copy of the base. Training uses verl with vLLM rollouts on 900 Japanese Wikipedia-derived prompts, with K = 4 rollouts per prompt, group-normalized advantages, and an adaptive in-reward KL penalty (initial β 0.05, target KL 0.05). The paper does not report wall-clock training budgets beyond per-step time (about 115 s with Whisper in the reward loop).

The reward is a CER-zone template. Whisper large-v3 transcribes each decoded waveform and CER is computed against the prompt. With CER at or below 0.10 (CLEAN), the reward is the normalized predictor score plus a bonus of 0.5. Between 0.10 and 0.30 (FEASIBLE) it is the normalized score alone. Above 0.30 (VIOLATE) it is a fixed penalty of -1.0. A high perceptual score therefore cannot numerically compensate for a transcription failure. Checkpoints are selected by constraint-aware validation reward on a disjoint 100-prompt set, not by raw predictor score; for the main AnimeScore run, step 1400 is chosen although raw AnimeScore keeps rising to step 1710, because violations also rise (Appendix J, Table 15).

Four learned predictors serve as rewards, one per run:

- **AnimeScore**, a pairwise-preference anime-likeness ranker from the same authors (WavLM-base encoder, BiLSTM, RankNet-style head), shifted by +3.0 and clipped at zero.
- **UTMOS** (UTMOS22-strong, off the shelf), divided by 5.
- **Likability**, a WavLM-base-plus classifier trained for this study on CocoNut-Humoresque with class-weighted cross-entropy over six classes (test SRCC 0.712). The authors chose classification over regression to keep score spread for GRPO.
- **VAD-Arousal**, the MSP-Dim wav2vec2 arousal output, used only for a training-dynamics diagnostic.

Two baselines use the same reward signal. Target-only GRPO optimizes the predictor without the CER zone. Best-of-N reranking samples N base outputs, scores them with the CER-gated reward, and serves the top one. Evaluation runs on 50 held-out Japanese prompts in five groups. First-shot evaluation uses one fixed-seed sample. For listening tests, a symmetric CER-retry protocol regenerates either side when CER exceeds 0.30 and keeps all 50 items. Pairwise A/B tests on Lancers use 50 items per axis, 5 ratings per item and 10 distinct Japanese listeners per axis, with no listener overlap across axes. The metrics are item-level human win rate (HWR), machine win rate (MWR), and machine-human agreement.

## Key Results

**Axis specificity.** Each single-reward run moves its own axis most (Table 1). AnimeScore GRPO raises AnimeScore by +1.353 while Likability moves +0.075 and UTMOS -0.037. Likability GRPO raises Likability by +0.167, against +0.029 AnimeScore and +0.090 UTMOS. UTMOS GRPO raises UTMOS by +0.485, against -0.072 AnimeScore and +0.140 Likability. Note that the UTMOS reward lifts Likability almost as much as the Likability reward itself does. All three runs lower mean CER slightly relative to base (-0.018 to -0.030).

**CER zone and reranking (Table 2, first-shot).** Removing the CER zone hurts intelligibility on every axis. On UTMOS, Target-only reaches only +0.25 with median CER 0.154 and 14.0% violations, while Zone-CER reaches +0.49 with median CER 0.074 and 6.0% violations (base: 0.058 and 24.0%). Zone-CER gives the largest target gain of the five methods on all three axes, but the margins over Best-of-8 differ by axis: +1.35 vs +1.21 on AnimeScore, +0.49 vs +0.47 on UTMOS (near identical), +0.17 vs +0.14 on Likability. Best-of-8 has fewer violations on AnimeScore (10.0% vs 16.0%) and lower median CER on UTMOS (0.055 vs 0.074) and Likability (0.043 vs 0.098). Zone-CER has the lowest median CER only on AnimeScore (0.054) and the fewest violations only on UTMOS and Likability (6.0%).

**Human transfer (Table 3).** Against the base, listeners prefer AnimeScore GRPO on 80.0% of items (agreement 88.0%), UTMOS GRPO on 62.0% (agreement 80.0%), and Likability GRPO on only 36.0% (agreement 76.0%, MWR 56.0%). Likability is the negative average-transfer case: listeners prefer the base. The Wilson 95% interval for UTMOS HWR is [48.2, 74.1], which includes chance (Table 10). Against Best-of-8, HWR is near chance on every axis (52.0, 46.0, 48.0), so listeners do not clearly prefer GRPO over reranking.

**Reward-gap calibration (§6.3, Table 12).** A vote-level logistic regression over 1000 votes from four studies, with axis fixed effects and item-clustered standard errors, gives an odds ratio of 1.93 (95% CI [1.38, 2.70], p = 1.4 × 10⁻⁴) per standard deviation of signed reward gap in favour of GRPO. The CER-gap odds ratio is 0.96 ([0.67, 1.38], p = 0.83), and a Wald test rejects equal slopes (p = 7.4 × 10⁻³). Per axis, the reward-gap slope is significant for Likability (OR 2.98) and UTMOS (OR 3.15) but not for Japanese AnimeScore (OR 1.19, p = 0.63) (Table 17). The axis with the strongest average transfer therefore has the weakest within-axis calibration. In the binned analysis, Likability items with an absolute reward gap above 0.3 get 90 to 100% GRPO votes, but only 10 of 50 items reach that regime (Table 18).

**Other diagnostics.** On base outputs, AnimeScore has the widest predictor spread (std 1.53) and Likability the narrowest (std 0.44) (Table 5), in line with their strongest and weakest transfer. The VAD-Arousal run raises validation reward from -0.34 to -0.05 mainly by cutting violations (0.62 to 0.45), while validation arousal moves only about +0.014 (Appendix I). No listening test was run for arousal. An auxiliary English AnimeScore study reaches 70.0% item-level HWR, but Japanese-native listeners rated it (Appendix H).

Excluding residual CER violators changes vote-level HWR by +1.9, -2.1 and -4.5 points for AnimeScore, UTMOS and Likability without changing the qualitative pattern (Table 9).

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is mainly the controlled study design and the diagnostics, not a new model. GRPO, Llasa, UTMOS and Best-of-N reranking all exist. The CER-zone gate is a simple piecewise reward that keeps intelligibility as a hard constraint. It is a useful scaffold, and the Target-only ablation shows its effect, but it is a modest step beyond the CER-based reward terms already used in TTS GRPO work. The new parts are three. The paper puts predictor-based RL and same-reward reranking side by side with human A/B tests. It separates average transfer from within-axis reward-gap calibration with a regression that controls for residual CER. It frames each result as a predictor-axis-base tuple instead of a property of the perceptual axis. The GRPO vs Best-of-8 listening result is a useful cautionary point for RL papers that compare only against the base. The evidence is small in scale: one base model, one language for the main study, 50 items and 10 listeners per axis, and single-seed training.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper provides direct listener evidence that optimizing a learned perceptual predictor with GRPO does not guarantee listener-perceived gains, and that listeners do not clearly prefer GRPO over same-reward Best-of-8 reranking on any of the three tested axes. It contributes a reusable screening procedure (reward-gap calibration from a small A/B study, base-output score spread, domain match, within-zone signal). Its conclusions rest on one 1B codec LM and a modest Japanese listener study.

## Claims

- **complicates:** Gains on a learned perceptual reward predictor during RL post-training of codec-based TTS do not reliably transfer to listener preference, and transfer varies by predictor, attribute and base model.
  > *Evidence:* Under the same GRPO scaffold, listeners prefer the AnimeScore-optimized policy over the base on 80.0% of items and the UTMOS-optimized policy on 62.0%, but the Likability-optimized policy on only 36.0%, despite positive target-predictor gains in all three runs. *(§6.1, Table 3)*
- **contradicts:** Policy-gradient RL with a learned perceptual reward yields perceptual quality beyond what reranking base-model samples with the same reward achieves.
  > *Evidence:* Item-level human win rates of GRPO against Best-of-8 under the same CER-gated reward are 52.0% (AnimeScore), 46.0% (UTMOS) and 48.0% (Likability), all near chance, and Best-of-8 reaches target gains close to GRPO's (for example +0.47 vs +0.49 UTMOS). *(§5.2, §6.2, Tables 2 and 3)*
- **supports:** Gating perceptual rewards behind an intelligibility constraint prevents RL from trading transcription fidelity for predictor score in TTS.
  > *Evidence:* Removing the CER zone raises median CER from 0.074 to 0.154 on the UTMOS axis and from 0.098 to 0.119 on Likability, with equal or smaller target gains (+0.25 vs +0.49 UTMOS; +0.15 vs +0.17 Likability). *(§5.2, Table 2)*
- **refines:** Whether item-level predictor score differences explain listener choices is a separate property from whether a predictor-optimized TTS system wins on average, so predictor validity for RL needs both checks.
  > *Evidence:* In a pooled vote-level regression, each standard deviation of signed reward gap multiplies the odds of choosing GRPO by 1.93 while the CER gap has no effect (OR 0.96). Per axis, the reward-gap slope is significant for Likability (the average-transfer failure) and UTMOS but not for Japanese AnimeScore (the average-transfer success). *(§6.3, §7.1, Tables 12 and 17)*
- **complicates:** A predictor that is meaningful as a standalone evaluator can still fail as a constrained RL reward when its variation inside the feasible intelligibility zone is small relative to the constraint penalty.
  > *Evidence:* With the arousal predictor, validation reward improves from -0.34 to -0.05 mainly because CER violations fall from 0.62 to 0.45, while validation arousal stays within 0.61 to 0.64 (net change about +0.014); no listening test was run on this axis. *(§7.3, Appendix I)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> All results come from one base model (Llasa-1B-Multilingual), single-seed training, and listening tests with 50 items, 5 ratings per item and 10 crowdsourced Japanese listeners per axis. The reward predictors also differ in training data, scale and construct, so the authors state that per-axis outcomes cannot be attributed to the perceptual attribute itself.

- The UTMOS item-level HWR interval against the base ([48.2, 74.1]) includes chance, so its "modest positive transfer" is not statistically established at the item level.
- Per-axis regressions (Table 17) give a significant negative CER-gap slope for Japanese AnimeScore (-1.91, p = 0.014). The main text does not discuss this coefficient.
- The English AnimeScore study was rated by the Japanese-native listener pool, so it does not test cross-lingual human transfer.
- The arousal result is a training-dynamics observation only, and per-rollout reward decompositions were not retained.
- Best-of-8 is the only reranking budget tested in the listening study; larger N, fixed-KL comparisons and multi-reward combinations are left open.
- The likability and anime-likeness predictors are trained on Japanese data (CocoNut-Humoresque and the authors' AnimeScore corpus), and their behaviour on other languages or base models is untested.
