> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Haowei Lou et al.** (UNSW Sydney / Dolby Laboratories) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11590) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ?
>
> A single 43.51M-parameter flow-matching DiT generates both speech and singing by expanding content, style and prosody conditions to the acoustic frame rate, so text or voice style prompts can be freely combined with discrete prosody tokens (tones, stresses, MIDI notes) or a continuous F0 contour.

## Problem

Speech synthesis, singing voice synthesis, style-controllable TTS and voice cloning are usually built as separate systems with task-specific architectures and control signals. The authors argue that autoregressive token-prediction models (CosyVoice, IndexTTS) set duration and prosody only implicitly, which blocks fine temporal control, while non-autoregressive systems fix a particular content-prosody alignment (phoneme durations, note-to-phoneme binding, or an utterance-level prosody reference). Those fixed assumptions break down when several control signals act together, for example lyrics against score timing, or new content that must follow a reference F0 trajectory frame by frame.

## Method

CookVoice frames voice generation as conditional generation of a continuous acoustic latent from three factors: content, prosody and style. The latent comes from an autoencoder with a HiFi-GAN-like design that encodes a 513-bin linear spectrogram and decodes to waveform with transposed 1D convolutions. It is trained with reconstruction and adversarial losses and then frozen *(§2.2)*.

Each condition is expanded to the latent's frame length T and the three are concatenated into a 3D x T conditioning tensor *(§3.4)*:

- **Style** comes from one of two sources. A text description is embedded by a frozen MPNet sentence encoder plus a trainable linear layer. A reference voice is encoded into the same acoustic latent and passed through a Transformer encoder with attentive pooling. The global style vector is repeated across all frames *(§3.1)*.
- **Content** is a phoneme sequence from the group's multilingual G2P module (LanStyleTTS), processed by FastSpeech-style feed-forward Transformer blocks and expanded by per-phoneme durations. For speech, training durations come from alignments produced by a pre-trained ParaStyleTTS, and inference uses ParaStyleTTS's duration predictor. For singing, durations are computed deterministically from score beats as proportions of the total frame count *(§3.2)*.
- **Prosody** is either discrete or continuous. The discrete form uses lexical tokens (Mandarin tones, English stress) expanded with phoneme durations for speech, or MIDI notes expanded with beat-derived durations for singing. The note sequence may have a different length from the phoneme sequence. The continuous form is a frame-level F0 contour. It is log-scaled with a 50 Hz floor and mean-removed per utterance, with unvoiced frames set to -2, so it carries relative intonation rather than the speaker's absolute pitch range *(§3.3, §4.2)*.

A DiT-S backbone, conditioned on this tensor through cross-attention, is trained with the optimal-transport flow-matching MSE objective and sampled with a first-order Euler ODE solver *(§3.4)*. Multi-task ability comes from per-sample condition switching during training: style is drawn from text or voice with probability 0.5 each, and prosody from discrete or continuous with probability 0.5 each *(§3.5)*. At inference, tasks are defined by which conditions are supplied. Appendix Table 5 lists TTS, text- and voice-style TTS and singing, F0-controlled TTS and singing, voice mimicry, voice conversion, singing voice conversion, editing, sketch-to-voice and humming-to-voice. Only the TTS and text-to-singing (TTSV) configurations are evaluated quantitatively.

Training uses about 123k clips (168 hours, 6,361 speakers) of English and Mandarin speech and singing from seven public corpora. The model trains on 110k samples at a 1:9 singing-to-speech ratio, for 800K steps with batch size 32 on a single RTX 5090 *(§4.1, §4.3)*. The number of ODE steps used for the main comparison tables is not stated.

## Key Results

Evaluation uses held-out samples that the model did not see in training *(§4.3)*. It compares against six TTS configurations (CosyVoice in two modes, F5-TTS, ParaStyleTTS, IndexTTS, Vevo2) and five singing systems (DiffSinger, StyleSinger, TCSinger, Vevo1.5, Vevo2). CookVoice is reported in all four style-by-prosody combinations.

**Controllability (Table 2).** For speech, CookVoice's best configuration (voice style + continuous F0) reaches S-SIM 91.65% and F0-CORR 0.7102. The strongest baselines are Vevo2 at 75.11% S-SIM and IndexTTS at 0.2548 F0-CORR. CookVoice's lowest TTS F0-RMSE is 74.98, against 123.65 for the best baseline (units not stated). In the matched text style + discrete setting against ParaStyleTTS, CookVoice gains 41.48% relative S-SIM and 121.10% relative F0-CORR. For singing, voice style + continuous F0 gives S-SIM 95.00%, F0-RMSE 56.96 and F0-CORR 0.8425, compared with the best baselines at 88.09% (Vevo2), 79.56 and 0.7125 (both Vevo1.5). One matched comparison goes against CookVoice. With voice style + discrete notes, its singing F0-RMSE (95.01) is 2.71% worse than TCSinger's 92.50.

These comparisons favour CookVoice by design. The continuous-F0 configurations receive an F0 contour extracted from a reference voice, and the baselines are run with discrete prosody only (except Vevo1.5 and Vevo2 in singing). S-SIM is computed with a style encoder from the same group's AutoSIFT work, not with an independent speaker-verification model.

**Quality and intelligibility (Table 3, Table 8).** Quality is CookVoice's weak point for speech. Its best TTS MOS (3.98) is below every baseline configuration, which range from 4.03 (ParaStyleTTS) to 4.42 (IndexTTS); ground truth scores 4.05. TTS WER sits mid-range: 8.13 Chinese and 4.19 English, against IndexTTS's 4.16 and 2.88. For singing, the best MOS of 3.40 is above DiffSinger, StyleSinger, TCSinger and Vevo1.5 and just below Vevo2 (3.42). MC-MOS is the highest of all systems (0.28 vs. Vevo2's 0.11). The best Chinese singing WER (9.79) beats DiffSinger, StyleSinger and TCSinger but not Vevo1.5 (8.85) or Vevo2 (4.66). The best English singing WER (19.56) is second to Vevo2 (11.37). Neither the number of MOS raters nor the number of rated samples is reported.

**Control-source effects (Figure 2).** In speech, a voice reference beats a text description on every metric, and continuous F0 beats discrete tokens on every metric. Singing is mixed: discrete notes give higher S-SIM and better English WER than continuous F0. With text style, swapping notes for F0 drops S-SIM from 85.75% to 72.51%.

**ODE steps and efficiency (§5.4, Figures 3-6, Table 4).** Style and F0 metrics level off at 4-8 Euler steps. WER, PhoER and ProER are best at about 4 steps and degrade slightly beyond that. MOS peaks at 16 steps while MC-MOS peaks at 4. CookVoice runs at RTF 0.04 with 1.37 GB of CUDA memory. Against Vevo2, the other model covering both speech and singing, that is 4.99% of the parameters, 20.03% of the memory and 0.27% of the RTF. ParaStyleTTS (RTF 0.03) is faster, and DiffSinger (26.74M) and StyleSinger (42.00M) are smaller.

## Novelty Assessment

The pieces are established. FastSpeech-style duration expansion, frame-level F0 conditioning, MPNet text-style prompts and reference-voice style encoders all have precedent in the group's ParaStyleTTS and AutoSIFT work, and the backbone is a standard DiT trained with OT flow matching. What is new is the packaging. All controls are expanded to the frame rate and concatenated, so one small model trained with random condition switching covers speech and singing with text or voice style and discrete or continuous prosody. Deriving singing durations from beat proportions lets lyrics and notes have different sequence lengths without binding notes to phonemes. Mean-removed log-F0 keeps the pitch input separate from speaker range. This is an engineering integration rather than a new generative mechanism.

The headline controllability gains need careful reading. The large F0-CORR margins come mostly from configurations that receive a reference F0 contour the discrete-prosody baselines never see. S-SIM uses the authors' own style encoder. Speech MOS trails every baseline. The fairest like-for-like comparison is the matched discrete-prosody settings, where CookVoice still improves style similarity and pitch correlation over the strongest baseline. Most of the advertised tasks (voice conversion, editing, mimicry, humming-to-voice) are listed as formulations without quantitative evaluation.

## Field Significance

Moderate — CookVoice shows that a 43.51M-parameter non-autoregressive model can cover speech and singing with interchangeable text, voice, symbolic and F0 controls. In matched settings it gives stronger style similarity and pitch following than much larger autoregressive and unified baselines, at a clear cost in speech naturalness. It offers a useful reference point for compact, controllable unified voice generation. It does not settle whether frame-level conditioning or the extra reference F0 explains the controllability gains.

## Claims

- **supports:** Expanding all control signals to the acoustic frame rate lets one non-autoregressive model handle speech and singing with interchangeable symbolic and continuous prosody controls.
  > *Evidence:* Concatenated frame-aligned style, phoneme and prosody embeddings with per-sample random switching between text/voice style and discrete/continuous prosody let a single DiT-S produce TTS and text-to-singing in all four control combinations. In each domain, the best configuration has the highest S-SIM and F0-CORR among the systems compared. *(§3.2-§3.5, §5.1, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Dense frame-level pitch conditioning improves pitch following, and for speech it also improves naturalness and intelligibility, compared with phoneme-level prosody tokens.
  > *Evidence:* In CookVoice's TTS results, continuous F0 control beats discrete lexical prosody tokens on F0-RMSE, F0-CORR, MOS and both WERs (normalized marginal scores 0.84/0.89 vs. 0.23/0.22 on the F0 metrics). Speech MOS rises from 3.60 to 3.98 under voice style. *(§5.3, Figure 2a, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** In singing synthesis, continuous reference pitch contours can trade style fidelity and lyric intelligibility for melody accuracy relative to symbolic note conditioning.
  > *Evidence:* For TTSV, discrete notes give higher S-SIM and better English WER than continuous F0. Under text-based style, replacing notes with F0 lowers S-SIM from 85.75% to 72.51%, even though F0-CORR rises from 0.5383 to 0.8401. *(§5.3, Figure 2b, Table 2)*
- **refines:** Reference audio conveys paralinguistic style more faithfully than natural-language style descriptions in a shared style space.
  > *Evidence:* With the same model, voice-based style conditioning beats text-based conditioning on TTS S-SIM by a wide margin (normalized 0.93 vs. 0.20) and also improves F0-CORR, MOS and WER. In singing, intelligibility does not improve under voice-based style (similar Chinese WER, worse English WER). *(§5.3, Figure 2)*
- **complicates:** Small unified voice generators can lead on control-fidelity metrics while trailing larger task-specific systems on perceived speech naturalness.
  > *Evidence:* CookVoice's best TTS MOS (3.98) is below all six baseline configurations (4.03-4.42), even though its S-SIM and F0-CORR exceed every baseline, with 7-14 times fewer parameters than F5-TTS and IndexTTS. *(§5.1, §5.5, Tables 2-4)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The controllability gains over baselines are partly built into the comparison. CookVoice's continuous-F0 configurations receive a reference pitch contour that the discrete-prosody baselines do not, and style similarity is scored with the authors' own AutoSIFT style encoder. Voice conversion, editing, mimicry and humming-to-voice are listed as supported tasks but are not evaluated.

- Speech naturalness trails every baseline; the authors attribute this to small scale (DiT-S, 168 hours) and leave scaling unexplored *(§7)*.
- MOS and MC-MOS tests do not report rater counts or sample counts. MC-MOS is a comparative score whose reference system is not described.
- The paper does not establish whether evaluation speakers or reference voices are unseen; it says only that evaluation samples are held out. Zero-shot cloning is not demonstrated.
- Speech duration depends on a separately trained ParaStyleTTS aligner and duration predictor, so speech rhythm is inherited from that model rather than learned jointly.
- The singing baseline labelled Vevo1.5 cites the original Vevo paper, so it is unclear which checkpoint was evaluated.
- Coverage is English and Mandarin only. The number of ODE steps used for the main tables is not stated, and code release is not mentioned.
