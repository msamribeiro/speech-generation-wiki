> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Zifan Guan et al.** (The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen / Taobao & Tmall Group of Alibaba) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20124) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✗
>
> Distills Gemini's pairwise prosody judgments for live-streaming TTS into a fine-tuned Qwen3-Omni judge, then breaks the judge's tendency to copy its overall verdict into every rubric dimension by dropping the overall target, masking uncertain pair-dimensions, and assigning each dimension's GRPO advantage only to its own rationale span.

## Problem

Live-streaming (e-commerce host) speech needs high energy, varied intonation, matched emotion and switches between product explanation and audience address. Reference-free MOS predictors such as UTMOS and NISQA regress to naturalness or transmission quality and do not capture these properties. Proprietary audio-language models such as Gemini can make the judgments, but they cost too much for scoring thousands of candidates or for providing RL feedback during TTS optimization. A second problem appears once a cheaper judge is trained on a multi-dimensional rubric: the judge tends to give every dimension the same winner as its overall verdict (the authors call this "verdict coupling"), so a seven-dimension rubric reduces to one preference bit.

## Method

The judge receives two clips rendering the same transcript and outputs, per dimension, a rationale and a pair of 1–10 scores; the verdict is the sign of the score difference (§2). The rubric has four always-scored core dimensions (C1 fluency and naturalness, C2 intonation variation, C3 emotional expression, C4 live-streaming expressiveness) and three transcript-triggered dimensions (L1 key-information emphasis, L2 emotional-state switching, L3 interaction-mode switching) (Table 1).

**Live-ProsodyJudge (LPJ).** Gemini-3.1-pro-preview labels each training pair four times, twice in each A/B order, and votes are mapped back to the physical clips. LPJ keeps only unanimous 4-0 outcomes and balances which slot holds the winner. Training follows a curriculum from human-versus-TTS pairs (large quality gap) to cross-model and same-model pairs. A rank-32 LoRA on Qwen3-Omni attention projections is trained with a dimension-weighted SFT loss over rationale sections, summary and conclusion (Eq. 2), then merged (§3.1). A GRPO stage rewards +1/−1 for matching the target overall verdict, with one advantage broadcast over the whole completion (§3.2).

**Decoupled-LPJ (D-LPJ).** D-LPJ covers only C1–C4. Gemini is queried four times per core dimension in isolation, so teacher rationales never see an overall verdict. A pair-dimension is retained when votes are 4-0, 3-1, or three agreeing non-tie votes plus one abstention; other pair-dimensions get zero SFT loss while the rest of the pair stays trainable. The target has no summary or overall conclusion. Two SFT stages use rank-64 QLoRA (§3.2). Span-local GRPO then computes a separate ±1 reward per confident dimension, normalizes it within the rollout group (G = 8) per dimension, and applies that advantage only to tokens in that dimension's rationale span (Eqs. 4–5). The comparison baseline, aggregate-reward GRPO, broadcasts one reward derived from an externally summed verdict. Overall decisions from D-LPJ are made outside the model with a weighted sum of per-dimension score differences (§3.2).

At inference, the students use ten sampled judgments, five in each A/B order, average each clip's scores and take the sign of the mean difference; ties count as errors (§4.1). For Best-of-8 selection, LPJ judges all 28 pairs among eight candidates and picks the candidate with the most pairwise wins (Eq. 6).

The evaluation suite holds 1,043 human-annotated pairs from a matched-transcript live-streaming domain with 135 speakers and three host styles: Qwen3-TTS base vs an internal SFT variant (T1, 417; T1⋆ is a 119-pair unanimous subset), two samples from an internal Qwen3-TTS-GDPO variant (T2, 111), that variant vs BERT-CosyVoice (T3, 315), and human recordings vs Qwen3-TTS (T4, 200). T5 adds 222 IndexTTS2-vs-FireRedTTS pairs from a system combination absent from coupled-judge training. Three trained contractors, blinded to system and source, label each pair (§2). Model size is not reported beyond the Qwen3-Omni backbone name.

## Key Results

**Overall-verdict agreement (Table 2).** With ten balanced-order samples, LPJ v1+GRPO agrees with human labels at 71.22 / 82.35 / 70.27 / 82.86 / 83.50% on T1 / T1⋆ / T2 / T3 / T4, above single-call Gemini (65.23 / 72.27 / 65.77 / 73.33 / 58.00) in every column. SpeechJudge-GRM with ten samples reaches 55.88 / 72.27 / 48.65 / 53.97 / 42.00. The Gemini comparison is not like for like: the student uses ten samples and order balancing, Gemini one call in presented order (§4.5). LPJ v1+GRPO is not the top row in every column: single-stage SFT is higher on T1 (72.66) and single-sample LPJ v1+GRPO is higher on T1⋆ (83.19). Paired bootstrap intervals for GRPO minus SFT all include zero (Table 3), so the GRPO stage gives descriptive, not significant, changes. Dropping the large-gap first curriculum stage is associated with a 34.5-point lower T4 score (45.5 vs 80.0), and keeping 3-1 labels (v0) is lower than 4-0-only v1 on four of five columns.

**Transfer (§4.1).** On T5, LPJ v1+GRPO gets 192/222 (86.49%), Gemini 189/222 (85.14%) and SpeechJudge-GRM 185/222 (83.33%); the three-pair gap over Gemini has no confidence interval.

**Position bias (Table 4).** With Δpos defined as second-slot mean score minus first-slot mean score, SpeechJudge-GRM gives the second-presented clip +0.978 points and prefers it in 66.8% of judgments (t = +11.7), while LPJ shows +0.012 and 50.7% (t = +0.4).

**Decoupling (§4.3, Table 5).** On the 140-pair set with full C1–C4 human annotation, coupled LPJ produces 0/140 non-unanimous core verdict vectors and 48.0% agreement on confident human dimension labels. D-LPJ SFT produces 90/140 non-unanimous vectors at 67.9% agreement; span-local GRPO gives 100/140 and 71.4%. On the larger unanimous dimension-specific sets, ten-sample span-local GRPO has the highest pooled agreement in Table 5 (86.10%), compared with 85.10% for aggregate-reward GRPO, 84.10% for SFT and 78.65% for single-call Gemini. It is not highest in every dimension: aggregate-reward GRPO is higher on C3 (87.32 vs 85.21) and ties on C2 (83.14). All per-dimension paired intervals against SFT and aggregate-reward GRPO include zero (Table 6).

**Best-of-8 (§4.5).** Two contractors ranked the top three of eight candidates for 400 transcripts; on the 136 sets where their ordered top-three rankings matched, the LPJ tournament winner is in the human top 1 / 2 / 3 in 72.06 / 77.94 / 85.29% of sets, against random baselines of 12.5 / 25.0 / 37.5%. These rates apply only to the high-agreement subset.

## Novelty Assessment

Distilling a proprietary audio-LLM judge into an open audio LLM with LoRA, swap-consistent labeling and a curriculum is engineering on known components. The new parts are the diagnosis of verdict coupling in multi-dimensional pairwise speech judges and the D-LPJ recipe: dimension-isolated teacher queries, pair-dimension SFT masking, and span-local GRPO, which restricts each dimension's normalized advantage to its own rationale tokens. Span-local GRPO is a new training objective on an unchanged architecture. Its measured contribution is small: the clearest evidence of decoupling comes from removing the overall target and from masking (0 to 90 non-unanimous vectors), while span-local GRPO adds 10 more and every interval against the baselines includes zero. The rubric and human-annotated test suite are a domain-specific evaluation contribution, but the audio is not released and all training results use one seed.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper provides a concrete measurement of verdict collapse in an LLM-based multi-dimensional speech judge (0/140 non-unanimous vectors) and a training recipe that reduces it, which matters for anyone using rubric-based audio-LLM judges as dimension-wise TTS rewards. It also shows that a distilled open judge can match or exceed single-call Gemini agreement on expressive live-streaming prosody at lower inference cost, and that it can pick human-preferred candidates in Best-of-8 selection. Its evidence is limited to one proprietary domain, and the judge has not yet been used to train a TTS model.

## Claims

- **complicates:** LLM-based speech judges trained on multi-dimensional rubrics with an overall verdict tend to collapse per-dimension judgments onto the overall preference, so dimension scores from such judges may not carry independent information.
  > *Evidence:* The coupled LPJ produces 0/140 non-unanimous core-dimension verdict vectors on a set where three annotators label C1–C4 separately, with 48.0% agreement on confident human dimension labels. *(§4.3)*
- **supports:** Removing the shared overall-verdict target and masking uncertain labels per dimension, rather than per sample, lets a learned speech judge produce distinct dimension verdicts with higher agreement against human labels.
  > *Evidence:* D-LPJ SFT, trained on dimension-isolated teacher rationales with pair-dimension masking and no overall conclusion, yields 90/140 non-unanimous vectors and 67.9% agreement, versus 0/140 and 48.0% for coupled LPJ; span-local GRPO raises these to 100/140 and 71.4%. *(§3.2, §4.3)*
- **supports:** Distilling a proprietary audio-language model judge into a fine-tuned open audio LLM can match or exceed the teacher's agreement with human preferences on expressive TTS prosody when the student aggregates repeated order-balanced samples.
  > *Evidence:* Ten-sample LPJ v1+GRPO exceeds single-call Gemini-3.1-pro-preview on all five main test columns (e.g. 83.50 vs 58.00% on human-vs-TTS T4) and on the 222-pair transfer set (86.49 vs 85.14%), though the student uses ten samples and order balancing. *(§4.1, Table 2)*
- **refines:** Pairwise learned speech judges can carry large presentation-order bias, and swap-consistent teacher labeling with order-balanced inference can bring a judge close to slot symmetry.
  > *Evidence:* SpeechJudge-GRM gives the second-presented clip +0.978 points and prefers it in 66.8% of 4,170 T1 judgments, while LPJ shows +0.012 points and 50.7%; the authors note this is not a causal ablation of either component. *(§4.2, Table 4)*
- **supports:** Confidence-filtered teacher supervision matters more than label volume when distilling pairwise speech-quality judges.
  > *Evidence:* Admitting 3-1 teacher votes (LPJ v0) scores lower than 4-0-only LPJ v1 on four of five test columns at ten samples. *(§4.1, Table 2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> All test sets are internal live-streaming data whose audio is not redistributed, all training comparisons use one random seed, and every paired interval for the GRPO stages (Tables 3 and 6) includes zero, so the method-level gains cannot be independently verified or treated as significant.

- The Gemini comparisons give the students ten order-balanced samples against one Gemini call in presented order; the effects of repeated sampling and order balancing are not separated (§4.4).
- D-LPJ covers only the four core dimensions; the transcript-triggered L1–L3 dimensions remain coupled.
- Using the judge's dimension-wise rewards to post-train a TTS model is proposed but not done; the only downstream use evaluated is Best-of-8 selection.
- Best-of-8 hit rates are computed on the 136 of 400 sets where both annotators agreed on the ordered top three, which excludes harder cases.
- Most pairs in the dimension-specific test sets lack complete C1–C4 annotations; only the 140-pair set has full coverage.
- CEAEval's adaptive audio-attention bias trained stably but did not improve held-out accuracy and was excluded (§4.4).
- The backbone size, training-set size and language of the live-streaming data are not reported.
