> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Zhe Zhang et al.** (National Institute of Informatics / University of Science and Technology of China) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12432) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ?
>
> Releases seven utterance-aligned, speaker-anonymized versions of the 1.29M-utterance VoxTube English subset, built with embedding-transform VC, latent-space VC, and negatively guided prompt TTS. The paper then trains a speaker verifier on each version and shows that no anonymization method wins on privacy, utility, speaker diversity, and fairness at once.

## Problem

Large in-the-wild speaker corpora carry a biometric risk: the voice in each recording can identify its speaker. Access to such corpora is also fragile; the VoxCeleb website, for example, no longer distributes source media. Voice-privacy research (the VoicePrivacy challenges) evaluates anonymization mostly at the utterance level, on privacy, intelligibility, and naturalness. Strong utterance-level anonymization does not by itself yield a useful *training corpus*. Prior work, such as the SynVox2 anonymized VoxCeleb2 derivative, studied a single anonymization recipe. No controlled study compared several anonymization families as corpus builders on the same source data. Such a study would need to measure conversation-level linkage, downstream speaker-verification utility, speaker-space diversity, and subgroup fairness together.

## Method

**Source data.** VoxTube is a speaker-recognition corpus built from CC BY-licensed YouTube videos and distributed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. VoxTubeS inherits that licence. VoxTube has no transcripts, so the authors transcribe its English-labelled subset with Whisper large-v3. They then filter it: language-ID confidence of at least 0.9, at least four words and 20 alphabetic characters, no repetitive transcripts, and no videos identified as non-English. This leaves 1,292,167 utterances from all 1,511 speakers, split 9:1 into train and development (§II-A). The English restriction comes from the TTS-based family. The OHNN and SALT pipelines can also process the multilingual inventory, and the authors release multilingual versions of those two.

**Environment preservation.** For OHNN and SALT, each recording is separated into estimated speech and environment components. Only the speech is anonymized, and the estimated environment is remixed with the output, following the SynVox2 background-restoration recipe. DAIEN-TTS models speech and environment with separate prompts, so it produces the final mixture itself (§II-B, Fig. 1).

**Three method families, seven variants (Table I).** All variants are utterance-aligned with the source, so linguistic content, speaker inventory, and splits are held fixed across them.

- *Embedding transform (OHNN).* An orthogonal Householder neural network maps each speaker embedding to a pseudo-speaker embedding. OHNN-HiFiGAN follows the SynVox2 recipe with a HiFi-GAN vocoder. OHNN-BigVGAN-SC replaces it with BigVGAN and adds a speaker-consistency (SC) objective during vocoder training. The SC objective uses ECAPA-TDNN embeddings to push the vocoder to preserve the speaker characteristics given by the input mel spectrogram and speaker embedding (§II-B a).
- *Latent conversion (SALT).* A WavLM-feature voice-conversion method. SALT builds a target latent pool from a weighted mixture of reference speakers sampled from LibriSpeech. It blends each source frame with target features by frame-level kNN regression and vocodes the result with HiFi-GAN. SALT-k4 uses a four-speaker mixture with k = 4; SALT-k8 uses eight speakers with k = 8 (§II-B b).
- *Prompted TTS (DAIEN-NCFG).* DAIEN-TTS, an environment-aware flow-matching TTS with separate speaker and environment audio prompts, re-synthesizes each utterance from its Whisper transcript. The authors set the speaker-prompt classifier-free-guidance weight γ below zero (negative CFG, "NCFG"). A negative weight steers generation away from the prompted speaker instead of towards it. Three settings are released: γ = −1.0, −0.75, −0.5 (§II-B c). The paper describes no retraining of DAIEN-TTS; the change is at inference.

Model sizes for the anonymizers and the vocoders are not reported.

**Evaluation (§III).** Six axes extend the VoicePrivacy metric set:

- *Utterance-level unlinkability:* EER of a pretrained ECAPA-TDNN attacker trained on VoxCeleb, with original enrollment and anonymized test audio. Higher is more private.
- *Conversation-level privacy:* top-1 linkability and singling-out risk, with authentic enrollment embeddings matched against mean embeddings of anonymized conversations. Conversation lengths are L ∈ {1, 3, 30} and populations N ∈ {20, 100, 1000}, averaged over five runs and ten folds.
- *Downstream ASV utility:* a separate ECAPA-TDNN is trained on each variant (16 epochs, AAM loss, speed/reverb/noise augmentation) and scored by EER on VoxCeleb1-O.
- *Linguistic consistency:* pseudo-WER (pWER), the WER of Whisper large-v3 on anonymized audio against Whisper's transcript of the original audio, on 29,109 development utterances. It is not WER against ground truth.
- *Speaker diversity:* mean pairwise cosine distance d̄ between speaker centroids, and the effective rank r_eff of the centroid covariance.
- *Subgroup fairness:* fairness discrepancy rate (FDR) at the pooled-EER threshold on VoxCeleb2 gender trials and ten VoxAccent accent trial sets. Higher FDR means smaller subgroup gaps.

No listening test or naturalness metric is reported.

- **Privacy versus utility.** The original audio is almost fully linkable (unlinkability EER 1.49%, top-1 linkability 1.000 at L = 30, N = 1000) and gives the best verifier (ASV EER 4.75%). DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) is the most private variant on all three privacy columns (44.28% EER, 0.010 linkability, 0.383 singling-out). It is also the least useful: ASV EER is 44.71%, and r_eff collapses to 2.93. Across the three DAIEN settings, a stronger negative weight raises unlinkability and worsens downstream EER monotonically (33.39 → 39.97 → 44.28; 14.05 → 23.70 → 44.71).
- **Speaker-consistency vocoder training.** OHNN-BigVGAN-SC improves on OHNN-HiFiGAN in unlinkability (38.02 vs. 32.70), linkability (0.025 vs. 0.054), d̄ (0.673 vs. 0.592), r_eff (36.27 vs. 27.03), and pWER (28.31 vs. 28.53). Downstream ASV EER is nearly unchanged (12.40 vs. 12.32, slightly worse) and singling-out is tied (0.507). Its unlinkability is the highest among the OHNN and SALT variants, though below DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) and (−0.75). Its linkability is the lowest of all variants except DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0). On singling-out, however, both DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) (0.383) and (−0.75) (0.436) are lower.
- **SALT gives the most usable corpora.** SALT-k4 has the best anonymized ASV EER (8.86%), and SALT-k8 the lowest pWER (25.92%). Both have the weakest unlinkability among anonymized variants (26.84% and 26.08%), the lowest d̄ (0.257 and 0.250), and low accent FDR (0.772 and 0.798).
- **Aggregation defeats utterance-level privacy.** Linkability attack success rises with the number of aggregated utterances for every method (Fig. 3). At 30 utterances and 1,000 speakers, all anonymizers except the most aggressive DAIEN setting keep non-trivial linkage (0.025 to 0.175).
- **Diversity.** OHNN-BigVGAN-SC keeps the broadest anonymized speaker space (r_eff 36.27, d̄ 0.673), still far below Auth (97.25, 0.928). The DAIEN variants have the smallest r_eff (2.93 to 8.03). Utility tracks r_eff broadly (§IV-E). In the Spearman analysis over the seven variants, d̄ correlates with accent FDR (ρ = 0.82; Fig. 5).
- **Fairness does not follow aggregate performance.** Female EER exceeds male EER in every condition except DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) (44.69 vs. 45.86). Spanish is the worst accent for six of the eight systems (Table III). Among anonymized systems, the OHNN variants reach gender FDR of 0.987 and 0.986, but DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) scores higher still (0.998). That value reflects uniform failure: gender EERs are near 45% and accent EERs run from 38.04% to 45.04%. OHNN-BigVGAN-SC has the highest anonymized accent FDR (0.895, vs. 0.930 for Auth).
- **Gender structure.** In a UMAP fitted on authentic centroids, the original speakers form a male cluster (800 male, 21 female) and a female cluster (659 female, 31 male). The OHNN variants keep 87.6% (HiFiGAN) and 82.7% (BigVGAN-SC) of speakers in their original cluster. SALT-k4 moves 1,321 of 1,511 speakers into the female-dominated cluster. DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) places 1,504 speakers in the male-dominated region (§IV-F, Fig. 4).

The comparison is controlled: all variants share source utterances, speaker inventory, splits, and ASV recipe. Privacy is measured with ECAPA-TDNN attackers only, and no naturalness metric is reported.

## Novelty Assessment

The main contribution is the resource and the joint evaluation. The anonymization systems are existing ones: OHNN (Miao et al.), SALT (Lv et al.), and DAIEN-TTS. The two adaptations the authors list are small. One adds a speaker-consistency objective to BigVGAN vocoder training inside the OHNN pipeline, described in one sentence with no ablation beyond the comparison with OHNN-HiFiGAN. That comparison also changes the vocoder, so the SC objective's effect cannot be separated from the vocoder swap. The other runs DAIEN-TTS with a negative speaker-prompt CFG weight, an inference-time reuse of classifier-free guidance. More novel is the evaluation framing: corpus-level linkability and singling-out under aggregation, utility of verifiers trained on anonymized data, speaker-space geometry, and subgroup fairness, all on paired corpora from one source. The scale (seven variants of about 1.29M utterances each) and the redistributable licence give the dataset its practical value.

## Field Significance

Moderate. VoxTubeS provides a redistributable, paired testbed for comparing voice-anonymization and speech-synthesis methods as producers of training data rather than as per-utterance privacy filters. It demonstrates that utterance-level unlinkability, conversation-level linkage, downstream utility, speaker diversity, and subgroup fairness can rank the same anonymizers differently. Single-score privacy evaluation can therefore misjudge whether an anonymized corpus is fit for release or for training.

## Claims

- **complicates:** Utterance-level anonymization strength does not guarantee privacy once an attacker can aggregate many utterances per speaker; residual identity cues accumulate across a conversation.
  > *Evidence:* Linkability attack success grows with the number of aggregated utterances for every anonymizer. At 30 utterances and 1,000 speakers, top-1 linkability stays at 0.138 to 0.175 for SALT variants even with unlinkability EER near 26%. *(§IV-B, Fig. 3, Table II)*
- **supports:** Stronger speaker-identity suppression in anonymized or synthetic training corpora trades off against downstream speaker-verification utility and speaker-space diversity.
  > *Evidence:* Making the negative speaker-guidance weight of DAIEN-TTS stronger (−0.5 → −1.0) raises unlinkability EER from 33.39% to 44.28%. Over the same range, ASV EER of a verifier trained on the corpus degrades from 14.05% to 44.71%, and r_eff falls from 8.03 to 2.93. Across the seven variants, unlinkability is negatively rank-correlated with both utility measures. *(§IV-A, §V, Table II, Fig. 5)*
- **refines:** Vocoder training objectives in anonymization pipelines can change privacy and speaker-space diversity without changing downstream utility, so vocoder choice is a privacy-relevant design decision rather than a pure quality decision.
  > *Evidence:* With the same OHNN pseudo-speaker transform, replacing HiFi-GAN by BigVGAN trained with an ECAPA-based speaker-consistency objective raises unlinkability EER from 32.70% to 38.02%. It also lowers L = 30 linkability from 0.054 to 0.025 and raises r_eff from 27.03 to 36.27, with downstream ASV EER at 12.40% vs. 12.32%; the vocoder swap and the objective are not ablated separately. *(§IV-A, §IV-E, Table II)*
- **complicates:** Aggregate fairness discrepancy scores on speaker verification can hide uniform failure, so subgroup fairness of anonymized training data must be read alongside absolute subgroup error rates.
  > *Evidence:* DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) has the highest gender FDR of all systems (0.998) only because female and male EERs are both near 45% (44.69% and 45.86%). Gender FDR is also nearly uncorrelated with utility and diversity across the seven variants. *(§IV-D, §V, Tables II and III, Fig. 5)*
- **complicates:** Speaker anonymization can systematically distort the population structure of a corpus, for example by collapsing speakers towards one gender region of the embedding space.
  > *Evidence:* In a UMAP fitted on authentic speaker centroids, SALT-k4 moves 1,321 of 1,511 speakers into the female-dominated cluster and DAIEN-NCFG(−1.0) places 1,504 speakers in the male-dominated region. OHNN variants keep 82.7% to 87.6% of speakers in their original cluster. *(§IV-F, Fig. 4)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] Privacy, diversity, and the SC training objective all rely on ECAPA-TDNN embeddings
> The attacker models, the speaker-diversity measures, and the speaker-consistency objective used to build OHNN-BigVGAN-SC all use ECAPA-TDNN speaker embeddings. The privacy gains of the SC variant could therefore partly reflect the shared embedding family. Results may not hold against attackers with other speaker encoders or stronger assumed knowledge, as the authors acknowledge in §V.

- **No perceptual evaluation.** There is no listening test and no automatic naturalness score (UTMOS or DNSMOS). The perceptual quality of the released corpora and of their remixed environment component is not measured.
- **Pseudo-WER only.** Linguistic consistency is Whisper-on-anonymized against Whisper-on-original, not WER against references. The same Whisper transcripts also drive DAIEN-NCFG synthesis, which ties the pWER of that family to Whisper's errors.
- **Confounded SC comparison.** OHNN-BigVGAN-SC changes both the vocoder (HiFi-GAN → BigVGAN) and the training objective, so the SC objective's contribution is not isolated.
- **English-only paired comparison.** The seven-way comparison uses the English subset only, because DAIEN-TTS is English-only. The released multilingual OHNN and SALT versions are not evaluated here.
- **Single downstream task.** Utility is measured only for speaker verification, apart from pWER. Usefulness for ASR, diarization, or TTS training is not tested.
- **Persistent subgroup gaps.** Female EER stays above male EER and Spanish-accent trials remain hardest for most variants. The authors name reducing these disparities as future work.
- **Licence scope.** VoxTubeS inherits CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so commercial use is excluded.
