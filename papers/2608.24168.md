>
> FireRedAudio is a unified 9B audio language model that feeds audio to one trainable LLM through two separate, unfused continuous input pathways (a Whisper-initialized encoder for understanding, a RedAE latent pathway for generation) and generates speech as continuous latents with a flow-matching DiT, covering ASR, audio understanding, zero-shot TTS, instruction-driven TTS and free-form speech editing.

## Problem

Unified audio language models must serve two kinds of audio input with conflicting needs. Understanding (ASR, audio question answering, long-form analysis) benefits from compact, task-oriented features that keep long contexts tractable, while speech generation needs representations that can be decoded back into a waveform with timbre, prosody and style intact. Most prior unified models generate discrete codec tokens (Qwen3-Omni's Thinker-Talker split, Kimi-Audio, UniAudio 2.0, DualSpeechLM), which the authors argue loses information and adds multi-codebook overhead. Continuous-latent alternatives such as Ming-UniAudio route understanding and generation through a single VAE-rooted representation, and that paper's own ablations show that training the shared semantic module jointly early on hurts both tasks. The open question is whether one shared LLM can consume different continuous representations according to each audio segment's role.

## Method

FireRedAudio keeps one shared language backbone (Qwen3.5-9B) and gives it two continuous audio input pathways whose outputs are never added or fused; each audio segment is inserted at its position in a ChatML conversation through the pathway that matches its role.

- **Understanding pathway.** A 16 kHz log-Mel front end feeds an Audio Encoder initialized from the Whisper-large-v3 encoder, applied in non-overlapping windows of up to 30 s (50 Hz output). A separate Audio Adapter compresses to 12.5 Hz and projects into the LLM embedding space; cross-window context is left to the LLM, which allows inputs of up to one hour.
- **Generation pathway.** Speech used as a generation condition (zero-shot reference, source audio for editing) is encoded by the frozen RedAE Encoder, a deterministic causal autoencoder (no KL term) built from Qwen3 Transformer blocks that maps 24 kHz audio to 64-dimensional latents at 25 Hz. RedAE is pretrained separately with an X-Codec-style GAN objective (reconstruction, adversarial and feature-matching losses) plus an MSE distillation loss that aligns its latents with a frozen teacher audio encoder trained on understanding tasks, on a mix of 50% clean speech, 25% noisy speech, 10% sound effects and 15% music. Its decoder uses a Vocos-style iSTFT head. A Patch Encoder (a local Transformer) groups four RedAE frames (160 ms, one "audio step") into a single RedAE-Patch at 6.25 Hz for the LLM.

![Figure 1: Overview of FireRedAudio. Decoupled continuous pathways encode inputs for understanding and generation. The shared LLM produces text or conditions a DiT to generate RedAE latents, which are decoded into waveforms. The inset illustrates the DiT conditioning for one audio step.](assets/2608.24168/figure-1.png)

For text outputs the LLM decodes tokens normally. For speech outputs it predicts an audio-start token, a sequence of audio-step tokens and an audio-end token. At each audio step a DiT trained with flow matching generates the next four RedAE latent frames, conditioned on (a) the LLM hidden states of the current and two preceding steps, repeated four times to align with frames, and (b) eight clean historical RedAE frames from the two preceding steps, concatenated with the four noisy current frames into a 12-frame input; only the last four positions enter the loss. The LLM condition is randomly dropped during training for classifier-free guidance. The generated frames go back through the Patch Encoder as LLM context, and the full latent sequence is rendered to a 24 kHz waveform by the RedAE Decoder. Zero-shot TTS seeds the acoustic history with the last two steps of the reference; Instruct TTS and editing start from zeros, and editing resynthesizes the whole target waveform from the source audio and instruction rather than inpainting source latents.

The joint loss sums next-token cross-entropy and the flow-matching MSE with equal weights; audio-step tokens get a cross-entropy weight of 0.01 inside both numerator and denominator. Training follows five stages measured in multimodal tokens (Table 1): adapter alignment on ASR (180B, encoder and LLM frozen), Audio Encoder adaptation on ASR plus understanding (390B, LLM frozen), unified mid-training that first introduces zero-shot TTS and trains all modules except the frozen RedAE Encoder (990B), multitask post-training adding Instruct TTS, speech editing and chain-of-thought supervision (511B), and long-context extension to 200k-token sequences and one-hour audio (591B). Task and dataset sampling uses two-level power-law smoothing (exponents 0.5 and 0.7). The paper does not name its training corpora.

## Key Results

**Zero-shot TTS (Seed-TTS-Eval, Table 5).** FireRedAudio has the lowest Chinese CER (0.83%) and the lowest average CER/WER (1.20% vs 1.23% for Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B and 1.36% for DiTAR-1B); its English WER (1.56%) is second to Qwen3-Omni (1.39%). Speaker similarity is not best: average SIM is 0.71 against 0.78 for Seed-TTS and 0.75 for DiTAR-1B and CosyVoice 3-1.5B, and its 0.74 (zh) and 0.68 (en) trail several specialized systems. Among unified models that report SIM in both languages, 0.71 is the highest (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B 0.70, Ming-UniAudio 0.64). Baseline numbers appear to be taken from prior reports, with FireRedAudio scored by Paraformer-zh, Whisper-large-v3 and WavLM-large.

**Instruct TTS (InstructTTSEval, Table 6).** FireRedAudio is highest in all six zh and en settings (e.g. zh APS 86.0 vs 84.6 for Ming-Omni-TTS; en RP 70.3 vs 64.2 for Qwen3-TTS-VD, a 6.1-point margin). Several baselines report Chinese only. Scores are instruction-following accuracy judged by Gemini 2.5 Pro, because the benchmark's original preview judge is no longer available; all systems were re-scored with it.

**Speech editing (Ming-Freeform-Audio-Edit, Tables 7 and 8).** Against Ming-UniAudio-Edit, the only compared system, FireRedAudio matches or improves every value except zh SIM under open deletion (0.80 vs 0.81). The largest semantic-editing gains are on open deletion (overall WER 10.49/16.65 vs 22.92/27.60 for zh/en; no-edit WER 7.84/25.43 vs 17.50/35.21). Acoustic edits improve sharply on speed (relative duration error 2.60/4.02 vs 6.36/5.92) and volume (relative amplitude error 2.39/3.74 vs 14.90/11.70), and pitch-edit SIM rises from 0.36/0.24 to 0.52/0.44.

**Understanding and ASR (Tables 3, 4).** FireRedAudio has the highest accuracy on MMAU test-mini (82.0), MMAU test (80.9) and MMSU (83.3); Gemini 3.1 Pro and Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus numbers there come from the authors' own evaluation, and only those two report MMAU test. In ASR it is lowest on LibriSpeech test-clean (0.67), FLEURS English (2.53) and the FLEURS-102 macro-average (14.94 vs 18.23 for Gemini 3.1 Pro and 23.66 for Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus), but not elsewhere: Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus is lower on WenetSpeech Net (4.30 vs 5.18), LibriSpeech test-other (2.23 vs 2.91), KeSpeech and Opencpop, and Kimi-Audio and Step-Audio 2 are lower on AISHELL-1. Per-language FLEURS results (Table 10) show FireRedAudio behind Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus on many high-resource European languages and far ahead on many low-resource African and South Asian languages, which drives the macro-average.

**Long-form organization (Table 9).** On 50 in-house speech recordings of 5 to 50 minutes, FireRedAudio's strict interval pass rate is 73.6% vs 56.6% for Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus; with a 1-second tolerance the two are close overall (96.7% vs 96.5%), and Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus is slightly ahead in the 20-minute group (98.4 vs 98.0). Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus also serves as the audio judge for both systems.

## Novelty Assessment

The new element is structural: routing each input audio segment through one of two separate continuous encoders according to its role, inside a single trainable AR LLM, with no fusion of the two streams. The authors claim no prior unified audio LM reports this. The generation side (AR LLM conditioning a per-step flow-matching DiT over continuous latents) is in the line of the continuous AR modeling that the authors cite from DiTAR and VibeVoice, and RedAE with its distillation-regularized latents is shared with the companion FireRedTTS3 system; the understanding side is a conventional Whisper-encoder-plus-adapter design. The staged curriculum and two-level sampling are training-recipe contributions. The paper contains no ablation comparing decoupled against shared input representations, so the benefit of the central design is argued from the results of the full system and from prior work, not isolated experimentally.

## Field Significance

Moderate — FireRedAudio provides a working example of a moderately sized unified audio LM that keeps strong understanding and multilingual ASR while generating continuous latents for zero-shot TTS, instruction-driven TTS and free-form speech editing, using separate input encoders for the two roles. It is evidence that continuous-latent generation can coexist with competitive understanding in one shared 9B backbone, but the paper does not isolate how much the decoupling itself contributes.

## Claims

- **supports:** A single shared LLM can serve both audio understanding and continuous-latent speech generation when each input audio segment is encoded by a role-specific continuous encoder instead of one shared representation.
  > *Evidence:* With a Whisper-initialized encoder for analysed audio and a RedAE-Patch pathway for generation conditions feeding one Qwen3.5-9B backbone, the model reaches 82.0/80.9 on MMAU test-mini/test and 83.3 on MMSU alongside a 1.20% average CER/WER on Seed-TTS-Eval; no shared-representation ablation is reported. *(§2.1, §4.1, §4.2, Table 3, Table 5)*
- **complicates:** In unified audio models, content accuracy in zero-shot synthesis can match or beat specialized TTS systems while speaker similarity still lags them.
  > *Evidence:* FireRedAudio has the lowest Seed-TTS-Eval average CER/WER (1.20%) but an average SIM of 0.71, below Seed-TTS (0.78), DiTAR-1B (0.75) and CosyVoice 3-1.5B (0.75); the authors note it uses no dedicated speaker embedding. *(§4.2, Table 5)*
- **supports:** Instruction-driven speech editing can be performed by full resynthesis from source audio and an editing instruction, without edit locations, alignments or latent inpainting, while preserving unedited content.
  > *Evidence:* Conditioning on the source audio only through the LLM, FireRedAudio lowers no-edit WER relative to Ming-UniAudio-Edit in all six semantic-editing settings (e.g. open deletion 7.84/25.43 vs 17.50/35.21 zh/en) and cuts the relative amplitude error for volume edits from 14.90/11.70 to 2.39/3.74. *(§2.3, §4.4, Table 7, Table 8)*
- **refines:** Regularizing a reconstruction autoencoder's latents toward a frozen understanding encoder is proposed as a way to make continuous latents easier for an autoregressive LLM to model while staying waveform-reconstructible.
  > *Evidence:* RedAE adds an MSE distillation term toward a frozen teacher audio encoder to its GAN reconstruction objective, motivated by the observation that reconstruction-only latents are dominated by low-level acoustic variation; the paper reports no reconstruction metrics or ablation of this term. *(§2.2.2)*
- **complicates:** Comparisons of instruction-following TTS and long-form temporal grounding increasingly rest on proprietary or competing LLM judges, which limits how far reported margins can be trusted.
  > *Evidence:* InstructTTSEval scores were all re-computed with Gemini 2.5 Pro because the original judge is no longer available, and the long-form grounding comparison uses Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus both as the audio judge and as the only compared system. *(§4.3, §4.5, Table 6, Table 9)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The central claim, that decoupled input representations are what let one LLM balance understanding and generation, is not tested by any ablation: there is no variant with a single shared input representation, no ablation of the RedAE distillation term, and no study of the staged curriculum. All evidence comes from comparisons of the full system against differently sized and differently trained baselines.

- No human listening tests are reported. TTS quality is judged only by ASR-based CER/WER and WavLM speaker similarity, Instruct TTS only by a Gemini judge; naturalness and audio quality are not measured.
- Baseline numbers for zero-shot TTS and most ASR results appear to come from prior publications rather than a common re-run, while FireRedAudio is scored with its own ASR and SIM pipeline.
- The long-form grounding test uses 50 in-house recordings and makes the competitor its own judge; with a 1-second tolerance the two systems differ by 0.2 points overall.
- The training data are described only by task family and token volume; the corpora, languages for TTS training and amount of speech data are not named, which limits reproducibility despite the code release.
- RedAE is reused from the companion team pipeline and frozen; its reconstruction quality is not evaluated in this paper, and the total parameter count and inference latency are not reported.
- Speech generation is evaluated only in Chinese and English, while ASR spans 102 languages.
