> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Kunlin Cai et al.** (University of California, Los Angeles / University of Tennessee, Knoxville) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01723) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✗
>
> A black-box membership inference attack (MIA) on fine-tuned TTS models shows that an auditor who can only submit text plus reference audio and listen to the output can tell whether a speaker, or a specific recording, was in the private fine-tuning set, across CosyVoice2, F5-TTS and XTTS-v2.

## Problem

Zero-shot TTS foundation models are routinely fine-tuned on private voice corpora to clone specific speakers. Such fine-tuning can embed both biometric identity and the linguistic content of the training recordings in the model weights. Membership inference is the standard auditing tool for this kind of leakage, but prior audio MIAs target discriminative models (ASR, speaker verification), and the one TTS-related prior study is white-box and limited to text-only diffusion TTS. Porting black-box MIAs from image or language generators to modern TTS hits two obstacles. First, a TTS query has two coupled conditions (synthesis text and reference speech), so the query space is large and no criterion exists for choosing a query. Second, the output is a continuous, variable-length waveform in which identity, content and prosody are entangled, so low-level features (Mel, MFCC) and point-to-point distances used in earlier MIAs do not apply.

## Method

The threat model is strictly black-box: the adversary submits queries q = (synthesis text, reference speech) to a fine-tuned TTS model and observes only the generated waveform. Two audit granularities are defined. A **speaker-level** MIA asks whether any data of a target speaker was in the fine-tuning set, given n public clips of that speaker. A **record-level** MIA asks whether a specific text-audio pair was in the fine-tuning set, given that exact pair; it additionally assumes the adversary knows the public base checkpoint and can fine-tune a shadow copy on disjoint data.

**Query generation.** The paper maps the dual-conditioning query space along attacker knowledge and attack strategy into five representative queries: recitation (the full target transcript and full target audio as reference), continuation (speech prefix as reference, transcript suffix as text), partial reference (a 50% window), audio perturbation (reference with additive noise at 20 dB SNR), and text perturbation (50% of content words swapped for near-homophones). Two criteria rank them: *scorable extent* (how much of the output can be compared with the target, governed mainly by the text) and *memorization elicitation* (how strongly the query cues target-specific details learned in fine-tuning, governed mainly by the reference). Recitation is the only query that fully satisfies both, so it is the default. Each query is repeated with several random seeds (m = 5 at speaker level, m = 10 at record level).

**Representation engineering.** For speaker-level audits, the n generated clips and the n target clips are each concatenated in time and passed through a WavLM + ECAPA-TDNN speaker-verification encoder; the membership score is the cosine similarity of the two global embeddings, thresholded directly. For record-level audits, unpooled hidden states from all 24 WavLM layers are extracted for the target and generated audio. A modified dynamic time warping (DTW) aligns the generated features to the target time axis independently per layer, producing a frame-by-layer cosine similarity sequence. A lightweight LSTM aggregates this sequence into a score. The LSTM is trained on sequences from a shadow model fine-tuned with the same recipe on a disjoint 50-speaker set, using mean and variance statistics across the repeated queries.

**Victim setup.** CosyVoice2 (CosyVoice2-0.5B, a hybrid of an autoregressive LM over speech tokens and a flow-matching decoder), F5-TTS (F5-TTS-v1-Base, flow matching, trained on Emilia) and XTTS-v2 (autoregressive) are fine-tuned with their official recipes and default hyperparameters on VCTK (N = 5,000 utterances) or the British Dialect corpus (N = 3,000). For each dataset, 100 speakers are split into a victim set and a shadow set of 50 speakers each.

## Key Results

All results are AUC, ACC and TPR@1%FPR; the random baseline is AUC 0.5.

- **Speaker level (Figure 2a, 2b).** AUC is 0.980 (CosyVoice2), 0.925 (F5-TTS) and 0.841 (XTTS-v2) on VCTK, and 1.000, 1.000 and 0.949 on British Dialect. XTTS-v2 is the least vulnerable on both datasets; the authors attribute this to its fine-tuning recipe not adapting the acoustic decoder.
- **Record level (Figure 2c, 2d).** AUC is 0.896 (CosyVoice2), 0.817 (XTTS-v2) and 0.800 (F5-TTS) on VCTK, and 0.888, 0.882 and 0.844 on British Dialect. F5-TTS is the least vulnerable on both datasets. CosyVoice2 record-level AUC is slightly lower on British Dialect than on VCTK (0.888 vs. 0.896); the paper's claim that British Dialect is stronger at record level refers to low-FPR TPR for CosyVoice2 and XTTS-v2.
- **Same-speaker non-members (Figure 3).** When all non-member records come from speakers seen in fine-tuning, record-level MIA keeps low-FPR performance comparable to the full evaluation set, indicating that it detects record-specific memorization rather than speaker membership.
- **Query choice (Figure 4, CosyVoice2 on VCTK).** Recitation is best at both levels: speaker AUC 0.980 vs. 0.971 for text perturbation (TPR@1%FPR 0.853 vs. 0.802); record AUC 0.896 vs. 0.837 for text perturbation (TPR@1%FPR 0.281 vs. 0.154). Queries with an incomplete or degraded reference fall to record-level AUC 0.690 to 0.746.
- **Features (Figure 5).** At speaker level the SV encoder reaches AUC 0.980 against 0.629 (Mel) and 0.595 (MFCC). At record level multi-layer WavLM reaches 0.896, the SV embedding 0.808, MFCC 0.696 and Mel 0.672. Lower WavLM layers are individually more informative, and aggregating more layers raises AUC monotonically from about 0.857 (best single layer) to 0.896 (all 24) (Figure 7).
- **Alignment (Figure 8).** DTW reaches AUC 0.896, against 0.807 for a fixed-offset shift alignment, 0.697 for uniform stretching, and near-random for pad/truncate.
- **Component ablation on CosyVoice2 (Figure 9).** Speaker-level AUC is 0.980 (full fine-tuning), 0.978 (flow decoder only) and 0.728 (LM only). Record-level AUC is 0.896 (full), 0.818 (LM only) and 0.685 (flow only). Speaker leakage tracks the flow-matching decoder; record leakage tracks the autoregressive LM.
- **Attack budget (Figures 10, 11).** Speaker-level AUC rises from about 0.87 with one attacker clip to about 0.96 with three, and converges near 1.0 at 7 to 9 clips. Record-level AUC rises from 0.863 with one seed to 0.896 with ten or more.
- **Vulnerable data (Tables III to VI, Figure 12).** Record-level AUC falls from 0.943 to 0.748 as silence ratio rises from below 20% to above 60%, and rises with high-frequency energy (0.879 to 0.937) and duration (0.857 to 0.916). Male speakers show a larger speaker-level member/non-member gap than female speakers (0.041 vs. 0.034; TPR@1%FPR 0.881 vs. 0.833).
- **Defenses (Tables IX to XI).** Early stopping lowers record-level AUC to 0.748 but leaves speaker-level AUC at 0.900. Reference noise at 25 dB SNR leaves speaker AUC at 0.923 and record AUC at 0.808; truncating the reference to 20% lowers them to 0.695 and 0.657. DP-SGD at ε = 4 or 10 reduces both attacks to AUC 0.518 to 0.543.

The comparisons are internal ablations; there is no prior black-box TTS MIA to compare against, so the Mel and MFCC feature baselines and the alternative queries serve as the reference points.

## Novelty Assessment

The paper is framed as the first black-box MIA for modern dual-conditioned TTS. The individual components are established: speaker-verification embeddings, WavLM features, DTW alignment, shadow-model training of an attack classifier, and reconstruction-based scoring borrowed from black-box MIAs on diffusion models. The new parts are the systematic treatment of the dual-conditioning query space with explicit selection criteria, the per-layer DTW alignment that turns variable-length speech into a fixed-length similarity sequence, and the separation of speaker-level from record-level leakage, including the CosyVoice2 component ablation that ties each to a different subsystem. The main contribution is empirical: a measured privacy risk of speaker fine-tuning across three architecture families and two corpora, with ablations that justify each design choice. It is not a speech generation method.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper demonstrates that fine-tuning a zero-shot TTS model on a private voice corpus leaves membership traces recoverable through the normal synthesis interface, at both speaker and individual-recording granularity, and that simple inference-time defenses do not remove them. It provides an auditing procedure and a component-level account (decoder for identity, autoregressive LM for record detail) that can inform privacy-preserving fine-tuning of speech generators.

## Claims

- **supports:** Fine-tuning a zero-shot TTS model on a target speaker's data leaves identity traces that an output-only observer can detect from synthesized speech.
  > *Evidence:* Speaker-level MIA using concatenated speaker-verification embeddings reaches AUC 0.841 to 1.000 across CosyVoice2, F5-TTS and XTTS-v2 fine-tuned on VCTK or British Dialect, and 0.900 even under early stopping. *(§IV-B1, Figure 2; §Appendix G1, Table IX)*
- **supports:** TTS models fine-tuned on a corpus memorize recording-specific acoustic realizations beyond speaker identity, and this memorization is expressed when the model is prompted with the original text and audio.
  > *Evidence:* Record-level MIA reaches AUC 0.800 to 0.896 and keeps comparable low-FPR performance when non-members are drawn from fine-tuning speakers; recitation of the full record beats queries with partial or degraded references (AUC 0.896 vs. 0.690 to 0.746). *(§IV-B2, Figure 3; §IV-C1, Figure 4)*
- **refines:** In hybrid TTS that pairs an autoregressive token LM with a flow-matching acoustic decoder, speaker identity is absorbed mainly by the acoustic decoder while utterance-specific prosodic and temporal detail is absorbed mainly by the autoregressive LM.
  > *Evidence:* On CosyVoice2, flow-only fine-tuning keeps speaker-level AUC at 0.978 (vs. 0.728 for LM-only), while LM-only fine-tuning keeps record-level AUC at 0.818 (vs. 0.685 for flow-only). *(§IV-C6, Figure 9)*
- **supports:** Comparing generated and reference speech at the utterance-detail level requires multi-layer self-supervised features with non-linear temporal alignment; spectral features and global alignment lose most of the signal.
  > *Evidence:* Multi-layer WavLM features score AUC 0.896 against 0.672 to 0.808 for Mel, MFCC and SV embeddings, and DTW alignment scores 0.896 against 0.807 for shift alignment and 0.697 for uniform stretching. *(§IV-C2, Figure 5; §IV-C5, Figure 8)*
- **complicates:** Inference-time perturbations and early stopping do not reliably protect fine-tuned TTS from membership leakage; only formal differential privacy removed it in the tested setting, at an unquantified quality cost.
  > *Evidence:* With 25 dB reference noise, speaker and record AUC remain 0.923 and 0.808; DP-SGD at ε = 4 or 10 brings both to 0.518 to 0.543, while the generation-quality trade-off is asserted but not measured. *(§V; §Appendix G2, Table X; §Appendix G3, Table XI)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

- The record-level attack assumes the adversary knows the base checkpoint and can fine-tune a shadow model with the same recipe on disjoint data from the same corpus; the effect of a mismatched shadow recipe or data distribution is not tested.
- Experiments cover two English read-speech corpora (VCTK, British Dialect) and fine-tuning with default official recipes. Most ablations and all vulnerability analyses use CosyVoice2 on VCTK only.
- The DP-SGD section states that formal privacy costs generation quality, but the appendix reports only attack metrics, so the utility side of the trade-off is not quantified.
- The attack targets fine-tuned models; membership of data in the original pre-training of the base models is out of scope.
- No code or audio release is mentioned, which limits reproducibility of the shadow-model and LSTM components.
