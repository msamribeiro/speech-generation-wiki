> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Ziyang Ma et al.** (Tencent Hunyuan / Shanghai Jiao Tong University / Shanghai Innovation Institute) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08936) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> AuK is an open-weight, single flow-matching model that maps a natural-language instruction plus optional audio to a waveform, covering zero-shot and instruction TTS, content, paralinguistic and acoustic editing, and enhancement and separation, with a 4-step distilled variant (AuK-Flash).

## Problem

Zero-shot voice cloning, instruction-controlled synthesis, speech editing and speech restoration are usually served by separate task-specific models. Unifying them is hard for three reasons the report names. The output constraints differ: generation creates new speech, content editing must change only a region, paralinguistic and acoustic editing must keep the words, and enhancement or separation must keep only the components the instruction specifies. The conditioning differs: some tasks use text alone, others need joint reasoning over an instruction and source audio. Supervision differs: generation has scalable automatic rewards (ASR accuracy, speaker similarity), while open-ended editing depends on subjective judgments with no existing preference data or reward model.

## Method

**Task formulation and data.** Every task is cast as (natural-language instruction, optional input audio) → target waveform. The pre-training corpus has about 3.03 billion instruction-audio instances and 1.95 million hours of effective supervision across five families (§2). Most editing pairs are synthesized with existing systems:

- **Zero-shot TTS** is transcript-free and cross-utterance. For a speaker with n utterances, every ordered pair of distinct utterances becomes a (prompt audio, target text) → target audio instance; the prompt transcript is never given (§2.1).
- **Instruct TTS** pairs Qwen3-Omni-generated captions and structured attributes with target text and audio, with no reference audio.
- **Acoustic editing** uses deterministic signal processing: five rate multipliers, six loudness offsets, six pitch shifts.
- **Paralinguistic editing** covers emotion (Qwen3-TTS plus IndexTTS2 resynthesis), timbre (the X-VC corpus built with Seed-VC), de-accenting of 13 Chinese dialect categories (CosyVoice 2 plus OmniVoice), 39 nonverbal event types (removal by F5-TTS infilling) and whisper conversion.
- **Content editing** uses F5-TTS masked infilling for speech and YingMusic-Singer-Plus for lyric edits, filtered by WER.
- **Enhancement and separation** includes instruction-selective targets: a denoising request may keep reverberation. It also covers conversational multi-speaker mixtures and music stem separation.

**Architecture** (§3). Three components:

1. A frozen Qwen2.5-Omni encodes the instruction, jointly with the input audio when present. A learnable weighted sum of layer-normalized hidden states from all LLM layers forms the semantic condition.
2. A frozen AuK-VAE encodes the input audio to 64-dimensional continuous latents at 50 Hz (24 kHz audio). The encoder is non-causal; the decoder is a causal BigVGAN-style generator with 3-frame look-ahead. A normalizing flow regularizes the latent during VAE training only. The VAE is trained for 1.24M updates on about 3M hours of speech, music and general audio (6:3:1), with multi-scale mel, multi-period and multi-scale CQT discriminator (least-squares GAN), feature-matching and KL losses (§4.1).
3. A FLUX-style backbone of about 1.5B parameters: 10 dual-stream MMDiT blocks, where semantic and acoustic streams share joint attention but keep separate residual paths, then 20 single-stream DiT blocks over the concatenated sequence. It predicts rectified-flow velocity for the target latent. Reference latents and noisy target latents are concatenated in the acoustic stream; text-only tasks use the target latent alone.

**Training** (§4.2 to §5). Only the backbone and layer-fusion weights are trained.

- **Pre-training** is a 50k-update generation-only warm-up, then 600k updates of joint generation and editing with fixed family sampling probabilities (generation 28.1%, enhancement/separation 23.2%, content 23.0%, paralinguistic 21.8%, acoustic 4.0%). Hierarchical condition dropout supports classifier-free guidance (CFG). Training runs on 256 GPUs.
- **Editing preference optimization** collects human three-level ratings (fail / partial / success) on 10 or 20 sampled candidates per instruction (818 groups, 9,080 rated candidates). It optimizes a Diffusion-DPO-style implicit score with a LiPO listwise ordinal loss for 104 updates.
- **Generation RL** applies Flow-GRPO with a MixGRPO-style six-step SDE window. Zero-shot TTS uses a content reward that is homophone-tolerant for the pass/fail decision and strict for ranking, plus a speaker-similarity reward standardized only among content-correct candidates to block reward hacking. Instruct TTS uses majority votes from a Qwen2.5-Omni-7B style-consistency judge trained on 30k labelled samples.
- **Acceleration** produces AuK-Flash: consistency-distillation initialization, then Decoupled DMD with adaptive projected guidance (APG) replacing CFG in the CFG-augmentation branch. Separation examples are routed to a supervised clean-latent regression loss and excluded from DMD ("task-routed"), because uniform DMD made separation outputs regress toward the unprocessed mixture.

**Inference** (§6). An LLM-based Prompt Enhancer routes free-form requests to a task, normalizes parameters and estimates output duration (an F5-TTS-style byte-length heuristic). AuK samples 32 NFE with CFG 2.0. AuK-Flash samples 4 NFE without CFG, a reported 4.5× wall-clock speedup under matched hardware, duration and batch size (Table 2). The generation benchmarks bypass the Prompt Enhancer; MMAE-Speech uses it.

## Key Results

Generation results are means over three runs (§7.2). The report does not state whether baseline numbers were rerun or copied from prior publications.

**Zero-shot TTS (Seed-TTS-Eval, Table 5).**
- AuK has the lowest average error, 2.65%, against Qwen3-TTS at 3.07% and AuK-Flash at 2.85%. It also has the highest average SIM, 0.795, against Seed-TTS at 0.778. Only six baselines report all three subsets; MegaTTS 3, DiTAR and VibeVoice lack test-zh-hard and have no average.
- Per subset, AuK is best on test-en WER (1.02) and test-en SIM (0.788). It is also best on test-zh-hard CER (5.91).
- AuK is not best on test-zh CER: VoxCPM2 has 0.97, against 1.02 for AuK and DiTAR.
- AuK and AuK-Flash tie for the best test-zh SIM (0.814). AuK-Flash has the best test-zh-hard SIM (0.784).

**Instruction TTS (InstructTTSEval, Table 6).** AuK is best on only one of six columns: Chinese DSD, 83.37 against 81.10 for Qwen3-TTS-VD. On English DSD, AuK-Flash ties Qwen3-TTS-VD at 82.40. AuK trails elsewhere:
- Chinese APS: 83.28, against 85.20 for Qwen3-TTS-VD.
- Chinese RP: 68.23, against 74.00 for MOSS-VoiceGenerator.
- English APS: 75.57, against 83.00 for Hume AI and 82.90 for Qwen3-TTS-VD.
- English RP: 65.93, against 68.70 for MOSS-VoiceGenerator.

**General speech editing.**
- **MMAE-Speech (Table 7):** AuK has the highest IFR (48.23) and CR (88.11); Step-Audio-EditX has 43.52 and 77.27. AuK-Flash has the highest EMR (13.85, against 12.44 for AuK and 7.04 for Ming-UniAudio). The ∗-marked baselines were scored only on inputs of 10 seconds or less.
- **SpeechEditBench (Table 3):** AuK is best on content (91.83 vs. 76.46 for Ming-UniAudio), emotion (9.94), prosody (71.33 vs. 26.50) and acoustic (37.07). AuK-Flash is best on paralinguistic (39.25; AuK 38.50, Step-Audio-EditX 31.25). Emotion-edit success stays below 10% for every system.
- **Ming-Freeform-Audio-Edit semantic editing (Tables 3, 8, 9):** AuK has the lowest WER and no-edit WER in all four Basic/Full × ZH/EN settings. On Full-EN, WER drops from 14.28 (Ming-UniAudio) to 3.96. Editing accuracy is not uniformly best: AuK-Flash has 92.01 on Basic-ZH against 91.95 for AuK.
- **Ming-Freeform-Audio-Edit acoustic editing (Table 10):** AuK has the lowest average WER (2.02 ZH, 3.48 EN) and AuK-Flash the highest SIM. Ming-UniAudio is more accurate at reaching the requested magnitude. Its speed-change relative duration error (RDE) is 6.36 against 10.57 for AuK (ZH) and 5.92 against 11.96 (EN). Its volume relative amplitude error (RAE) is 14.90 against 32.89 (ZH) and 11.70 against 25.40 (EN).

**Enhancement, separation and super-resolution (Tables 11 to 14).**
- **DNS Challenge 2020:** AuK has the lowest dWER, 2.66 (RE-USE 3.31). Both variants have the highest SIM, 0.99. AuK-Flash has the highest UTMOS, 4.05. AnyEnhance has the best DNSMOS OVRL, 3.41, against 3.35 for AuK and 3.38 for AuK-Flash.
- **CHiME-4:** AuK-Flash has the lowest WER (7.84) and the highest UTMOS (3.91). Its OVRL of 3.35 ties Resemble-Enhance.
- **Libri2Mix:** AuK has the lowest WER (9.12, against 9.34 for MossFormer2-SS) and PER (6.63). Both variants tie MossFormer2-SS on SIM at 0.96.
- **VCTK-SR:** AuK-Flash is best on six of seven metrics (OVRL 3.25, UTMOS 4.05, WER 2.92, PER 4.19, plus SIG and BAK). AuK has the best SIM, 0.97.

**VAE reconstruction (Table 4).** AuK-VAE is best on PESQ, STOI, mel distance and STFT distance in all three domains. On speech, PESQ is 4.143 against 3.633 for MiniMax-H3-AudioVAE. The report does not give the frame rate or latent size of the compared VAEs, so the comparison is not shown to be rate-matched.

A consistent pattern across tasks: the full model gives lower recognition error, while the distilled AuK-Flash often gives higher predicted perceptual quality (UTMOS) or speaker similarity.

## Novelty Assessment

Most of the system integrates known components. These include the FLUX-style MMDiT/DiT backbone from image generation, F5-TTS positional design, and Qwen2.5-Omni conditioning. Post-training adds Diffusion-DPO with LiPO, Flow-GRPO with MixGRPO, consistency distillation, Decoupled DMD and APG. Each is cited to prior work.

The genuinely new pieces are smaller:
- task-routed Decoupled DMD, which excludes separation samples from distribution matching;
- an ordinal listwise flow-DPO objective on human three-level editing ratings;
- a constrained zero-shot reward that standardizes speaker similarity only among content-correct candidates;
- learnable all-layer fusion of MLLM states for audio conditioning;
- a transcript-free, cross-utterance zero-shot training recipe.

None of these has an isolating ablation in the report. The cross-utterance gain and the reported advantage of consistency initialization over ODE regression and MeanFlow are stated without numbers.

The main contribution is the scale and breadth of the unified data recipe, with an open release that covers both generation and restoration in one checkpoint.

## Field Significance

> [!tip]
> High — This report shows that one open-weight latent flow-matching model can match or exceed specialized systems on zero-shot TTS intelligibility and speaker similarity while also performing instruction-guided editing, enhancement and separation. It supports the view that speech generation and editing can share one instruction-conditioned interface. It also provides a full post-training and distillation recipe for flow-based speech models.

## Claims

- **supports:** A single latent flow-matching model trained jointly on generation and many editing and restoration tasks can match or exceed dedicated zero-shot TTS systems on intelligibility and speaker similarity.
  > *Evidence:* AuK reaches the lowest Seed-TTS-Eval average error (2.65%) and highest average SIM (0.795) among systems reporting all three subsets, ahead of Qwen3-TTS (3.07%) and Seed-TTS (0.778 SIM), while also reaching the best DNS Challenge dWER and Libri2Mix WER. *(§7.2, Table 5; §7.3.2, Tables 11 and 13)*
- **complicates:** Few-step distribution-matching distillation of flow models does not degrade all tasks equally: perceptual-quality predictors often improve while linguistic accuracy declines, and distillation can break tasks whose outputs must remove input content.
  > *Evidence:* The 4-step, CFG-free AuK-Flash has higher UTMOS than the 32-NFE AuK on DNS Challenge (4.05 vs. 3.86) and Libri2Mix (4.03 vs. 3.87) but higher WER on Libri2Mix (10.07 vs. 9.12) and Seed-TTS-Eval (2.85% vs. 2.65% average). Uniform Decoupled DMD made separation outputs regress toward the unprocessed mixture, which required routing separation samples to a supervised loss. *(§5, Tables 5, 11, 13)*
- **complicates:** Instruction-guided speech editors reliably execute content edits but remain weak at emotion edits and at reaching a requested magnitude for acoustic attribute changes.
  > *Evidence:* On SpeechEditBench, AuK reaches 91.83 content-edit success but at most 9.94 on emotion edits, with every compared system below 10%. On Ming-Freeform-Audio-Edit, AuK's speed-change duration error (10.57 ZH / 11.96 EN) and volume amplitude error (32.89 / 25.40) are worse than Ming-UniAudio's (6.36 / 5.92 and 14.90 / 11.70) despite lower WER. *(§7.3.1, Tables 3 and 10)*
- **refines:** Zero-shot voice cloning can be trained without prompt transcripts by pairing distinct utterances of the same speaker as prompt and target, rather than splitting a single recording.
  > *Evidence:* AuK enumerates n×(n−1) cross-utterance prompt/target instances per speaker with no prompt transcript; the authors report that this improves expressiveness and speaker similarity and enables transcript-free cloning, but give no quantitative ablation. *(§2.1, §7.4)*
- **complicates:** Large instruction-conditioned speech models can possess an editing capability yet fail to invoke it reliably from unconstrained user requests, so a separate prompt-rewriting stage remains necessary.
  > *Evidence:* An agent-based pipeline for free-form editing SFT pairs increased instruction diversity but did not generalize to arbitrary requests; reaching the model's ceiling still requires the Prompt Enhancer to route tasks and normalize parameters. *(§6.1, §7.4)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

There is no human listening test of output quality. MOS, CMOS and SMOS are not reported, and perceptual quality relies on UTMOS and DNSMOS predictors. Human ratings enter only as preference-training data for editing.

- **No component ablations.** The report has no ablations of the MLLM layer fusion, the dual-/single-stream split, the warm-up curriculum, the editing DPO stage or the generation RL stage. The contribution of each design choice is unknown.
- **Qualitative-only findings.** Cross-utterance training and the emergent transfers (whisper synthesis from text, English de-accenting learned from Chinese dialect data) are described qualitatively.
- **Bilingual scope and narrow benchmarks.** Generation is Chinese and English only. Timbre conversion, de-accenting, nonverbal editing and lyric editing are trained but not evaluated on a dedicated benchmark.
- **Dependence on synthetic targets.** Many editing targets are produced by other synthesis systems (IndexTTS2, Qwen3-TTS, F5-TTS, OmniVoice, CosyVoice 2, Seed-VC, YingMusic-Singer-Plus). Editing quality is therefore bounded by those generators.
- **Prompt Enhancer dependence.** Free-form use relies on an external LLM prompt rewriter, and the MMAE-Speech results are reported with it enabled.
- **Speedup measurement.** The 4.5× speedup is a relative wall-clock figure. No absolute latency or real-time factor is reported.
