> DunDun scores lexical tone in synthesized Yorùbá speech without a reference recording or a tone-labelled corpus: it reads the gold High/Mid/Low sequence from the input text's diacritics and the predicted sequence from the audio's pitch track. Applied to a fine-tuning sweep, it shows that intelligibility (CER) and tone accuracy change independently.

## Problem

In Yorùbá, pitch alone separates words such as ọkọ (husband, Mid), ọkọ̀ (vehicle, Low) and ọkọ́ (hoe, High). CER, the default automated intelligibility measure for TTS, compares an ASR transcript against the input text. In practice that transcript carries no tone marks, so a system that flattens every tone loses nothing measurable. Existing tone-error-rate protocols for synthesized speech either go through ASR transcripts, which measure lexical recoverability rather than acoustic realization, or rely on an expert phonetician for gold labels. Reference-free prosody metrics treat prosody as gradient and paralinguistic and emit no categorical, word-identity-bearing label per tone-bearing unit (TBU). Automatic tone scoring exists in computer-assisted pronunciation training (CAPT), but it is fitted on tone-labelled data that does not exist for Yorùbá.

## Method

DunDun maps a waveform and its intended tone-marked text to a balanced tone accuracy plus a coverage figure. The authors state openly that the recipe (forced alignment, per-TBU F0, register normalization, thresholding) comes from CAPT; their contribution is carrying it to TTS evaluation without tone labels.

- **Gold tones from orthography.** Text is NFD-normalized. Acute, grave and macron marks give High, Low and Mid; an unmarked vowel gives Mid. Each orthographic vowel or tone-marked character is one TBU.
- **Locating TBUs.** Character-level CTC forced alignment against MMS (`mms-1b-all`, Yorùbá adapter) supplies one time window per TBU, with only the three tone marks stripped. The acoustic model supplies timing and never a label. Alignment failure falls back to equal-width windows over the voiced span, and the paper reports the rate of that fallback.
- **Pitch.** F0 comes from SwiftF0 with a pyworld fallback (65 to 400 Hz). A TBU's F0 is the median of voiced frames over the late half of its window, converted to semitones.
- **Declination and register.** A Theil-Sen slope fitted to all valued TBUs (clamped to [-2, 0] st/s) removes downdrift, using no tone labels. Residuals are re-centred on the per-utterance median, which assumes Mid is the modal tone in the clip. A residual at or above θ_h is High, at or below -θ_l is Low, otherwise Mid.
- **Calibration.** The two thresholds were grid-searched once on 126 OpenSLR-86 clips (θ_h = 1.0, θ_l = 1.25 semitones) and frozen. On a sentence-disjoint 254-clip test split they read 0.580 at coverage 0.917, against 0.334 for a label-permutation control.
- **Aggregation.** DunDun is the unweighted mean of per-class recalls. Pooled chance is exactly 1/3 for any label-independent predictor. Unvoiced or unaligned TBUs abstain rather than default to Mid, and coverage is reported alongside accuracy. On 300 native recordings from 37 speakers the score is 0.596 (95% CI [0.578, 0.614]), so the authors read scores against this native anchor, not against 1.0.

For the case study, the authors fine-tune the OmniVoice base model on clean, tone-marked Yorùbá from OpenSLR-86 and BibleTTS at budgets of 0, 1, 5, 15 and 28.5 hours (ten fixed epochs, 16,384 batch tokens, learning rate 1e-5; the 28.5 h run instead uses 5,000 steps). Each checkpoint is scored on a fixed 27-utterance held-out probe for CER (MMS recognizer), DunDun, and speaker similarity (ECAPA-TDNN cosine to the prompt speaker's reference). A Swahili control repeats the recipe on the OpenBible Swahili portion at 0, 1, 2.5 and 5 hours.

## Key Results

**Validation.** Three controls test whether DunDun tracks tone rather than a correlated property.

- *PSOLA pitch flattening:* compressing F0 excursions from factor 1.0 to 0.0 moves DunDun 0.543, 0.549, 0.518, 0.372, 0.333 (r = +0.92 with the factor), landing at the three-way chance level. CER over the same conditions reads 0.084, 0.080, 0.073, 0.081, 0.110 and does not follow the flattening. The two least-flattened settings differ by 0.006 and are within run-to-run noise.
- *Answer-key inversion (Table 1):* with predictions held byte-identical on 300 native recordings, scrambling the key gives 0.34, and the two-class High/Low swap gives 0.14 against a two-class chance level of 0.35. The authors show that this value follows algebraically from the correct-key result and the metric's Mid rate (2 × 0.349 - 0.558 = 0.140), so it is a consistency check on the scoring path, not independent evidence that DunDun reads pitch.
- *Human A/B listening:* three native listeners chose the correctly toned PSOLA twin over a tone-flipped twin in 60 of 67 scoreable trials (89.6%, Wilson 95% CI [80.0, 94.8], p < 1e-4). The shipped metric ranked the correct twin higher in 15 pairs, lower in 4 and tied 5 (sign test p = 0.019). With an oracle register anchor frozen from the clean twin, it separated 19 correctly and 3 wrongly, with 2 ties (p = 0.0009). Per-trial agreement between metric and listeners is not established: AUROC of the metric's per-pair margin against human correctness is 0.612 (95% CI [0.343, 0.879]), and the point-biserial correlation is 0.152 (CI [-0.076, 0.396]).

**Yorùbá budget sweep (Table 2).** Training-seed means show CER falling from 5.62% zero-shot to 3.6% at 1 h and 1.8% at 5 h, then 2.1% at 15 h and 2.20% at 28.5 h. DunDun reads 0.5674 zero-shot, 0.560 (1 h), 0.628 (5 h), 0.676 (15 h) and 0.633 (28.5 h). The zero-shot score is already 0.89 of the way from chance (1/3) to the native anchor (0.596), and the five-decode-seed series (0.567, 0.594, 0.631, 0.661 over 0, 1, 5, 15 h, with CER 5.6%, 3.4%, 2.7%, 1.7%) sits at or above the anchor from 5 h onward. The authors interpret this as saturation of the metric, not super-native tone. Speaker similarity stays within 0.81 to 0.84 throughout. The 5 h and 15 h training-seed intervals overlap, and the authors do not claim 15 h beats 5 h. The paper contrasts the zero-shot tone score with the 21.4% Yorùbá CER that the base model's own paper reports, though that figure comes from a different test set than the 27-utterance probe.

The paper keeps decode-seed and training-seed error bars in separate, labelled columns, and points out that neither includes item variance. The native anchor's clip-level standard deviation (0.168) implies a 95% item-sampling half-width of about ±0.063 on any 27-clip estimate, larger than either printed bar. Adjacent budgets therefore cannot be ordered by this design.

**Swahili control.** CER falls from 5.54% zero-shot to 3.56% (1 h), 4.22% (2.5 h) and 2.94% (5 h) from single-seed runs, with speaker similarity flat (0.886, 0.888, 0.875, 0.879). DunDun is inapplicable because Swahili orthography marks no tone, so no DunDun score is reported.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is evaluation methodology, and the paper is unusually explicit that the pipeline itself is not new. The CAPT literature already scores lexical tone automatically by forced alignment, per-unit F0, register normalization and thresholding. What changes is the use case: DunDun scores a synthesizer rather than a learner, needs no tone-labelled corpus (two thresholds fitted once on 126 clips and frozen), abstains and reports coverage, and is calibrated for a three-level register system rather than Mandarin contours. Compared with existing tone-error protocols for synthesized speech, it removes both the ASR transcript and the expert labeller from the loop.

The validation design is the strongest part. Pitch flattening and answer-key inversion are constructed so that a tone-blind score cannot pass them by accident. The paper also states precisely what each control does and does not establish, for example that the inversion result is algebraically derivable. The weak point is the human correspondence: the listening study confirms the manipulations are audible, but it cannot show that the metric tracks human tone judgments trial by trial. The fine-tuning sweep applies an existing model with a fixed recipe and is a demonstration case rather than a methodological contribution.

## Field Significance

Moderate. The paper provides an automated, label-free tone axis for TTS in tone-marking orthographies and demonstrates on one Yorùbá sweep that CER and tone accuracy can dissociate. This makes it a concrete cautionary data point for intelligibility-only evaluation of tonal languages. Its reach is bounded by a usable range that ends at a native anchor of 0.596, validation on one language and one base model, and human agreement that is still unresolved.

## Claims

- **supports:** ASR-based character error rate is structurally blind to lexical tone errors when the recognizer's output omits tone marks.
  > *Evidence:* PSOLA flattening of F0 from factor 1.0 to 0.0 drives DunDun from 0.543 to the 0.333 chance level while CER on the same utterances stays between 0.073 and 0.110, with no downward trend. *(§Validating DunDun)*
- **supports:** Intelligibility and lexical-tone accuracy can change independently during low-resource fine-tuning of a multilingual TTS model, so improvement on one does not imply improvement on the other.
  > *Evidence:* Fine-tuning OmniVoice on 1 to 15 h of clean Yorùbá cuts CER from 5.62% to about 2%, while the zero-shot tone score already covers 0.89 of the chance-to-native-anchor span and later scores sit at or above the anchor, within overlapping training-seed intervals. *(§Case Study, Table 2, Figure 2)*
- **refines:** Reference-free lexical-tone scoring can be built from orthographic tone marks plus F0 without a tone-labelled corpus, but its usable range on correctly toned native speech falls well short of perfect accuracy, so scores must be read against a measured native anchor.
  > *Evidence:* With thresholds fitted on 126 clips and frozen, 300 native recordings from 37 speakers score 0.596 (95% CI [0.578, 0.614]) against chance 1/3, with per-class recall 0.56/0.67/0.56 for H/M/L at coverage 0.92. *(§The DunDun Metric, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** A pitch-based tone metric can detect audible tone manipulations without tracking which individual trials listeners find hard.
  > *Evidence:* Listeners pick the correctly toned twin in 89.6% of 67 trials and the metric ranks it higher in 15 of 19 untied pairs, but AUROC of the metric margin against listener correctness is 0.612 with a 95% CI of [0.343, 0.879] that contains chance. *(§Human A/B listening)*
- **complicates:** Small fixed probe sets in TTS fine-tuning sweeps carry item-sampling variance larger than the decode-seed or training-seed error bars usually reported.
  > *Evidence:* The native anchor's clip-level standard deviation of 0.168 implies a 95% half-width of about ±0.063 on a 27-clip DunDun estimate, several times the printed decode-seed (±0.0197) and training-seed (±0.014) bars. *(§Case Study, "What the sweep does not show")*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> DunDun is validated on Yorùbá alone. Its usable range runs only from 1/3 to a native anchor of 0.596, and it stops ranking systems at or above that anchor, which is where most fine-tuned checkpoints land. Agreement with human tone judgments at the trial level is unresolved.

- **Orthographic scope.** Gold labels come only from input diacritics, so the metric requires reliably tone-marked text. It does not transfer as written to Hausa (no tone marking) or Igbo (partial marking, downstep with no cell in a three-way key).
- **Flattering bias from under-marking.** Because Yorùbá leaves Mid unmarked, an omitted diacritic turns gold High or Low into gold Mid. This biases the key toward rewarding flat-toned systems, so scores over scraped, under-diacritized text would flatter the worst systems most.
- **Untested assumptions.** Forced alignment is validated on native read speech and only assumed to transfer to synthesized audio, and a misplaced window scores silently against the wrong gold label. The per-utterance median register anchor assumes Mid is the modal tone, and the frozen-anchor oracle shows this estimate costs part of the metric's sensitivity. Abstention is class-dependent (utterance-final Low is often creaky), so per-class recall is optimistic without per-class coverage.
- **Small human study.** Three listeners took part, two of them at ceiling, and only one reached the kit's 24-trial threshold. More raters of the same calibre would add trials without adding discrimination. The authors propose stimuli graded in difficulty.
- **Narrow sweep.** One base model, one 27-utterance probe, a single off-recipe 28.5 h run, and a Swahili control with one seed per budget and no error bars.
- **Code.** The authors state they release the metric, frozen calibration and validation protocol, but the paper gives no repository link.
