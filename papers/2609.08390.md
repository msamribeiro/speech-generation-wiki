> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Bella Godiva et al.** (KAIST) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08390) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✓
>
> AV-STE is a plug-in streaming front-end that uses the speaker's lip video to restore the noise-corrupted semantic speech tokens a full-duplex dialogue model listens with, so a frozen Moshi gives more on-topic replies in noise and overlapping speech without any retraining of the speech LLM.

## Problem

Full-duplex spoken dialogue models listen and speak at the same time, but they perceive the user through audio alone. Background noise and competing talkers corrupt the user's speech tokens, and the model then answers something unrelated. Lip movements carry complementary phonetic information, but the two obvious ways to use them are a poor fit. Audio-visual speech recognition outputs text, which would add a speech-to-text and text-to-token conversion stage in front of a model that natively consumes speech tokens. Grounding the dialogue model itself in audio-visual input, as AV-Dialog does, needs large-scale training on paired audio-visual conversation and risks eroding the pretrained model's conversational ability. The paper asks whether visual cues can make a full-duplex dialogue model robust while the speech LLM stays untouched.

## Method

AV-STE sits between the user's audio-visual input and a frozen full-duplex speech LLM. The paper uses Moshi and its Mimi tokenizer. Mimi's first codebook is semantically distilled and the rest carry acoustic detail, so AV-STE predicts clean versions of only the first (semantic) token stream from the noisy audio and the speaker's mouth-region video. The noisy acoustic codebooks pass through unchanged, and Moshi receives the enhanced semantic tokens plus the original acoustic tokens. The choice follows from what lips can supply: phonetic content, but little about timbre, prosody or background.

The **streaming audio-visual encoder** is initialised from AV-HuBERT Large, a self-supervised audio-visual speech model (§3.2). The original model is bidirectional, so the authors make it streamable in two ways. Temporal convolutions in the video front end become causal (left padding only), and every Transformer layer gets a bounded-lookahead attention mask that lets frame *i* see up to *L* future frames. The main configuration uses *L* = 4, which is 160 ms at 25 Hz. A strided causal convolution after the encoder downsamples the features to Mimi's token rate.

The **noise-adaptive fusion** module combines the audio-visual features with the tokenizer's own prediction (§3.3). It has two parts:

- **Soft-token cross-attention.** Instead of embedding Mimi's arg-max token, the module embeds the full softmax distribution over Mimi's logits (temperature τ = 1.0) and projects it. The audio-visual features act as queries and these soft token embeddings as keys and values. This keeps the tokenizer's uncertainty available under noise, and the same bounded-lookahead mask applies.
- **Noise-adaptive modulation.** A per-frame gate λ_t ∈ [0, 1] mixes the soft token embedding with the cross-attended audio-visual representation. Its prior is one minus the normalised entropy of Mimi's token distribution: a sharp distribution means the audio tokens are trusted. A zero-initialised MLP on the concatenated token and audio-visual features learns a residual correction, and the gate passes through a sigmoid with a learnable scale.

A linear layer maps the fused representation to Mimi's 2,048-entry semantic vocabulary. The model is trained end to end with frame-wise cross-entropy against Mimi tokens extracted from the clean speech. The full AV-STE model has 335M parameters (§4.4). AV-STE itself is a non-autoregressive streaming Transformer encoder with a classification head. The `autoregressive-LM` architecture tag refers to the frozen Moshi dialogue model it feeds.

**Training data** (§4.2). Training uses LRS3 (about 433 hours of audio-visual speech). Each utterance is left clean (20%), mixed with AudioSet non-speech noise (40%), or mixed with AudioSet conversational speech (40%), at SNRs drawn uniformly from -10 to 10 dB. A second stage replaces the AudioSet speech with 1-4 interfering LRS3 speakers mixed on the fly, which removes dataset-specific shortcuts. This second-stage checkpoint is used for the same-dataset interference and out-of-domain evaluations. Training runs for up to 6 epochs, about 73 GPU hours on a single A6000 (§4.3).

## Key Results

Evaluation works at two levels. **Token level**: semantic-token accuracy against clean Mimi tokens, plus WER of speech reconstructed from the enhanced tokens. **Dialogue level**: following Full-Duplex-Bench, single-turn tests on 125 utterances each from LRS3 and Seamless Interaction. These score take-over rate (TOR), response latency, and a GPT-4o relevance score from 1 to 5 for the response. The coherence score is an automatic LLM judgement and no human listening test is reported. Seamless Interaction is never used for training.

**Semantic-token recovery** (Table 1). Average accuracy is taken over the five noisy SNRs.

| Setting | Mimi | Mimi + AV-STE |
|---|---|---|
| LRS3, AudioSet non-speech noise | 27.63% | 68.02% |
| LRS3, AudioSet speaker interference | 26.97% | 68.09% |
| LRS3, same-dataset (LRS3) speaker interference | 12.61% | 65.60% |
| Seamless, AudioSet non-speech noise | 16.27% | 36.76% |
| Seamless, Seamless speaker interference | 15.80% | 34.96% |

The gap widens as noise increases. At -10 dB non-speech noise, accuracy is 4.81% for Mimi and 59.92% with AV-STE. Under same-dataset interference at -10 dB it is 1.31% for Mimi and 56.18% with AV-STE. The cost is on clean input. Mimi's clean accuracy is 100% by definition because its clean tokens are the targets, while AV-STE reproduces them only 76.78% of the time on LRS3 and 43.79% on Seamless. On Seamless, every AV-STE accuracy is far below its LRS3 counterpart, which the authors attribute to domain shift.

**Ablation** (Table 2; LRS3, average accuracy under non-speech / speaker noise):

| Variant | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| Streaming AV-HuBERT, audio only | 59.25 / 58.15 |
| Streaming AV-HuBERT, video only | 35.13 / 35.27 |
| Streaming AV-HuBERT, audio + video | 67.48 / 67.77 |
| + hard-token cross-attention | 66.37 / 66.60 |
| + soft-token cross-attention | 67.64 / 67.86 |
| + soft-token cross-attention + noise-adaptive modulation (AV-STE) | 68.02 / 68.09 |

Most of the gain comes from adding video to the streaming encoder. The two fusion components together add about 0.3-0.5 points over plain audio-visual fusion. Hard-token cross-attention is worse than no cross-attention at all. Figure 4 shows accuracy at 0 dB peaking at a lookahead of 4 frames.

**Reconstructed WER** (Table 8, Appendix C; LRS3). On clean input, WER is 7.6% from Mimi tokens and 7.8% with AV-STE. Under noise, AV-STE lowers WER at every SNR, for example from 186.2% to 139.2% at -10 dB non-speech noise and from 126.8% to 100.2% at -10 dB speaker interference. The WER values stay very high at low SNR, and the full AV-STE configuration does not have the lowest WER at every SNR. At -10 dB non-speech noise, the hard-cross-attention variant (127.2%) and the audio-only streaming encoder (136.4%) both score lower. The authors treat WER as a secondary diagnostic only, because Moshi consumes tokens rather than reconstructed audio. In the cross-attention-direction ablation (Table 6), using token embeddings as queries instead of audio-visual features raises noisy WER slightly (66.33% vs 65.72% and 59.72% vs 58.74%) and lowers clean WER slightly (7.5% vs 7.7%).

**Dialogue with frozen Moshi**, AudioSet noise on LRS3 (Table 3). AV-STE gives higher GPT coherence than Moshi at every noisy SNR for both noise types, for example 1.25 → 2.00 (non-speech) and 1.22 → 1.94 (speaker interference) at -10 dB. On clean input, coherence drops from 2.34 to 2.11. TOR and latency stay in the same range but are not uniformly better. On clean input, TOR rises from 0.520 to 0.608 and latency falls from 0.720 to 0.534 s. At -5 dB non-speech noise, TOR falls from 0.624 to 0.552 and latency rises from 0.660 to 1.125 s.

**Comparison with other systems** (Table 5; GPT coherence averaged over the five noisy SNRs):

| Setting | PersonaPlex | Moshi | PASE + Moshi | AV-STE + Moshi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LRS3, non-speech noise | 1.31 | 1.66 | **2.11** | 2.02 |
| LRS3, LRS3 speaker interference | 1.17 | 1.42 | 1.67 | **1.91** |
| Seamless, non-speech noise | - | 1.58 | - | 2.10 |
| Seamless, Seamless speaker interference | - | 1.50 | - | 2.03 |

PASE is an offline, non-causal generative speech enhancer applied to the whole utterance before Moshi, so it is only a non-streaming reference. Under same-dataset speaker interference, AV-STE + Moshi has the highest average. Per SNR, PASE + Moshi is higher at 10 dB (2.15 vs 2.00) and AV-STE + Moshi is higher from 5 dB down to -10 dB. Under LRS3 non-speech noise, the offline PASE cascade averages higher than AV-STE (2.11 vs 2.02). The body text does not discuss this row. On Seamless, AV-STE also raises clean-input coherence (2.38 → 2.70), even though its clean token accuracy there is only 43.79%.

## Novelty Assessment

The new element is where vision enters the system. Earlier audio-visual dialogue work either feeds images into the dialogue model (MoshiVis) or trains the dialogue model on paired audio-visual conversation (AV-Dialog). AV-STE instead restores the speech-token stream before the dialogue model sees it, so the speech LLM needs no retraining. The individual pieces are not new: AV-HuBERT, causal convolutions, bounded-lookahead masks from streaming ASR, and entropy-based confidence from ASR confidence estimation. The contributions specific to this paper are cross-attention to the tokenizer's soft distribution and an entropy-initialised gate between tokenizer and audio-visual evidence. The ablation credits these components with a small share of the gain, about 0.5 points of average accuracy over plain streaming audio-visual fusion. Most of the improvement comes from adding lip video to a streaming AV-HuBERT and predicting Mimi's semantic tokens directly. The contribution is therefore mainly an architectural recipe for a modular front-end: a new input-side module and token-restoration objective for a frozen full-duplex model. The fusion module itself is a modest refinement. The approach depends on a tokenizer whose semantic stream is separable from its acoustic streams, and it is tested only with Mimi and Moshi.

## Field Significance

moderate: The paper shows that the input side of a full-duplex speech LLM can be hardened against noise and competing talkers by restoring one token stream with lip video, while the dialogue model stays frozen and keeps roughly its turn-taking behaviour. Its token-level and dialogue-level results show that speech-token corruption, not only the dialogue model, limits noisy full-duplex interaction, and they give a streaming alternative to offline speech enhancement in front of a speech LLM. The evidence is limited to one model and tokenizer, an LLM-judged coherence score, single-turn tests, and a visible cost on clean input. The authors state that the paper is accepted to EMNLP 2026; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** Restoring corrupted semantic speech tokens with visual speech cues before they reach a frozen full-duplex speech LLM improves the relevance of its responses under acoustic noise without retraining the dialogue model.
  > *Evidence:* With frozen Moshi on LRS3, AV-STE raises GPT-4o coherence at every noisy SNR under both AudioSet noise types (for example 1.25 → 2.00 at -10 dB non-speech noise), and the average under same-dataset speaker interference rises from 1.42 to 1.91; on out-of-domain Seamless Interaction, the average rises from 1.58 to 2.10 and from 1.50 to 2.03. *(§5.2, Tables 3 and 5)*
- **supports:** Visual speech information gives the most help with speech-token recovery when acoustic corruption is most severe, especially when interfering talkers come from the same distribution as the target speaker.
  > *Evidence:* Mimi's semantic-token accuracy falls to 1.31% at -10 dB with 1-4 same-dataset LRS3 interferers, while AV-STE keeps 56.18% (average 12.61% → 65.60%); in the ablation, the audio-visual streaming encoder beats the audio-only one by 0.8 points on clean input and by 22 points at -10 dB non-speech noise. *(§5.1, Tables 1 and 7, Figure 3)*
- **complicates:** A token-restoration front-end trained for noisy input adds a representation mismatch on clean speech and loses much of its token-level accuracy under domain shift.
  > *Evidence:* AV-STE reproduces only 76.78% of clean Mimi tokens on LRS3 and 43.79% on Seamless Interaction, and with clean LRS3 input Moshi's coherence falls from 2.34 to 2.11 when AV-STE is inserted. *(§5.1, §7, Tables 1 and 3)*
- **refines:** Streaming audio-visual token enhancement competes with offline audio-only speech enhancement placed in front of a speech LLM mainly at low SNR and under same-distribution talker interference, not uniformly.
  > *Evidence:* Under LRS3 speaker interference, AV-STE + Moshi beats the non-causal PASE + Moshi cascade from 5 dB down to -10 dB (average 1.91 vs 1.67) but trails it at 10 dB (2.00 vs 2.15); under LRS3 non-speech noise, the PASE cascade averages higher (2.11 vs 2.02). *(§5.2, Table 5)*
- **complicates:** Token accuracy and reconstructed-speech WER can rank variants of a speech-token front-end differently, so the token metric a speech LLM consumes is not a proxy for intelligibility of decoded audio.
  > *Evidence:* The full AV-STE configuration has the best average semantic-token accuracy, but at -10 dB non-speech noise its reconstructed WER (139.2%) is higher than the hard-cross-attention variant (127.2%) and the audio-only streaming encoder (136.4%). *(Appendix B–C, Tables 7 and 8)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

- The method assumes clean, synchronised frontal lip video. The authors state that robustness to visual corruption, occlusion or missing video is untested (§7), and no condition combines acoustic and visual degradation.
- Dialogue quality is measured only by a GPT-4o relevance judgement on 125 single-turn utterances per dataset. There is no human evaluation, no multi-turn dialogue, and no measure of the quality or naturalness of Moshi's spoken output.
- The method is evaluated with one tokenizer and one dialogue model (Mimi and Moshi). Transfer to other speech LLMs is only suggested (§2.3), and it relies on a tokenizer whose semantic stream can be replaced independently of its acoustic streams.
- Clean-input performance degrades: the token mismatch, lower clean coherence on LRS3, and the sharp drop in clean token accuracy on Seamless (§7). The paper does not test switching AV-STE off or bypassing it when the tokenizer is confident beyond the learned gate.
- Turn-taking is called "largely preserved", but TOR and latency move in both directions across conditions (Table 3), and only one noisy run per condition is reported, with no variance.
- Reconstructed WER exceeds 100% at -5 and -10 dB for every system. Absolute intelligibility at those SNRs is therefore very poor even after enhancement.
- AV-STE's added latency, beyond the 160 ms lookahead, and its compute cost in a real-time full-duplex loop are not measured.
