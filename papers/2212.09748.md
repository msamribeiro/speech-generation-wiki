> [!abstract] ICCV · 2023 · Conference
> **Peebles et al.** (UC Berkeley / New York University) · [→ Paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Peebles_Scalable_Diffusion_Models_with_Transformers_ICCV_2023_paper.html) · [→ Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748) · Demo: ? · Code: ✓
>
> Replaces the convolutional U-Net of latent diffusion models with a plain Vision-Transformer-style backbone over latent patches (the Diffusion Transformer, DiT), conditioned through zero-initialized adaptive layer norm (adaLN-Zero), and shows that its sample quality improves steadily with forward-pass compute.

## Problem

Diffusion models for images all used a convolutional U-Net as the denoising backbone, a design inherited from PixelCNN++ through the original DDPM work and only tuned since then. Transformers had taken over language, vision and autoregressive image generation, but not diffusion. The paper asks whether the U-Net inductive bias matters for diffusion at all. It also asks how a transformer denoiser scales, measured by forward-pass Gflops rather than parameter count, which the authors argue is a poor proxy for complexity when input resolution changes.

## Method

DiT works inside the latent diffusion framework. An off-the-shelf, frozen variational autoencoder from Stable Diffusion maps a 256 × 256 × 3 image to a 32 × 32 × 4 latent (downsampling factor 8), and the diffusion model is trained in that latent space. The diffusion setup follows ADM: 1000 steps with a linear variance schedule from 1 × 10⁻⁴ to 2 × 10⁻², a noise-prediction network trained with the simple mean-squared-error loss, and a learned reverse-process covariance trained on the full variational bound (§3.1, §4).

The first layer "patchifies" the latent: each p × p patch is linearly embedded into one token, and fixed sine-cosine positional embeddings are added. Patch size sets the sequence length T = (I/p)². Halving p quadruples the token count and at least quadruples Gflops, but leaves the parameter count almost unchanged. The design space covers p = 2, 4, 8 (§3.2, Figure 4).

The tokens pass through N transformer blocks. The paper compares four ways to inject the timestep t and class label c:

- **In-context:** t and c are appended as two extra tokens, as with a cls token in ViT.
- **Cross-attention:** t and c form a length-two sequence that the image tokens attend to through an added cross-attention layer (about 15% more Gflops).
- **adaLN:** the layer-norm scale and shift (γ, β) are regressed from the sum of the t and c embeddings, applying the same modulation to every token. This adds the fewest Gflops.
- **adaLN-Zero:** adaLN plus a dimension-wise scale α before each residual connection. The MLP that predicts α is zero-initialized, so every block starts as the identity function, mirroring zero-initialization practice in ResNets and diffusion U-Nets.

Model size follows ViT configurations: DiT-S (12 layers, d = 384), DiT-B (12, 768), DiT-L (24, 1024) and a new DiT-XL (28 layers, d = 1152, 16 heads) (Table 1). With patch sizes this spans 0.3 to 118.6 Gflops. A final (adaptive) layer norm and a linear decoder map each token back to a p × p × 2C tensor, giving the predicted noise and diagonal covariance. Parameter counts are not reported in the proceedings version.

Training uses AdamW with a constant learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁴, no weight decay, batch size 256, horizontal flips only, and an EMA of weights (decay 0.9999). Hyperparameters are shared across all sizes and mostly taken from ADM. The authors report no warmup or regularization was needed and no loss spikes appeared. Sampling uses 250 DDPM steps with optional classifier-free guidance (§4).

## Key Results

All experiments are class-conditional image generation on ImageNet. There is no speech or audio experiment.

- **Block design (§5, Figure 5):** among DiT-XL/2 models, adaLN-Zero gives the lowest FID at every point in training. At 400K iterations its FID is nearly half that of in-context conditioning. It also clearly beats vanilla adaLN, so the identity initialization matters, not only the modulation. All later models use adaLN-Zero.
- **Scaling (§5, Figures 6 and 8):** across 12 models (S, B, L, XL × p = 8, 4, 2), FID-50K improves throughout training both when the model gets deeper and wider at a fixed patch size and when the patch size shrinks at a fixed model size. FID correlates strongly with Gflops: configurations with similar Gflops, such as DiT-S/2 and DiT-B/4, reach similar FID.
- **Training compute (§5, Figure 9):** small models trained longer become compute-inefficient relative to larger models trained for fewer steps; XL/2 overtakes XL/4 after about 10¹⁰ training Gflops.
- **ImageNet 256 × 256 (Table 2):** after 7M steps, DiT-XL/2 with guidance scale 1.50 reaches FID 2.27, the lowest FID in Table 2, ahead of StyleGAN-XL (2.30) and the best prior diffusion entry, LDM-4-G at cfg 1.50 (3.60). Its IS of 278.24 is also the highest in the table. It does not lead every column: sFID 4.60 trails StyleGAN-XL (4.02), precision 0.83 trails BigGAN-deep and LDM-4-G cfg 1.50 (both 0.87), and recall 0.57 is below several rows, including unguided DiT-XL/2 (0.67). The paper also reports FID 2.55 for a model trained 2.35M steps, similar to ADM's budget (§5.1). DiT-XL/2 uses 118.6 Gflops, against 103.6 for LDM-4 and 1120 for ADM (§5.1, Figure 2).
- **ImageNet 512 × 512 (Table 3):** after 3M steps, guided DiT-XL/2 reaches FID 3.04 and IS 240.82, ahead of the best diffusion entry (ADM-G plus ADM-U, FID 3.85) at 524.6 Gflops against ADM's 1983 and ADM-U's 2813. At this resolution StyleGAN-XL keeps the lower FID (2.41) and higher IS (267.75).
- **Guidance trade-off (Tables 2 and 3):** at 256 × 256, raising the guidance scale from none to 1.25 to 1.50 lowers FID (9.62, 3.22, 2.27) and raises precision (0.67, 0.76, 0.83) while recall falls (0.67, 0.62, 0.57). DiT's recall stays above LDM-4 and LDM-8 at every matched guidance setting.
- **Model versus sampling compute (§5.2, Figure 10):** extra sampling steps do not close the gap to a bigger model. DiT-L/2 with 1000 steps (80.7 Tflops per image) reaches FID-10K 25.9, while DiT-XL/2 with 128 steps (15.2 Tflops, 5× less) reaches 23.7.

Baseline rows in Tables 2 and 3 are prior systems with different backbones, latent spaces and training budgets. The cleanest architectural comparison is against LDM-4, which shares the latent-diffusion setup.

## Novelty Assessment

The transformer block, the patchify stem, latent diffusion, the ADM diffusion recipe and classifier-free guidance all predate the paper. The new parts are the specific conditioning design, adaLN-Zero (regressed scale, shift and zero-initialized residual gates), the controlled comparison showing it beats cross-attention and in-context tokens, and the first systematic scaling study of transformer denoisers. The scaling study measures complexity in Gflops and separates token count (patch size) from depth and width. The contribution is mostly architectural simplification plus scaling evidence. Concurrent attention-based diffusion architectures existed (the paper cites one), so the novelty is a pure, unmodified-ViT backbone rather than attention in diffusion as such. All hyperparameters come from ADM without tuning, which makes the gains easier to attribute to the backbone.

## Field Significance

> [!important]
> Foundational: the paper shows that a diffusion denoiser does not need a convolutional U-Net and that a plain transformer over latent patches, conditioned by adaLN-Zero, improves predictably as forward-pass compute grows. Because the backbone is a sequence model over tokens of a continuous latent, it provides a modality-agnostic template that can serve as the denoiser for continuous speech representations such as mel-spectrogram or latent frames.

## Claims

- **supports:** A plain transformer backbone can replace the convolutional U-Net in latent diffusion models without loss of sample quality at comparable compute.
  > *Evidence:* On class-conditional ImageNet 256 × 256, guided DiT-XL/2 (118.6 Gflops) reaches FID 2.27 against 3.60 for the U-Net latent diffusion model LDM-4-G (103.6 Gflops), and 3.04 against 3.85 for the best pixel-space U-Net entry at 512 × 512. *(§5.1, Table 2, Table 3, Figure 2)*
- **supports:** The sample quality of diffusion transformers tracks forward-pass compute rather than parameter count, so shorter patches (more tokens) improve quality at a fixed model size.
  > *Evidence:* Across 12 DiT models, FID-50K at 400K steps falls with Gflops; shrinking the patch size at fixed depth and width (parameters roughly unchanged) improves FID throughout training, and DiT-S/2 and DiT-B/4 reach similar FID at similar Gflops. *(§5, Figure 6, Figure 8)*
- **supports:** Conditioning a diffusion transformer through adaptive layer norm with zero-initialized residual gates works better than cross-attention or extra conditioning tokens for global conditions such as timestep and class.
  > *Evidence:* Among DiT-XL/2 variants, adaLN-Zero has the lowest FID at every training stage, roughly half the FID of in-context conditioning at 400K steps, beats vanilla adaLN, and costs fewer Gflops than cross-attention (+15%). *(§3.2, §5, Figure 5)*
- **complicates:** Extra sampling steps cannot make up for a smaller diffusion denoiser.
  > *Evidence:* DiT-L/2 with 1000 sampling steps spends 80.7 Tflops per image for FID-10K 25.9, while DiT-XL/2 with 128 steps spends 15.2 Tflops for 23.7. *(§5.2, Figure 10)*
- **complicates:** Stronger classifier-free guidance improves fidelity metrics at the cost of sample diversity.
  > *Evidence:* For DiT-XL/2 at 256 × 256, guidance scales none / 1.25 / 1.50 give FID 9.62 / 3.22 / 2.27 and precision 0.67 / 0.76 / 0.83, while recall drops 0.67 / 0.62 / 0.57. *(§5.1, Table 2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The paper has no speech or audio experiment, so its relevance to speech generation is a transfer of architecture, not measured evidence. Every result is class-conditional ImageNet generation on a fixed 2D latent grid with sine-cosine positional embeddings. Variable-length sequences, and alignment between a conditioning sequence and the output, are not studied.

- **Conditioning scope.** The block comparison covers only a two-element global condition (timestep and class label). The finding that adaLN-Zero beats cross-attention does not cover long sequence conditions such as text, phonemes or reference audio, which speech models need.
- **Frozen autoencoder.** All models use the Stable Diffusion VAE unchanged, so the effect of the latent space on scaling is not separated from the backbone.
- **Metrics.** Evaluation is automatic (FID, sFID, IS, precision/recall), with no human study. FID is the main scaling signal, and the paper notes it is sensitive to implementation details.
- **Not uniformly best.** At 512 × 512 StyleGAN-XL keeps the lower FID and higher IS, and at 256 × 256 the guided model trades recall for FID.
- **Compute and reporting.** The best models train for millions of steps on TPU v3 pods, and the proceedings version reports Gflops but not parameter counts. Hyperparameters are not tuned, so the scaling trends may change under tuned recipes.
