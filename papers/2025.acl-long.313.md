# F5-TTS: A Fairytaler that Fakes Fluent and Faithful Speech with Flow Matching

**Paper:** [https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.313](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.313)

**One-sentence contribution:** F5-TTS is a fully non-autoregressive, zero-shot TTS system that combines a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone with ConvNeXt V2 text refinement and an inference-time Sway Sampling strategy to achieve fast (RTF 0.15), robust, and naturally expressive multilingual speech generation without phoneme alignment, duration models, or text encoders.

## Problem

Prior non-autoregressive zero-shot TTS systems faced a fundamental tension between simplicity and robustness. E2 TTS proved feasible the idea of padding raw character sequences with filler tokens to match mel-frame length — avoiding phoneme alignment, duration predictors, and text encoders — but the resulting model converged slowly and exhibited persistent alignment failures (7% of test samples in Mandarin had WER > 50% that could not be resolved by re-ranking). The deep entanglement of semantic and acoustic features in the flat U-Net Transformer used by E2 TTS was identified as the root cause. Alternative approaches like NaturalSpeech 3 and Voicebox required heavy alignment machinery (frame-wise phoneme alignment or monotonic alignment search), while DiTTo-TTS needed a pretrained language model and semantically infused codec, adding complexity. Autoregressive models addressed speaker similarity and prosody well but suffered from inference latency and exposure bias.

## Method

F5-TTS operates as a text-guided speech-infilling model trained with conditional flow matching (CFM) using the optimal transport (OT) path. The acoustic representation is 100-dimensional log mel-filterbank features at 24 kHz (hop 256). Input text is converted to a character sequence (alphabets for English, full pinyin for Chinese), then padded with filler tokens `<F>` to equal the mel frame length, forming a sequence where text characters are followed by fillers up to the total audio length.

**Architecture.** The backbone is a latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with adaLN-zero adaptive Layer Norm blocks rather than the U-Net Transformer used by E2 TTS. The critical departure from E2 TTS is the insertion of ConvNeXt V2 blocks (4 layers, 512 embedding / 1024 FFN dim) to refine the padded character sequence before it is concatenated with the speech input in the feature dimension. This individual modeling space allows text to "prepare itself" for in-context learning with the speech modality, directly addressing the semantic-acoustic entanglement problem. The DiT has 22 layers, 16 heads, 1024/2048 embedding/FFN dim. The flow step t is provided as an adaLN condition via sinusoidal positional embedding rather than being appended to the input sequence. Rotary position embedding (RoPE) is used for self-attention; a convolutional position embedding is applied to the concatenated input. Total parameters: 335.8M. A pretrained Vocos vocoder converts the generated mel spectrograms to waveform.

**Training.** The model is trained on the Emilia multilingual dataset (filtered to ~95K hours of English and Chinese). Training uses AdamW with a peak learning rate of 7.5e-5, linear warm-up for 20K steps, linear decay over 1.2M total updates, batch size of 307,200 audio frames (~0.91 hours), on 8 NVIDIA A100 80G GPUs for over one week. A random 70–100% of mel frames is masked per sample. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is applied in two stages: first masking the masked-speech input with probability 0.3, then additionally masking the text input with probability 0.2.

**Inference.** Given reference mel features and transcription plus target text, duration is estimated from the character-count ratio of reference to target text. An ODE solver (Euler by default) integrates the learned vector field from noise to speech. The key inference-time contribution is **Sway Sampling**: rather than uniform flow step sampling, a monotonic transform `f_sway(u; s) = u + s * (cos(π/2 * u) − 1 + u)` is applied with s < 0, biasing sampling toward smaller t values. This causes the model to spend more integration steps on the early phase (speech silhouette formation, text-speech alignment) and fewer on the fine-grained embellishment phase. Sway Sampling can be applied to any existing CFM-based model without retraining. With 16 NFE and Sway Sampling (s = -1), the system achieves an RTF of 0.15 on an NVIDIA RTX 3090.

## Key Results

**LibriSpeech-PC test-clean (1127 samples, 4–10s):** F5-TTS (32 NFE) achieves WER 2.42% and SIM-o 0.66, vs. E2 TTS (32 NFE) WER 2.95% and SIM-o 0.69, and CosyVoice WER 3.59% and SIM-o 0.66. The RTF of 0.15 (16 NFE) compares favorably to E2 TTS (0.68), Voicebox (0.64), and CosyVoice (0.92).

**Seed-TTS test-en:** F5-TTS (32 NFE) achieves WER 1.83%, SIM-o 0.67, CMOS +0.31, SMOS 3.89. E2 TTS (32 NFE) achieves WER 2.19%, SIM-o 0.71, CMOS +0.06, SMOS 3.81. Closed-source Seed-TTS DiT reports WER 1.733% and SIM-o 0.790 (from the Seed-TTS paper, with much larger model and data).

**Seed-TTS test-zh:** F5-TTS (32 NFE) achieves WER 1.56%, SIM-o 0.76, CMOS +0.21, SMOS 3.83. Ground truth achieves WER 1.26%, SIM-o 0.76.

**Ablation (small ~155M models, WenetSpeech4TTS, 945h Mandarin):** F5-TTS (32 NFE) reaches WER 4.17% / SIM 0.54 vs. E2 TTS WER 9.63% / SIM 0.53 at 800K updates, demonstrating substantially better robustness. Pure DiT without ConvNeXt (F5-TTS -Conv2Text) fails to learn alignment entirely.

**Sway Sampling ablation:** On LibriSpeech-PC test-clean, removing Sway Sampling degrades WER from 2.41% to 2.84% (32 NFE) and UTMOS from 3.89 to 3.70. The strategy generalizes: E2 TTS with Sway Sampling improves from WER 2.95% to 2.84%.

**ELLA-V hard sentences:** F5-TTS achieves WER 4.40% vs. E2 TTS 8.58% and CosyVoice 8.30%, confirming robustness advantage.

Comparisons to baselines are reasonably controlled on the same test sets; however, Seed-TTS DiT (the strongest baseline) is evaluated from reported scores in the original paper with a much larger model and dataset, making direct comparison difficult.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is primarily **architectural (ConvNeXt text refinement) plus inference recipe (Sway Sampling)**. The key genuine insight is diagnosing E2 TTS's failure mode as semantic-acoustic entanglement and proposing a principled fix — individual modeling space for text via ConvNeXt before concatenation — that is both simple and empirically validated. This directly addresses a real-world deployment limitation (7% unrecoverable failure rate). The DiT backbone choice (replacing U-Net Transformer) also contributes to lower GFLOPs and faster training.

Sway Sampling is genuinely novel as an inference-time improvement: a non-uniform flow-step schedule derived from a principled hypothesis about early vs. late denoising steps. Its universality (applies to any CFM model without retraining) gives it broad utility.

The system is not a new paradigm — it builds directly on E2 TTS and Voicebox — but it is a well-motivated incremental advance that resolves concrete failure modes. Data scale (95K hours) is comparable to MaskGCT and lower than Seed-TTS.

## Limitations and Open Questions

1. **Mel sequence length inefficiency.** The mel-spectrogram representation is much longer than the text sequence, creating a large padding overhead. More efficient continuous representations (or latent compression) could improve both efficiency and quality.
2. **No fine-grained paralinguistic control.** F5-TTS cannot independently control emotion, speaking rate (beyond global character-count ratio), or other prosodic attributes. The architecture provides no explicit mechanism for this.
3. **Duration estimation is simple and approximate.** Character-count ratio ignores phonetic duration variability, speaking rate variation, and prosodic structure. A lightweight learned duration predictor could improve rhythm and expressiveness.
4. **Closed-source gap.** Seed-TTS DiT (trained on "millions of hours") substantially outperforms F5-TTS on speaker similarity (SIM-o 0.790 vs. 0.67 on test-en), raising questions about whether the gap is architectural or purely data-scale.
5. **Sway Sampling interaction with training-time schedules.** The paper acknowledges future work combining Sway Sampling with training-time noise schedulers and distillation, which could further boost efficiency.
6. **Code-switching and multilingual extension.** While English+Chinese code-switching is demonstrated, broader multilingual coverage (beyond the two languages in Emilia) is not evaluated.
