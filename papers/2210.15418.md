>
> FreeVC turns the end-to-end VITS framework into a text-free one-shot voice converter by replacing its text-driven prior with a narrow bottleneck over WavLM features, trained on speaker-distorted audio from a spectrogram-resize augmentation.

*A [peer-reviewed version](https://doi.org/10.1109/icassp49357.2023.10095191) appeared at ICASSP 2023. This page, including its metrics and claims, summarizes the arXiv preprint.*

## Problem

One-shot voice conversion (VC) separates content from the source utterance and speaker identity from a single target utterance, then rebuilds a waveform from the two. The authors identify two weaknesses in prior systems. First, text-based content extraction (ASR phonetic posteriorgrams, or linguistic knowledge borrowed from a TTS model) needs large annotated corpora, while text-free methods (information bottleneck, vector quantization, instance normalization) leak source-speaker information and trail text-based systems in quality. Second, most VC systems use a two-stage pipeline in which a separately trained vocoder receives predicted features that differ from the real features it was trained on, which degrades the waveform. VITS ([[2106.06103|VITS]]) removes this mismatch with a single conditional VAE trained adversarially, but it needs text and supports only many-to-many conversion between seen speakers.

## Method

FreeVC keeps the VITS backbone: a conditional VAE with a posterior encoder over linear spectrograms, a normalizing flow, a waveform decoder and a discriminator, all following VITS and trained with GAN losses (§2, §2.1). The changes are in the prior encoder, the speaker encoder, the training data and the inference path.

The prior encoder takes raw waveform instead of text. A WavLM model ([[2110.13900|WavLM]]) produces 1024-dimensional self-supervised features. A bottleneck extractor projects them down to d = 192 dimensions, a size the authors choose so that the narrow representation must drop speaker and noise information while keeping content (§2.1.1, §3.1). This representation is projected to the mean and variance of the prior, and a volume-preserving flow of affine coupling layers, conditioned on the speaker embedding, maps between prior and posterior latents. The paper does not state whether WavLM is frozen or fine-tuned.

Speaker identity comes from one of two encoders (§2.1.2). FreeVC uses a pretrained speaker-verification encoder (the one from BNE-PPG-VC). FreeVC-s uses a small LSTM encoder trained jointly from scratch, to test whether clean content features let a simple encoder learn the missing speaker information.

Spectrogram-resize (SR) augmentation is the main training contribution (§2.2.1). The authors compute a mel-spectrogram, resize it vertically by a ratio r between 0.85 and 1.15 with bilinear interpolation, pad (r < 1) or cut (r > 1) the top bins back to the original shape, and resynthesize a waveform with a HiFi-GAN v1 vocoder ([[2010.05646|HiFi-GAN]]). This shifts pitch and formant spacing while leaving the content unchanged. The prior encoder sees the augmented waveform while the reconstruction target stays the original, so the model learns to extract content that is invariant to these speaker-like distortions. This replaces careful tuning of the bottleneck width. Horizontal (time-axis) resizing is described as an option, but the paper does not say whether the reported models use it.

![Fig. 1: Training and inference procedure of FreeVC. The generator loss is the VITS sum of an L1 mel reconstruction loss, a KL term between posterior and flow-transformed prior, a least-squares adversarial loss and a feature-matching loss (§2.2.2, Eq. 3). At inference, unlike VITS, content comes from WavLM and the bottleneck (not from the posterior encoder and the forward flow), so the source speaker embedding plays no part in content extraction (§2.3). The inverse flow, conditioned on the target embedding, feeds the decoder. Models train for 900k steps at batch size 64 on one RTX 3090, using 16 kHz audio. Parameter count is not reported.

## Key Results

All systems are trained on VCTK only (107 speakers). Subjective tests use 15 listeners and six target speakers per pool, in three settings: seen-to-seen (VCTK), unseen-to-seen (LibriTTS source to VCTK target) and unseen-to-unseen (LibriTTS) (§3.2). Baselines are VQMIVC (text-free), BNE-PPG-VC (text-based, pretrained speaker encoder) and YourTTS ([[2112.02418|YourTTS]], text-based VITS extension). The paper does not say how the baselines were trained or which checkpoints were used.

- **Naturalness and similarity (Table 1).** All three FreeVC variants score higher MOS and SMOS than every baseline in all six columns. FreeVC reaches MOS 3.99 / 4.06 / 4.06 and SMOS 3.80 / 3.77 / 2.83 across the three settings. The strongest baseline per column scores MOS 3.46 (YourTTS), 2.89 and 3.44 (both BNE-PPG-VC), and SMOS 3.25 (YourTTS), 2.83 and 2.63 (both BNE-PPG-VC). Converted unseen-to-seen and unseen-to-unseen MOS (4.06) comes close to the source recordings (4.11, 4.17).
- **Unseen targets remain hard.** FreeVC's SMOS drops from 3.80 (seen-to-seen) to 2.83 (unseen-to-unseen). Its unseen-to-unseen margin over BNE-PPG-VC (2.83 ± 0.08 vs 2.63 ± 0.10) is the smallest in the table, and FreeVC-s (2.78 ± 0.09) overlaps the baseline's interval.
- **Intelligibility and prosody (Table 2).** FreeVC-s has the lowest WER (4.23%) and CER (1.46%), with FreeVC next (4.35%, 1.53%). The best baseline is BNE-PPG-VC (6.54%, 2.50%), which uses an ASR-trained content encoder. FreeVC has the highest F0 Pearson correlation with the source (F0-PCC 0.778, vs 0.768 for FreeVC-s and 0.736 for YourTTS).
- **SR augmentation ablation.** Removing SR lowers FreeVC's scores in all six Table 1 columns. The clearest drop is seen-to-seen SMOS (3.80 to 3.50); unseen-to-unseen differences (MOS 4.06 vs 3.97, SMOS 2.83 vs 2.80) fall within the confidence intervals. Objectively, WER rises from 4.35% to 4.92%, CER from 1.53% to 1.77%, and F0-PCC falls from 0.778 to 0.762.
- **Speaker encoder.** The jointly trained LSTM encoder (FreeVC-s) matches FreeVC on naturalness (MOS within 0.04 in every setting) but has lower SMOS in all three settings, with the largest gap on unseen-to-seen (3.68 vs 3.77).

The authors also report that baselines degrade on low-quality or unclearly pronounced source speech while FreeVC does not (§3.3.1). They give no numbers for this robustness observation.

## Novelty Assessment

The VITS backbone, WavLM features and the information-bottleneck idea (from AutoVC) all predate this paper. The new parts are their combination into a text-free one-shot VC system that keeps VITS's single-stage waveform generation, the switch to SSL-plus-bottleneck content at inference, and SR augmentation as a cheap way to make the bottleneck speaker-invariant without hand-tuning its width or using signal-processing perturbation chains. SR augmentation is the most transferable idea, and it is the only component the paper ablates. The bottleneck dimension, the use of WavLM over other features and the choice of layer are not ablated, so the separate contribution of each component remains unknown. The comparison favours FreeVC strongly, but baseline training details are missing and all models train on VCTK's 107 speakers, a narrow speaker pool for a one-shot claim.

## Field Significance

> [!tip] High
> FreeVC shows that a text-free VC system built on self-supervised features, a narrow bottleneck and speaker-distorting augmentation can match or beat text-based VC systems on naturalness, similarity and intelligibility, without vocoder mismatch. It provides a compact, single-stage, openly released recipe for one-shot conversion that later systems can reuse as a backbone or baseline.

## Claims

- **supports:** Self-supervised speech features passed through a narrow bottleneck can give text-free voice conversion content representations that are as intelligible as those from ASR-trained phonetic posteriorgram encoders.
  > *Evidence:* FreeVC (WavLM features reduced from 1024 to 192 dimensions) reaches 4.35% WER and 1.53% CER, against 6.54% and 2.50% for the text-based BNE-PPG-VC and 12.87% and 5.70% for YourTTS. *(§2.1.1, §3.3.2, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Training the content encoder on inputs whose pitch and formants are artificially perturbed reduces source-speaker leakage in bottleneck-based voice conversion.
  > *Evidence:* Removing spectrogram-resize augmentation lowers seen-to-seen SMOS from 3.80 to 3.50 and MOS from 3.99 to 3.85, and raises WER from 4.35% to 4.92%. *(§2.2.1, §3.3.1, Tables 1 and 2)*
- **supports:** A single-stage VAE-GAN that generates waveforms end to end avoids the acoustic-model and vocoder mismatch of two-stage voice conversion and yields near-source naturalness.
  > *Evidence:* With the VITS conditional VAE and adversarial decoder, FreeVC reaches MOS 4.06 in unseen-to-seen and unseen-to-unseen conversion, against 4.11 and 4.17 for the source recordings. *(§2, §3.3.1, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** One-shot voice conversion trained on a small speaker pool keeps high naturalness on unseen target speakers but loses much of its speaker similarity.
  > *Evidence:* Trained on VCTK's 107 speakers, FreeVC's SMOS falls from 3.80 (seen-to-seen) to 2.83 (unseen-to-unseen) while MOS stays at 4.06. *(§3.1, §3.3.1, Table 1)*
- **refines:** When content features are clean, a jointly trained speaker encoder can match a pretrained speaker-verification encoder on naturalness, but pretraining on many speakers still helps similarity for unseen targets.
  > *Evidence:* FreeVC-s (LSTM encoder trained from scratch) matches FreeVC's MOS within 0.04 in every setting and has slightly lower WER, but has lower SMOS in all three settings (2.78 vs 2.83 unseen-to-unseen). *(§2.1.2, §3.3.1, Tables 1 and 2)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

Speaker similarity on unseen targets is the main weakness. Unseen-to-unseen SMOS (2.83) is about one point below seen-to-seen SMOS. The authors name speaker adaptation for unseen targets as future work (§4).

- All systems train on VCTK only, a 107-speaker read-speech corpus. Generalization to more diverse speakers, languages and expressive speech is not tested.
- Only SR augmentation is ablated. The bottleneck width (fixed at 192), the WavLM layer choice and whether WavLM is frozen are not reported or tested.
- Baseline training data and checkpoints are not described, so the size of the margins over VQMIVC, BNE-PPG-VC and YourTTS cannot be checked for fairness.
- The objective evaluation has no speaker-similarity metric (such as speaker-verification similarity or EER). Speaker similarity rests on 15 listeners rating six target speakers per pool.
- The robustness claim for low-quality source speech is qualitative and has no supporting numbers.
- F0-PCC measures how closely output pitch contours follow the source. It rewards prosody preservation, which is a design goal of identity-only conversion, not target-style transfer.
- With one reference utterance per target speaker, how quality varies with reference length and recording condition is not examined.
