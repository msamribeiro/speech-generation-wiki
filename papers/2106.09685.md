> Introduces Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), which freezes a pre-trained Transformer and trains only a pair of low-rank matrices per adapted weight, matching or exceeding full fine-tuning with a small fraction of the trainable parameters and no added inference latency.

*This page, its metrics, and its claims summarize the ingested version. A peer-reviewed version is available at [ICLR 2022, LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nZeVKeeFYf9).*

## Context in Speech Generation

LoRA keeps a pre-trained weight matrix W0 frozen and learns its update as a low-rank product BA (rank r much smaller than the layer dimensions), initialised so the update is zero at the start of training; after training, BA can be merged into W0, so the adapted model runs with no extra inference latency, unlike adapter layers *(§4.1)*. The experiments are text-only (RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3 175B on GLUE, E2E NLG, WikiSQL, SAMSum and related tasks), where adapting only the attention query and value projections with a rank as low as one or two is competitive, and on GPT-3 175B the trainable parameters drop by about 10,000 times and training VRAM from 1.2TB to 350GB *(§4.2, §5, §7)*. The paper states that the principle applies to any network with dense layers *(§8)*, which is what makes it usable for speech: it enables parameter-efficient adaptation of large LLM backbones inside speech LMs and LLM-based TTS models, and small swappable per-task modules (for example, per-language or per-style adapters) on top of one shared frozen model.
