> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Che Hyun Lee et al.** (Seoul National University / University of Seoul) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01016) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ?
>
> A training-free inference-time guidance rule for a frozen discrete diffusion TTS model that steers only the frames of a code-switched foreign phrase toward that phrase's language, locating the phrase from the model's own self-attention, so the inserted phrase is spoken with a native accent while the surrounding utterance keeps its speaker and language.

## Problem

Multilingual zero-shot TTS models clone a voice across languages, but when a foreign phrase is embedded inside a sentence in another language (intra-utterance code-switching), the phrase is pronounced with the accent of the primary ("matrix carrier") language. The authors attribute this cross-lingual accent leakage to two factors: zero-shot cloning entangles the speaker's identity with the prompt's native language habits, and classifier-free guidance (CFG) applies one global language condition at a uniform scale over the whole sequence, which flattens the embedded phrase toward the carrier accent. Prior code-switching TTS approaches build the capability into model weights (bilingual posteriorgrams, cross-lingual embeddings, synthetic fine-tuning, dedicated diffusion architectures, or the dual-level language injection of X-Voice), all of which require training, and none offers localized, frame-level guidance inside a non-autoregressive speech LM. The authors also note that existing code-switching benchmarks are dominated by single-word or unmanaged alternations rather than dense multi-word phrasal insertions.

## Method

The method runs on the public 0.81B-parameter OmniVoice model, a discrete diffusion language model (DLM) TTS system with 28 decoder layers over an 8-codebook audio codec that iteratively unmasks the acoustic token sequence over T = 32 steps with two-pass CFG (global text guidance scale γ = 2). The backbone is frozen throughout: no weights are trained or fine-tuned, and no auxiliary module is added at inference. The input prefix carries a language tag, the reference voice prompt and the text; the speaker reference for each language direction is a single fixed monolingual carrier-language voice.

**Phrase localization by self-attention probing.** At each denoising step, for every acoustic frame the method takes the argmax over transcript tokens of the decoder self-attention weights; a frame whose argmax token falls inside the embedded phrase's character span is marked 1 in a binary frame mask. On KSS (Korean) and LJSpeech (English) pilot sets scored against forced-alignment ground truth, the authors found that alignment tracking concentrates in mid-decoder layers, and that an ensemble of layers 8 and 12 with head-wise max-pooling maximizes worst-case phrase recall across the two languages. The phrase's character span must be supplied as input (the authors note it can often be derived from Unicode script boundaries or a language identifier).

**Mask refinement for recall.** The authors argue an asymmetric error tolerance: a missed phrase frame falls back to the carrier accent, while a falsely marked carrier frame is overridden by the surrounding carrier text context and smoothed by iterative bidirectional denoising. They therefore widen the mask in two zero-overhead steps. A dual-tag union (XU) ORs the mask from the carrier-tag forward pass with the mask from a second pass in which the language tag is swapped to the phrase language, since swapping the tag shifts the alignment track at boundaries. Margin dilation then applies a graded symmetric max-filter of radius k(k+1)/2 frames; k = 4 (a ±10-frame window) with the union is the final "M4+XU" configuration.

**Language-Contrastive Guidance (LCG).** Standard CFG combines the carrier-conditioned logit c_src and the unconditional logit u as c_src + γ(c_src − u). A third, phrase-conditioned logit c_phr is computed by swapping the input language tag to the embedded phrase's language. A naive "Swap" rule substitutes c_phr for c_src inside the mask; the authors show algebraically that Swap equals global CFG plus a masked corrective term (1 + γ)(c_phr − c_src), so its accent-steering strength is rigidly tied to the text guidance scale (equivalent to a steering scale of 3 at γ = 2). LCG replaces the coupled coefficient with an independent scale λ: l_LCG = [c_src + γ(c_src − u)] + m · λ(c_phr − c_src). λ = 0 recovers the unguided baseline, λ = 3 reproduces Swap, and λ = 7 is the chosen operating point.

**Benchmark.** The authors build a 1,200-utterance synthetic code-switching text corpus (generated with an OpenAI GPT model) covering five languages (English, German, French, Japanese, Korean) in twelve directions (six Latin-script/Japanese-Korean pairs in both directions, 100 utterances each), with 3 to 5 dense multi-word embedded phrases per utterance (mean about 3.6 phrases of about 6 words) across 18 domains and 6 registers, and state that they open-source it. Evaluation is region-aware: Qwen3-ForcedAligner segments each output into carrier and embedded regions. Metrics are Mixed Error Rate (MER: WER for Latin scripts, CER for Japanese and Korean phrases, from Whisper-large-v3 with forced language), embedded-phrase language accuracy (LA_e, the share of isolated phrase clips that unconstrained Whisper transcribes in the target script) and language-ID confidence (LID_e, Whisper's language posterior), WavLM-base-plus-sv speaker similarity to the reference (SIM) and between carrier and phrase regions (SIM m↔e), and UTMOS. Human tests on Amazon Mechanical Turk cover the JA→EN and KO→EN directions only: a full-utterance quality MOS (199 workers, 275 valid HITs, a low-quality anchor and an attention-check item) and a phrase-nativeness AB test on isolated English phrase segments (101 workers, 488 pair ratings), restricted to the subset where every compared system reached LA_e = 1.

## Key Results

On the 12-direction benchmark (Table 1, macro-average), LCG at λ = 7 lowers MER from 0.564 to 0.445, raises LA_e from 0.233 to 0.518 and LID_e from 0.247 to 0.588, while UTMOS drops from 3.411 to 3.285, SIM from 0.973 to 0.969 and SIM m↔e from 0.961 to 0.959 (all deltas computed as LCG minus baseline). Every one of the twelve directions improves on MER, LA_e and LID_e, but the size of the gain varies widely. The largest LA_e and LID_e gains are on EN→JA (LA_e 0.000 → 0.580, LID_e 0.043 → 0.900), and the largest MER reduction is on DE→JA (0.746 → 0.460, −0.286), followed by EN→KO (−0.226) and EN→JA (−0.220). The smallest gains are on KO→FR (LA_e +0.042, LID_e +0.046, MER −0.004), where LA_e stays at 0.064. The UTMOS cost is also uneven: −0.66 on EN→KO (4.37 → 3.71) and −0.39 on EN→JA, but within ±0.13 on the other ten directions, with small rises on JA→EN and KO→EN.

**Mask source.** Against an offline oracle mask from Qwen3-ForcedAligner at the same λ (Table 2), the self-probed M4+XU mask gives lower MER (0.445 vs 0.472), lower embedded-region MER (0.329 vs 0.365), higher LA_e (0.518 vs 0.459) and higher LID_e (0.588 vs 0.526), at slightly lower UTMOS (3.285 vs 3.333) and SIM (0.969 vs 0.972). The authors read this as evidence that boundary recall matters more than precision for localized steering.

**Mask refinement ablation.** Across six mask configurations at λ = 7 (Table 3), M4+XU has the lowest MER (0.445), the highest LA_e (0.518) and the highest LID_e (0.588, against 0.587 for k = 2 without union), but also the lowest UTMOS of the six (3.285; the raw k = 0 mask gives 3.325). Differences among mask variants are small: the raw argmax mask (k = 0) already reaches MER 0.455, LA_e 0.499 and LID_e 0.568, so most of the gain over the unguided baseline comes from the guidance itself rather than the refinement.

**Guidance strength.** In the λ sweep (Figure 4, values shown only graphically), LA_e and LID_e rise steeply up to λ = 7 and then flatten, while UTMOS declines roughly linearly with λ and drops more sharply beyond 7. Swap (λ = 3) gives a weaker accent effect than LCG at λ = 7.

**Human evaluation.** In the phrase-nativeness AB test (Tables 4 and 6), LCG was preferred over the unguided baseline in 292 of 488 ratings, the baseline in 95, with 101 ties: 75.5% excluding ties (75.7% JA→EN, 75.2% KO→EN; z = 10.01, p < 0.001), or 59.8% including ties. Full-utterance quality MOS was 4.007 ± 0.110 for the baseline, 3.967 ± 0.092 for Swap and 3.931 ± 0.092 for LCG (Table 4), with overlapping 95% confidence intervals in both directions (Table 5). The baseline scores numerically highest in both directions; on JA→EN LCG (3.757) sits above Swap (3.728), and on KO→EN below it (4.101 vs 4.201). The authors interpret the gap between the UTMOS drop and the near-flat human MOS as UTMOS treating code-switched transitions as out-of-distribution.

**Human-authored text (Appendix E, Table 9).** On 200 real Korean-English conversational transcripts (synthesized with the KO→EN reference voice), the ordering Baseline < Swap < LCG holds on LA_e and LID_e in both strata (overall LA_e 0.206 → 0.360, LID_e 0.149 → 0.268), with UTMOS and SIM flat. The gain is larger for long phrases (≥4 words; embedded MER 0.284 → 0.232) than for single-word insertions, where embedded MER is slightly worse with LCG (0.898 → 0.943) and the LA_e confidence intervals of the three systems overlap. One single-word item was excluded because its baseline output sent the ASR into a repetition loop; the authors note the exclusion favours the baseline.

**External reference (Appendix F, Table 10).** MOSS-TTS-v1.5 (8B), run with zero-shot cloning from the same reference voices and a single utterance-level language parameter, scores MER 0.584, LA_e 0.280 and LID_e 0.302 overall, close to the unguided OmniVoice baseline and below LCG. It is strong on the four English-Japanese/Korean directions (LA_e 0.59 to 0.88; 0.884 vs LCG's 0.887 on EN→KO) but below 0.21 on the eight German- or French-involving directions. The authors present this as a cross-backbone reference point, not a controlled comparison.

**Cost (Appendix C, Table 7).** Because LCG needs attention weights, it runs on an eager attention backend; on an NVIDIA B200 over 50 utterances of about 38 s, RTF rises from 0.024 (baseline, SDPA) to 0.053 (LCG, eager), a 2.26× slowdown that the authors split into 1.61× from the eager backend and 1.40× from the third guidance branch.

## Novelty Assessment

The genuinely new pieces are an inference-time procedure: a frame-gated contrastive guidance term between two language conditions, with its own scale decoupled from text CFG, plus phrase masks obtained by probing the model's own self-attention during the denoising passes it already runs. The algebraic observation that naive conditional substitution (Swap) is global CFG plus a contrastive term fixed at (1 + γ) is a clean justification for the extra knob. Spatially masked and composed guidance is established in text-to-image diffusion, and selective or token-restricted CFG has appeared in TTS (Selective CFG), so the contribution is the transfer to frame-level accent control in a discrete diffusion TTS model, with an attention-derived mask instead of an external aligner. The evidence is entirely on one backbone; the authors do not test other diffusion TTS models or autoregressive models. Baselines are the backbone's own unguided and Swap variants, with one external system offered only as a reference point. The benchmark is synthetic text generated by an LLM, with a single reference voice per direction, and human tests cover only the two directions into English, so the strongest objective gains (English carrier into Japanese or Korean) are not checked by listeners. The open-sourced 12-direction phrasal code-switching benchmark and region-aware metric setup are a secondary contribution.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper shows that accent leakage in code-switching TTS can be reduced substantially at inference time in a frozen multilingual discrete diffusion model, by contrasting two language conditions only on the inserted phrase, with no retraining. It also provides a concrete case where self-attention in a non-autoregressive TTS model is a usable alignment signal, and where an automatic MOS predictor penalizes an output change that human raters do not clearly detect.

## Claims

- **supports:** Language conditioning in multilingual TTS can be applied per region at inference time, steering only a code-switched phrase toward its own language without retraining and with little cost to speaker identity.
  > *Evidence:* Gating a contrastive term between phrase-language and carrier-language logits to the phrase frames of a frozen OmniVoice raises embedded-phrase language accuracy from 0.233 to 0.518 and LID confidence from 0.247 to 0.588 across 12 directions, while speaker similarity moves from 0.973 to 0.969; listeners prefer the guided phrase in 75.5% of non-tie AB ratings. *(§3.4, §5.1, §5.4, Table 1, Table 6)*
- **refines:** Substituting a different condition inside a region during classifier-free guidance is equivalent to adding a contrastive correction whose strength is tied to the global guidance scale, so independent control requires a separate scale.
  > *Evidence:* The Swap rule expands to global CFG plus m(1 + γ)(c_phr − c_src); at γ = 2 this fixes the contrastive scale at 3, and raising an independent λ to 7 increases phrase nativeness further before saturating. *(§3.4, §5.3, Figure 4)*
- **supports:** Self-attention inside a non-autoregressive speech generation model can provide text-to-frame alignment good enough to localize sub-utterance control, and for steering tasks recall-oriented masks can beat a precise external aligner.
  > *Evidence:* Argmax attention from decoder layers 8 and 12, widened by a dual-tag union and ±10-frame dilation, gives MER 0.445 and LA_e 0.518 versus 0.472 and 0.459 for an offline forced-aligner mask at the same guidance scale. *(§3.2, §3.3, §5.2, Table 2, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Automatic naturalness predictors can penalize code-switched speech more than human listeners do, so they are an unreliable sole gate for code-switching TTS quality.
  > *Evidence:* UTMOS falls from 3.411 to 3.285 overall (by 0.66 on EN→KO), whereas full-utterance human MOS on JA→EN and KO→EN moves from 4.007 to 3.931 with overlapping 95% confidence intervals. *(§5.4, Appendix A.2, Table 1, Table 5)*
- **complicates:** Inference-time accent steering in code-switching TTS gains far less on short single-word insertions and on some language pairs than on dense multi-word phrases.
  > *Evidence:* On human-authored Korean-English text, embedded-phrase MER improves for ≥4-word insertions (0.284 → 0.232) but worsens slightly for single words (0.898 → 0.943); on the synthetic benchmark the KO→FR direction gains only +0.042 LA_e. *(Appendix E, Table 9, Table 1)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The method depends on the internals of one discrete diffusion backbone (attention access, three-branch guidance, language tags). The authors state that whether masked logit steering transfers to autoregressive TTS, where errors accumulate, is unverified, and no second diffusion backbone is tested. It also needs the character span of the embedded phrase as input.

Evaluation relies on LLM-generated code-switching text with one fixed reference voice per direction; the human-authored check in Appendix E covers only Korean-English with one voice, and single-word insertions (the common case in natural code-switching) show small and noisy gains. Human tests are limited to the two directions into English, rated by crowdworkers in English-speaking regions, so nativeness for Japanese, Korean, German and French phrases rests on Whisper-based proxies. The phrase-nativeness AB test is run only on the subset where all systems produced the correct script, which isolates accent but excludes the cases where the systems differ most.

The λ = 7 operating point is chosen on the same benchmark used for reporting, and the UTMOS cost is concentrated in the English-carrier directions with the largest gains. LCG requires an eager attention backend, which more than doubles synthesis time relative to the SDPA baseline in the authors' measurement. The multilingual coverage comes from the frozen backbone, not from any training in this paper, so this page does not tag the method as multilingual TTS. The authors note that fully native rendering of inserted phrases is a design target rather than a universal ideal, and flag the risk of stereotyped accent caricatures at high λ.
