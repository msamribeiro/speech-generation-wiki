> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Hamid Soltani et al.** (Luqia Technologies) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10765) · Demo: ? · Code: ✓
>
> Builds two French versions of Full-Duplex-Bench v1.0 from real conversations (Canadian French telephone calls and European French Wizard-of-Oz dialogues). It uses them to show that voice-activity timing metrics change little across evaluation languages, while the LLM-judged interruption rating collapses under language mismatch.

## Problem

Full-duplex benchmarks such as Full-Duplex-Bench (FDB) measure whether a spoken dialogue model stays silent during user pauses, backchannels, takes the turn smoothly, and handles interruptions. These benchmarks are English-only, and some of their tasks use synthetic speech. Two questions were open. First, no French full-duplex benchmark existed, although French has its own interactional cues (*euh*, *ben*, *ouais*, *d'accord*). Second, nobody had checked which FDB metrics stay valid when a model is evaluated in a language other than its own. A related gap is the lack of a human reference: FDB metrics define "better" as moving a score in one direction, with no measurement of where real human conversations fall.

## Method

The paper adapts the four core FDB v1.0 tasks (pause handling, backchanneling, smooth turn-taking, user interruption) and keeps the FDB v1.0 metrics: takeover rate (TOR), backchannel frequency, Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) of backchannel timing against ground truth, turn-taking and interruption latency, and an LLM-judged 0-5 interruption response rating (§2.1, §2.2).

Two benchmarks are built from two-channel human conversations (§2.3, §2.4):

- **CALLFC-FDB** comes from CALLFRIEND French Canadian (26 h of informal 1996 telephone calls). The manual transcripts lack speaker assignment and keep only the dominant speaker in overlaps. The authors transcribe each channel with Whisper large-v3, align it with the Montreal Forced Aligner, and merge it with the manual transcript by longest-common-subsequence alignment. This recovers 44,165 overlapped words (about 11% of a 386K-word transcript). Turns are chunks with gaps of at most 2.0 s. Because neither speaker is a system, each conversation is processed twice with the "user" and "system" roles swapped.
- **MEDIA-FDB** comes from MEDIA, a task-oriented French hotel-reservation corpus collected in a Wizard-of-Oz setup. Its manual transcripts already carry speaker and overlap annotations, and MFA supplies word timings. It has no backchanneling task because too few valid system backchannels exist.

Event extraction follows FDB v1.0 rules: pauses longer than 0.7 s inside a turn, isolated backchannel words, inter-turn gaps under 1.0 s, and user onsets before the system turn ends (§2.4.3). Hard filters and a quality score then rank the candidates, with a cap of 150 samples per task. The final sets are CALLFC-FDB with 150/37/150/150 and MEDIA-FDB with 150/–/96/38 samples for pause handling, backchanneling, smooth turn-taking and user interruption (§2.4.4, Table 1).

The evaluated systems are two English full-duplex speech-to-speech models, Freeze-Omni and PersonaPlex-7B-v1. Two Pipecat cascades are built with matched components: Vosk STT, qwen3:8b via Ollama, and Piper TTS, each in an English and a French configuration. Human-human responses from the source conversations serve as a natural reference (§3). The FDB pipeline's ASR is switched from Parakeet-TDT-0.6b-v2 to the multilingual v3 so it can transcribe French. The interruption rating uses llama3.3:70b as the judge, with the official FDB prompt and rubric.

## Key Results

Table 2 reports all systems on English FDB, CALLFC-FDB and MEDIA-FDB. Appendix Table 3 gives 95% confidence intervals.

- **Pause handling is language-insensitive for the speech-to-speech models.** Freeze-Omni scores TOR 0.530 on English FDB and 0.540 on both French sets. PersonaPlex moves in both directions: 0.300 on English, 0.447 on CALLFC-FDB, 0.227 on MEDIA-FDB.
- **Backchanneling depends on the model, not the language.** Among the evaluated models, PersonaPlex has the highest backchannel frequency and the lowest JSD on both English FDB (0.096, 0.756) and CALLFC-FDB (0.110, 0.818). Every cascade has JSD of 0.912 or higher. Human-human responses on CALLFC-FDB reach JSD 0.586 and frequency 0.114.
- **Turn-taking shows a partial language-match effect.** On English FDB the English cascade has a higher smooth-turn-taking TOR than the French one (0.529 vs 0.193). On CALLFC-FDB the order reverses (0.147 vs 0.307). On MEDIA-FDB the English cascade still scores slightly higher (0.500 vs 0.438), and the confidence intervals overlap. PersonaPlex drops from 0.941 on English to 0.787 and 0.802 on the French sets. Freeze-Omni stays near 0.32 to 0.41.
- **The content rating is the one strongly language-dependent metric.** The French cascade scores 0.070 on English FDB, while the English cascade scores 3.211. The English models score 0.145 to 0.417 on the French sets, against 3.354 (Freeze-Omni) and 4.702 (PersonaPlex) on English FDB. The French cascade reaches 1.385 on CALLFC-FDB and 3.471 on MEDIA-FDB.
- **Cascade latency comes from the LLM, not the benchmark.** Both cascades take about 7 to 8 s to take the turn smoothly on every dataset, and the logs place most of the delay in LLM generation. The English cascade has the lower smooth-turn-taking latency on all three datasets. For interruption latency it is lower on English FDB and MEDIA-FDB but not on CALLFC-FDB (5.300 s vs 5.230 s). Vosk WER on the pause-handling subsets is 21.7% for French (MEDIA-FDB) and 26.7% for English (English FDB).
- **Humans do not optimize FDB metrics.** On both French sets, human-human responses show strong turn-taking and interruption TOR (0.773 and 0.990 for turn-taking; 0.860 and 0.921 for interruption). Their interruption latency (1.585 s and 2.153 s) is slower than Freeze-Omni (1.107 s, 1.023 s) and PersonaPlex (0.347 s, 0.596 s). On MEDIA-FDB, the French cascade's rating (3.471) is above the human reference (2.829), although the confidence intervals overlap ([3.06, 3.88] vs [2.23, 3.42]). On CALLFC-FDB, the human pause-handling TOR (0.193) is higher than the French cascade's (0.127).

Several comparisons are weak because of small samples and wide intervals. MEDIA-FDB interruption has 38 samples, and Freeze-Omni's rating interval there is [-0.05, 0.89]. CALLFC-FDB backchanneling has 37 samples. The comparisons also cross corpora that differ in domain and formality, not only in language.

## Novelty Assessment

The benchmark construction is careful adaptation work rather than a new evaluation method. It reuses the FDB v1.0 tasks, metrics, extraction rules and scoring code. The genuinely new parts are (1) the first French full-duplex test sets drawn from real two-channel conversations, including a transcript-merging step that recovers overlapped speech in CALLFRIEND; (2) a language-mismatch experiment that separates voice-activity metrics from content metrics; and (3) human-human reference values for FDB metrics. The third finding is useful for interpreting FDB numbers in any language, beyond the French setting. The system pool is small (two English speech-to-speech models and two simple cascades), and no native French full-duplex speech-to-speech model is evaluated. This limits how far the language-versus-architecture conclusions generalize.

## Field Significance

Low — The paper provides French full-duplex test sets compatible with Full-Duplex-Bench v1.0. It also gives evidence that FDB's timing metrics can be read across evaluation languages, while its LLM-judged content rating cannot. Its human-human reference values show that a model which beats humans on an FDB score is not therefore more natural, a caution for anyone ranking full-duplex systems by these metrics. The authors state that the paper is accepted to EMNLP 2026 (Industry Track); this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** Voice-activity-based full-duplex timing metrics, such as takeover rate during user pauses, stay largely stable when a model is evaluated in a language it was not built for.
  > *Evidence:* Freeze-Omni, an English model, scores pause-handling TOR 0.530 on English FDB and 0.540 on both French benchmarks. Its smooth-turn-taking TOR stays between 0.319 and 0.406 across the three datasets. *(§4, Table 2, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** LLM-judged content scores for full-duplex interruption handling mix language competence with interaction competence, so they are not comparable across evaluation languages.
  > *Evidence:* The French cascade scores 0.070 on English FDB, while the matched English cascade scores 3.211. English speech-to-speech models score 0.145 to 0.417 on the French benchmarks, against 3.354 and 4.702 on English FDB. Their interruption TOR remains between 0.593 and 0.868 on the French sets. *(§2.2, §4, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** Moving full-duplex benchmark metrics in their "better" direction does not necessarily make behaviour closer to natural human conversation.
  > *Evidence:* Human-human responses on CALLFC-FDB and MEDIA-FDB have slower interruption latency (1.585 s, 2.153 s) than Freeze-Omni and PersonaPlex. On MEDIA-FDB they receive a lower interruption rating (2.829) than the French cascade (3.471), although the 95% intervals overlap. *(§4, Table 2, Table 3)*
- **refines:** Backchannel timing quality in full-duplex dialogue depends more on model architecture than on whether model and evaluation language match.
  > *Evidence:* The English full-duplex model PersonaPlex has the highest backchannel frequency and lowest JSD among evaluated models on both English FDB (0.096, 0.756) and Canadian French CALLFC-FDB (0.110, 0.818). Both STT-LLM-TTS cascades have JSD of 0.912 or higher on both datasets, including the language-matched French cascade. *(§4, Table 2)*
- **complicates:** The benefit of language matching for turn-taking decisions in full-duplex systems varies across corpora.
  > *Evidence:* The language-matched cascade has the higher smooth-turn-taking TOR on English FDB (0.529 vs 0.193) and CALLFC-FDB (0.307 vs 0.147). On MEDIA-FDB, the English cascade scores 0.500 against 0.438 for the French cascade, with overlapping intervals ([0.40, 0.60] vs [0.34, 0.54]). *(§4, Table 2, Table 3)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The language comparison is confounded with corpus, domain and formality. English FDB, informal CALLFRIEND telephone calls and task-oriented MEDIA Wizard-of-Oz dialogues differ in more than language. No native French full-duplex speech-to-speech model is evaluated, so the language effect cannot be cleanly separated from architecture.

- Only FDB v1.0 tasks are covered. Overlap handling (FDB v1.5) and multi-turn examiner dialogue (FDB v2) remain untested in French (§Limitations).
- Several subsets are small: 37 backchanneling samples in CALLFC-FDB and 38 interruption samples in MEDIA-FDB. Many differences in Table 3 fall within overlapping confidence intervals. MEDIA-FDB has no backchanneling task.
- The evaluation pipeline departs from the original FDB setup: Parakeet-TDT v3 replaces v2 for ASR, and llama3.3:70b is the rating judge. Absolute English FDB ratings may therefore not match published FDB v1.0 numbers exactly.
- CALLFC-FDB imposes user and system roles on symmetric human-human calls, using heuristic turn and sentence boundaries (2.0 s and 0.2 s gaps). The human-human reference is therefore a reconstruction from natural speech, not a measurement of how people respond to a dialogue system.
- No human listeners judge naturalness or satisfaction. The authors note that FDB metrics do not capture these properties and should be read alongside human-centered evaluation.
- The benchmark audio is not redistributed. Users must license CALLFRIEND and MEDIA and regenerate the samples with the released preparation scripts.
