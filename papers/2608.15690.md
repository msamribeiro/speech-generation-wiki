> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Ivan Mikheev et al.** (Kandinsky Lab) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15690) · Demo: ? · Code: ?
>
> Turns a pretrained text-to-audio-video (T2AV) diffusion transformer into a reference-based voice cloner by prepending the reference clip's VAE latents to the audio stream and adding one zero-initialised FiLM layer driven by a frozen speaker embedding, then fine-tuning in a single short stage.

## Problem

Text-to-audio-video models generate a clip and its soundtrack from a prompt, but give no control over whose voice speaks: a voice description in the prompt yields an arbitrary sample from the training distribution. Dedicated voice-cloning TTS systems copy a timbre from a short reference, but they produce audio only and usually rely on a purpose-built speaker branch. Adding cloning to an existing T2AV foundation model otherwise means retraining from scratch or invasive architectural changes that risk damaging the learned audio-visual prior.

## Method

The backbone is an in-house asymmetric audio-video diffusion transformer (AV-DiT) with 32 fused decoder blocks, a video stream of width 1792 and an audio stream of width 896, joined by cross-modal attention. Each stream follows the CrossDiT design of Kandinsky 5.0. Audio latents come from an off-the-shelf MMAudio audio VAE at 44.1 kHz and video latents from the HunyuanVideo VAE; a shared text condition feeds both streams (§2.1, §A.1). Two checkpoints are used: K5 (5B parameters) and K5-LITE (about 3B parameters, of which the audio stream is about 0.6B). After fine-tuning they are called K6A 5B (full audio-video inference) and K6AV LITE (the 3B checkpoint run audio-only).

Two reference signals are added to the audio path (§2.2):

- **Prepended reference latents.** A 1.2 to 4 s reference clip is encoded with the same audio VAE and concatenated in front of the target audio latents. Target tokens reach it through the existing self-attention, so no new attention layers are needed. During inference the reference segment is held fixed and only the target segment is denoised.
- **Speaker FiLM.** A 1024-dim embedding from a frozen Qwen3-TTS speaker encoder passes through one new linear layer that outputs FiLM scale and shift for each target audio token (reference tokens are not modulated). The layer's weights and bias start at zero, so the augmented model initially reproduces the base model exactly.

Training drops the text condition and the reference signal (latents and embedding jointly) independently with probability 0.1, which supports a split classifier-free guidance with separate text weight (w_t = 5) and reference weight (w_r = 4) (§2.3, §A.3). The whole audio backbone is fine-tuned with the new layer in one voice-aware stage (AdamW, base LR 1e-5, 5x for the FiLM layer, 8000 warmup steps). Training references are drawn from later speech segments of the same clip with a 0.5 s buffer, and 10% of steps each run audio-only or video-only losses to keep joint behaviour (§2.4, §A.2). The paper names the process as diffusion with noise-prediction CFG notation and does not state a flow-matching objective.

For audio-only inference, the video sub-block of every fused block and the video VAE decoder are skipped without weight changes. This cuts each step from a 3.18B joint pass to about 0.58B, which the paper reports as a ~30x speed-up (§3.5, §B). Output length is set by a word-count heuristic (words / 2.6 + 1.3 s, clamped to 4 to 12 s), since the model has no duration conditioning (§3.1).

## Key Results

**Voice cloning on a VCTK benchmark** (674 samples, 30 native-English speakers, unique 5 to 16 word texts, all audio resampled to 16 kHz; Table 1). SECS is reported with ECAPA-TDNN (E), WavLM-SV (W) and Resemblyzer (R), against the single reference and against a four-clip speaker centroid.

| System | E ref | W ref | R ref | E cen | W cen | R cen | WER% | CER% | WER0% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K6A 5B | 0.766 | 0.944 | 0.866 | 0.770 | 0.951 | 0.878 | 5.76 | 5.21 | 86.6 |
| Qwen3-TTS 0.6B | 0.678 | 0.936 | 0.840 | 0.711 | 0.946 | 0.864 | 0.81 | 0.36 | 95.7 |
| Qwen3-TTS 1.7B | 0.674 | 0.938 | 0.839 | 0.709 | 0.951 | 0.864 | 0.74 | 0.17 | 95.3 |
| IndexTTS2 | 0.695 | 0.885 | 0.854 | 0.693 | 0.878 | 0.867 | 0.96 | 0.22 | 93.2 |
| XTTS-v2 | 0.575 | 0.923 | 0.816 | 0.611 | 0.940 | 0.841 | 1.14 | 0.32 | 93.2 |
| K6AV LITE | 0.640 | 0.847 | 0.826 | 0.630 | 0.846 | 0.843 | 5.08 | 3.38 | 69.4 |
| NAVA | 0.624 | 0.852 | 0.759 | 0.625 | 0.850 | 0.780 | 5.82 | 3.21 | 69.1 |

- K6A 5B has the highest SECS in five of six columns and ties Qwen3-TTS 1.7B on WavLM vs. centroid (0.951 each). The largest margin is on ECAPA-TDNN vs. reference (0.766 vs. 0.695 for IndexTTS2). On WavLM the lead over Qwen3-TTS is small (0.944 vs. 0.938 vs. reference).
- Averaged over the three encoders, the margin over Qwen3-TTS 0.6B is 0.041; the paper reports a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test with p < 10^-89 against each competitor (§3.2). The text does not say which per-sample quantity enters the test (the three-encoder average is implied) or whether any correction for multiple comparisons is applied.
- Intelligibility goes the other way. K6A 5B's WER (5.76%) and CER (5.21%) are several times those of Qwen3-TTS, IndexTTS2 and XTTS-v2 (0.74 to 1.14% WER), and its zero-error rate is 86.6% against 93.2 to 95.7%. The authors attribute this to occasional hallucinated words from the T2AV prior, and argue the TTS baselines re-render the prompt in a cleaner voice further from the reference.
- Against the only other audio-video model, NAVA, K6AV LITE is higher on ECAPA and Resemblyzer SECS but lower on both WavLM columns (0.847 vs. 0.852, 0.846 vs. 0.850). It has lower WER (5.08 vs. 5.82) but higher CER (3.38 vs. 3.21).
- K6AV LITE loses about 0.09 SECS relative to K6A 5B (averaged over the three vs. reference columns).

**No regression in reference-free generation** (Table 2, 400 prompts, 30 speakers, fixed seeds). Relative change of the fine-tuned model against the base model, where negative is better for ↓ metrics and positive is better for ↑ metrics: FAD vs. real speech -30.6%, WER -46.0%, CER -47.0%, WER0 +113%, CLAP +29.4%, UTMOS +0.6%. Absolute values are not reported.

**Human side-by-side study** (Table 3, 100 held-out text-to-audio prompts, no reference). Fine-tuned win rates are 52% (prompt following), 57% (technical quality) and 53% (speech/aesthetic quality). Most pairs are judged ties ("both good" 75 to 78%). Fine-tuned-only wins outnumber base-only wins on every axis (14 vs. 9, 23 vs. 8, 17 vs. 11). Annotator count and background are not described, and no significance test is given.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is mainly engineering integration. Each piece is an established technique: in-context reference prefixes, FiLM speaker modulation, zero-initialised adapter layers, and multi-condition classifier-free guidance. What is new is the combination applied to an existing T2AV backbone with a minimal parameter change and a single fine-tuning stage, plus the observation that an asymmetric AV-DiT can be run audio-only from the same checkpoint. NAVA already offers in-context timbre control in a joint audio-video diffusion model, so reference-conditioned voice in T2AV is not a first.

The evaluation compares against strong TTS cloners with careful pre-processing and three speaker encoders. However, its central SECS win comes with a large intelligibility deficit. The FiLM conditioning uses the Qwen3-TTS speaker encoder, and Qwen3-TTS is also a baseline; the paper does not discuss whether this shared encoder interacts with the comparison (none of the three evaluation encoders is the Qwen3-TTS one). No ablation separates the contribution of the prepended latents from the FiLM path.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper shows that a T2AV diffusion model can gain reference-based voice cloning through a small, zero-initialised conditioning addition while keeping (and in its measurements improving) reference-free generation. Its results add a clear data point on the trade-off between speaker similarity and intelligibility: the diffusion model copies the reference more closely than autoregressive and hybrid TTS cloners but makes far more word errors.

## Claims

- **supports:** A pretrained generative audio model can gain reference-based voice cloning from a small zero-initialised conditioning layer plus in-context reference latents, without new attention modules or retraining from scratch.
  > *Evidence:* Prepending reference VAE latents and adding one zero-initialised FiLM linear layer, fine-tuned in a single stage, gives K6A 5B the highest or tied-highest SECS in all six encoder/target columns on a 674-sample VCTK benchmark, against Qwen3-TTS, IndexTTS2, XTTS-v2 and NAVA. *(§2.2, §3.2, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** Higher speaker similarity in voice cloning can come at a large cost in intelligibility, so speaker-similarity leads should be read together with WER.
  > *Evidence:* K6A 5B leads on SECS but reaches 5.76% WER and 5.21% CER, against 0.74 to 1.14% WER and 0.17 to 0.36% CER for the TTS baselines; its zero-error rate is 86.6% versus 93.2 to 95.7%. *(§3.2, Table 1)*
- **supports:** Zero-initialised conditioning additions let a fine-tuned model keep its unconditioned generation ability.
  > *Evidence:* In the reference-free regime the fine-tuned model shows FAD -30.6%, WER -46.0% and UTMOS +0.6% relative to the base model, and human side-by-side raters prefer it slightly on all three axes (52 to 57% win rate). *(§3.3, §3.4, Table 2, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Dropping one modality stream of a joint audio-video generator at inference speeds up audio generation but lowers voice-cloning fidelity.
  > *Evidence:* The audio-only K6AV LITE runs a ~0.58B pass instead of a 3.18B joint pass (reported ~30x speed-up) and scores about 0.09 lower in SECS than K6A 5B, although it is also a smaller checkpoint, so the two factors are not separated. *(§3.5, §B, Table 1)*
- **refines:** Speaker similarity from diffusion-based cloning grows with reference length up to a plateau, with very short references limited by the speaker encoder.
  > *Evidence:* An 82-run sweep finds SECS rises from about 1 s to a plateau at 4 s, with a sharp Resemblyzer SECS drop below 2 s; cross-lingual prompts lower mean SECS by about 0.05. *(§Appendix C)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] Unseen-speaker status and intelligibility
> The training data is an internal audio-video corpus that the paper does not describe, and it never states that the 30 VCTK benchmark speakers are absent from training or fine-tuning. The results therefore cannot be read as zero-shot cloning of unseen speakers. Intelligibility is also far below the TTS baselines (5.76% vs. under 1.2% WER).

- **Confounded audio-only comparison.** K6AV LITE differs from K6A 5B in both checkpoint size (about 3B vs. 5B) and inference mode (audio-only). The paper attributes its ~0.09 SECS drop to the missing video stream acting as a regulariser, but no same-checkpoint comparison isolates this. The 3.18B joint pass used for the ~30x figure refers to the LITE checkpoint, and no wall-clock timings are reported.
- **Model size reporting.** The introduction gives K5-LITE as 0.6B parameters, while §2.1 and §A.1 give about 3B total with a 0.6B audio stream.
- **Significance testing.** The Wilcoxon result is stated only for the three-encoder SECS average. No test is reported for WER/CER, for the Table 2 changes, or for the side-by-side study.
- **Limited evaluation scope.** The benchmark is English-only studio speech from one corpus, there is no subjective speaker-similarity or naturalness test for the cloning task, and the audio-visual quality of cloned outputs (lip sync, video fidelity) is not evaluated. The side-by-side study covers only reference-free generation, and its annotators are not described.
- **No component ablation** separates the prepended latents from the FiLM path, and duration is set by a heuristic that may contribute to WER errors on short prompts.
- Code, weights and demo availability are not stated.
