> **Haohe Liu et al.** (University of Surrey / Shanghai Jiao Tong University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00233) · [→ Peer-reviewed version](https://doi.org/10.1109/jstsp.2024.3506286) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> SemantiCodec compresses speech, music and general sound into 25 to 100 tokens per second (0.31 to 1.40 kbps) by pairing a frozen k-means codebook over self-supervised AudioMAE features with a small learned acoustic codebook, and reconstructs audio with a latent diffusion decoder.

*A [peer-reviewed version](https://doi.org/10.1109/jstsp.2024.3506286) appeared in the IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Signal Processing (2024). This page, including its metrics and claims, summarizes the arXiv preprint.*

## Problem

Neural audio codecs used as tokenizers for audio language models run at high token rates: a 6 kbps Descript codec (DAC) emits 600 tokens per second from six codebooks. Autoregressive models then face long sequences, high training cost and weak use of long context. Low-bitrate settings exist (EnCodec at 1.5 kbps), but they introduce strong artefacts. The authors also argue that codec tokens carry little semantic information. Probing DAC 6 kbps latents on HEAR classification tasks gives about 33% average accuracy, against about 61% for an unfine-tuned AudioMAE encoder. Using only the first VQ layer, which AR models such as VALL-E typically predict, drops DAC to 25% *(§I)*.

## Method

SemantiCodec works on 16 kHz mel-spectrograms and has two quantization layers that are concatenated, not residual.

- **Semantic layer.** A frozen AudioMAE (self-supervised masked autoencoder pretrained on AudioSet, embedding size 768) encodes the mel-spectrogram into patch embeddings. Adjacent patches are stacked by a factor K ∈ {1, 2, 4}, which sets the token rate. Each stacked vector is quantized to its nearest centroid in a frozen codebook built offline by k-means. The k-means models are trained separately on speech, music and general sound and merged (an ensemble idea borrowed from HuBERT), with twice as many centroids for general sound. Merged codebooks of 4096, 8192, 16384 and 32768 entries are sampled at random per training batch, so one model supports several vocabulary sizes and bitrates *(§III-B, §IV-D)*.
- **Acoustic layer.** A bidirectional LSTM takes the unquantized AudioMAE features and their semantic quantization together, and outputs an acoustic feature. A learnable EMA-updated VQ codebook (8192 entries by default) quantizes it, trained with a commitment loss. This layer compensates for detail lost in semantic quantization *(§III-C)*.
- **Decoder.** The concatenated semantic and acoustic features condition a latent diffusion model through cross-attention, with fixed positional embeddings added. The decoder follows AudioLDM 2: a T-UNet reduced to 75M parameters (from 346M) operating in a frozen VAE latent space, followed by a frozen HiFi-GAN-style mel vocoder. Training uses velocity prediction and a zero-terminal-SNR cosine schedule. Inference uses DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance *(§III-D, §IV-D)*.

![Fig. 2. SemantiCodec architecture. For an input audio clip, quantized semantic representation E s is obtained via a pre-computed codebook using k-means clustering on the AudioMAE embeddings. Then Y and E s are concatenated and fed to a residual encoder to complement acoustic details, which is discretized to E a by a vector quantization module. SemantiCodec encoder output E is obtained by concatenating E s and E a. A latent diffusion model is trained to generate the original audio clip conditioned on E. The snowflake and fire symbols denote frozen and learnable parameters, respectively.](assets/2405.00233/figure-2.png)

Only the acoustic encoder, acoustic codebook and diffusion model are trained, jointly, with the sum of the diffusion loss and the commitment loss. AudioMAE, the k-means centroids, the VAE and the vocoder stay frozen. Each of the three token-rate variants (25, 50, 100 tokens/s; half semantic, half acoustic) trains for 500k steps on two A100 GPUs over about 10k hours of GigaSpeech plus music and general-sound corpora. Audio is processed in 10.24 s windows; longer inputs are chunked and decoded with 6.25% overlap-add *(§III-E, §IV-A, §IV-D)*.

## Key Results

**Reconstruction (Table II).** Evaluation covers 300 LibriTTS utterances, 500 AudioSet clips and MUSDB18 segments, scored with MEL and STFT distances, ViSQOL, and Whisper-large-v3 WER on speech. Against DAC retrained on the same data at matched bitrates, SemantiCodec is better on every reported column:

| System | kbps | Tokens/s | Avg ViSQOL ↑ | Speech WER ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SemantiCodec | 0.36 | 25 | 3.17 | 19.6 |
| DAC (retrained) | 0.47 | 47 | 2.39 | 28.2 |
| SemantiCodec | 0.70 | 50 | 3.55 | 5.1 |
| DAC (retrained) | 0.78 | 78 | 2.82 | 11.6 |
| SemantiCodec | 1.40 | 100 | 3.81 | 3.4 |
| DAC (retrained) | 1.41 | 141 | 3.13 | 5.0 |
| EnCodec | 1.50 | 150 | 3.33 | 5.0 |
| HiFi-Codec | 2.00 | 200 | 3.57 | 3.6 |
| EnCodec | 3.00 | 300 | 3.82 | 3.7 |
| DAC (public) | 6.00 | 600 | 4.54 | 3.0 |

Comparisons with higher-bitrate codecs are closer than the abstract suggests. The 1.40 kbps model ties 3.0 kbps EnCodec on average ViSQOL (3.81 vs. 3.82). The 0.70 kbps model roughly matches 2.0 kbps HiFi-Codec on ViSQOL (3.55 vs. 3.57) but has higher WER (5.1 vs. 3.6). Against 1.5 kbps EnCodec it wins on ViSQOL (3.55 vs. 3.33) but not on WER (5.1 vs. 5.0). The public 6 kbps DAC remains clearly ahead at 600 tokens/s. The 25 tokens/s variant keeps acceptable ViSQOL but its WER of 19.6 shows speech intelligibility breaking down. Feeding unquantized AudioMAE features to the decoder gives an upper bound of 4.61 average ViSQOL and 2.7 WER *(§V-A, Table II)*.

**Listening test (Figure 3).** In a MUSHRA test on 10% of the evaluation set, with at least 10 raters per set and post-screening, SemantiCodec at 0.35 kbps scores 55.7. EnCodec at 1.5 kbps scores 47.8 and the reference 93.9. Only codecs at or below 1.5 kbps are included, so HiFi-Codec and 6 kbps DAC are not rated. The per-domain plots (Figure 4) follow the same trend, and the authors note the clearest advantage on music *(§IV-C3, §V-A)*.

**Semantic richness (Table III).** Clip-level probing with a two-layer MLP on six HEAR tasks gives SemantiCodec 52.5% average accuracy at 1.40 kbps, against 43.5% for DAC at 1.41 kbps, 40.3% for HiFi-Codec at 2.0 kbps and 37.1% for EnCodec at 6.0 kbps. Unquantized AudioMAE scores 60.6%. Using only the first VQ layer, the semantic layer of the 100 tokens/s model (0.70 kbps) scores 53.1%, while the first layer of 6 kbps DAC drops to 24.6%. The advantage is not uniform: on NSynth Pitch, DAC at 1.41 kbps (80.9%) beats SemantiCodec at 1.40 kbps (73.3%) *(§V-B, Table III)*.

**Ablations.** Removing the acoustic VQ layer collapses reconstruction (WER 55.6, average ViSQOL 2.61). Replacing the frozen k-means codebook with a learnable VQ barely changes reconstruction (ViSQOL 3.60 vs. 3.57) but cuts semantic accuracy from 53.1% to 28.5%. Random codebook sizes during training beat a fixed 32768-entry codebook at every stack factor. A larger k-means codebook raises semantic accuracy steadily (49.0% at 4096 to 53.1% at 32768) at a bitrate cost. A unidirectional LSTM raises WER from 4.57 to 4.80. Quality improves markedly above 25 DDIM steps, and a guidance scale near 3.0 works best *(§V-C, Tables IV-VII, Figures 6-7)*.

## Novelty Assessment

The main new element is the split of the token stream into a frozen k-means semantic layer over self-supervised features and a learned acoustic layer, with a generative decoder conditioned on both. The authors state that it is the first neural audio codec with a diffusion-based decoder. Semantic k-means tokens (as in HuBERT-style units) and latent diffusion decoders (AudioLDM 2) existed separately. The contribution is combining them into a codec that is evaluated for both reconstruction and token semantics. The learnable-VQ ablation (Table VII) supports the design choice directly: a jointly trained two-layer VQ does not learn semantic tokens by itself.

The evaluation has weak points. Low-bitrate DAC baselines are retrained by the authors, not released checkpoints. The MUSHRA test excludes the stronger higher-bitrate codecs. "Semantic richness" is measured by probing averaged clip-level features, not by training an audio language model on the tokens. Inference cost of the diffusion decoder is not reported, although Figure 6 shows that at least about 25 DDIM steps are needed for good quality.

## Field Significance

> [!tip]
> High — SemantiCodec shows that a codec can pair a frozen self-supervised semantic codebook with a diffusion decoder to run at 25 to 100 tokens per second over speech, music and general sound. Its ablations show that semantic content in codec tokens comes from the k-means-on-SSL layer and is lost when that layer is learned for reconstruction alone. Its main cost is iterative diffusion decoding, which the paper does not time.

## Claims

- **supports:** Codec tokens derived by clustering self-supervised audio features carry far more semantic information than tokens from reconstruction-trained residual quantizers at equal or higher bitrate.
  > *Evidence:* On six HEAR probing tasks, SemantiCodec reaches 52.5% average accuracy at 1.40 kbps, against 43.5% for DAC at 1.41 kbps and 37.1% for EnCodec at 6.0 kbps; its semantic layer alone scores 53.1%, while the first layer of 6 kbps DAC scores 24.6%. *(§V-B, Table III)*
- **complicates:** A codec trained only for reconstruction does not learn semantically rich tokens on its own, even when it has a dedicated first quantization layer.
  > *Evidence:* Replacing the frozen k-means semantic codebook with a learnable VQ layer leaves reconstruction almost unchanged (ViSQOL 3.60 vs. 3.57, WER 4.57 vs. 5.10) but drops semantic accuracy from 53.1% to 28.5%. *(§V-C3, Table VII)*
- **supports:** A generative diffusion decoder lets an audio codec keep usable perceptual quality at token rates far below those of GAN-trained RVQ codecs.
  > *Evidence:* At 0.35 kbps (25 tokens/s) SemantiCodec scores 55.7 MUSHRA against 47.8 for EnCodec at 1.5 kbps, and at 1.40 kbps (100 tokens/s) it ties 3.0 kbps EnCodec on average ViSQOL (3.81 vs. 3.82). *(§V-A, Table II, Figure 3)*
- **complicates:** Semantic tokens alone are not sufficient for intelligible speech reconstruction; a complementary acoustic stream is required, and intelligibility degrades sharply at the lowest token rates.
  > *Evidence:* Without the acoustic VQ layer, WER rises to 55.6 and average ViSQOL falls to 2.61; with both layers, WER is 19.6 at 25 tokens/s against 3.4 at 100 tokens/s. *(§V-A, Table II)*
- **complicates:** Diffusion-decoded codecs trade token rate for decoding cost, since reconstruction quality depends on the number of iterative sampling steps.
  > *Evidence:* Reconstruction quality is only moderate at about ten DDIM steps and improves markedly above 25 steps, with a guidance scale near 3.0 working best. *(§V-C4, Figure 6)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The diffusion decoder needs tens of DDIM steps per 10.24 s window, but the paper reports no real-time factor or latency, so its suitability for streaming or interactive use cannot be judged from this paper. The encoder is also non-causal (bidirectional LSTM; AudioMAE over full windows).

- All variants run at 16 kHz; higher sampling rates are left for future work *(§IV-A)*.
- Low-bitrate DAC baselines are the authors' retrained models, and MUSHRA covers only codecs at or below 1.5 kbps, so HiFi-Codec and 6 kbps DAC were not rated by listeners.
- Speech evaluation is limited to 300 English LibriTTS utterances; WER relies on Whisper-large-v3.
- Semantic richness is measured by MLP probing of time-averaged features. The paper does not train an audio language model on SemantiCodec tokens.
- A gap remains between quantized (53.1%) and unquantized AudioMAE features (60.6%) in semantic accuracy, which the authors leave open *(§V-B)*.
