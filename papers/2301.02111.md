> VALL-E is the first TTS system to treat speech synthesis as conditional codec language modeling, achieving strong zero-shot speaker generalization by pretraining an autoregressive LM on 60K hours of speech quantized with EnCodec.

## Problem

Prior zero-shot TTS systems relied on speaker encoding or speaker adaptation techniques requiring fine-tuning, pre-designed acoustic features, or complex speaker encoders, and were trained on orders of magnitude less data (hundreds of hours). They struggled to generalize to unseen speakers and could not preserve speaker emotion or acoustic environment. The gap between in-domain and out-of-domain speaker quality was large.

## Method

VALL-E reframes TTS as conditional codec language modeling. Audio is first tokenized using EnCodec, a neural audio codec that encodes waveforms at 75 Hz into a residual vector quantization (RVQ) matrix of 8 codebooks with 1024 entries each (6K bitrate). Given a phoneme sequence and a 3-second enrolled recording from an unseen speaker, VALL-E generates the corresponding acoustic code matrix, which is decoded back to a waveform by the EnCodec decoder.

The architecture is hierarchical and comprises two models:

1. **AR model**: A decoder-only transformer language model that autoregressively generates first-quantizer tokens (c[:,1]) conditioned on the phoneme sequence and the first-layer acoustic tokens of the enrolled recording. Uses sampling-based decoding at inference.
2. **NAR model**: A non-autoregressive transformer that generates tokens for quantizers 2–8, conditioned on the phoneme sequence, the full enrolled recording (all 8 codebooks), and the already-predicted codes from earlier quantizers. Uses greedy decoding. The current stage index is injected via Adaptive Layer Normalization.

Both models share the same transformer architecture: 12 layers, 16 attention heads, embedding dimension 1024, feed-forward dimension 4096, dropout 0.1. Training data is LibriLight (60K hours, ~7000 speakers), with transcriptions generated by a hybrid DNN-HMM ASR model. Models are trained on 16 NVIDIA V100 32GB GPUs, batch size 6K tokens per GPU, 800K steps, AdamW optimizer with warmup to 5e-4.

At inference, the enrolled recording's phoneme transcription is prepended to the target phoneme sequence, and the enrolled acoustic tokens serve as the AR prefix.

## Key Results

On LibriSpeech test-clean (40 unseen speakers, 3-second enrollment):
- WER: VALL-E 5.9 vs YourTTS 7.7 (lower is better)
- SPK-SIM (WavLM-TDNN): VALL-E 0.580 vs YourTTS 0.337 vs GroundTruth 0.754
- SMOS: VALL-E 4.38 ± 0.10 vs YourTTS 3.45 ± 0.09 vs GroundTruth 4.50
- CMOS: VALL-E +0.12 over YourTTS baseline

On VCTK (108 unseen speakers; YourTTS saw 97 of them in training, VALL-E saw none):
- SPK-SIM at 3s: VALL-E 0.382 vs YourTTS 0.357 (despite YourTTS in-domain advantage)
- SMOS: VALL-E 3.81 ± 0.09 vs YourTTS 3.70 ± 0.09
- CMOS: VALL-E +0.23 over YourTTS; VALL-E achieves +0.04 CMOS over ground truth on VCTK

Ablations confirm that both the phoneme prompt (for content control) and acoustic prompt (for speaker identity) in the NAR model are essential. Removing the AR acoustic prompt drops speaker similarity from 0.585 to 0.236.

## Novelty Assessment

VALL-E is a foundational contribution: the first system to apply the GPT-style pretraining-at-scale paradigm to TTS using neural audio codec tokens as intermediate representations. The key insight — treating TTS as codec language modeling rather than mel-spectrogram regression — is architecturally clean and conceptually important. The scale jump (60K hours vs. hundreds of hours) is decisive. The in-context learning framing (3-second enrollment, no fine-tuning) is a qualitative shift from prior speaker adaptation approaches. Secondary novelties are the hierarchical AR+NAR decomposition and the ability to preserve speaker emotion and acoustic environment zero-shot.

## Limitations and Open Questions

- **Robustness**: AR decoding can produce word deletions, insertions, or repetitions due to disordered attention — the same issue seen in transformer-based TTS, later addressed by non-autoregressive or attention-constrained approaches.
- **Data coverage**: LibriLight is audiobook speech; prosodic and speaking-style diversity is limited. Accent speakers (especially VCTK) are underrepresented.
- **Speed**: NAR is fast (O(1) per stage), but the AR model remains sequential.
- **Two-model architecture**: The paper notes that a single universal model predicting all codebooks is a natural future direction.
- **Misuse risk**: Zero-shot voice cloning raises spoofing concerns; the authors propose detection models as a mitigation.
