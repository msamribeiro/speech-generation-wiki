> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Charin Polpanumas** (AWS) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12814) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✓
>
> A dictionary-first Thai grapheme-to-phoneme front end (text normalization, PyThaiNLP segmentation, a 62,112-word IPA lexicon and a cached rule-based fallback) that phonemizes an utterance in 0.15 ms on CPU, shown driving a Thai fine-tune of Kokoro-82M at RTF 0.25.

## Problem

Phoneme-based Thai TTS needs a G2P stage that handles unsegmented script, five lexical tones, discontinuous vowel graphemes, irregular mappings (leading-consonant tone shift, unwritten reduced vowels, false clusters, homographs) and dense non-speakable text such as numbers, abbreviations and code-switched tokens. The paper argues that existing Thai G2P tools each miss at least one requirement: TLTK has no pre-built dictionary and recompiles regexes on every call (about 2 ms per utterance), Epitran drops tones and needs pre-segmented input, and the neural thai-g2p and CharsiuG2P models need external segmentation, lack normalization and decode autoregressively. Grapheme-based Thai TTS models avoid G2P but need 336M to 2B parameters and GPU inference, which the author considers unsuitable for CPU-only, low-latency voice agents with a 500 to 1,000 ms budget for the whole TTS pipeline.

## Method

FastThaiG2P runs four stages in sequence: text normalization, tokenization, dictionary lookup and fallback G2P (§2). The normalizer applies a fixed-order rule cascade covering 15 categories of non-speakable text, including maiyamok repetition, Thai numerals, emails, English brand transliteration, units, symbols, times, phone numbers, alphanumeric IDs and Thai abbreviations. Numbers of up to 6 digits use place-value reading and longer ones are read digit by digit (§2.1). The author positions it as a deterministic safeguard when an upstream LLM in an ASR, LLM, TTS cascade emits unvocalizable text.

Tokenization uses PyThaiNLP's `newmm` maximum-matching segmenter with the IPA dictionary's key list as its custom word list, so every in-vocabulary token maps directly to an IPA entry (§2.2). The 62,112-entry dictionary merges, in priority order, manual overrides, about 13,000 English-Wiktionary-convention entries from the Thai Wiktionary dump, and about 49,000 entries transcribed by Claude Opus 4.6 in batches of 100. Generated entries are rejected when any character falls outside a 38-codepoint phoneme inventory whitelist (§2.3). Before batch generation, the prompt was tuned on 500 random Wiktionary entries, with errors split into non-fatal (tone, vowel length, diphthong marker, coda variant) and fatal (wrong syllable count, initial consonant or vowel quality).

Out-of-vocabulary words go to a rule-based fallback vendored from TLTK: trigram syllable segmentation, then romanization from consonant class, vowel pattern and tone rules, then conversion to the project's IPA convention with Chao tone letters (§2.4). The main engineering change is an unbounded explicit regex cache at the three hot loops of the fallback, plus loading syllable rules at import time. Python's 512-entry LRU regex cache otherwise overflows and forces repeated recompilation.

For TTS, IPA is mapped to the Kokoro-82M phoneme vocabulary (§2.5, Figure 2). Kokoro was pretrained on English and has four intonation markers, so the author repurposes unused token ID 170 as the high-tone marker to keep all five Thai tones distinct, strips IPA-only diacritics and substitutes affricates and the g character. The demonstration model fine-tunes Kokoro-82M (a StyleTTS 2-based model) on Som-TTS, about 20 hours of single-speaker Thai, with the kikiri-tts recipe: stage 1 for text-to-mel alignment and duration prediction, stage 2 for adversarial training with a multi-scale discriminator (§4). A voicepack vector conditions speaker identity, output is 24 kHz, and the checkpoint is exported to ONNX. A post-processing step uses voicing detection to remove noise generated in the BOS and EOS pad windows.

## Key Results

- **G2P latency (§3, Figure 3):** 0.15 ms per utterance on average and 6,600 utterances per second over 27,242 synthetic Thai utterances, single-threaded on CPU with Python 3.11. The paper reports this as up to a 15x reduction from the roughly 2 ms per utterance of the original TLTK path (§1, §2.4). The CPU model is not reported.
- **Where time goes (Figure 3):** fallback G2P takes 58% of wall-clock time although only 0.5% of tokens are OOV; tokenization takes 30%, normalization 12%, and dictionary lookup under 1%.
- **Dictionary quality proxy (§2.3):** the final LLM transcription prompt reaches 78.8% exact match and a 1.8% fatal error rate against 500 Wiktionary entries. No phoneme or word error rate is reported for the assembled dictionary or for the full G2P pipeline on held-out text.
- **TTS demonstration (§4, Figure 4):** the Thai Kokoro-82M runs at RTF 0.25 (4x real time) with ONNX on a single CPU thread, with a 330 MB checkpoint (model, voicepack and config) and a 510-phoneme input limit. Quality is described only as intelligible with recognizable tonal patterns for short to medium utterances; no MOS, WER, CER or speaker similarity is measured (§5).

No experiment compares FastThaiG2P's accuracy or the resulting TTS quality against Epitran, thai-g2p, CharsiuG2P or the grapheme-based Thai TTS models discussed in §1. The only head-to-head number is the latency against TLTK, and it is stated as an approximate figure.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is engineering integration and a released resource. Dictionary lookup over a segmenter, rule-based OOV fallback, and regex caching are standard techniques; the speedup comes from fixing a cache overflow in vendored TLTK code rather than from a new G2P method. The new parts are the assembled 62k-word Thai IPA lexicon (bootstrapped with LLM transcriptions under a whitelist check), the Thai normalization rule set, and the tone-preserving IPA-to-Kokoro mapping. The TTS model is an off-the-shelf architecture fine-tuned with a third-party recipe and serves only as a feasibility demo.

## Field Significance

Low — The paper provides a practical, open-source Thai phonemization front end for compact phoneme-based TTS and documents its latency profile in detail. Its evidence about speech generation is thin: neither the G2P accuracy of the final system nor the quality of the fine-tuned Thai voice is measured, so it mainly serves as tooling for low-resource, CPU-only Thai TTS rather than as evidence about synthesis quality.

## Claims

- **supports:** Dictionary-first phonemization can keep the G2P stage of a phoneme-based TTS front end well below a millisecond per utterance on CPU, a negligible share of a real-time voice-agent latency budget.
  > *Evidence:* Normalization, PyThaiNLP segmentation and hash-table IPA lookup average 0.15 ms per utterance (6,600 utterances per second) over 27,242 synthetic Thai utterances on a single CPU thread, against a stated 500 to 1,000 ms budget for the whole TTS pipeline. *(§3, Figure 3; §1)*
- **complicates:** In dictionary-plus-fallback G2P, the rarely invoked out-of-vocabulary path can dominate runtime, so lexicon coverage alone does not guarantee low latency.
  > *Evidence:* Only 0.5% of tokens are OOV, yet the trigram-based fallback consumes 58% of wall-clock time; caching its regexes and preloading rules cut latency from about 2 ms to 0.15 ms per utterance. *(§2.4; §3, Figure 3)*
- **refines:** LLM-generated pronunciations can bootstrap a large lexicon for a low-resource tonal language when filtered by an inventory check and validated against a human reference, but residual tone and length errors remain.
  > *Evidence:* About 49,000 of 62,112 entries come from Claude Opus 4.6 with a 38-codepoint whitelist filter; the final prompt reaches 78.8% exact match and 1.8% fatal errors on 500 Wiktionary entries, with tone mismatch and vowel length counted as non-fatal errors. *(§2.3)*
- **complicates:** Compact phoneme-based TTS can run several times faster than real time on a single CPU thread, but without listener or intelligibility measurement its usable quality for a new language stays unverified.
  > *Evidence:* The Thai-finetuned Kokoro-82M reaches RTF 0.25 with ONNX on CPU and a 330 MB checkpoint, while quality is described only qualitatively and the paper states that no formal MOS study has been conducted. *(§4, Figure 4; §5)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> No accuracy evaluation of the delivered system: the paper reports no phoneme or word error rate for FastThaiG2P on held-out text, and no MOS, WER or CER for the Thai TTS voice it trains. The only accuracy number validates the LLM transcription prompt on 500 Wiktionary entries.

- The latency benchmark uses synthetically generated utterances whose generation procedure is not described, and the CPU hardware is not reported.
- The 15x speedup over TLTK rests on an approximate 2 ms baseline; no per-component profile of the original TLTK path is shown.
- The dictionary targets standard Central Thai and has gaps in regional vocabulary, recent loanwords, slang and code-switched Thai-English text; the rule-based fallback can mispronounce Pali/Sanskrit compounds and loanwords, and dialectal tone variation is not modeled (§5).
- About 79% of dictionary entries come from LLM transcription, and the 21.2% non-exact rate on the validation set suggests that tone and vowel-length errors may persist in the lexicon; their effect on synthesized speech is not measured.
- The TTS demo is single-speaker and trained on about 20 hours; whether the repurposed high-tone token yields correct tone realization is not tested.
