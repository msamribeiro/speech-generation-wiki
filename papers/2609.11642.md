> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Luca Della Libera et al.** (Concordia University / Mila-Quebec AI Institute / Université Laval) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11642) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> ZipCodec is a fully streaming, single-stream speech codec at 6.25 Hz and 0.80 kbps (160 ms theoretical latency). It distills WavLM layer-6 features through a normalization-free, position-free transformer and a scalar spherical quantizer, and it beats streaming codecs at 12.5 to 80 Hz on most resynthesis and downstream probes.

## Problem

Frame rate sets the token-sequence length a speech language model must handle, so lower frame rates make sequence modeling cheaper. Codecs below 12.5 Hz exist, but each gives up something. U-Codec (5 Hz) is offline, TaDiCodec (6.25 Hz) needs the transcript as side information, and FlexiCodec and DyCAST use variable frame rates, run offline and lose quality as the rate falls. Among streaming codecs that carry both semantic and acoustic information, Mimi at 12.5 Hz was the lowest the authors know of. The paper asks how far a streaming codec's frame rate can be pushed while keeping reconstruction and representation quality.

## Method

ZipCodec redesigns FocalCodec-Stream and keeps its five-part structure: encoder, compressor, quantizer, decompressor and decoder. Every module is causal. A non-causal WavLM teacher supplies the distillation targets.

![Fig. 1. ZipCodec architecture. The encoder extracts features containing both acoustic and semantic information. These features are then mapped to a low-dimensional space by the compressor, quantized, and projected back by the decompressor. The decoder resynthesizes the waveform from these features. All these modules are causal, while a non-causal teacher is used for distillation to align causal features with their non-causal counterparts.](assets/2609.11642/figure-1.png)

- **Encoder.** A causal 80-bin log-mel frontend (25 ms Hann window, 10 ms hop, 100 Hz) replaces FocalCodec-Stream's learned convolutional encoder.
- **Compressor.** A temporal patching layer groups 16 log-mel frames into one 160 ms vector, which brings the rate from 100 Hz to 6.25 Hz. A causal ErfFormer backbone then processes the sequence. ErfFormer uses LLaMA-style blocks (grouped-query attention, SiLU-gated feed-forward) with two changes. It replaces RMSNorm with the DynamicErf activation, and it drops positional encodings entirely, so streams can run past the training context length. The backbone has 6 blocks, model dimension 2048, feed-forward dimension 8192, 16 query heads and 4 key-value heads.
- **Quantizer.** Scalar spherical quantization (SSQ, taken from DyCAST) projects each frame to 64 dimensions, normalizes it to the unit hypersphere and quantizes each dimension to one of 4 levels. That gives 64 two-bit symbols per frame, 0.80 kbps at 6.25 Hz, and an implicit 4^64 joint codebook with no explicit codebook table.
- **Decompressor.** A mirrored ErfFormer plus temporal unpatching expands each 6.25 Hz frame into 8 1024-dimensional WavLM layer-6 vectors at 50 Hz.
- **Decoder.** A streaming Vocos (20 ConvNeXt blocks, iSTFT head) keeps its overlap-add state across steps. The latency-aware part is that all 8 WavLM vectors for a 160 ms step arrive together. The decoder can therefore use left and right convolutional padding inside that window without adding latency beyond the bottleneck's 160 ms.

**Training.** FocalCodec-Stream used four distillation stages. ZipCodec trains encoder, compressor, quantizer and decompressor jointly in one stage, using an L2 loss to continuous WavLM layer-6 features plus an entropy loss for level usage. Training runs for 4M steps on about 94,000 hours of Libri-Light, VoxPopuli and GigaSpeech, in 40.96 s segments. WavLM's own noise and overlapping-speech augmentation is reproduced: 20% of utterances are mixed with another utterance or DNS noise. The same augmented waveform goes to the student and the frozen teacher. The decoder is trained separately on LibriTTS-100 at 16 kHz, with HiFi-GAN multi-scale and multi-period discriminators, a multi-resolution discriminator and a WavLM-base-SV speaker-consistency loss, for about 5M steps. At inference, both transformers keep a 256-frame (40.96 s) key-value cache. The cache is reset every 256 frames instead of using a sliding window. The full model has 842M parameters.

## Key Results

Baselines are streaming codecs at their configuration closest to 0.80 kbps: EnCodec, AudioDec, HILCodec, Mimi, PAST and FocalCodec-Stream (FocalCodec-S@50). Non-streaming FocalCodec@50 serves as an offline reference (Table 1). All metrics are automatic: UTMOS, dWER (Whisper-small), WavLM speaker similarity, and DNSMOS. There is no human listening test.

**Resynthesis (Table 2).**
- LibriSpeech test-clean: ZipCodec gets UTMOS 3.89, dWER 2.83 and Sim 98.0. FocalCodec-Stream gets 3.85 / 3.68 / 97.0 and Mimi gets 3.44 / 4.77 / 96.6.
- ZipCodec leads all streaming codecs on all three metrics. Its Sim also exceeds offline FocalCodec@50 (97.4), but its UTMOS and dWER trail FocalCodec@50 (4.05 / 2.18).
- MLS multilingual: ZipCodec gets UTMOS 2.69, dWER 15.52 and Sim 98.7, against 2.65 / 19.88 / 98.1 for FocalCodec-Stream. It again leads all streaming codecs on all three metrics, and its Sim exceeds FocalCodec@50 (98.3).
- Code usage is 100% in both settings. Normalized entropy (96.2 / 96.5) is below FocalCodec-Stream's (98.7 / 98.3).

**One-shot voice conversion on VCTK (Table 2).**
- ZipCodec has the highest UTMOS among streaming codecs (3.15 vs. 3.10 for FocalCodec-Stream).
- Its Sim (91.5) is second to FocalCodec-Stream (92.5) and just ahead of Mimi (91.3).
- Its dWER (25.90) trails PAST (18.28) and FocalCodec-Stream (22.71).

**DASB downstream probes (Table 3).**
- ZipCodec has the lowest streaming error rates on SI (0.49), SER (33.64), KS (3.95) and IC (26.76).
- SER and KS also beat offline FocalCodec@50 (34.79, 4.23).
- On ASR, its WER of 16.07 is second to PAST (10.74), which is fine-tuned with supervision.
- Speech enhancement: ZipCodec has the best DNSMOS (3.60) and Sim (91.0) of all systems. Its dWER (20.10) is behind PAST (18.19), FocalCodec-Stream (19.56) and FocalCodec@50 (12.35).
- Speech separation: ZipCodec is best on all three metrics (DNSMOS 3.77, dWER 66.67, Sim 91.0). The SE and SS models run directly on the 6.25 Hz representation.

**Efficiency (Tables 2 and 4).**
- On a consumer laptop CPU (i7-10875H), single-stream RTF is 1.33, where higher means faster. The p99 compute time per 160 ms step is 124.68 ms.
- On an RTX 3070, RTF is 13.96 at batch size 1 and 4.49 at batch size 16, with 3.33 to 3.84 GiB of VRAM.
- On a 1/8 H100 partition, ZipCodec is slower than most baselines: RTF 62 on English, against 106 for FocalCodec-Stream and 154 for Mimi. The cause is its 842M parameters, compared with 249M for FocalCodec-Stream and 82M for Mimi.

The comparisons are fair on bitrate (0.80 to 1.60 kbps). All evaluation protocols come from the authors' earlier FocalCodec papers.

## Novelty Assessment

Several individual pieces come from prior work. SSQ comes from the authors' DyCAST, DynamicErf from a CVPR 2026 normalization-free transformer, the decoder from Vocos, and the distillation recipe from FocalCodec-Stream. The new parts are three. First, the ErfFormer backbone has neither normalization nor positional encoding, a design aimed at unbounded streaming. Second, the decoder exploits the long 160 ms step to relax causality inside each step. Third, the paper shows that a WavLM-distilled streaming codec still works at 6.25 Hz once single-stage distillation is scaled to about 94k hours with WavLM-matched augmentation.

How much each change contributes is unknown, because the paper has no ablations. The gains over FocalCodec-Stream combine a 3.4x larger model, much more data, a new backbone, a new quantizer and a new frontend.

## Field Significance

Moderate. This is the first streaming codec below 12.5 Hz that the authors know of. It retains linguistic, speaker and paralinguistic information well enough to beat 12.5 to 80 Hz streaming codecs on most probes. This supports the idea that frame rate can be cut sharply through SSL distillation rather than through text side information or offline variable-rate coding. The paper does not yet show that the shorter sequences help a downstream speech LM or TTS model.

## Claims

- **supports:** Distilling self-supervised speech features into a causal codec can keep semantic and acoustic information at frame rates well below those of existing streaming codecs.
  > *Evidence:* At 6.25 Hz and 0.80 kbps, ZipCodec beats every streaming baseline (12.5 to 80 Hz) on English and multilingual resynthesis UTMOS, dWER and speaker similarity. It also has the lowest streaming error rates on SI, SER, KS and IC probes. *(§4.1, Table 2; §4.2, Table 3)*
- **refines:** At very low frame rates, a streaming codec's decoder can use non-causal context within each frame without adding latency beyond the frame duration.
  > *Evidence:* Each 160 ms token expands to 8 WavLM vectors that arrive together. The streaming Vocos decoder therefore pads left and right inside the 160 ms window, keeping theoretical latency at the 160 ms bottleneck. *(§2.1, "Decoder")*
- **complicates:** Lowering a codec's frame rate does not by itself make it cheaper to run, because extra capacity is needed to keep quality.
  > *Evidence:* ZipCodec has 842M parameters, against 249M for FocalCodec-Stream and 82M for Mimi. Its RTF on a 1/8 H100 partition is 62 for English resynthesis, against 106 for FocalCodec-Stream and 154 for Mimi, though it remains real-time on a laptop CPU (RTF 1.33). *(§3; Table 1; Table 2; §4.3, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Low-frame-rate codecs that win on reconstruction can still lose on content preservation in voice conversion and ASR to codecs with supervised phonetic fine-tuning or higher frame rates.
  > *Evidence:* ZipCodec's VC dWER (25.90) trails PAST (18.28) and FocalCodec-Stream (22.71). Its ASR probe WER (16.07) trails PAST (10.74), even though ZipCodec leads on resynthesis dWER. *(§4.1, Table 2; §4.2, Table 3)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The paper has no ablations. The gains over FocalCodec-Stream cannot be traced to any one change: the ErfFormer backbone, SSQ, the log-mel frontend, single-stage distillation, the decoder redesign, the 3.4x larger model, or the move to about 94k hours with augmentation.

- Quality is measured only with automatic metrics (UTMOS, DNSMOS, Whisper dWER, WavLM similarity). There is no MUSHRA or MOS test, so perceptual claims at 6.25 Hz rest on predictors.
- The codec is motivated by speech language modeling, but no TTS model or speech LM is trained on its tokens. Downstream generative evidence is limited to non-autoregressive SE and SS probes.
- Training data is English only. Multilingual resynthesis is far worse than English: UTMOS 2.69 vs. 3.89 and dWER 15.52 vs. 2.83, although the reference UTMOS is also lower (2.84 vs. 4.09).
- Output is 16 kHz only. The decoder is trained on LibriTTS-100.
- The key-value cache is hard-reset every 40.96 s. The paper does not measure the effect at reset boundaries in long streams.
- The 160 ms figure is theoretical. Table 4 excludes audio capture, transfers, resampling and network overhead. On CPU, per-step compute (p99 124.68 ms) adds to the 160 ms frame wait.
- The paper is five pages long, so details of the SE/SS probes and the VC procedure are deferred to the earlier FocalCodec papers.
