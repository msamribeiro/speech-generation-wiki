> [!abstract] DravidianLangTech 2026 · 2026 · Workshop
> **Bhavana Nali et al.** (IIT Bhubaneswar) · [→ Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.dravidianlangtech-1.3) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✓
>
> A controlled comparison of a cascaded ASR, machine translation and TTS pipeline against SeamlessM4T-v2 for speech-to-speech translation into and between Tamil, Telugu and Kannada, with both systems LoRA-adapted on identical splits; the cascade scores above the end-to-end model in every reported cell, though several margins are very small.

## Problem

Speech-to-speech translation progress concentrates on high-resource languages. Tamil, Telugu and Kannada have limited parallel speech data, and no prior study compared the cascaded and end-to-end paradigms for these three languages under matched zero-shot and fine-tuned conditions. Mann-ki-Baat also lacks explicit Dravidian-to-Dravidian pairs, so a direct DL→DL evaluation set has to be built first.

## Method

The study compares two pipelines on two directions: English to Dravidian (EN→DL) and Dravidian to Dravidian (DL→DL) *(§2, Figure 1)*.

- **Cascaded Modular.** ASR is Whisper Large-v3 for English input and IndicConformer for Dravidian input. IndicTrans2 translates the transcript (English-to-Indic or Indic-to-Indic configuration). Indic Parler-TTS synthesises the target speech from the translated text and a speaker prompt.
- **End-to-End.** SeamlessM4T-v2 maps source speech to target speech without exposing intermediate text.

Both systems receive LoRA adapters (rank 16, scaling 32, dropout 0.1, under 1% trainable parameters). In the cascade, LoRA goes into Whisper, IndicConformer and IndicTrans2's attention layers. In SeamlessM4T-v2 it goes into the cross-attention projections of the speech encoder and text decoder, trained with a speech-to-text cross-entropy objective. The TTS stage is frozen in all settings: Indic Parler-TTS is never fine-tuned because the datasets lack paired target-language audio at the needed scale *(§2)*. No module on the speech-generation side of either system is trained, so fine-tuning changes only what text (or text-decoder state) reaches synthesis. Training runs 4,000 steps at batch size 8 and learning rate 1e-4, with checkpoint selection on validation BLEU *(§3)*.

Data come from FLEURS (predefined splits, about 3,000 training utterances per language) and the Mann-ki-Baat subset of BhasaAnuvaad. For DL→DL pairs, Mann-ki-Baat sentences are aligned with LaBSE embeddings, cosine similarity and Hungarian one-to-one matching at a 0.6 threshold. This gives about 15,284 pairs, split 80/10/10 *(§3, Appendix B)*.

Evaluation uses BLEU, chrF++ and COMET (WMT22 COMET-DA) for translation. For SeamlessM4T-v2, chrF++ and COMET come from its speech-to-text (S2TT) output head. Speech output is scored by back-ASR: IndicConformer re-transcribes the generated audio and WER and CER are computed. The paper does not state which text the back-ASR transcripts are scored against. A component diagnostic reports standalone ASR WER and MT BLEU on gold versus ASR transcripts for the cascade *(§3, Table 3)*.

## Key Results

Relative changes below are (new − old) / old, recomputed from the tables.

- **Cascade above E2E in every cell.** Across Tables 1 and 2 (3 EN→DL languages and 6 DL→DL pairs, zero-shot and fine-tuned, FLEURS and Mann-ki-Baat, all ten metric columns), the cascade has higher BLEU, chrF++ and COMET and lower back-ASR WER and CER than SeamlessM4T-v2 in the same setting. The paper's own wording ("across most settings") is more cautious than its tables.
- **Headline examples.** On FLEURS EN→Telugu, cascade BLEU is 18.96 vs. 15.72 zero-shot (+20.6%) and 19.18 vs. 16.84 fine-tuned (+13.9%) *(Table 1)*. On FLEURS Tamil→Telugu it is 8.13 vs. 6.84 zero-shot (+18.9%) and 10.12 vs. 8.97 fine-tuned (+12.8%) *(Table 2)*. Larger relative gaps occur elsewhere, e.g. zero-shot EN→Kannada on Mann-ki-Baat (8.70 vs. 6.24, +39.4%) *(Table 1)*.
- **Near-ties.** Several BLEU margins are within the paper's own "below about 1 BLEU" caution zone. EN→Kannada on Mann-ki-Baat after fine-tuning is 7.03 vs. 7.01 (0.02 BLEU). EN→Tamil on Mann-ki-Baat zero-shot is 3.17 vs. 3.02. EN→Kannada on FLEURS after fine-tuning is 14.33 vs. 13.67. Telugu→Tamil on FLEURS after fine-tuning is 9.11 vs. 8.54 *(Tables 1-2)*. No significance tests are reported.
- **Fine-tuning gains are uneven.** For the cascade's DL→DL pairs, BLEU rises by 24.5% to 39.3% on FLEURS and 22.0% to 37.2% on Mann-ki-Baat *(Table 2)*. For EN→DL the gains are small on FLEURS: Tamil 13.75 to 14.43, Telugu 18.96 to 19.18 and Kannada 14.32 to 14.33. Cascade EN→Kannada on Mann-ki-Baat drops from 8.70 to 7.03 BLEU (−19.2%) after fine-tuning, while its chrF++ and COMET rise *(Table 1)*. The diagnostic shows the same direction: EN→Kannada Mann-ki-Baat MT BLEU falls from 11.23 to 10.14 on gold transcripts and 9.72 to 8.29 on ASR transcripts *(Table 3)*. The paper reports the overall effect as "approximately 20-30%" for Dravidian pairs and does not discuss the Kannada drop.
- **Back-ASR error rates are very high.** Back-ASR WER ranges from 0.7687 to 1.0687 across the two tables. Values above 1.0 occur for all three E2E zero-shot EN→DL rows on Mann-ki-Baat, for E2E fine-tuned EN→Tamil on Mann-ki-Baat (1.0341), for E2E zero-shot EN→Tamil on FLEURS (1.0234) and for cascade zero-shot EN→Tamil on Mann-ki-Baat (1.0214) *(Table 1)*. The paper attributes values above 1.0 to insertion-dominated errors *(§3)*.
- **Error propagation.** MT BLEU on ASR transcripts is below MT BLEU on gold transcripts in every Table 3 row; for EN→Tamil on FLEURS zero-shot it falls from 17.42 to 14.81. ASR fine-tuning lowers ASR WER in every row, e.g. English FLEURS (Tamil target) from 0.124 to 0.089 *(Table 3)*.

The comparison controls training data and splits, but it is not fully symmetric. IndicConformer is both the cascade's DL→DL ASR and the back-ASR judge, which the authors note may favour the cascade *(§3, §6)*. E2E translation metrics come from SeamlessM4T-v2's text head rather than its speech output.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is empirical. Every component is an existing pretrained model, LoRA is applied with a single fixed configuration, and the evaluation reuses standard scorers. The new elements are the matched cascaded-versus-end-to-end comparison for three Dravidian languages and a LaBSE-aligned pseudo-parallel DL→DL test set from Mann-ki-Baat, which the authors note has not been human-validated. Because the TTS stage is frozen and speech output is judged only through back-ASR, the study says little about speech generation itself. Its findings concern recognition and translation quality upstream of synthesis.

## Field Significance

low — This is a short single-family study with automatic-only evaluation, no significance tests and a frozen synthesiser. It adds a consistent data point that a cascade of strong language-specific modules can stay ahead of a general multilingual end-to-end model for low-resource Dravidian speech-to-speech translation. Its small margins and the shared back-ASR judge limit how far that point generalises.

## Claims

- **supports:** For low-resource language pairs, a cascade of language-specialised ASR, machine translation and TTS modules can outperform a general multilingual end-to-end speech-to-speech translation model under matched adaptation data.
  > *Evidence:* With LoRA on identical FLEURS and Mann-ki-Baat splits, the Whisper/IndicConformer + IndicTrans2 + Indic Parler-TTS cascade scores above SeamlessM4T-v2 on BLEU, chrF++, COMET and back-ASR WER/CER in every EN→DL and DL→DL cell, e.g. FLEURS EN→Telugu BLEU 19.18 vs. 16.84 after fine-tuning. *(§4, Tables 1-2)*
- **complicates:** The advantage of cascaded over end-to-end speech-to-speech translation can shrink to within noise for some language pairs and domains, so rankings need significance testing.
  > *Evidence:* Fine-tuned EN→Kannada BLEU differs by 0.02 on Mann-ki-Baat (7.03 vs. 7.01) and by 0.66 on FLEURS (14.33 vs. 13.67); the authors performed no significance tests. *(§4, §6, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** Back-ASR intelligibility scores for translated speech conflate recognition, translation and synthesis errors and can favour systems that share the judging ASR model.
  > *Evidence:* Back-ASR WER ranges from 0.7687 to 1.0687, exceeding 1.0 for several EN→DL settings; IndicConformer scores both systems while also serving as the cascade's DL→DL ASR, which the authors flag as a possible bias. *(§3, §6, Tables 1-2)*
- **supports:** ASR errors propagate measurably into downstream translation in cascaded speech-to-speech systems, and adapting the ASR stage narrows the loss.
  > *Evidence:* MT BLEU on ASR transcripts is below MT BLEU on gold transcripts in every diagnostic row (EN→Tamil FLEURS zero-shot 17.42 vs. 14.81); LoRA lowers ASR WER in every row (English FLEURS 0.124 to 0.089). *(§4, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Parameter-efficient domain adaptation of translation components does not uniformly improve speech-to-speech translation quality.
  > *Evidence:* Cascade DL→DL BLEU rises 22.0% to 39.3% after LoRA, but EN→Kannada on Mann-ki-Baat drops from 8.70 to 7.03 BLEU, with MT BLEU on gold transcripts also falling from 11.23 to 10.14. *(§4, Tables 1-3)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> Speech generation is not studied directly. Indic Parler-TTS is frozen, no human listening test is run, and speech output is judged only by back-ASR WER/CER using IndicConformer, which is also part of the cascade. Naturalness, prosody and speaker quality of the translated speech are unmeasured.

- No significance testing; the authors ask readers to treat differences below about 1 BLEU with caution, which covers several of the cascaded-versus-E2E margins *(§6)*.
- The DL→DL Mann-ki-Baat pairs come from automatic LaBSE alignment and are not human-validated *(§6)*.
- GPU limits restricted training to a subset of Mann-ki-Baat, and FLEURS (read speech) and Mann-ki-Baat (broadcast speech) differ in domain *(§6)*.
- The paper does not state the reference text for back-ASR WER/CER, or whether BLEU is computed on translated text or back-ASR transcripts; for the E2E system, chrF++ and COMET come from the S2TT text head rather than its generated speech *(§3)*.
- Only one LoRA configuration is tried, and the cascade EN→Kannada Mann-ki-Baat BLEU drop after fine-tuning is not analysed.
- Whether adapting the TTS stage would change the comparison remains open *(§6)*.
