> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Qian Chen et al.** (Alibaba Token Foundry) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16514) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✗
>
> Reinterprets the CosyVoice to Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS lineage as a sequence of relocations of the dominant bottleneck in a stable planner-renderer design (an autoregressive language model that plans speech tokens and a flow-matching model that renders acoustics), and formalizes the planner-renderer boundary as a four-part interface contract: representation, ownership, availability and gradient reach.

## Problem

Progress in speech synthesis is usually narrated as larger models, better tokenizers and more data, and cross-release comparisons are usually read off leaderboards. For a lineage of systems built by overlapping teams ([[2407.05407|CosyVoice]], [[2412.10117|CosyVoice 2]], [[2505.17589|CosyVoice 3]] and [[2607.23938|Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS]]), such accounts do not say which constraint each release actually targeted, and cross-paper score differences confound the intervention with changing data, checkpoints, recognizers, speaker encoders and test sets. The paper asks a narrower question than "which release is best": what was targeted after the previous constraint had been addressed, and how can modular generators be diagnosed and compared without misleading cross-paper leaderboards?

## Method

The paper is a technical retrospective written by authors of the reviewed systems; it trains no new model and runs no new experiments. Its analytical apparatus has three parts.

**The interface contract.** In a cascade, the planner models p(z | x) over a discrete speech plan z and the renderer models p(y | e(z), x) over continuous acoustics y from token embeddings e(z). In the coupled Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS system the renderer is instead conditioned on the language model (LM) hidden states h, with a joint loss L = L_token + λ·L_flow whose flow term sends nonzero gradients into the LM parameters (§2, Equation 2). The boundary is characterized as I = (R, O, A, G): R is the representation passed across it, O the ownership of factors such as content and speaker identity, A when interface values become available at inference, and G which objectives may send gradients across it (§2).

**Evidence discipline.** "Bottleneck" is an analytical label used only when a release explicitly targets a constraint and reports a controlled ablation or a capability evaluation tied to that intervention; otherwise the relocation is presented as interpretation. Cross-release numbers are reported only when the newest work evaluates earlier and newer tokenizers in the same table (§2). The paper also separates temporal rate from per-token capacity with a nominal index budget B_max = r·log2 K bits/s, explicitly an upper bound under uniform independent codes rather than a measured entropy rate (§2).

**The four relocations.** (1) CosyVoice targets representation: vector quantization inside a supervised multilingual ASR encoder (4,096 entries) yields content-aligned semantic tokens, and generation is factorized into token planning plus optimal-transport conditional flow matching to mel-spectrograms (§4.1). (2) CosyVoice 2 targets availability and ownership: finite scalar quantization (FSQ) replaces VQ, the LM is initialized from a pretrained text LLM and loses its utterance-level speaker embedding (speaker conditioning moves to the renderer), and bistreaming plus a chunk-aware causal flow-matching model make outputs causally available (§4.2). (3) CosyVoice 3 targets learnability and coverage: a multitask-supervised 25 Hz FSQ tokenizer, larger data and model scale, and differentiable reward optimization (DiffRO), which makes the discrete plan "rewardable" through Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and token-domain reward models (§4.3). (4) Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS targets interface capacity and coordination: a 12.5 Hz tokenizer with a larger FSQ space, continuous LM hidden states replacing token embeddings as renderer conditioning (a mechanism the source report aligns with JoyVoice [[2512.19090|JoyVoice]]), and a five-stage curriculum (§4.4, §5, Table 2): independent pretraining, joint training with quality annealing, LM reinforcement learning (GRPO plus filtered DiffRO) with the renderer fixed, renderer training for degraded-prompt robustness with the LM frozen, and renderer reinforcement learning on speaker-similarity, intelligibility and DNSMOS rewards with the LM fixed.

The paper then places the lineage in a design space organized by which object carries long-range planning and which mechanism performs local acoustic realization (discrete autoregressive, continuous non-autoregressive, discrete non-autoregressive, continuous autoregressive and hybrid), derives transferable design principles (§6), and proposes an evaluation protocol for future lineage studies (§7).

## Key Results

The paper reports no new measurements; its numbers are restated from the source papers.

- **Rate versus capacity (restated, Table 1, from the Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS tokenizer ablation on Seed-TTS-Eval).** Halving the frame rate from 25 Hz to 12.5 Hz at a fixed 6,561-entry space degrades every column: test-zh CER rises from 1.45 to 2.59 and SIM falls from 80.6 to 72.44; test-en WER rises from 2.57 to 3.21 and SIM falls from 73.6 to 61.64; test-hard error rises from 6.83 to 7.94 and SIM falls from 77.6 to 69.78. Enlarging the space at 12.5 Hz recovers the loss. The 59,049-entry variant has the lowest content error among the 12.5 Hz variants (1.23 / 2.37 / 6.68 against 1.48 / 2.56 / 6.70 for 19,683 entries), and also lower error than the 25 Hz CosyVoice 3 reference row; speaker similarity is marginally highest with 19,683 entries (83.25 / 77.58 / 80.85 against 83.09 / 77.49 / 80.61). The paper notes that the source labels the test-hard metric as CER while the CosyVoice 2 modular table labels the same 6.83 baseline as WER (§4.4, Table 1).
- **Index budget.** By the paper's arithmetic, 25 Hz codes with 6,561 entries have a nominal budget of about 317 bits/s, against about 198 bits/s for 12.5 Hz codes with 59,049 entries; the lower-budget, lower-rate tokenizer performs at least as well in Table 1, which the paper uses to argue that frame rate is not capacity and that supervision and utilization govern how a budget is used (§4.4).
- **Ownership and codebook utilization (restated from CosyVoice 2).** In the CosyVoice 2 modular ablation, pretrained-LM initialization, removing the LM's speaker embedding and replacing VQ with FSQ progressively reduce recognition error; the FSQ step alone moves Chinese CER from 2.56 to 1.45, English WER from 3.81 to 2.57 and hard-set WER from 9.66 to 6.83, with comparatively small similarity changes; the FSQ space reaches full utilization of 6,561 codes against 23% for the 4,096-entry VQ baseline (§4.2).
- **Streaming (restated qualitatively).** Offline and streaming LM/renderer combinations in CosyVoice 2 preserve similar accuracy and similarity on standard sets, while the hard subset is more sensitive to streaming scheduling (§4.2). No numbers are given.
- **Scaling (restated qualitatively).** The CosyVoice 3 tokenizer study shows large downstream gains from 3,000 to 170,000 hours, with one-million-hour full-system results beginning to plateau; the paper explicitly flags that the last point is not a matched tokenizer-only intervention and treats the trend as descriptive (§4.3).

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is conceptual. The interface contract I = (R, O, A, G) is a new vocabulary for comparing modular planner-renderer speech generators, and the rate-versus-capacity framing gives a simple, explicitly bounded way to read tokenizer ablations. None of the system mechanisms are new to this paper: each is documented in the source reports, and the paper itself states that hidden-state conditioning of the renderer is not a first (§4.4). Its evidential base is narrow: one lineage, two within-paper ablations carrying the quantitative argument, and a retrospective written by authors of the reviewed systems. The paper is unusually careful about this: it separates within-paper evidence from cross-paper comparison, labels the bottleneck sequence as interpretation rather than measured causation, and supplies a falsifiable protocol (§7) for the matched longitudinal study it does not run.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper offers a reusable diagnostic framing for hybrid autoregressive-planner plus flow-matching-renderer systems: progress is located in the contract between modules (what crosses the boundary, which module owns which factor, when values arrive, and which losses reach upstream) rather than in either module alone. Its practical value lies in the design principles and the proposed evaluation protocol (reporting rate and vocabulary size together with utilization and entropy, ablating conditioning location per module, and crossing gradient reach with initialization), which can serve as a template for analysing other modular generators; the specific bottleneck narrative remains a single-lineage interpretation.

## Claims

- **supports:** In token-based speech generation, lowering the token frame rate at fixed vocabulary size degrades both content accuracy and speaker similarity, and increasing per-token vocabulary capacity can recover the loss while keeping the shorter sequence.
  > *Evidence:* In the tokenizer ablation restated from Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS, halving the rate from 25 to 12.5 Hz at 6,561 entries raises test-zh CER from 1.45 to 2.59 and lowers test-en SIM from 73.6 to 61.64, while 12.5 Hz tokenizers with 19,683 or 59,049 entries match or improve on the 25 Hz reference (59,049 entries: CER 1.23, WER 2.37, test-hard 6.68). *(§4.4, Table 1)*
- **refines:** Token frame rate and codebook size are each poor proxies for the information a speech tokenizer carries; supervision and code utilization determine how effectively the nominal index budget is used.
  > *Evidence:* The 12.5 Hz, 59,049-entry tokenizer has a nominal budget of about 198 bits/s against about 317 bits/s for the 25 Hz, 6,561-entry tokenizer, yet yields lower content error; separately, CosyVoice 2's FSQ reaches full utilization of 6,561 codes against 23% for a 4,096-entry VQ baseline, and the FSQ step reduces Chinese CER from 2.56 to 1.45. *(§2, §4.2, §4.4, Table 1)*
- **supports:** In hybrid speech generators, removing the utterance-level speaker embedding from the autoregressive planner while keeping explicit speaker conditioning in the acoustic renderer can improve content accuracy without a large loss of speaker similarity.
  > *Evidence:* The restated CosyVoice 2 modular ablation reports that removing the LM's speaker embedding, alongside pretrained-LM initialization and FSQ, progressively reduces recognition error on Chinese, English and hard subsets, with comparatively small similarity changes; the paper reads this as conditioning relocated to the renderer rather than eliminated. *(§4.2, §6)*
- **complicates:** Release-level score comparisons across successive speech-generation systems do not isolate which design change caused an improvement, because data, checkpoints, recognizers, speaker encoders and test sets change between releases.
  > *Evidence:* The paper restricts cross-release numbers to the one table in which the newest report evaluates earlier and newer tokenizers together, notes that the CosyVoice 3 data-scaling trend mixes a tokenizer-only study with a full-system point and is therefore not a controlled curve, and calls for frozen evaluation artifacts and factorial designs crossing token rate with vocabulary size and conditioning location with module. *(§2, §4.3, §7)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] No original experiments, single lineage, authored by the system builders
> Every quantitative result on this page is restated from earlier reports, the bottleneck sequence is the authors' interpretation of one product lineage that they helped build, and the paper explicitly does not claim causal attribution across releases (§2, §8). The framing has not been tested on systems from other groups.

- The benefits of the five-stage curriculum, hidden-state conditioning and DiffRO are described but not quantified on this page's source; the paper presents no ablation of the staged schedule against joint-from-scratch or frozen-cascade training, so the argument that coupling only after independent pretraining avoids destabilizing the planner rests on design rationale (§4.4, §5).
- The streaming and data-scaling arguments are qualitative restatements without numbers (§4.2, §4.3).
- The framework concentrates on the planner-renderer boundary and gives little attention to data governance, front-end normalization, vocoder engineering, serving infrastructure and human preference measurement, any of which can become the dominant constraint in production (§8).
- Whether the interface contract transfers to fully end-to-end continuous autoregressive or discrete non-autoregressive systems is asserted as a design-space placement (§3) rather than demonstrated.
- The paper flags that degraded-prompt robustness lowers the quality threshold for voice enrollment, which raises impersonation risk and calls for consent-aware enrollment and provenance measures (§8).
