> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Hui Li et al.** (Fudan University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02739) · Demo: ✗ · Code: ✓
>
> EntangleCodec is a single-stream, single-codebook 50 Hz audio tokenizer whose encoder is trained with a CLIP-style contrastive loss against LLM-written rich captions before quantization, so one token stream carries both semantic and acoustic information for reconstruction, audio understanding, TTS and text-to-audio.

## Problem

Discrete audio tokenizers for audio language models tend to fall into two camps. Reconstruction-oriented codecs (EnCodec, DAC, WavTokenizer) keep acoustic detail but carry little semantic information. Semantic-aware codecs (SpeechTokenizer, X-Codec, XCodec2, XY-Tokenizer) add a separate semantic stream or teacher and fuse it with an acoustic stream, which the authors argue adds redundancy or misalignment. The semantic supervision in these designs is usually tied to ASR transcripts, which ignore speaker identity, emotion, prosody, acoustic scene and musical structure. The paper asks whether a single encoder, supervised with richer text descriptions, can serve reconstruction and LLM-based understanding and generation at once.

## Method

The codec has three parts: a unified Transformer encoder, a single-codebook vector quantizer and a flow-matching decoder. Audio is resampled to 24 kHz and converted to a 128-bin log-Mel spectrogram with a 480-sample hop, which gives a 50 Hz frame rate. A learnable [CLS] token is prepended to the projected Mel frames. The frame outputs form the semantic-acoustic sequence that is quantized. The [CLS] output is the global audio embedding used for semantic alignment.

Semantic alignment uses audio-text contrastive learning against rich captions rather than transcripts. The captions are generated by [[2512.23808|MiMo-Audio]] with a structured prompt covering speaker attributes (gender, age, accent, speaking style, emotion), acoustic environment, musical attributes and sound events. For LibriSpeech and LibriTTS the ASR transcript is appended to the caption. A 12-layer Transformer text encoder embeds the caption, and a bidirectional CLIP-style loss aligns it with the projected [CLS] embedding. Before quantization the frame sequence passes through a two-layer MLP to a 14-dimensional space and is L2-normalized (Table 5). The codebook size is not reported.

![Figure 2: Overview of EntangleCodec. Compared with single-encoder acoustic codecs and dual-encoder semantic-acoustic codecs, EntangleCodec learns caption-aligned unified tokens that support both reconstruction and downstream audio-language tasks. The right panel shows the two-stage training strategy: joint codec learning with rich captions, followed by decoder refinement.](assets/2606.02739/figure-2.png)

The decoder is a Llama-style Transformer trained with a rectified-flow objective to predict the Mel-spectrogram velocity field, with the quantized tokens as a conditioning prefix. Training has two stages. Stage 1 (500k steps) trains the audio encoder, text encoder, quantizer and decoder jointly with flow, contrastive and VQ commitment losses. Stage 2 (200k steps) freezes the encoder and quantizer, drops the text encoder, and refines the decoder with the flow loss plus an adversarial loss from a frozen pre-trained discriminator (§Appendix B.3). Inference uses a single Euler step with classifier-free guidance (scale 1.0), and Vocos converts the Mel output to a waveform. The codec is trained on about 3,259 hours: LibriSpeech and LibriTTS (speech), MusicBench (music), and AudioSet, AudioCaps and WavCaps (sound) (Table 7). The codec parameter count is not reported.

For downstream evaluation the frozen tokenizer feeds Qwen3 language models. In the codec-controlled setting, identical Qwen3-0.6B models are trained on each codec's tokens. The TTS and TTA model is trained for 3 epochs on LibriSpeech and LibriTTS (text, speech) pairs and IFCaps and AudioCaps (caption, audio) pairs (Tables 6 and 8). The paper does not describe speaker prompting for the TTS model. Larger 4B and 8B variants are reported for understanding only.

## Key Results

**Speech reconstruction (Table 1, LibriSpeech held-out; Table 9, LibriTTS test-clean).** EntangleCodec reaches UTMOS 3.96, STOI 0.87 and SIM 0.66 at 50 tokens/s with one codebook. It is second on UTMOS behind XCodec2 (4.02), third on STOI behind WavTokenizer at 75 Hz (0.90) and XCodec2 (0.88), and second on SIM behind XCodec2 (0.76). Voiced/unvoiced F1 is 0.98, tied with WavTokenizer (75 Hz) and XCodec2. On LibriTTS the pattern holds: UTMOS 3.94 against XCodec2's 4.00 and SIM 0.65 against 0.75. Reconstruction-only codecs at matched rates score far lower on UTMOS (DAC 1.30, EnCodec 1.57, SpeechTokenizer at one layer 1.27). §4.1.4 lists PESQ as a reconstruction metric, but no table reports PESQ.

**Music and sound reconstruction (Table 1).** Averaging the four AudioBox Aesthetics sub-scores, EntangleCodec is second on music (5.42) behind X-Codec (6.44). On sound its average is 4.11, behind X-Codec (4.47), EnCodec (4.34) and DAC (4.12). The text states that it ranks second on sound as well, but the Table 1 values put it fourth.

**Audio understanding (Table 2, codec-controlled Qwen3-0.6B).** EntangleCodec tokens give 34.2% on MMAU-mini, 35.1% on MMAU and 34.3% on MMAR, the highest of the six tokenizers on each benchmark. The next best scores are WavTokenizer on MMAU-mini (32.7%) and MMAR (26.9%) and X-Codec on MMAU (32.6%). The MMAR margin (+7.4 points) is the largest. Among baselines, XCodec2 tokens give the weakest understanding (25.7% average). These benchmarks mix speech, music and sound, and no per-domain breakdown is reported. The understanding training data in Table 8 is music QA, sound QA and audio reasoning, with no speech-specific QA set. Against continuous-representation audio LLMs (Figure 3), the 0.6B model is reported to beat SALMONN-13B by 0.3 to 1.1 points on the three benchmarks. The 8B model reaches 56.2% (MMAU-mini), 52.6% (MMAU) and 42.6% (MMAR), and Figure 3 marks it first on MMAU and MMAR among the plotted systems.

**TTS (Table 3, Seed-TTS eval, codec-controlled Qwen3-0.6B).** EntangleCodec tokens give WER 9.8% and UTMOS 3.89, the best of the five tokenizers on both. XY-Tokenizer is next on WER (12.1%), and X-Codec and XY-Tokenizer tie for second on UTMOS (2.95). The other WERs are WavTokenizer 17.2%, XCodec2 23.2% and X-Codec 25.6%. WER is measured with Whisper-large-v3. The TTS model is a 0.6B probe trained only on LibriSpeech and LibriTTS, so these numbers compare tokenizers rather than measure a competitive TTS system.

**Text-to-audio (Table 3).** CLAP score is 0.17 against at most 0.04 for the baselines. AudioBox Aesthetics is below X-Codec (average 4.27 against 4.84).

**Ablations (Table 4, reconstruction only).** Removing the contrastive loss drops speech UTMOS from 3.96 to 3.04 and SIM from 0.66 to 0.50. Replacing rich captions with ASR transcripts drops UTMOS to 3.24 and SIM to 0.51. Skipping Stage 2 drops UTMOS to 3.40 and SIM to 0.54. Music and sound AudioBox scores fall in every variant.

## Novelty Assessment

The new element is the training objective: contrastive alignment of the codec encoder's global embedding with LLM-generated rich captions, applied before quantization in a single-stream, single-codebook tokenizer. Semantic supervision of codecs is not new: the paper itself groups SpeechTokenizer, X-Codec and XCodec2 as semantic-aware designs. The departure is replacing transcript-level supervision with free-text descriptions of speaker, emotion, scene and music inside one encoder. The flow-matching Mel decoder, the adversarial decoder refinement and the Vocos vocoder are established components.

The evidence is uneven. The codec-controlled downstream comparison is a useful design because it holds the LM fixed. However, baselines differ in token rate and codebook count (SpeechTokenizer and XY-Tokenizer use 8 layers in the downstream tables), and Mimi, EnCodec and DAC are absent from the understanding and generation tables. The ablations cover reconstruction only, so the paper does not show that the caption objective itself causes the understanding, TTS or TTA gains. The finding that contrastive supervision improves reconstruction quality is interesting but is shown only on these reconstruction metrics.

## Field Significance

Moderate — This paper shows that a single-codebook speech-and-audio tokenizer supervised with rich natural-language captions can keep speech reconstruction close to XCodec2 while producing tokens that a small LM uses better for TTS and audio understanding than several semantic and acoustic codecs. It is useful evidence that semantic supervision beyond transcripts can be folded into a single token stream, but the causal link to downstream gains is not isolated.

## Claims

- **supports:** Semantic supervision of a codec encoder from natural-language audio descriptions can be added to a single token stream without a separate semantic stream, while keeping speech reconstruction close to that of strong semantic codecs.
  > *Evidence:* The single-codebook 50 Hz tokenizer reaches UTMOS 3.96 and SIM 0.66 on LibriSpeech against XCodec2's 4.02 and 0.76, and UTMOS 3.94 against 4.00 on LibriTTS test-clean, while SpeechTokenizer at one layer scores UTMOS 1.27. *(§4.2.1, Table 1; §Appendix E, Table 9)*
- **refines:** In a codec trained jointly with a contrastive text-alignment objective, removing or narrowing that objective degrades reconstruction quality rather than improving it.
  > *Evidence:* Without the contrastive loss speech UTMOS falls from 3.96 to 3.04 and SIM from 0.66 to 0.50; using ASR transcripts instead of rich captions gives UTMOS 3.24 and SIM 0.51, with AudioBox scores for music and sound falling in both variants. *(§4.3, Table 4)*
- **complicates:** Codec reconstruction scores are a weak predictor of how well a fixed language model generates speech or understands audio from that codec's tokens.
  > *Evidence:* With an identical Qwen3-0.6B backbone, XCodec2 has the best reconstruction UTMOS (4.02) and SIM (0.76) but yields TTS WER 23.2% and UTMOS 1.53 and the lowest understanding average (25.7%); WavTokenizer reconstructs at UTMOS 3.79 but gives TTS UTMOS 1.29. *(§4.2.1 to §4.2.3, Tables 1 to 3)*
- **complicates:** Single-stream semantically aligned codecs still lose speaker similarity relative to the best reconstruction-focused semantic codecs.
  > *Evidence:* Speaker similarity is 0.66 against XCodec2's 0.76 on LibriSpeech and 0.65 against 0.75 on LibriTTS test-clean, a larger gap than on UTMOS. *(§4.2.1, Table 1; §Appendix E, Table 9)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] Downstream gains are not causally isolated
> Ablations (Table 4) measure reconstruction only. No experiment removes the caption objective and re-measures understanding, TTS or TTA, so the attribution of those gains to caption alignment rests on cross-codec comparisons that also differ in architecture, token rate and training data.

- Understanding results mix speech, music and sound with no per-domain breakdown, and the understanding training data contains no speech-specific QA set. The tables therefore do not show how much the tokens help speech understanding in particular.
- The downstream TTS probe is trained only on LibriSpeech and LibriTTS. The Seed-TTS subset, speaker prompting and any zero-shot setup are not described, so the TTS numbers are relative comparisons between tokenizers rather than evidence of competitive synthesis.
- The text claims a second-place sound reconstruction score, but Table 1 places EntangleCodec fourth by average AudioBox score. PESQ is listed as a metric but not reported.
- Codec size and codebook size are not reported. The comparison with continuous audio LLMs mixes official checkpoints and previously reported numbers, and the 4B and 8B training setups are not detailed.
- The authors note that caption quality bounds semantic granularity (subtle emotion shifts, complex harmony) and that scaling beyond 8B is unexplored (§Limitations).
