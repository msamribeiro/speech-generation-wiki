> **Xintong Wang et al.** (National University of Singapore) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16417) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ?
>
> Joycent separates accent from speaker identity twice, once in the accent representation (a Whisper-based accent encoder trained with gradient reversal against a speaker classifier) and once in conditioning (accent injected at the first Conformer block of the text encoder, speaker at the last), and improves accent transfer to unseen speakers whose own accent differs from the target.

## Problem

Prompt-based accent TTS takes two speech prompts: one sets the target accent, the other sets the target voice. The hard case is cross-accent synthesis, where the two prompts come from different speakers with different inherent accents. In multi-accent corpora each speaker usually has one accent, so an accent encoder trained on accent labels can lean on speaker cues, and the speaker prompt's own accent can leak into the output. A second open question is where accent information should enter the model: prior accent TTS systems typically add or concatenate accent embeddings at the text-encoder input or condition the decoder, treating accent and speaker through one shared pathway.

## Method

Joycent is a Grad-TTS-style non-autoregressive acoustic model with three inputs: a phone sequence, an accent prompt and a speaker prompt. Text is converted to tone-marked Pinyin with G2PW and split into initials and tone-marked finals. The accent prompt goes through WhisAID to give an utterance-level accent embedding; the speaker prompt goes through the frozen timbre extractor of the pre-trained NaturalSpeech 3 FACodec to give a speaker embedding. A PreNet (3 Conv1D layers) feeds a Conformer text encoder with K = 4 standard blocks between two conditioned blocks. A duration predictor trained with monotonic alignment search expands the encoder output to frame level, and a score-based diffusion decoder following the Grad-TTS configuration generates 80-bin mel-spectrograms in 10 reverse steps. A Parallel WaveGAN vocoder, trained by the authors on CSMSC plus the accent data, produces 16 kHz audio. Model size is not reported.

WhisAID (Whisper-based Accent IDentification) takes a frozen Whisper-medium encoder, mean-pools its frames, and projects them through a bottleneck (linear, GELU, layer norm; 1024 to 256 dimensions). A linear accent classifier trains the bottleneck with cross-entropy. A second linear speaker classifier is attached through a gradient reversal layer, so the speaker loss (weight λ = 0.05) pushes speaker information out of the accent embedding. After training, both heads are discarded and the encoder plus bottleneck are frozen as the accent extractor. The design follows the accent-identification stage of AccentBox, with a Whisper backbone and gradient reversal in place of AccentBox's XLSR backbone and uniform-distribution adversarial loss.

Layer-specific conditioning replaces the layer normalization after the first Conformer block with an accent-conditioned conditional layer normalization (CLN), and the one after the final block with a speaker-conditioned CLN. Each CLN projects its embedding to FiLM scale and shift parameters. The authors argue that accent mainly changes phone realization, so it should modulate early token-level representations, while speaker identity is applied last to preserve timbre after accent-conditioned linguistic modeling. Training has two stages: WhisAID first, then the TTS model from scratch for 400k steps with the sum of the prior (MAS), duration and diffusion losses.

The authors also assemble the Mandarin Regional Accent Corpus (MRAC): 138 hours and 336 training speakers across 9 accent categories, from 7 MagicHub regional-accent sets (Guangdong, Shanghai, Sichuan, Tianjin, Henan, Wuhan, Singapore) plus AISHELL-3 speakers grouped into North and South (Table 1). Each accent has seen-speaker and held-out unseen-speaker test sets. The latest arXiv version (v4) is retitled "Joycent: Multi-Accent TTS via Disentangled Accent Modeling and Layer-Specific Conditioning"; earlier versions framed the contribution as avoiding accented-phone prediction.

## Key Results

Evaluation covers three settings: Seen-Inherent (seen speaker, prompts share an accent), Unseen-Inherent (unseen speaker, same accent), and Unseen-Cross (unseen speaker whose inherent accent differs from the target). Each setting has 1,680 synthesized samples. Accent similarity is the cosine similarity between output and accent-prompt embeddings from WhisAID (W-CS) and from the adversarially trained WhisAID variant (WU-CS); speaker similarity uses WavLM-Large embeddings and CER uses Qwen3-ASR-1.7B. Guangdong and Shanghai are excluded from the objective evaluation because those sets also contain dialectal variation. All three baselines, DDGM-Acc (diffusion accent modelling), DART (VAE/VQ accent-speaker factorization) and AccentBox, are retrained on the same data.

**Accent encoder (Table 2).** GenAID, the AccentBox accent identifier, scores higher on seen speakers (F1 0.94 vs 0.89 for WhisAID at λ = 0.05) but drops to 0.57 F1 on unseen speakers, where WhisAID reaches 0.66. Speaker clustering in the accent space (silhouette coefficient, SCSC, lower is better) falls from 0.28 without gradient reversal to 0.22 with GenAID-style adversarial training and 0.12 with gradient reversal, at similar accent accuracy. Whisper-large-v3-turbo gives the lowest SCSC (0.09) but lower unseen-speaker F1 (0.63).

**Objective TTS results (Table 4).**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDGM-Acc | 0.397 / 0.473 / 0.382 | 0.427 / 0.510 / 0.419 | 0.639 / 0.601 / 0.567 | 0.311 / 0.410 / 0.285 |
| DART | 0.321 / 0.350 / 0.344 | 0.357 / 0.403 / 0.387 | 0.555 / 0.557 / 0.524 | 0.153 / 0.165 / 0.150 |
| AccentBox | 0.404 / 0.401 / 0.337 | 0.434 / 0.448 / 0.376 | 0.550 / 0.509 / 0.512 | 0.535 / 0.593 / 0.555 |
| Joycent | 0.490 / 0.469 / 0.412 | 0.526 / 0.523 / 0.448 | 0.626 / 0.618 / 0.584 | 0.253 / 0.302 / 0.258 |

Joycent has the highest WU-CS in all three settings and the highest W-CS in two; DDGM-Acc is marginally higher on Unseen-Inherent W-CS (0.473 vs 0.469). Joycent has the highest speaker similarity in both unseen-speaker settings, while DDGM-Acc leads on Seen-Inherent (0.639 vs 0.626). DART has the lowest CER in every setting, with Joycent second, but DART also has much lower accent similarity. Per accent under Unseen-Cross (Table 5), Joycent leads on both accent metrics for Henan, Sichuan, Wuhan and Singapore; DDGM-Acc leads on Tianjin, and the Singapore margin is very small (0.427 vs 0.424 W-CS). Under Unseen-Inherent, the per-accent picture is mixed.

**Subjective results (Table 6).** Fifteen native Chinese listeners familiar with the accents rated naturalness (5-point MOS) and accent and speaker similarity (4-point SMOS-A, SMOS-S). Joycent has the highest MOS in all three settings, with the largest gap under Unseen-Cross (3.38 vs 2.62 for DDGM-Acc). It has the highest SMOS-S on Seen-Inherent and Unseen-Cross (DDGM-Acc is higher on Unseen-Inherent, 2.69 vs 2.62) and the highest SMOS-A in both unseen settings (DDGM-Acc is higher on Seen-Inherent, 3.06 vs 2.94).

**Ablations (Table 7).** Replacing CLN with additive conditioning at the encoder input roughly halves accent similarity (Unseen-Cross W-CS 0.412 to 0.208) while giving the lowest CER in the table (0.144). Removing gradient reversal lowers accent similarity in every setting (Unseen-Cross WU-CS 0.448 to 0.418). Moving accent conditioning from the first to the third Conformer block gives a small but consistent drop. Using Whisper-small or Whisper-large-v3-turbo inside WhisAID cuts accent similarity sharply; the large-v3-turbo variant has the highest speaker similarity in both unseen settings, which the authors read as weaker accent transfer. An F0 contour plot (Fig. 4) shows different pitch trajectories for one text and speaker under seven target accents.

## Novelty Assessment

The individual parts are known: gradient reversal for speaker suppression, conditional layer normalization and FiLM, the Grad-TTS decoder, and a Whisper encoder as a feature extractor. AccentBox already introduced a speaker-suppressed accent identifier as the source of accent embeddings. The new elements are the specific combination: a GRL-trained Whisper bottleneck in place of AccentBox's XLSR adversarial setup, and placing accent and speaker conditioning at different depths of the text encoder. The ablations support both choices, and the gain from CLN over additive conditioning is the largest single effect. The three-way evaluation split, which separates cross-accent from inherent-accent synthesis for unseen speakers, is a useful protocol contribution. MRAC is a regrouping of existing MagicHub and AISHELL-3 data rather than new recordings, and the paper does not state that the splits are released.

## Field Significance

Moderate — Joycent provides controlled evidence that where accent and speaker information enter a TTS encoder matters as much as how the accent embedding is learned, with an ablation that isolates each choice. Its result that the lowest-CER system is often the one that transfers the least accent is a useful caution for accent TTS evaluation. The evidence comes from one mid-sized Mandarin corpus and a diffusion acoustic model, so transfer to codec language models or other languages is untested.

## Claims

- **supports:** In prompt-based accent TTS, suppressing speaker information in the accent embedding improves accent transfer to unseen speakers.
  > *Evidence:* Gradient reversal against a speaker classifier lowers speaker clustering in the accent space (SCSC 0.28 to 0.12) at similar accent accuracy, and removing it from the full TTS system lowers WU-CS in all three settings (Unseen-Cross 0.448 to 0.418). *(§5.1.1, Table 2; §5.2.3, Table 7)*
- **supports:** Injecting accent and speaker conditioning at separate depths of the text encoder, accent early and speaker late, gives stronger accent control than adding the embeddings at the encoder input.
  > *Evidence:* Replacing the two CLN modules with additive input conditioning drops Unseen-Cross W-CS from 0.412 to 0.208; moving accent CLN from block 1 to block 3 gives a smaller, consistent drop. *(§3.3; §5.2.3, Table 7)*
- **complicates:** In accent TTS, a lower ASR error rate can signal weaker accent transfer rather than better quality, so intelligibility and accent similarity must be read together.
  > *Evidence:* The DART baseline and the no-CLN ablation have the lowest CER in every setting (for example 0.150 and 0.144 on Unseen-Cross) alongside much lower accent similarity than Joycent. *(§5.2.1, Table 4; §5.2.3, Table 7)*
- **complicates:** Adversarial speaker suppression reshapes the geometry of an accent embedding space more than it removes linearly recoverable speaker information.
  > *Evidence:* A linear speaker probe on frozen accent embeddings drops only from 0.46 to 0.42 F1 with gradient reversal, while the speaker silhouette coefficient falls from 0.28 to 0.12. *(§5.1.2, Table 3; Table 2)*
- **complicates:** A stronger or larger pretrained speech encoder does not reliably give better accent representations for synthesis.
  > *Evidence:* Whisper-large-v3-turbo gives the lowest speaker clustering (SCSC 0.09) but lower unseen-speaker accent F1 than Whisper-medium (0.63 vs 0.66), and its TTS variant loses most of the accent similarity (Unseen-Cross W-CS 0.173 vs 0.412). *(§5.1.1, Table 2; §5.2.3, Table 7)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

- All experiments use one corpus of regional Mandarin accents; Guangdong and Shanghai, two of the nine accents, are excluded from the objective evaluation because of dialect mixing, and the per-accent table covers five accents.
- Accent similarity is measured with WhisAID and its adversarial variant, both trained by the authors on the same data. Using two embedding models reduces bias toward one representation, but both share the training set and the Whisper-medium backbone, and the proposed system is conditioned on one of them.
- The ground-truth row has lower WavLM speaker similarity than several synthesized systems (0.574 vs 0.626 for Joycent on Seen-Inherent), so the speaker-similarity metric's absolute values are hard to interpret.
- Absolute quality is modest: the best MOS is 3.54 against 3.96 for ground truth, and Joycent's CER is roughly three times the ground-truth CER on Seen-Inherent (0.253 vs 0.085).
- Baselines are older diffusion and VAE accent systems retrained on MRAC; no codec language model or flow-matching zero-shot system is compared.
- The v4 PDF links only the demo page; an earlier arXiv abstract named a code repository, which the current version does not mention.
