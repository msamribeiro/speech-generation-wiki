> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Yuxiang Wang et al.** (The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen / Tencent Hunyuan / Shenzhen Loop Area Institute / Amphion Technology Co., Ltd. / Tsinghua University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10581) · Demo: ? · Code: ✓
>
> Turns a brittle inference-time "attend to paralinguistic cues" prompt into stable speech LM behaviour by on-policy self-distillation: the same model, shown the prompt, acts as a dense token-level teacher for its own prompt-free rollouts, with no curated dialogues, human labels or reward model.

## Problem

Speech LMs can often recognise paralinguistic cues (a child's voice, a fearful tone, a child audible in the background) on closed-form perception tests, but they rarely use those cues to change their replies in open-ended dialogue. The authors call this the perception-behaviour gap. On VoxSafeBench's child-voice task, Qwen3-Omni-thinking adjusts its answer to a safety-sensitive request voiced by a child only 6.11% of the time scaffold-free (§1, Table 1). Prepending a short instruction (a "scaffold") that tells the model to attend to paralinguistic and background cues lifts average Safety-Awareness Rate (SAR) from 14.6% to 29.0% and EchoMind from 3.27 to 4.31, which suggests the cue information is already latent. But a prompt reminder competes with persona, format, tool and safety instructions in deployed systems and loses influence in long contexts. Existing training routes have costs: SFT on paralinguistically annotated dialogues needs curated data, rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) collapses the scaffolded policy into single accepted targets, and GRPO with an external judge gives only a sparse scalar reward (§1, §2).

## Method

ParaBridge queries one speech LM under two contexts (§3.3). The scaffold-free context is the student; the same weights with the paralinguistic scaffold prepended form the teacher. For each training audio query, the student samples a response without the scaffold. Both views then score that fixed trajectory token by token. The teacher's full-vocabulary next-token distribution is detached with a stop-gradient, and the loss is the per-token symmetric Jensen-Shannon divergence between student and teacher, averaged over the sequence (Eq. 5-6, Algorithm 1). Because parameters are shared, the teacher drifts with the student across updates, so the target stays on-policy. Only the scaffold-free student is used at inference, so there is no extra inference cost or added parameters. The scaffolds are VoxSafeBench's fixed official prompts, one for speaker-state tasks and one for background-acoustic tasks (Appendix C.1).

![Figure 3: Overview of ParaBridge. For each audio example, a shared SLM produces a scaffolded teacher and a scaffold-free student. On student-sampled trajectories, token-level symmetric JSD aligns the student with the stop-gradient teacher (Eq. 6). Only the scaffold-free student is used at inference.](assets/2606.10581/figure-5.png)

The primary backbone is Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking (30.5B total, 3.3B active parameters), with MiMo-Audio-7B-Instruct ("MiMo-Audio-thinking") as a second backbone (§4.1, Appendix K). Training uses LoRA (rank 64, alpha 128) on all linear layers of the LLM, with the audio and vision encoders frozen. It runs on a generalised knowledge-distillation trainer with on-policy fraction 1.0, JSD mixture coefficient 0.5, temperature 1.2, 15 epochs and a global batch of 28 on 7 H20 GPUs plus one vLLM rollout GPU (Appendix A.1-A.2, Table 4). Training data are 1,000 audio queries drawn from the union of child-voice and child-presence pools. The authors built them with the VoxSafeBench construction pipeline from TTS-synthesised or de-identified public audio, disjoint from the test split, and checked them for prompt and acoustic leakage (§4.1, Appendix A.4). The system is evaluated on its text responses; spoken output is not generated or scored.

The baselines share the same query pool and scaffold. RFT samples scaffolded responses, keeps those a DeepSeek judge scores as safety-aware, and fine-tunes the scaffold-free context on them. GRPO (group size 8) optimises scaffolded rollouts against the same judge, because scaffold-free positive rollouts are too rare for stable advantages (§3.2, §4.1, Appendix A.3).

## Key Results

All behaviour metrics are LLM-judge scores on text responses: SAR on VoxSafeBench is graded by DeepSeek-chat, and EchoMind's four 1-5 dimensions are graded by GPT-4o (Appendix C). Results come from single near-greedy runs without error bars (Appendix L).

- **Scaffold-free dialogue behaviour (Table 1).** Average VoxSafeBench SAR rises from 14.64% to 40.33%, above RFT (31.64%), GRPO (15.67%) and the scaffolded baseline (29.02%). ParaBridge does not lead every sub-task: on Impaired_capacity, RFT scores 16.2% against ParaBridge's 14.93%. EchoMind's average rises from 3.27 to 3.92 (RFT 3.80, GRPO 3.28), with the largest change on speech-information relevance (2.64 to 3.46).
- **Scaffolded inference (Table 1).** Adding the scaffold to ParaBridge barely changes VoxSafeBench (40.33% to 39.85%) but still lifts out-of-domain EchoMind (3.92 to 4.39). On scaffolded Child_presence, GRPO (60.97%) is above ParaBridge (57.01%). GRPO is weak scaffold-free because it was trained on scaffolded rollouts, so its scaffold-free row is not a like-for-like test of a reward-based method.
- **Capability preservation (Table 1).** MMAU-Pro (-0.22), VoiceBench (-0.35) and GPQA (+0.09) stay within 0.4 points of the baseline. MMSU perception splits are essentially unchanged (-0.40, -0.03) and reasoning splits rise by about one point. RFT drops GPQA from 71.34% to 68.45%.
- **Generalisation (Table 2).** Trained only on child voice and child presence, the default model gains +26.15 SAR points on held-out Emotion and +8.48 on unseen Symbolic_background, relative to the scaffolded baseline. Safety-trained variants all improve the four EchoMind dimensions; RFT loses 18.12 points on Emotion. On MiMo-Audio the gains are consistent but smaller (e.g. Emotion 13.79 to 16.50), which the authors attribute to that backbone's smaller scaffold-induced gap.
- **Data and compute efficiency (§4.4-5.1, Figures 4-5).** SAR reaches 37.59% with 500 samples and 40.33% with 1,000. Doubling to 2,000 adds 1.35 points. ParaBridge reaches 40.3% in about 2.7 h of wall-clock training, reported as a 5.7x speedup over GRPO; RFT plateaus near 33.5%. A full ParaBridge run takes about 20 wall-clock hours (Appendix L).
- **Ablations (Table 3).** Forward KL (39.23%) and reverse KL (39.55%) sit just below JSD (40.33%). Replacing the audio teacher with a text teacher, which gets the transcript plus a verbal paralinguistic description, drops SAR to 29.19% and EchoMind to 3.54 (Table 3A). On benign counterfactuals with the cue removed, ParaBridge's average false-alarm rate is 3.36% against 6.05% for the scaffolded baseline and 6.42% for RFT (Table 3B). In three-turn dialogues, all methods degrade, and ParaBridge keeps the highest third-turn SAR (7.89% average against 4.95% for RFT and 4.09% for the scaffolded baseline), though absolute levels are low (Table 3C).
- **Mechanism (§5.2, Figure 6).** Linear CKA between baseline and ParaBridge hidden states stays above 0.998 through layer 46 and falls only at the last two MoE layers (0.966 and 0.907). The authors read this as a change to how existing audio representations are read out, not a rewrite of the backbone.

## Novelty Assessment

The on-policy self-distillation template is not new: the authors place ParaBridge alongside GKD, MiniLLM, OPSD, SDPO, Vision-OPD and the speech-side X-OPD, which all use one backbone, two contexts and a rollout-level divergence (§2). The new element is the choice of privileged context. A paralinguistic instruction scaffold serves as the teacher view so that cue-to-response behaviour transfers inside a speech LM, applied here as a new training procedure on an unchanged architecture. The comparison with RFT and GRPO on a shared query pool and scaffold is a useful controlled contrast between dense distribution matching, selected targets and sparse rewards. The audio-versus-text-teacher ablation is the strongest evidence that the gain comes from the audio-conditioned teacher rather than from injected verbal labels. The evaluation is narrow in domain, though. Training data follow the VoxSafeBench construction pipeline, the scaffolds are VoxSafeBench's own prompts, the headline benchmark is VoxSafeBench (which shares its first author with this paper), and all behaviour scores come from LLM judges.

## Field Significance

Moderate — The paper demonstrates that latent paralinguistic sensitivity in a speech LM can be converted into prompt-free dialogue behaviour by distilling the model's own scaffolded distribution, without labelled dialogues or a reward model, and that this preserves general audio and text benchmarks. It is primarily evidence that the perception-behaviour gap is partly a read-out problem rather than a perception deficit, shown on one large backbone and a smaller transfer to a second. The authors state that the paper is accepted to EMNLP 2026; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** Speech LMs can encode paralinguistic cues that they fail to act on in open-ended dialogue, and a short instruction to attend to those cues exposes much of the latent behaviour.
  > *Evidence:* Prepending the paralinguistic scaffold lifts Qwen3-Omni-thinking from 14.64% to 29.02% average VoxSafeBench SAR and from 3.27 to 4.31 EchoMind average, while MMSU paralinguistic perception accuracy is already about 53%. *(§1, Table 1)*
- **supports:** Dense on-policy self-distillation from a model's own prompted distribution can internalise prompt-elicited cue-aware behaviour in speech LMs more effectively than rejection-sampling fine-tuning or judge-based policy-gradient training on the same data.
  > *Evidence:* Scaffold-free VoxSafeBench SAR reaches 40.33% for ParaBridge against 31.64% for RFT and 15.67% for GRPO, and EchoMind average reaches 3.92 against 3.80 and 3.28, with the same 1,000-query pool and scaffold. *(§4.2, Table 1)*
- **refines:** When distilling paralinguistic behaviour inside a speech LM, the teacher must hear the audio; a text description of the cue is a much weaker teacher signal than the audio-conditioned distribution, while the choice of divergence matters little.
  > *Evidence:* A text teacher using the transcript plus a paralinguistic description drops SAR from 40.33% to 29.19% and EchoMind from 3.92 to 3.54, whereas forward and reverse KL stay within about 1.1 SAR points of JSD. *(§4.5, Table 3A)*
- **supports:** Targeted behavioural post-training of a speech LM can preserve general audio-understanding and text-reasoning ability when the representational change stays confined to the final layers.
  > *Evidence:* MMAU-Pro, VoiceBench and GPQA move by -0.22, -0.35 and +0.09 points, and layer-wise CKA stays above 0.998 through layer 46, dropping only at layers 47 (0.966) and 48 (0.907). *(§4.2, §5.2, Table 1, Figure 6)*
- **complicates:** Cue-aware behaviour gained by training on short single-turn queries degrades sharply when the paralinguistic cue must persist across multi-turn context, and the size of the gain depends on how much prompt-elicited behaviour the backbone already has.
  > *Evidence:* Third-turn SAR averages 7.89% for ParaBridge (7.16% Child_presence, 8.61% Child_voice), against single-turn scaffold-free SAR of 58.56% and 18.43% on the same two tasks; and on MiMo-Audio, which has a smaller scaffolded gap, gains are much smaller (e.g. Emotion SAR 13.79% to 16.50%). *(§4.3, §4.5, Table 2C, Table 3C)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> All behaviour gains are measured by LLM judges (DeepSeek-chat for SAR, GPT-4o for EchoMind) on text responses, with no human rating. Training data, scaffolds and the headline benchmark all come from the VoxSafeBench construction pipeline, so the strongest results are in-domain to the training setup.

- **Backbone scope.** Headline numbers come from one backbone with an unusually large scaffold-induced gap. MiMo-Audio gains are smaller, and the authors do not claim benefits for backbones whose gap is small (§7).
- **Cue and language scope.** Training covers two VoxSafeBench axes (child voice, child presence) in a Chinese-English bilingual setting. Sarcasm, politeness, finer age ranges, accent, intoxication, fatigue, other languages and dialects are not validated (§7).
- **Multi-turn persistence.** Third-turn SAR stays below 9% for every method, so robustness to long dialogue context remains largely unsolved (Table 3C).
- **Residual over-conditioning.** False alarms on benign audio fall but remain non-zero (3.36% average), and the authors note the same mechanism could profile speakers along sensitive attributes (Appendix J.1).
- **Speech output not evaluated.** Only the model's text response is trained and scored. Whether cue-aware replies also change the prosody or style of generated speech is not tested.
- **Statistical reporting.** Single deterministic runs without error bars; the nested data-scaling curve is the only indication of variance (Appendix L).
