> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Rongxiang Wang et al.** (Argmax, Inc. / University of Virginia / Bilkent University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16989) · Demo: ? · Code: ✗
>
> Introduces LACI, an inference-only harness that watches emergent text-audio alignment heads in autoregressive TTS models, detects skips and hallucinations within seconds of generated audio, rolls back to the failure onset and regenerates under a temporary attention mask, closing the long-form reliability gap of Qwen3-TTS without retraining.

## Problem

Open-weights autoregressive TTS models such as Qwen3-TTS and VoxCPM2 score well on short prompts but become unreliable when the input text grows to thousands of words or when voice cloning uses minutes of reference audio. On the AppTek Call Center data, Qwen3-TTS-0.6B's worst-of-N WER (the WER of the worst of 10 RNG seeds, higher is worse) rises from 5.4% on prompts under 500 words to 35.2% on prompts of 1500 words or more *(§3.1, Table 2)*. Existing remedies fall short: training-time alignment objectives (location-sensitive attention, duration models, monotonic-alignment objectives in codec LMs) require retraining; best-of-N candidate selection with an external ASR system scales inference cost linearly and cannot repair a failure that persists across seeds; and Attention-Constrained Inference (ACI), the closest inference-time method, applies an always-on hard attention mask that introduces new errors where the baseline was correct and has no failure detection *(§2)*.

## Method

The paper first defines two failure types that account for the short-to-long-form gap: a **skip**, a contiguous deletion of 10 or more words, and a **hallucination**, a contiguous run of 20 or more substituted and inserted words. Other errors occur at the same rate in short- and long-form generation and are treated as the model's accuracy floor *(§3.1)*.

**Failure detection.** Analysing attention weights, the authors find heads in Qwen3-TTS-0.6B with emergent text-audio alignment whose diagonal pattern is consistent across prompts and speakers in accurate sessions and breaks in inaccurate ones; the same analysis locates alignment heads in Qwen3-TTS-1.7B and VoxCPM2 *(§3.2, Figure 2)*. The detector splits the input text into equal-sized token bins and counts how many generation steps the alignment head's argmax position spends on each bin. A skip is flagged when the reading position moves past a bin that received far fewer steps than earlier bins; a hallucination is flagged when the position stops advancing for much longer than a bin normally takes; generation ends once the last bin has received its share of attention. The detector uses only attention weights available during streaming generation, and the paper reports detection latency in seconds of generated audio ("a few seconds") rather than wall-clock time. The exact instability measure, thresholds and recovery settings are deferred to code that the authors say will be released on publication *(§3.2)*.

**Failure recovery.** On detection, LACI (Localized Attention-Constrained Inference) rolls back to the failure onset, reseeds the RNG, and applies the ACI hard attention mask on the alignment heads only for a probationary window until generation moves past the detection point. A cap on retries bounds overhead when failures persist *(§3.3)*. The method changes no weights and adds no model.

![Fig. 3. Detecting and recovering from a skip failure. At each generation step the system attends to one text position, the argmax over attention weights. LACI monitors cumulative attention coverage across bins of input text tokens. When a bin crosses the coverage threshold before the bins before it do, a skip is detected. LACI rolls back and temporarily applies a hard attention mask to regularize attention coverage and recover.](assets/2609.16989/figure-3.png)

**wSIM.** To measure voice cloning reliability over long outputs, the paper introduces wSIM, a sliding-window speaker similarity (8-second windows, 4-second stride) using the same WavLM-Large speaker-verification embeddings as the Seed-TTS SIM protocol. Worst-of-N wSIM is the lowest window score across all windows and RNG seeds for a given reference and prompt; it targets short spans of identity switching or distortion that whole-clip SIM averages away. The authors state they manually verified that the lowest wSIM scores correspond to real identity switches or severe distortions *(§4)*.

**Setup.** Evaluation uses 144 prompts curated from the AppTek Call Center dataset in three length buckets (speech turn, 15 to 316 words; full session, 331 to 1579 words; multi-session, 1349 to 2449 words), each run with 10 RNG seeds, on Qwen3-TTS-0.6B-CustomVoice, Qwen3-TTS-1.7B-CustomVoice and VoxCPM2. Voice cloning uses Qwen3-TTS-0.6B-Base with references from a single speaker turn up to 120 seconds, generating the last minute of each session. WER uses Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v2 transcripts with Whisper-style text normalization *(§4, Table 1)*.

## Key Results

**Long-form reliability (Qwen3-TTS-0.6B, Table 2; mean / worst-of-N WER %).**

| Prompt length (words) | Baseline | ACI | LACI |
|---|---|---|---|
| < 500 | 2.7 / 5.4 | 2.7 / 6.0 | 2.7 / 5.4 |
| 500-1000 | 3.5 / 6.5 | 3.4 / 9.3 | 2.9 / 3.9 |
| 1000-1500 | 4.2 / 11.2 | 2.8 / 6.0 | 2.5 / 3.3 |
| ≥ 1500 | 16.6 / 35.2 | 3.7 / 11.5 | 2.9 / 3.4 |

LACI holds mean WER between 2.5% and 2.9% and worst-of-N WER between 3.3% and 5.4% across all buckets; its worst-of-N WER on prompts of 1500 words or more (3.4%) is lower than the baseline's on prompts under 500 words (5.4%). On short prompts LACI ties the baseline exactly, while ACI's always-on mask raises worst-of-N WER from 5.4% to 6.0% (under 500 words) and from 6.5% to 9.3% (500 to 1000 words). LACI's worst-of-N WER is the lowest of the three systems in every row of Table 2 *(§5, Table 2)*. On the 146 (prompt, seed) pairs where the baseline has at least one skip or hallucination, LACI brings all of them into a low band, whereas ACI lowers the median but leaves a long upper tail *(§5, Figure 4)*.

**Generalization (Table 3).** On Qwen3-TTS-1.7B, baseline worst-of-N WER is 45.7% (1000 to 1500 words) and 47.8% (1500 or more); LACI reduces these to 3.0% and 4.2%, while ACI leaves 8.5% and 22.7%. LACI's worst-of-N WER is the lowest in all four 1.7B rows. On VoxCPM2 the picture is mixed: LACI gives the lowest worst-of-N WER under 500 words (5.2%) and from 500 to 1000 words (4.4%), but in the 1000 to 1300 word bucket ACI is better (4.3% vs. LACI 8.1% vs. baseline 16.3%), and beyond 1300 words no method repairs the model (worst-of-N WER 35.0% baseline, 36.6% ACI, 32.3% LACI; mean 18.1%, 10.0% and 15.9%). The fraction of (prompt, seed) pairs with a skip or hallucination falls from 15.3% to 3.3% (LACI) and 2.0% (ACI) at 1000 to 1300 words, and from 70% to 57% (LACI) and 34% (ACI) beyond 1300 words; the authors attribute the residual tail to a coherence limit of VoxCPM2 rather than a detection gap *(§5, Table 3)*.

**Voice cloning (Qwen3-TTS-0.6B-Base, Figure 5).** The baseline's catastrophic failure rate (share of generations with WER above 30%) grows from 5% with references under 15 seconds to 26% with references near 120 seconds; LACI keeps it below 5% at every reference length and below 1% at the longest. At 120 seconds of reference, baseline worst-of-N wSIM collapses to 0.01 while LACI reaches 0.47; LACI also improves worst-of-N whole-clip SIM, though the corresponding values are given only in plot form *(§5, Figure 5)*.

Overhead is described as negligible, but no runtime, real-time factor, or retry-count statistics are reported. Comparisons are fair in the sense that all three systems share the same model, prompts and seeds; no comparison against best-of-N selection or training-time alignment methods is run. No human listening tests are reported.

## Novelty Assessment

The recovery mask is taken directly from ACI, and alignment-head monitoring has precedent in earlier decoder-only TTS work, so the novelty lies in the control loop: a lightweight attention-coverage detector with explicit skip and hallucination criteria, localized rollback to the failure onset, reseeding, and a mask applied only for a probationary window with bounded retries. This is a new inference procedure that turns an always-on constraint into a detect-rollback-guard scheme, and the ablation against always-on ACI shows why the localization matters (ACI hurts short prompts, LACI does not). wSIM is a simple but useful evaluation change: windowed scoring with a worst-window aggregate exposes transient identity failures that whole-clip SIM hides. Both contributions are incremental in mechanism but practically significant, since they need no retraining and transfer across model sizes and families.

## Field Significance

moderate — The paper demonstrates that the long-form degradation of current autoregressive TTS models is largely attributable to recoverable attention misalignment rather than a hard capacity limit, at least for Qwen3-TTS, and provides an inference-only repair that leaves short-form behaviour untouched. Its worst-of-N reporting over 10 seeds and the windowed wSIM metric also offer a reliability-oriented evaluation protocol for long-form generation and long-reference voice cloning, though the VoxCPM2 results show the repair does not reach every model's failure tail.

## Claims

- **supports:** Long-form failures of autoregressive TTS models can be detected from emergent text-audio alignment heads during generation and repaired at inference time without retraining.
  > *Evidence:* An attention-coverage detector flags skips and hallucinations and a rollback with a temporary hard mask reduces Qwen3-TTS-0.6B worst-of-N WER on prompts of 1500+ words from 35.2% to 3.4% and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B from 47.8% to 4.2%. *(§3.2, §3.3, Tables 2 and 3)*
- **refines:** Attention constraints on autoregressive TTS should be applied conditionally on detected failures rather than at every step, because always-on constraints introduce errors on inputs the unconstrained model already handles.
  > *Evidence:* Always-on ACI raises Qwen3-TTS-0.6B worst-of-N WER from 5.4% to 6.0% under 500 words and from 6.5% to 9.3% at 500 to 1000 words, while the localized variant ties the baseline at 5.4% and improves to 3.9%; ACI also leaves a long upper tail on failed (prompt, seed) pairs that LACI removes. *(§5, Table 2, Figure 4)*
- **complicates:** Inference-time alignment repair cannot fix long-form failures that stem from a model's coherence limits rather than from recoverable misalignment.
  > *Evidence:* On VoxCPM2 beyond 1300 words, worst-of-N WER stays at 32.3% with LACI and 36.6% with ACI versus 35.0% for the baseline, and the share of seeds with skips or hallucinations only falls from 70% to 57% with LACI; in the 1000 to 1300 word bucket always-on ACI (4.3%) outperforms LACI (8.1%). *(§5, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Longer reference audio does not automatically improve zero-shot voice cloning in autoregressive TTS, because generation reliability degrades as the conditioning context grows.
  > *Evidence:* Qwen3-TTS-0.6B-Base's rate of generations with WER above 30% rises from 5% with references under 15 seconds to 26% near 120 seconds; with LACI it stays below 5% at all lengths and below 1% at the longest. *(§5, Figure 5a)*
- **supports:** Whole-utterance speaker similarity understates speaker-identity failures in long generations, and windowed similarity with a worst-window aggregate exposes them.
  > *Evidence:* An 8-second, 4-second-stride sliding-window SIM shows baseline worst-of-N wSIM collapsing to 0.01 at 120 seconds of reference versus 0.47 with LACI; the authors manually checked that the lowest windows correspond to identity switches or severe distortion. *(§4, §5, Figure 5c)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] Reproducibility depends on unreleased code
> The instability measure, detector thresholds, bin sizes, mask window and retry cap are not specified in the paper and are deferred to code that will be released only upon publication *(§3.2)*. Overhead is described as negligible but never quantified.

- Evaluation uses a single domain (English customer-support calls from AppTek) and 144 prompts; generalization to audiobooks, other languages or expressive content is untested.
- How worst-of-N WER is aggregated across prompts within a length bucket is not spelled out beyond "worst-of-N across 10 RNG seeds".
- LACI does not repair VoxCPM2 beyond 1300 words, and always-on ACI beats it in the 1000 to 1300 word VoxCPM2 bucket, so the method's benefit is model-dependent.
- Alignment heads are identified by manual analysis for each model; whether this generalizes to architectures without clearly emergent alignment heads is open.
- wSIM is validated only by the authors' manual inspection of the lowest windows, not by a listening study; no human listening tests of naturalness or speaker similarity are reported.
- Voice cloning experiments use only Qwen3-TTS-0.6B-Base, and whole-clip SIM values are shown only in a plot.
- The future-work proposal of using LACI to generate contrasting rollouts for reinforcement learning is not tested *(§6)*.
