> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Qirui Zhan et al.** (Northwestern Polytechnical University / Nanjing University / yutuzhineng / Shenzhen Pimei Technology Co., Ltd.) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09947) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ?
>
> Builds a locally deployable, open-source multi-agent pipeline that writes 15-attribute segment-level annotations (speaker profile, prosody, voice quality, emotion, paralinguistics, acoustic scene, captions) for controllable speech generation training data, and releases a human-annotated benchmark (SA-Bench) with a three-part evaluation protocol (SA-Eval) to score such annotators.

## Problem

Controllable speech generation needs training data labelled well beyond transcripts: who speaks, how the utterance is delivered, which acoustic scene surrounds it, and what it implies in context. Existing data pipelines either rely on manual correction (costly at scale), on paid hosted multimodal APIs (recurring cost and throughput limits), or on fixed processing chains that cannot recover from errors across stages. Labels for long recordings also drift, because adjacent turns of the same speaker are usually processed independently. On the evaluation side, resources are split by task (diarization sets, audio-language reasoning benchmarks), and no single test set scores speaker-aware timelines, finite-set attributes and open-ended descriptions under one schema.

## Method

SpeechAnnotator is a training-free, twelve-stage pipeline assembled from open-source models. All intermediate artifacts (enhanced waveform, timeline, segment audio, transcripts, prior evidence, contracts, predictions, reviewed records) share a segment key in a common state.

- **Frontend.** FullSubNet enhances the audio. MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize produces speaker-homogeneous segments, speaker IDs and initial transcripts, which Qwen3-Omni then refines.
- **Prior evidence.** DataSpeech (the annotation toolkit from Parler-TTS) estimates pitch, energy, loudness and speed on enhanced segments. Emotion2vec and a sound event detection model run on the original audio to keep affective and background cues.
- **Planning Agent.** Reads the segment table, transcript, priors, a recording-level summary, same-speaker history and neighbouring segments, then writes a field-specific "contract" naming admissible evidence, context scope and conflict rules (for example, speaker-profile fields may use speaker history; scene fields must stay grounded in acoustic or SED evidence).
- **Labeling Agent.** Qwen3-Omni receives the segment audio, transcript, selected evidence and contract, and predicts the directly observable attributes.
- **Review Agent and bounded review loop.** Qwen3.6-27B checks each prediction against retained evidence, speaker history, neighbouring segments and related fields. Unsupported or inconsistent fields (an unsupported background sound, an emotion that conflicts with prosody, speaker-trait drift) get a field-specific revision request. Only those fields are relabelled; frontend outputs and accepted labels stay fixed. The loop stops on acceptance or when the revision budget runs out.
- **Deterministic output.** Normalizes speaker IDs and schema fields, builds the caption and contextual-inference fields from the reviewed state, and serializes JSON.

The 15 evaluated attributes fall into six groups: Semantic Understanding (caption, contextual inference), Speaker Profile (perceived gender, perceived age, accent), Prosody and Delivery (pitch, speed, rhythm, tone), Voice Quality and Articulation (texture, pronunciation), Affective and Paralinguistic Cues (emotion, paralinguistic cues) and Acoustic Scene (environment, background sound).

**SA-Bench** contains 8.87 hours of Chinese-dominant audio across nine source formats (Live Commentary is the largest at 1.46 h, 16.5%). Five trained annotators labelled boundaries, speaker IDs, transcripts and all 15 attributes; two inspectors reviewed every record. **SA-Eval** has three separately reported parts. Timeline-Eval uses CER, concatenated minimum-permutation CER (cpCER, adds speaker attribution) and time-constrained minimum-permutation CER (tcpCER, adds temporal validity). Closed-Eval scores perceived gender and age by canonicalized exact match. Open-Eval scores the 13 open-ended attributes by clipped cosine similarity between mean-pooled last-layer hidden states of a frozen Qwen3.6-27B, with no generative judging. Missing or unparsable outputs score zero.

## Key Results

All systems are evaluated zero-shot on SA-Bench, each run five times. Baselines are four commercial audio-capable models used as black boxes: Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, Qwen3.5-Omni-plus and Seed2.0 Lite.

**Timeline recovery (Table 1).** SpeechAnnotator has the lowest CER (12.42%) and tcpCER (33.69%) of the six systems. Qwen3.5-Omni-plus has the lowest cpCER (26.31% versus 28.16%). Most of SpeechAnnotator's timeline quality comes from its MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize frontend: the standalone MOSS output scores 12.84% CER and 34.08% tcpCER, so Qwen3-Omni refinement adds 0.42 and 0.39 points. The large tcpCER gap to the best commercial system (19.92 points below Seed2.0 Lite's 53.61%) therefore reflects a dedicated diarization model against general-purpose LLMs prompted for diarized transcripts.

**Attribute annotation (Table 2, fixed reference timeline and transcripts).** SpeechAnnotator's macro average across the 15 fields is 81.27%, second to Gemini 2.5 Pro (81.73%) and ahead of Seed2.0 Lite (79.40%), Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (78.25%) and Qwen3.5-Omni-plus (72.69%). It has the highest mean on seven fields: pitch, speed, rhythm, tone, texture, pronunciation and paralinguistic cues. The paralinguistic-cues lead is marginal (89.19% versus 89.10% for Gemini 2.5 Pro). The largest field margins over the best baseline are on speed (92.30% versus 70.70%, +21.60), texture (+9.38), rhythm (+8.09) and pronunciation (+7.49), all fields where DataSpeech acoustic statistics are available as evidence. The system trails clearly on environment (55.04%, 18.96 points below Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview), background sound (67.07% versus 82.60%), perceived age (75.57%, 16.63 points below Seed2.0 Lite), contextual inference (73.60% versus 86.40%) and emotion (62.85% versus 73.90%). SpeechAnnotator's run-to-run standard deviation (0.34 on the average) is much lower than the commercial systems' (1.11 to 1.58).

**Ablations (Table 3).** Removing context-aware planning causes the largest drop (macro average 79.79%, -1.48), concentrated in caption (-4.77) and contextual inference (-3.83). Removing prior evidence gives 80.07% (-1.20), mostly on acoustic-scene, emotion, speed and pitch fields; caption rises slightly without priors (87.16% versus 87.06%). Removing the review loop gives 80.42% (-0.85), with the largest field drops on perceived age (-2.59) and perceived gender (-1.91), and a small rise in run-to-run variance (0.34 to 0.39). Several fields barely move or slightly improve without the loop (tone, pronunciation, background sound).

Comparisons are fair in inputs (same recordings, segments and schema for all systems), but the benchmark is a single 8.87-hour Chinese-dominant set annotated for this paper, and Open-Eval scores depend on one embedding model.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is primarily engineering integration. Every model in the pipeline is off-the-shelf (FullSubNet, MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize, Qwen3-Omni, Qwen3.6-27B, DataSpeech, Emotion2vec, an SED model), and planner/labeller/reviewer agent loops follow existing agentic audio frameworks the paper cites. What is new is the field-level design: per-field evidence contracts and a review loop that relabels only failing fields rather than rerunning the pipeline. The ablations show each component helps, but each gain is small (0.85 to 1.48 macro points). SA-Bench is a useful new resource because it scores diarized timelines and 15 descriptive attributes together. Open-Eval's hidden-state cosine similarity is a pragmatic choice, but the paper does not validate it against human similarity judgments. No speech generation system is trained on the annotations, so the downstream value for controllable TTS is asserted rather than measured.

## Field Significance

Moderate. The paper provides an open, locally runnable alternative to hosted multimodal APIs for writing rich style labels on speech data. It shows that injecting explicit acoustic statistics as evidence makes an open audio LLM pipeline clearly more accurate than general-purpose commercial models on prosody and voice-quality descriptions. SA-Bench and SA-Eval can serve as a shared test for annotation pipelines that feed instruction- and description-conditioned TTS. The authors state that the paper is accepted to NCMMSC 2026; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** Feeding explicit signal-level acoustic measurements into an audio language model as labelling evidence improves automatic descriptions of prosody and voice quality over audio-only multimodal prompting.
  > *Evidence:* With DataSpeech pitch/energy/speed statistics as priors, SpeechAnnotator scores 92.30% on speed against at most 70.70% for four commercial audio LLMs, and leads on pitch, rhythm, tone, texture and pronunciation; removing the priors lowers speed to 89.10% and pitch to 84.80%. *(§4.2, Table 2; §4.3, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** General-purpose audio language models remain uneven annotators of speech style: no single system is best across speaker, prosody, affect and scene attributes, so pipelines built on them produce labels of very different reliability per field.
  > *Evidence:* On SA-Bench, the macro averages of the top two systems differ by only 0.46 points, yet SpeechAnnotator leads on seven fields while trailing by 18.96 points on environment and 16.63 on perceived age; emotion stays at or below 73.90% for every system. *(§4.2, Table 2)*
- **supports:** Recording-level and cross-segment context improves automatic annotation of semantic and speaker-dependent attributes in long-form speech, while locally audible attributes depend little on it.
  > *Evidence:* Removing the global summary, speaker history and neighbouring segments from planning lowers caption by 4.77, contextual inference by 3.83 and perceived age by 3.37 points, while Prosody and Delivery falls by only 0.76 and voice-quality and scene fields change little. *(§4.3, Table 3)*
- **refines:** Targeted self-review of LLM-generated speech labels gives modest accuracy gains, concentrated on speaker-profile fields, rather than uniform improvement across attributes.
  > *Evidence:* Removing the Review Agent's field-level relabelling lowers the macro average by 0.85 points (81.27% to 80.42%), led by perceived age (-2.59) and perceived gender (-1.91), while tone, pronunciation and background sound are marginally higher without the loop. *(§4.3, Table 3)*
- **complicates:** Diarized-transcript quality in automatic speech-data pipelines depends mainly on a dedicated diarization frontend, not on the general audio LLM layered on top.
  > *Evidence:* Standalone MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize reaches 34.08% tcpCER, close to SpeechAnnotator's 33.69% after Qwen3-Omni refinement, whereas commercial audio LLMs prompted for diarized transcripts score 53.61% to 69.59% tcpCER. *(§4.2, Table 1)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

The annotations are motivated by controllable speech generation, but no TTS or voice-design model is trained or evaluated on them. Whether the attribute gains (for example on speed or texture) translate into better controllability or instruction following in a generator is untested.

- **Single, Chinese-dominant benchmark.** SA-Bench is a single 8.87-hour set annotated for this paper, and the recording sources are not described beyond their nine formats. The paper lists generalization across languages and domains as future work.
- **Unvalidated open-ended metric.** Open-Eval uses cosine similarity of Qwen3.6-27B hidden states, clipped at zero, without a correlation study against human judgments of description similarity. The same model family also serves as the Review Agent, which could favour SpeechAnnotator's phrasing; the paper does not examine this.
- **Weak scene, emotion and age fields.** Environment (55.04%), emotion (62.85%) and background sound (67.07%) remain the weakest fields. The authors attribute the environment errors to inference drifting toward transcript semantics on short segments.
- **Commercial baselines prompted once per recording.** Baselines predict all 15 attributes per recording in one prompt and receive no acoustic priors, while SpeechAnnotator runs per-field contracts and review. The comparison therefore tests pipelines, not models.
- **Cost not measured.** Local deployment is the motivating advantage over hosted APIs, but the paper reports no throughput, latency or compute cost.
- **Release.** Code and SA-Bench are stated to be open-sourced at a GitHub repository; an online demo is available.
