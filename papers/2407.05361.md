> [!abstract] arXiv · 2024 · Preprint
> **He et al.** (CUHK Shenzhen / CAS / Shanghai AI Lab) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05361) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✓
>
> Emilia introduces the first large-scale, multilingual, in-the-wild speech dataset for speech generation (101k hours across six languages), together with Emilia-Pipe, an open-source preprocessing pipeline that converts raw internet speech into annotated training data at above-realtime throughput.

## Problem

Speech generation models trained on audiobook corpora (LibriTTS, MLS, Libri-Light) produce formal reading-style speech because that is what those datasets contain. Real human speech in casual and conversational contexts exhibits breathing, pauses, repetitions, speed changes, and emotional variation that audiobook recordings systematically lack. Prior large-scale in-the-wild pipelines (AutoPrepWild, WenetSpeech4TTS) were either proprietary, monolingual (Chinese only), or comparatively small (39 hours and 12k hours, respectively). There was no open, multilingual, large-scale corpus of spontaneous, in-the-wild speech suitable for training TTS systems targeting human-like naturalness.

## Method

The Emilia dataset is constructed via Emilia-Pipe, a six-stage preprocessing pipeline applied to raw speech harvested from video platforms and podcasts across talk shows, interviews, debates, sports commentary, and audiobooks.

The pipeline stages are: (1) Standardisation, converting all audio to 24 kHz mono WAV at -20 dBFS; (2) Source separation using UVR-MDX-Net to remove background music and noise; (3) Speaker diarisation using pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1 to segment long-form audio by speaker identity; (4) Fine-grained segmentation via Silero-VAD to split any segment exceeding 30 seconds and concatenate consecutive same-speaker segments into 3-30 second utterances; (5) ASR transcription using Whisper-Medium via the WhisperX backend (up to 4x faster than the official Whisper implementation) with VAD bypassed using stage-4 boundaries; (6) Filtering by language identification confidence (threshold: 80%), DNSMOS P.835 OVRL quality score (threshold: 3.0), and average phone duration outlier removal.

Running on 8 NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs, Emilia-Pipe processes approximately 2.5 hours of raw data per minute, well above real-time. Applied to approximately 600 hours of raw data in a benchmark test, the pipeline retained 29.43% of audio while lifting the average DNSMOS score from 2.50 to 3.26.

The resulting Emilia dataset contains 101,654 hours of speech at 24 kHz across six languages: English (dominant), Chinese, German, French, Japanese, and Korean. To validate the dataset, two TTS models are trained (AR+SoundStorm and VoiceBox/NAR) on Emilia and compared against counterparts trained on MLS on both formal (LibriSpeech-Test) and spontaneous (Emilia-Test) evaluation sets.

## Key Results

On LibriSpeech-Test (formal reading-style), models trained on Emilia and MLS perform comparably: the AR model achieves 8.4% WER on Emilia versus 8.9% on MLS, and the NAR model 7.2% versus 6.1%. Speaker similarity (SIM-O, SMOS) is also similar across both training sets, confirming that in-the-wild data after Emilia-Pipe processing reaches audiobook-quality training data parity for formal speech generation.

The differentiating gains appear on Emilia-Test (spontaneous speaking styles): the AR model trained on Emilia achieves 6.6% WER (vs. 7.7% on MLS) and improves FSD from 20.76 to 12.73, indicating better coverage of diverse spontaneous speech distributions. CMOS improves from 0.09 to 0.19 for the AR model. NAR models show smaller differentiation between training sets on this axis.

Multilingual TTS results (Table 5) confirm functional performance across all six languages, with WER ranging from 3.6% (Japanese, AR) to 17.5% (French, NAR), and speaker similarity (SIM-O) consistently above 0.55.

The Emilia dataset achieves a DNSMOS P.835 OVRL score of 3.26 (Table 3), comparable to LibriTTS (3.25) and above all existing in-the-wild datasets (GigaSpeech: 2.52), despite deriving entirely from raw internet audio.

## Novelty Assessment

The dataset itself is the primary contribution: 101k hours of open, multilingual, spontaneous speech is substantially larger and more diverse than any prior publicly available corpus for TTS. The second contribution, Emilia-Pipe, is an engineering integration of established components (vocal separation, speaker diarisation, VAD, Whisper ASR, DNSMOS filtering) into a cohesive and open-source pipeline with above-realtime throughput. None of the individual pipeline stages are novel; the value is in their combination, the quality filtering design, and the decision to open-source the pipeline and dataset together. The validation experiments confirm the dataset's effectiveness rather than proposing a new TTS architecture.

## Field Significance

> [!tip]
> High — Emilia fills a structural gap in the speech generation training ecosystem: a large-scale, multilingual, spontaneous-speech corpus that is both open-access and paired with a reproducible preprocessing pipeline. Prior work at comparable scale relied on audiobooks (MLS, Libri-Light) or proprietary pipelines (AutoPrepWild, WenetSpeech4TTS). By demonstrating that in-the-wild data processed through Emilia-Pipe can match audiobook-derived data on formal benchmarks while substantially improving coverage of spontaneous speaking styles, the paper provides a credible path toward TTS systems that generalise beyond studio and audiobook distributions.

## Claims

- Training speech generation models on large-scale in-the-wild data can match audiobook-derived training data quality on formal evaluation benchmarks while improving performance on spontaneous speech generation. *(§4.2, Table 4)*
- Automatic preprocessing pipelines combining source separation, speaker diarisation, VAD segmentation, ASR transcription, and quality filtering can transform raw internet audio into high-quality TTS training data at above-realtime throughput. *(§2, Table 2)*
- Acoustic diversity in training data, as measured in self-supervised feature space, correlates with improved distribution coverage for spontaneous speech generation. *(§3.2.2, Figure 3)*
- A single multilingual corpus spanning six languages enables functional zero-shot TTS across all covered languages when trained with autoregressive and flow-matching architectures. *(§4.3, Table 5)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning]
> The filtering step retains only 29.43% of raw audio, which means Emilia-Pipe discards roughly 70% of input data. The quality threshold (DNSMOS > 3.0) may systematically exclude emotionally expressive or stylistically rich speech that scores lower on the OVRL metric, potentially biasing the retained data toward cleaner but less spontaneous recordings.

Subjective evaluations were conducted only on English with twelve judges and a small sample (8 utterances per test set), limiting the reliability of CMOS and SMOS estimates. Multilingual evaluation (§4.3) relies entirely on objective metrics (WER, SIM-O, FSD) without subjective naturalness assessment. The paper does not report confidence intervals or statistical significance tests for subjective scores. Emilia-Test is constructed from the same distribution as the training data, which may favour models trained on Emilia and understate MLS-trained models' generalisation. Dataset curation and licensing considerations for internet-sourced audio are acknowledged but not discussed in depth.
