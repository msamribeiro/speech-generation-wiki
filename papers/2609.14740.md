> [!abstract] arXiv · 2026 · Preprint
> **Jingbin Hu et al.** (Northwestern Polytechnical University / Shenzhen Pimei Technology Co., Ltd. / Nanjing University) · [→ Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14740) · Demo: ✓ · Code: ✗
>
> A challenge system for the ISCSLP 2026 CoT-TTS Challenge that improves the official chain-of-thought TTS baseline through three successive stages: continued pre-training on cleaned data, supervised fine-tuning on distilled expressive speech, and preference optimization with LLM-ranked preference pairs.

## Problem

In conversational settings the appropriate speaking style is implied by the dialogue history rather than stated as an explicit instruction, which single-turn instruction-following TTS does not handle. The ISCSLP 2026 CoT-TTS Challenge frames this as context-aware TTS: the model first writes a chain-of-thought (CoT) analysis of the speaker's intent, emotion and situation from the dialogue history, then synthesizes the target sentence conditioned on that analysis. The authors identify three weaknesses of the official Track 1 baseline: its movie-domain training data is noisy and mislabelled (transcription errors, CoT annotations inconsistent with the history), supervised fine-tuning alone leaves high generation variance (wrong CoT reasoning, poor intelligibility, speaker drift), and there is no public benchmark that separates reasoning failures from execution failures.

## Method

The starting point is the official baseline (Figure 2): a Qwen3-0.6B language model with a LoRA adapter that takes the dialogue history text, the target text and global tokens extracted from a reference waveform, then autoregressively emits CoT text tokens, global tokens and semantic speech tokens. The paper does not name the speech tokenizer or the waveform decoder, and it does not change the architecture. All contributions are in data and training, organized into three stages, each built on the best variant of the previous stage.

**Stage 1, continued pre-training on cleaned data (§2.2).** The official data is about 16,000 hours of Chinese and English movie-domain conversational speech. A three-step cascade cleans it: FullSubNet denoising, re-transcription with Qwen3-ASR, and a Qwen3.5-35B-A3B consistency check that discards samples whose CoT annotation contradicts the dialogue history or reasons incoherently. About 82% of the samples survive (§3.1). Both LoRA and full-parameter continued pre-training are tested.

**Stage 2, distillation-based supervised fine-tuning (§2.3).** Because expressive data with CoT annotations is scarce, Qwen3-TTS Voice Design serves as a teacher: given target text and style instructions derived from the dialogue context, it synthesizes 545K samples. Seed-VC then converts their timbre to the target speaker. A four-way filter keeps only samples with Qwen3-ASR WER below 1%, acceptable WavLM-based speaker verification, acceptable UTMOSv2 quality, and style-content agreement as judged by Qwen3-Omni, leaving 127K samples (§3.1). LoRA and full fine-tuning are again compared.

**Stage 3, Context-Aware DPO (CA-DPO) (§2.4).** For each training prompt the model samples K = 10 candidates (CoT plus speech). A cascade builds preference pairs: Qwen3-ASR WER prescreening, a pairwise "king-of-the-hill" tournament judged by Gemini 3.1 on the whole Context → CoT → Speech chain, and a speaker-similarity check that rejects timbre-inconsistent champions. Pairs are (champion, non-champion), with the losers rotated across epochs, yielding 9K pairs. Training uses the standard DPO loss against a frozen reference model plus a weighted negative log-likelihood ("SFT anchor") term on the preferred response, which the authors add to prevent reward hacking. The weight λ, β and other training hyperparameters are not reported.

## Key Results

All results come from one progressive ablation (Table 1) on the authors' own bilingual 500-sample test set, where each sample has up to 5 dialogue turns of history, a target sentence and a reference speaker clip. Metrics are WER, speaker similarity (SIM), UTMOSv2, Gemini 3.1 LLM-as-Judge scores on a 1-5 scale for Context → Instruct reasoning (C2I), Instruct → TTS execution (I2T) and Overall chain consistency, and four human ratings (naturalness N-MOS, similarity S-MOS, context-to-instruct C2I-SC, and instruction-execution I2T-MOS). The number of listeners and the listening-test protocol are not reported. No external systems besides the official baseline are compared.

- **Final system vs. official baseline.** CA-DPO with the SFT anchor improves on the baseline on every reported metric: WER 3.47 to 2.71, SIM 0.365 to 0.373, UTMOS 2.59 to 3.05, Gemini C2I 2.80 to 2.90, I2T 4.41 to 4.61, Overall 3.61 to 3.82, N-MOS 3.12 to 3.25, S-MOS 2.05 to 2.08, C2I-SC 1.90 to 2.14, and I2T-MOS 2.72 to 3.30 (Table 1).
- **Best values within the table.** The final system has the lowest WER (2.71) and the highest UTMOS (3.05), Gemini C2I (2.90), I2T (4.61), Overall (3.82), N-MOS (3.25), C2I-SC (2.14) and I2T-MOS (3.30) of all rows. It does not have the best SIM (CA-DPO without the anchor, 0.378) or the best S-MOS (Stage 2 LoRA, 2.19).
- **Stage 1.** Full-parameter continued pre-training is chosen over LoRA, with lower WER (3.31 vs. 3.39), higher SIM (0.371 vs. 0.364), I2T (4.56 vs. 4.52) and Overall (3.73 vs. 3.70), but LoRA scores higher on Gemini C2I (2.87 vs. 2.83) and I2T-MOS (2.90 vs. 2.85).
- **Stage 2.** LoRA fine-tuning on the distilled data is chosen over full fine-tuning (WER 3.03 vs. 3.18, Overall 3.79 vs. 3.74, S-MOS 2.19 vs. 2.01); the authors attribute this to LoRA limiting over-adaptation to the teacher's distribution. Relative to Stage 1 Full, UTMOS rises from 2.66 to 3.00 and I2T-MOS from 2.85 to 3.15, the largest step-wise gains in the table, while Gemini C2I moves from 2.83 to 2.87.
- **Stage 3.** CA-DPO without the anchor reaches WER 2.98, SIM 0.378 and UTMOS 3.01; adding the anchor gives WER 2.71 and UTMOS 3.05 but lowers SIM to 0.373 and S-MOS from 2.15 to 2.08. The text (§3.3) states that the anchor "further improves all metrics" and that pure DPO degrades UTMOS and WER, but Table 1 shows the SIM and S-MOS drops above, and pure DPO's WER (2.98) and UTMOS (3.01) are both slightly better than the Stage 2 LoRA starting point (3.03 and 3.00).

Most step-to-step differences in the human ratings are smaller than the reported standard deviations (for example N-MOS 3.21 ± 0.24 vs. 3.25 ± 0.19), and no significance tests are reported.

## Novelty Assessment

The contribution is engineering integration on a fixed challenge baseline. The cleaning cascade combines existing tools (FullSubNet, Qwen3-ASR, an LLM consistency checker), the distillation stage pairs an existing instruction TTS teacher with an existing zero-shot VC model and standard automatic filters, and the training objective is standard DPO plus an NLL term on the preferred sample. What is specific to the setting is that preference pairs are ranked along the whole Context → CoT → Speech chain by a pairwise LLM tournament, so the preference signal covers the reasoning text and the speech jointly rather than acoustic quality alone. The evaluation protocol separating reasoning (C2I) from execution (I2T) is a useful diagnostic idea, but the test set is in-house, its release is not stated, and the LLM judge is not validated against the human ratings. The measured gains are modest, and the largest ones come from the distillation stage rather than from the preference optimization the paper names as its method.

## Field Significance

Low — this is a narrow challenge-system report showing that data cleaning, distillation from a stronger instruction TTS model and chain-level preference optimization each give small, cumulative gains to a chain-of-thought context-aware TTS baseline. It adds early evidence on how to train TTS models that infer speaking style from dialogue history, but the gains are small, measured only against the official baseline, and partly scored by the same LLM family that selected the preference pairs. The authors state that the paper is accepted to ISCSLP 2026; this page summarizes the arXiv preprint.

## Claims

- **supports:** Distilling expressive, instruction-following speech from a stronger TTS teacher, with voice conversion to the target speaker and strict automatic filtering, improves instruction execution and perceived quality in context-aware TTS.
  > *Evidence:* LoRA fine-tuning on 127K filtered Qwen3-TTS/Seed-VC samples raises UTMOS from 2.66 to 3.00 and I2T-MOS from 2.85 to 3.15 and lowers WER from 3.31 to 3.03 over the Stage 1 model, the largest step-wise gains in the ablation. *(§2.3, §3.3, Table 1)*
- **refines:** When fine-tuning a speech LM on synthetic distilled data after training on authentic data, parameter-efficient adaptation can outperform full fine-tuning by limiting over-adaptation to the teacher's distribution, while continued pre-training on cleaned authentic data favours full-parameter updates.
  > *Evidence:* In Stage 2, LoRA beats full fine-tuning on WER (3.03 vs. 3.18), SIM, Overall and S-MOS (2.19 vs. 2.01); in Stage 1, full-parameter training is selected over LoRA on WER, SIM, I2T and Overall, though LoRA scores higher on Gemini C2I and I2T-MOS. *(§2.2.3, §2.3.5, Table 1)*
- **supports:** Adding a likelihood term on the preferred sample to DPO for speech-token LMs helps preserve intelligibility and audio quality during preference optimization.
  > *Evidence:* CA-DPO with the SFT anchor reaches WER 2.71 and UTMOS 3.05 against 2.98 and 3.01 without it, with Gemini Overall 3.82 vs. 3.81 and I2T-MOS 3.30 vs. 3.22. *(§2.4.4, §3.3, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** Regularizing preference optimization toward the preferred samples can trade away speaker similarity, so anchor terms do not improve every dimension at once.
  > *Evidence:* Adding the SFT anchor lowers SIM from 0.378 to 0.373 and S-MOS from 2.15 to 2.08, although the text states that the anchor improves all metrics. *(§3.3, Table 1)*
- **complicates:** When the same LLM judge selects preference pairs and scores the evaluation, LLM-as-judge gains on context-aware TTS may overstate real improvements in contextual reasoning.
  > *Evidence:* Gemini 3.1 ranks the DPO candidates on the Context → CoT → Speech chain and also produces the C2I, I2T and Overall evaluation scores; the corresponding human C2I-SC ratings stay low (2.14 out of 5 for the final system) and move within their standard deviations between Stage 2 and Stage 3. *(§2.4.3, §3.2, Table 1)*

## Limitations and Open Questions

> [!warning] Small gains with a shared judge and no external comparison
> Step-to-step differences in the human ratings are mostly within the reported standard deviations, no significance tests or listener counts are given, and the only comparison is the official challenge baseline. Gemini 3.1 both selects the DPO preference pairs and scores the LLM-as-Judge metrics, so the C2I, I2T and Overall gains after Stage 3 are not independent of the training signal.

- Human context-to-instruct ratings remain low for every system (1.90 to 2.14 out of 5), so contextual reasoning stays largely unsolved even after all three stages.
- The test set is in-house and its release is not stated; the LLM-as-Judge scores are not correlated with the human ratings, so the protocol is not validated as a metric.
- Results are pooled over Chinese and English; no per-language breakdown is reported.
- Training details (learning rates, LoRA rank, DPO β and λ, compute) are not reported, and code is not released, which limits reproducibility.
- The pipeline relies on several large proprietary or external models (Gemini 3.1, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Qwen3-Omni, Qwen3-TTS), so its data and preference quality depend on those models' biases.
- Whether the test speakers are unseen in training is not stated.
