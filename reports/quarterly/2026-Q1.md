> [!abstract]
> Q1 research made it easier to change when a system speaks, what it edits, and which aspects
> of a voice it preserves. The quarter also made these capabilities easier to test. Studies
> measured how dialogue policies handle overlap, how voice controls affect correlated attributes,
> and how speech reward models agree with listeners.

> [!note] Scope and retrospective assessment
> Retrospectively assessed on **2026-11-28** using evidence published through **2026-03-31**.
> The baseline ends on **2025-12-31** and the activity window runs from **2026-01-01** through
> **2026-03-31**, inclusive. The snapshot contains 668 baseline papers and 115 Q1 papers.
> Both evidence boundaries use the same retrospective review.

## Executive synthesis

Interactive systems gained more ways to manage turns. Cascaded systems and plug-in state
predictors can decide when to respond or yield while the speech backbone remains fixed.
Training on different dialogue corpora can also change turn-taking behavior. These two results
make timing a property of both the controller and the data that shaped it.
([[2603.09180|DuplexCascade]], [[2603.14877|SoulX-Duplug]], [[2026.iwsds-1.10|Moshi dialogue-corpus study]])

Voice control expanded at two levels. Some systems use discrete masks to regenerate a local
segment with surrounding speech as context. Others change an internal representation or
phonological rule to steer accent. In both cases, evaluation needs to check which characteristics
outside the target change along with it.
([[2603.26364|LLaDA-TTS]], [[2603.05977|Accent activation steering]], [[2603.07550|Rule-based L2 accent synthesis]])

Speech reward models also gained a direct check on their practical utility. GSRM used a learned
naturalness evaluator to train a spoken model and assessed the resulting audio with listeners.
The same study found evaluator collapse under a different training reward, so it matters where
the feedback is applied and how it is validated. ([[2602.13891|GSRM]])

## Changes in assessed knowledge

Twenty of 464 local clusters have no assessed baseline evidence in this snapshot. Eight are
strongly supported at cutoff and 12 are emerging. Among clusters assessed at both boundaries,
three move to strongly supported. One concerns masked diffusion over discrete units for parallel
generation and sequence-length control. The other two concern the conditions under which
gradient reversal reduces attribute leakage and task-trained speech evaluators transfer.
The gradient-reversal conclusion depends on the adversarial target and where it is applied.
The evaluator conclusion depends on the tested domain and rating granularity.
([[2601.22889|DiffuSpeech]], [[2603.26364|LLaDA-TTS]], [[2601.00303|DepFlow]],
[[2603.22252|SelfTTS]], [[2603.01467|Conversational Speech Naturalness Predictor]])

Three reviewed broader claims move from contested at baseline to strongly supported at cutoff.
Their propositions already include the conditions that explain much of the disagreement.
Factorized control can improve target selectivity while leaving residual leakage. Learned
quality predictors can agree better with listeners on matched data and still shift rankings
under new conditions. Automatic judges need validation for the task, attribute, and scoring
granularity at hand. These transitions compare bounded assessments inside one snapshot and
retain the underlying caveats.
([[2603.09120|Emotion-Aware Prefix]], [[2603.01467|Conversational Speech Naturalness Predictor]],
[[2602.13891|GSRM]])

## New methods and capability directions

**Streaming policies and dialogue data.** The snapshot first represents explicit dialogue-state
control families in speech-to-speech and spoken-language modeling. The systems illustrate
cascaded and plug-in approaches to controlling turn transitions. The interaction trade-off
remains: more sensitive and immediate responses can also make a system yield to brief overlaps
that were intended as listener feedback.
([[2603.09180|DuplexCascade]], [[2603.14877|SoulX-Duplug]], [[2602.23266|Dual-track streaming response]])

**Local editing and representation design.** LLaDA-TTS provides emerging evidence for in-place
editing by masking a target region and using context on both sides. CosyEdit provides early
evidence for a separate flow-based approach that conditions on the original speech trajectory.
CodecFlow shows another use of latent flow in bandwidth extension. The relevant quality checks
depend on the purpose: unchanged context for editing and recovered acoustic detail for bandwidth
extension. ([[2603.26364|LLaDA-TTS]], [[2601.05329|CosyEdit]], [[2603.02022|CodecFlow]])

**Accent and expressive control.** Early methods steer accents through directions in parameter
space or phonological rules without target accented recordings. A timing intervention shows that
phoneme substitutions and rhythm can contribute separately to perceived accent. Voice conversion
adds evidence that the point where emotion enters the system affects its interaction with timbre
and intelligibility. ([[2603.07534|Accent Vector]], [[2603.07550|Rule-based L2 accent synthesis]],
[[2603.09120|Emotion-Aware Prefix]])

## Evaluation and evidence-quality changes

Conversational evaluations need multiple turns and enough interaction detail to identify why a
system stopped speaking, whether it interpreted an overlap correctly, and how it recovered.
Single-utterance tests and transcript-only scores omit parts of that behavior. The new
conversation-evaluation clusters in both evaluation concepts are strongly supported at the Q1
cutoff. ([[2026.iwsds-1.10|Moshi dialogue-corpus study]], [[2026.iwsds-1.16|Virtual standardized patients]],
[[2603.14877|SoulX-Duplug]])

Perceptual evaluation still needs task and population coverage. Yoruba speech for accessible
testing provides a setting where a broad benchmark score can obscure the evaluation context.
Conversational naturalness predictors and speech reward models provide scoped automatic evidence,
with listener checks remaining important when scores guide training or deployment.
([[2026.africanlp-main.23|Yoruba TTS for accessible testing]],
[[2603.01467|Conversational Speech Naturalness Predictor]], [[2602.13891|GSRM]])

## Contested, weakened, or unresolved findings

Fourteen local clusters remain contested at cutoff. The synthesis of reinforcement learning for
training speech evaluators retains mixed evidence. A shaped reward can improve the feedback
available to the generator, while an unsuitable reward can collapse the evaluator's predictions.
Report listener outcomes separately from changes in the optimized score.
([[2601.22661|Expressive role-play rewards]], [[2602.13891|GSRM]])

Fine-grained control also has lexical and acoustic limits. The consonant-induced pitch study
found that behavior on familiar words can fail to transfer to unseen words. Research on
synthetic emotional speech finds an acoustic domain gap with human speech, which limits transfer
for recognition. Both are emerging findings with scope tied to the tested conditions.
([[2603.21078|Consonant-induced pitch study]], [[2603.16483|Emotion understanding of synthesized speech]])

## Attention versus evidence caveat

Publication counts describe research attention in this reviewed corpus. Adoption requires evidence
of use across systems or settings. Evidence strength depends on the
quality, independence, and scope of the evaluations. The same system can contribute to several
concepts or method families. In particular, the new masked-diffusion method-family entries in
spoken-language modeling and speech-to-speech both include DiffuSpeech. They describe two uses of
one paper's evidence. ([[2601.22889|DiffuSpeech]])

## Representative reading path

1. **Start with turn control.** Compare the controller and the interaction evaluation in
   [[2603.09180|DuplexCascade]] and [[2603.14877|SoulX-Duplug]].
2. **Compare local editing methods.** Read [[2603.26364|LLaDA-TTS]] and [[2601.05329|CosyEdit]]
   for two ways to preserve context while changing a span.
3. **Separate the sources of accent.** Read [[2603.07534|Accent Vector]] and
   [[2603.07550|Rule-based L2 accent synthesis]] to compare internal steering with explicit timing
   and phonological changes.
4. **Follow an evaluator into training.** Read [[2602.13891|GSRM]] for both listener validation and
   an observed failure in evaluator training.
