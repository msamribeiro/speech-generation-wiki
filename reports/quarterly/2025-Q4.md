> [!abstract]
> Q4 2025 pushed speech generation toward complete interactive systems rather than isolated audio
> components. The quarter's most coherent developments were reasoning and tool use during
> listening, faster but explicitly qualified token generation, intervention-based tests of
> controllability, localized preference optimization, and broader evaluation of domain shift.
> The evidence also made a shared boundary clearer: component-level gains do not guarantee
> end-to-end responsiveness, perceptual quality, or reliable control.

> [!note] About this report
> **Scope and retrospective assessment.** This report covers papers published from **2025-10-01
> through 2025-12-31**, compared with the evidence available through **2025-09-30**. It derives
> from an immutable snapshot containing 668 papers: 497 in the pre-quarter baseline and 171
> published during Q4.

> Counts describe this corpus and its overlapping concept graph, not a complete census of the
> field.

## Executive synthesis

Five developments define the quarter.

First, **spoken systems began to use listening time as computation time**. Several systems reason,
retrieve information, or prepare actions while speech is still arriving instead of waiting for a
turn boundary. This can improve response quality without placing the full reasoning delay after
the user stops, but small chunks and premature actions can reduce accuracy or coherence
([[2510.02044|Stream RAG]], [[2510.02066|Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Streaming Full-Duplex
Dialogue]], [[2510.06917|SHANKS]]).

Second, **latency became an end-to-end scheduling problem**. Q4 work reduced language-model steps
through multi-token, low-rate, patchwise, and speculative generation, while also showing that ASR,
retrieval, language-model inference, acoustic decoding, vocoding, or orchestration can become the
actual critical path. Faster token prediction is therefore useful but does not by itself establish
a faster interactive system ([[2025.emnlp-main.989|VocalNet]], [[2511.13732|Principled
Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative Decoding in Speech]], [[2512.18706|X-Talk]]).

Third, **controllability was tested through interventions rather than reconstruction alone**.
Dedicated streams for content, identity, prosody, emotion, and environment made cross-source
composition more practical. Swapping those factors also exposed residual leakage: improving one
attribute can still alter intelligibility, naturalness, speaker identity, or another control
target ([[2511.12074|MF-Speech]], [[2512.09504|DMP-TTS]],
[[2512.13251|DisCo-Speech]]).

Fourth, **post-training became more local and more explicitly multi-objective**. Token-level
preference signals concentrate updates on the segments responsible for a failure, while multiple
rewards cover more than one quality axis. The same work reinforced the limits of reward models:
domain shift, close rankings, long contexts, and adversarial exploitation can make the optimization
target brittle ([[2510.05799|Data-efficient Targeted Token-level Preference Optimization]],
[[2510.00743|From Scores to Preferences]], [[2512.04552|RRPO]]).

Fifth, **evaluation expanded from output quality to system behavior and validity conditions**.
The quarter added more attention to interactive timing, fairness, accessibility, provenance,
multilingual coverage, and the difference between utterance-level and system-level judgments. The
field-level conclusion that automatic judges require task-, domain-, attribute-, and
granularity-matched human validation became strongly supported. This is a stronger case for
scoped use, not for replacing listeners universally.

## Publication activity during the quarter

The snapshot contains **171 Q4 publications**, producing **666 concept memberships** because one
paper can inform several concepts. The venue distribution is dominated by 130 arXiv records,
followed by 18 EMNLP and 9 NeurIPS papers. The remaining 14 records span ASRU, Interspeech,
workshops, and several regional or specialist venues. Venue counts reflect how this corpus records
publication activity; they do not weight evidence quality.

The most frequent Q4 memberships were subjective evaluation (75), evaluation metrics (61),
zero-shot TTS (59), neural codecs (57), spoken language models (46), speech-to-speech systems
(34), autoregressive codec TTS (31), and multilingual TTS, self-supervised speech, and voice
conversion (30 each). Flow matching contributed 28 memberships, emotion synthesis 25, GAN
vocoders 22, and prosody control and streaming TTS 21 each.

This distribution shows where the integrated corpus paid attention. In particular, evaluation
papers often connect to several generation concepts because they test common properties such as
intelligibility, identity, expressiveness, interaction, and robustness. The resulting membership
counts must not be read as independent discoveries, product adoption, or consensus.

## Changes in assessed knowledge

Q4 added **38 local claim clusters** with no pre-quarter assessment and strengthened **37 existing
clusters** from emerging to strongly supported. Three clusters became or first appeared contested.
These are graph-level assessment changes, not 78 independent discoveries: clusters overlap across
concepts and can share papers.

Several changes form coherent cross-concept groups:

- **Incremental reasoning became an assessed speech-system capability.** New clusters in
  speech-to-speech, spoken language modeling, and streaming TTS support reasoning or tool use
  during listening. The benefit depends on synchronization, chunk size, and whether the system can
  revise an early plan when later speech changes the user's intent ([[2510.05150|Chronological
  Thinking in Full-Duplex Spoken Dialogue]], [[2510.09592|Mind-Paced Speaking]]).

- **Autoregressive acceleration gained explicit boundaries.** Grouped and speculative prediction
  reduced sequential generation steps, and lower-rate codecs reduced language-model sequence
  length. The assessment now separates those gains from complete wall-clock latency and from the
  approximation introduced when multiple acoustic tokens are accepted together
  ([[2510.16718|U-Codec]], [[2511.13732|Principled Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative
  Decoding in Speech]]).

- **Domain-aware codec robustness became strongly supported.** Q4 evidence showed that token and
  reconstruction utility can change with language, bandwidth, noise, sampling rate, synthetic
  versus real speech, and downstream use. Frequency-structured representations and robust
  training help, but a clean-speech reconstruction score is not a sufficient deployment test
  ([[2510.03735|Soft Disentanglement in Frequency Bands]], [[2512.07168|JEPA as a Neural
  Tokenizer]], [[2512.20211|Aliasing-Free Neural Audio Synthesis]]).

- **Low-resource multilingual generation gained new data routes.** Synthetic or pseudo-parallel
  speech enabled direct translation and cross-lingual generation without recorded parallel data.
  These results strengthen feasibility while preserving uneven language, code-switching, accent,
  and identity performance as active limits ([[2511.12690|Improving Direct Persian-English
  Speech-to-Speech Translation]], [[2511.20974|RosettaSpeech]]).

- **Reference-free voice creation emerged as a distinct capability.** Generated speaker vectors,
  identity indices, and multimodal descriptions can produce novel voices without a conventional
  target recording. The capability is useful for controllable creation, but it is not equivalent
  to exact identity cloning: descriptions are one-to-many and may encode demographic priors
  ([[2025.findings-emnlp.1381|UniSpeaker]], [[2511.07135|Generating Novel and Realistic Speakers
  for Voice Conversion]]).

At the broader-claim level, ten of eleven claims retained their status and confidence while
gaining evidence. The one status change concerns **automatic-judge validation**: the scoped
proposition that judge reliability depends on task, domain, attribute, score separation, and
granularity moved from contested/high to strongly supported/high. The snapshot does not support a
blanket claim that automatic judges can replace human evaluation.

## New methods and capability directions

The snapshot records **six method families first represented during Q4** and **158 existing
families that gained papers**. “First represented” means absent at the baseline and present at the
cutoff; it is not a priority or invention claim.

- **Blockwise and speculative autoregression** groups token decisions or accepts approximate
  continuations to reduce serial decoding. Its usefulness depends on within-group dependency
  modeling and a speech-appropriate definition of acceptable equivalence
  ([[2025.emnlp-main.989|VocalNet]], [[2511.13732|Principled Coarse-Grained Acceptance for
  Speculative Decoding in Speech]]).

- **Guidance-based instruction control** strengthens emotion, style, or editing adherence during
  inference. Stronger or repeated guidance can also damage naturalness, intelligibility, identity,
  or untargeted content ([[2510.13293|Cross-modal Consistency Guidance]],
  [[2511.03601|Step-Audio-EditX]]).

- **Reward-optimized prosody control** and **localized token-level alignment** apply feedback to
  fine-grained expressive attributes or erroneous spans. They improve data efficiency and target
  qualities more precisely, while remaining dependent on reward coverage and validation
  ([[2510.05799|Data-efficient Targeted Token-level Preference Optimization]],
  [[2512.04552|RRPO]]).

- **Flow-matching singing generation** extends fast continuous generation into melody- and
  timbre-constrained settings. The singing graph remains comparatively small, and most of its
  conclusions are still emerging; the family should be read as an active direction rather than a
  settled replacement for GAN, diffusion, or hybrid systems.

- **Incremental concurrent reasoning agents** overlap hearing, reasoning, retrieval, and response
  preparation. This changes the latency question from “How fast is the TTS decoder?” to “Which
  pipeline work can safely happen before the user's intent is complete?”

Several established families also broadened substantially, including low-rate and hybrid speech
tokenizers, flow decoders inside codec-language-model pipelines, multilingual flow and
autoregressive systems, full-duplex spoken models, reward-model infrastructure, and evaluation
families for dialogue capability and objective–perceptual divergence.

## Evaluation and evidence-quality changes

Q4 evaluation work made **matched validity** the central question. Learned perceptual predictors
can outperform classical distortion measures in familiar conditions while still changing rank
order across languages, architectures, quality ceilings, domains, or scoring levels. A useful
system-level correlation does not guarantee reliable utterance-level selection
([[2510.19509|Which Evaluation for Which Model?]], [[2511.19734|Evaluating Objective Speech
Quality Metrics for Neural Audio Codecs]]).

Speech and audio-language-model judges gained broader diagnostic roles, including preference
explanations, multi-level zero-shot TTS evaluation, conversational benchmarks, and singing
feedback. Their limitations became more precise at the same time: fine-grained ranking, synthetic
speech domain shift, long-context reasoning, non-verbal cues, and close system comparisons remain
failure points ([[2508.20916|SageLM]], [[2510.20210|Vox-Evaluator]],
[[2512.02523|Generative Multi-modal Feedback for Singing Voice Synthesis Evaluation]]).

Interactive evaluation also widened. Q4 benchmarks test complex conversations, multimodal voice
assistants, multi-turn emotional intelligence, and role-play rather than only isolated response
quality. For streaming and full-duplex systems, first-packet delay, turn timing, backchannels,
floor holding, interruption recovery, cache behavior, and tool overhead are distinct measurements
([[2025.emnlp-main.1160|C3]], [[2025.emnlp-main.1447|MULTIVOX]],
[[2511.00850|MULTI-Bench]]).

The quarter also reinforced responsible evaluation. Pronunciation and accent errors, atypical
voices, low-resource languages, demographic bias, privacy, misuse, and provenance are not safely
summarized by a global naturalness score. Which dimensions are necessary remains task-specific;
multidimensional evaluation does not mean every benchmark needs every metric.

## Contested, weakened, or unresolved findings

Three local findings became or appeared contested.

- **A stronger instruction interpreter does not automatically improve a fixed renderer.** An
  upstream language model can produce a better control plan, but the downstream synthesizer may
  be unable to realize it. Improvements must be measured in the audio, not inferred from the
  intermediate plan.

- **Reward-model training recipes remain unsettled.** Reinforcement learning, nonlinear reward
  shaping, and explicit reasoning can help an evaluator in some settings, but current studies do
  not isolate which component produces robust gains. Reward hacking and domain shift further
  limit generalization ([[2510.00743|From Scores to Preferences]], [[2512.04552|RRPO]]).

- **Learned quality metrics need a narrower claim.** The broad statement that they outperform
  classical metrics became contested because performance depends on domain, scale, architecture,
  and whether the task is ranking systems or selecting utterances. The retained conclusion is
  conditional: learned metrics are often useful in matched settings and require human validation
  when those settings change.

Other unresolved boundaries did not require status reversals. Stronger guidance still trades
control against fidelity; multilingual averages can conceal language-specific failures; generated
speaker representations do not establish exact identity; and faster component decoding does not
establish end-to-end responsiveness. These qualifications are part of the supported claims, not
exceptions to ignore.

## Attention versus evidence caveat

Q4 publication and membership counts measure **attention inside this corpus**. They do not measure
deployment, adoption, market share, independent replication, or scientific consensus. One paper
can inform several concepts and several claim clusters, while a large set of papers can reuse the
same benchmark, training corpus, model family, or automatic evaluator.

Similarly, “strongly supported” is a bounded assessment of a proposition and its qualifications;
it does not mean universally true. New and strengthened cluster counts also depend on graph
coverage and cluster definitions. The immutable snapshot preserves those definitions so later
reports can compare like with like rather than reconstructing Q4 from the future living graph.

## Representative reading path

1. **See how listening time becomes reasoning time.** Read [[2510.02066|Chain-of-Thought Reasoning
   in Streaming Full-Duplex Dialogue]] and [[2510.02044|Stream RAG]] for concurrent reasoning and
   tool use, then compare [[2512.18706|X-Talk]] for the modular end-to-end latency argument.
2. **Understand why fewer token steps are not the whole speed story.** Compare
   [[2025.emnlp-main.989|VocalNet]], [[2510.16718|U-Codec]], and [[2511.13732|Principled
   Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative Decoding in Speech]].
3. **Test controllability by swapping factors.** Use [[2511.12074|MF-Speech]] and
   [[2512.13251|DisCo-Speech]] to see how cross-source composition reveals leakage that aggregate
   reconstruction scores miss.
4. **Follow post-training from local feedback to reward limits.** Read
   [[2510.05799|Data-efficient Targeted Token-level Preference Optimization]], then
   [[2510.00743|From Scores to Preferences]] and [[2512.04552|RRPO]] for reward construction,
   brittleness, and robust optimization.
5. **Finish with evaluation scope.** Compare [[2510.19509|Which Evaluation for Which Model?]],
   [[2511.19734|Evaluating Objective Speech Quality Metrics for Neural Audio Codecs]], and
   [[2025.emnlp-main.1160|C3]] to connect metric validity with interactive system evaluation.
