> [!abstract]
> Q4 2025 moved speech generation toward complete interactive systems, not only separate audio
> components. Key work covered reasoning and tool use during listening, faster token generation,
> direct tests of control, targeted preference optimization, and evaluation across domains. The
> evidence also showed a clear limit: a better component does not always produce a faster, better,
> or more reliable complete system.

> [!note] About this report
> **Scope and retrospective assessment.** This report covers papers published from **2025-10-01
> through 2025-12-31**. It compares them with evidence available through **2025-09-30**. The saved
> snapshot contains 668 papers: 497 from before the quarter and 171 published during Q4.
>
> This is a **retrospective** assessment. Reviewers assessed the fixed evidence set on 2026-11-01.
> “New,” “strengthened,” and “contested” compare the two evidence boundaries under the same review
> rules. These terms do not mean that the conclusions were known by 2025-12-31.
>
> The snapshot covers 23 concepts, 444 local claim clusters, and 11 reviewed broader claims.
> These counts describe this wiki corpus. They are not a complete count of the field.

## Executive synthesis

Five developments stand out.

First, **spoken systems began to think while they listened**. Several systems reason, retrieve
information, or prepare actions before the user stops speaking. This can reduce the delay after a
turn. However, small input chunks and early actions can reduce accuracy or coherence
([[2510.02044|Stream RAG]], [[2510.02066|Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Streaming Full-Duplex
Dialogue]], [[2510.06917|SHANKS]]).

Second, **latency became a problem for the whole system**. Q4 work reduced language-model steps
with multi-token, low-rate, patchwise, and speculative generation. But ASR, retrieval, language-
model inference, acoustic decoding, vocoding, or orchestration can still be the slowest stage.
Faster token prediction is useful, but it does not by itself make the complete system faster
([[2025.emnlp-main.989|VocalNet]], [[2511.13732|Principled
Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative Decoding in Speech]], [[2512.18706|X-Talk]]).

Third, **researchers tested control by changing factors directly**. Separate streams for content,
identity, prosody, emotion, and environment made it easier to combine factors from different
sources. These tests also exposed leakage. A change to one attribute can still change
intelligibility, naturalness, speaker identity, or another target
([[2511.12074|MF-Speech]], [[2512.09504|DMP-TTS]],
[[2512.13251|DisCo-Speech]]).

Fourth, **post-training used more local and more varied feedback**. Token-level preference signals
focus updates on the segments that caused a failure. Multiple rewards cover several quality
dimensions. However, reward models remain fragile under domain shift, close rankings, long
contexts, and adversarial use ([[2510.05799|Data-efficient Targeted Token-level Preference Optimization]],
[[2510.00743|From Scores to Preferences]], [[2512.04552|RRPO]]).

Fifth, **evaluation covered system behavior, not only output quality**. Q4 work gave more attention
to timing, fairness, accessibility, provenance, multilingual coverage, and the difference between
utterance-level and system-level judgments. Evidence now strongly supports a limited use of
automatic judges. They need human validation for the same task, domain, attribute, and level of
detail. The evidence does not support replacing listeners in all settings.

## Publication activity during the quarter

The snapshot contains **171 Q4 publications** and **666 concept memberships**. One paper can belong
to several concepts. The set includes 130 arXiv records, 18 EMNLP papers, and 9 NeurIPS papers.
The other 14 records come from ASRU, Interspeech, workshops, and regional or specialist venues.
These venue counts show publication activity in this corpus. They do not measure evidence quality.

The most frequent Q4 memberships were subjective evaluation (75), evaluation metrics (61),
zero-shot TTS (59), neural codecs (57), spoken language models (46), speech-to-speech systems
(34), autoregressive codec TTS (31), and multilingual TTS, self-supervised speech, and voice
conversion (30 each). Flow matching contributed 28 memberships, emotion synthesis 25, GAN
vocoders 22, and prosody control and streaming TTS 21 each.

These counts show which topics received attention in the integrated corpus. Evaluation papers
often connect to several generation concepts because they test shared properties such as
intelligibility, identity, expression, interaction, and robustness. Do not read membership counts
as independent discoveries, product adoption, or consensus.

## Changes in assessed knowledge

Q4 added **38 local claim clusters** that had no earlier assessment. It also moved **37 existing
clusters** from emerging to strongly supported. Three clusters became contested or first appeared
as contested. These are changes in the claim graph, not 78 independent discoveries. Clusters can
overlap across concepts and share papers.

Several changes appear across more than one concept:

- **Systems can reason or use tools while they listen.** New clusters in speech-to-speech, spoken
  language modeling, and streaming TTS support this capability. Its value depends on timing, chunk
  size, and whether the system can revise an early plan when later speech changes the user's intent
  ([[2510.05150|Chronological
  Thinking in Full-Duplex Spoken Dialogue]], [[2510.09592|Mind-Paced Speaking]]).

- **Autoregressive acceleration has clear limits.** Grouped and speculative prediction reduced
  sequential generation steps. Lower-rate codecs shortened language-model sequences. These gains
  are not the same as lower end-to-end latency. Grouped acceptance can also add approximation error
  ([[2510.16718|U-Codec]], [[2511.13732|Principled Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative
  Decoding in Speech]]).

- **Codec robustness across domains became strongly supported.** Token and reconstruction quality
  can change with language, bandwidth, noise, sampling rate, synthetic versus real speech, and
  downstream use. Frequency-structured representations and robust training help. However, a
  clean-speech reconstruction score is not enough for a deployment test
  ([[2510.03735|Soft Disentanglement in Frequency Bands]], [[2512.07168|JEPA as a Neural
  Tokenizer]], [[2512.20211|Aliasing-Free Neural Audio Synthesis]]).

- **Synthetic data opened new routes for low-resource multilingual generation.** Synthetic or
  pseudo-parallel speech supported direct translation and cross-lingual generation without
  recorded parallel data. Performance still varies by language, code-switching pattern, accent,
  and speaker identity ([[2511.12690|Improving Direct Persian-English
  Speech-to-Speech Translation]], [[2511.20974|RosettaSpeech]]).

- **Systems can create voices without a reference recording.** Generated speaker vectors, identity
  indices, and multimodal descriptions can produce new voices. This helps controllable voice
  creation, but it is not exact identity cloning. A description can match many voices and can
  encode demographic bias
  ([[2025.findings-emnlp.1381|UniSpeaker]], [[2511.07135|Generating Novel and Realistic Speakers
  for Voice Conversion]]).

Ten of the eleven broader claims kept the same status and confidence, but gained evidence. The one
status change concerns **automatic-judge validation**. Evidence moved the scoped claim from
contested/high to strongly supported/high. Judge reliability depends on the task, domain,
attribute, score separation, and level of detail. The snapshot does not support a general claim
that automatic judges can replace human evaluation.

## New methods and capability directions

The snapshot records **six method families that first appear in Q4**. It also records **158 existing
families that gained papers**. “First appear” only means that a family was absent before Q4 and
present at the Q4 cutoff. It does not mean that Q4 invented the method or made it a priority.

- **Blockwise and speculative autoregression** groups token decisions or accepts approximate
  continuations to reduce serial decoding. Its value depends on how the model handles dependencies
  inside each group. It also needs a suitable definition of an acceptable speech match
  ([[2025.emnlp-main.989|VocalNet]], [[2511.13732|Principled Coarse-Grained Acceptance for
  Speculative Decoding in Speech]]).

- **Guidance-based instruction control** can improve emotion, style, or editing during inference.
  Strong or repeated guidance can also harm naturalness, intelligibility, identity, or content
  that the instruction did not target ([[2510.13293|Cross-modal Consistency Guidance]],
  [[2511.03601|Step-Audio-EditX]]).

- **Reward-optimized prosody control** and **localized token-level alignment** apply feedback to
  detailed expressive attributes or incorrect spans. They can use data more efficiently and target
  qualities more precisely. Their success still depends on reward coverage and validation
  ([[2510.05799|Data-efficient Targeted Token-level Preference Optimization]],
  [[2512.04552|RRPO]]).

- **Flow-matching singing generation** brings fast continuous generation to melody- and
  timbre-constrained settings. The singing evidence set is still small, and most findings remain
  emerging. This is an active direction, not an established replacement for GAN, diffusion, or
  hybrid systems.

- **Incremental concurrent reasoning agents** hear, reason, retrieve, and prepare a response at the
  same time. This changes the latency question. The key issue is no longer only TTS decoder speed.
  It is also which work can safely begin before the user's intent is complete.

Several established families also gained substantial evidence. These include low-rate and hybrid
speech tokenizers, flow decoders in codec-language-model pipelines, multilingual flow and
autoregressive systems, full-duplex spoken models, reward-model infrastructure, and evaluation
methods for dialogue capability and disagreement between objective and perceptual scores.

## Evaluation and evidence-quality changes

Q4 evaluation work focused on **matched validity**. Learned perceptual predictors can outperform
classical distortion measures in familiar conditions. But their rankings can change across
languages, architectures, quality ranges, domains, or scoring levels. Good system-level
correlation does not guarantee reliable selection of individual utterances
([[2510.19509|Which Evaluation for Which Model?]], [[2511.19734|Evaluating Objective Speech
Quality Metrics for Neural Audio Codecs]]).

Speech and audio-language-model judges took on more evaluation tasks. These included preference
explanations, multi-level zero-shot TTS tests, conversation benchmarks, and singing feedback.
Their limits also became clearer. They still struggle with fine-grained ranking, synthetic-speech
domain shift, long contexts, non-verbal cues, and close system comparisons
([[2508.20916|SageLM]], [[2510.20210|Vox-Evaluator]],
[[2512.02523|Generative Multi-modal Feedback for Singing Voice Synthesis Evaluation]]).

Interactive evaluation also became broader. Q4 benchmarks test complex conversations, multimodal
voice assistants, multi-turn emotional intelligence, and role-play. They do not test only isolated
responses. For streaming and full-duplex systems, first-packet delay, turn timing, backchannels,
floor holding, interruption recovery, cache behavior, and tool overhead are separate measurements
([[2025.emnlp-main.1160|C3]], [[2025.emnlp-main.1447|MULTIVOX]],
[[2511.00850|MULTI-Bench]]).

Q4 also strengthened the case for responsible evaluation. One global naturalness score cannot
safely represent pronunciation and accent errors, atypical voices, low-resource languages,
demographic bias, privacy, misuse, and provenance. Each task needs a suitable set of dimensions.
This does not mean that every benchmark needs every metric.

## Contested, weakened, or unresolved findings

Three local findings became contested or first appeared as contested.

- **A stronger instruction interpreter does not automatically improve a fixed renderer.** An
  upstream language model can produce a better control plan, but the downstream synthesizer may
  not be able to produce it. Measure the improvement in the audio, not only in the intermediate
  plan.

- **Reward-model training recipes remain unsettled.** Reinforcement learning, nonlinear reward
  shaping, and explicit reasoning can help an evaluator in some settings. Current studies do not
  show which component produces robust gains. Reward hacking and domain shift also limit
  generalization ([[2510.00743|From Scores to Preferences]], [[2512.04552|RRPO]]).

- **Learned quality metrics need a narrower claim.** The broad statement that they outperform
  classical metrics became contested because performance depends on domain, scale, architecture,
  and the evaluation task. Ranking systems is different from selecting individual utterances.
  Learned metrics are often useful in matched settings. They need human validation when those
  settings change.

Other limits did not change a claim's status. Stronger guidance still trades control against
fidelity. Multilingual averages can hide failures in one language. Generated speaker
representations do not prove exact identity. Faster component decoding does not prove faster
end-to-end response. These limits are part of the supported claims.

## Attention versus evidence caveat

Q4 publication and membership counts measure **attention in this corpus**. They do not measure
deployment, adoption, market share, independent replication, or scientific consensus. One paper
can inform several concepts and claim clusters. Many papers can also reuse the same benchmark,
training corpus, model family, or automatic evaluator.

“Strongly supported” applies only to the proposition and limits recorded in this corpus. It does
not mean universally true. Counts of new and strengthened clusters also depend on graph coverage
and cluster definitions. The saved snapshot keeps those definitions fixed. Later reports can then
make a consistent comparison instead of rebuilding Q4 from a newer graph.

## Representative reading path

1. **See how systems think while they listen.** Read [[2510.02066|Chain-of-Thought Reasoning
   in Streaming Full-Duplex Dialogue]] and [[2510.02044|Stream RAG]] for concurrent reasoning and
   tool use. Then read [[2512.18706|X-Talk]] for the end-to-end latency argument.
2. **See why fewer token steps do not guarantee lower latency.** Compare
   [[2025.emnlp-main.989|VocalNet]], [[2510.16718|U-Codec]], and [[2511.13732|Principled
   Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative Decoding in Speech]].
3. **Test control by swapping factors.** Use [[2511.12074|MF-Speech]] and
   [[2512.13251|DisCo-Speech]] to see how cross-source composition reveals leakage. Aggregate
   reconstruction scores can miss this leakage.
4. **Follow post-training from local feedback to reward limits.** Read
   [[2510.05799|Data-efficient Targeted Token-level Preference Optimization]], then
   [[2510.00743|From Scores to Preferences]] and [[2512.04552|RRPO]] for reward construction,
   brittleness, and robust optimization.
5. **Finish with evaluation limits.** Compare [[2510.19509|Which Evaluation for Which Model?]],
   [[2511.19734|Evaluating Objective Speech Quality Metrics for Neural Audio Codecs]], and
   [[2025.emnlp-main.1160|C3]] to connect metric validity with interactive system evaluation.
