> [!abstract]
> Q3 2025 brought substantial work on speech representation, control, streaming, and evaluation.
> No single new architecture took over the field. Instead, the evidence showed that speech quality
> has several dimensions. A gain in one dimension can hide a loss in another.

> [!note] About this report
> **Scope and retrospective assessment.** This report covers papers published from **2025-07-01
> through 2025-09-30**. It compares them with evidence available through **2025-06-30**. The saved
> snapshot contains 497 papers: 133 from before the quarter and 364 published during Q3.
>
> This is a **retrospective** assessment. Reviewers assessed the fixed evidence set on 2026-09-12.
> “Strengthened” means that Q3 evidence changed the later assessment under the same review rules.
> It does not mean that reviewers had reached the conclusion by 2025-09-30.
>
> The snapshot tracks 23 concepts, 406 local claim clusters, and 11 reviewed broader claims. These
> counts describe this wiki corpus. They are not a complete count of the field.

## Executive synthesis

Four developments stand out.

First, **evaluation became more task-specific**. One overall score cannot represent naturalness,
intelligibility, speaker identity, prosody, interaction timing, and domain safety at the same time.
Learned quality models remained useful, but their behavior depended on the domain and comparison
([[2508.00317|Speech Quality Assessment challenges]],
[[2509.18823|Neural Audio Codec Embedding Distances]],
[[2509.24457|Neural-codec metric assessment]]).

Second, **more explicit representations improved control**. New work separated content, speaker,
pitch, emotion, and style through text alignment, orthogonality constraints, and direct signal-
level decomposition. These methods made some controls more reliable. They also showed that
perfectly independent factors remain an unrealistic goal
([[2509.09201|DeCodec]], [[interspeech-2025-0347|PeriodCodec]],
[[interspeech-2025-1397|VibE-SVC]]).

Third, **low latency became a problem for the whole system**. Progress came from causal codecs and
vocoders, compressed streaming latents, parallel or speculative decoding, and distillation. It did
not come only from making one component faster
([[2509.16195|FocalCodec-Stream]], [[2507.14534|Conan]]).

Fourth, **spoken interaction exposed a clear trade-off**. Systems that react quickly can mistake a
brief overlap or listener backchannel for a request to stop. Systems that protect their speaking
turn often respond more slowly to real interruptions. This was the only broader claim whose
assessment changed during Q3. It moved from emerging/low confidence to strongly supported/high
confidence
([[2507.23159|Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5]], [[2509.22243|FLEXI]],
[[interspeech-2025-0739|FD-Bench]]).

## Publication activity during the quarter

The corpus contains **364 Q3 publications**. It includes 185 arXiv records, 123 Interspeech papers,
and 34 ACL papers. The other 22 records span workshops, EMNLP, ASRU, ICML, CoNLL, IWSLT, UNLP, and
ACM Multimedia. These counts show publication activity, not evidence strength. One paper can refine
several concepts, and many papers can test closely related systems.

The 364 papers produce **1,598 concept memberships** because one paper can belong to several
concepts. The most frequent memberships were evaluation metrics (216), subjective evaluation
(145), zero-shot TTS (141), neural codecs (111), self-supervised speech (106), autoregressive codec
TTS (94), spoken language models (83), and disentanglement (79). Voice conversion (77), flow
matching and prosody control (73 each), and emotion synthesis (65) were also prominent.

These counts show **research attention in the corpus**. They do not show that a method was adopted
in products, became standard practice, or gained stronger evidence.

## Changes in assessed knowledge

Q3 added **117 local claim clusters** that had no earlier assessment. It also moved **96 existing
clusters** from emerging to strongly supported. These are changes in the claim graph, not 213
independent discoveries. Related evidence can affect several concepts, and one paper can support
more than one cluster.

Several changes appeared across more than one concept:

- **Layered models made speech representations easier to understand.** Codec and self-supervised
  representations often mix linguistic content with speaker and prosodic detail. Q3 evidence
  supported more explicit separation. It also showed that aggressive separation can harm
  reconstruction, identity, or intelligibility
  ([[2507.09070|Speaker-decoupled content representations]],
  [[2509.09201|DeCodec]]).

- **Expressive control became more detailed.** Explicit emotion conditioning and controls at the
  word, phoneme, frame, or sub-sentence level moved from tentative to strongly supported. Stronger
  expression often reduced naturalness, intelligibility, or speaker identity
  ([[2509.20378|Fine-Grained Emotional Speech Synthesis]],
  [[2509.25416|Emotion-Aligned Diffusion TTS]],
  [[interspeech-2025-2586|Spotlight-TTS]]).

- **Streaming gains became clearer.** Causal components approached offline quality. Distillation
  recovered some losses from causal models. Parallel or speculative decoding reduced serial token
  generation ([[2509.16195|FocalCodec-Stream]], [[2507.14534|Conan]]). Reports still need to
  separate first-audio delay, chunk timing, and total computation.

- **Preference optimization moved beyond one model family.** Continuous diffusion and
  autoregressive-diffusion systems joined earlier discrete-token work. The snapshot supports
  targeted gains in intelligibility, quality, or emotional alignment. Results still depend on
  preference-pair construction, reward coverage, regularization, and stopping time
  ([[2025.acl-long.598|Preference Alignment for Zero-shot TTS]],
  [[2509.18928|Speech DPO]], [[interspeech-2025-0063|RLHF for Diffusion TTS]]).

Ten of the eleven broader claims kept the same status and confidence. A busy quarter changed many
detailed assessments without overturning the main picture of the field.

## New methods and capability directions

The snapshot records **19 method families that first appear in Q3**. It also records **148 existing
families that gained papers**. “First appear” only means that a family was absent before Q3 and
present at the Q3 cutoff. It does not mean that Q3 invented every underlying idea.

The new families form several groups:

- **Faster iterative generation:** This group includes adversarial and distilled diffusion,
  bridges between diffusion and flow models, and continuous autoregressive flow heads. F5-TTS
  represents the quarter's wider flow-matching activity ([[2025.acl-long.313|F5-TTS]]).

- **More explicit factor separation:** Methods used ASR- or text-aligned content isolation,
  mutual-information minimization, orthogonality constraints, and signal-level prosody
  decomposition. Marco-Voice,
  DeCodec, LinearVC, DiffEmotionVC, and PeriodCodec show different versions of this design space
  ([[2508.02038|Marco-Voice]], [[2509.09201|DeCodec]],
  [[interspeech-2025-0438|LinearVC]], [[interspeech-2025-1210|DiffEmotionVC]],
  [[interspeech-2025-0347|PeriodCodec]]).

- **Contextual and privacy-aware control:** This group includes situated or context-conditioned
  TTS, prompt-based identity control, and privacy-aware prompting. Benchmark work also showed that
  listener judgments of instruction following can differ from automatic-judge scores
  ([[2509.13989|Instruction-Perception Gap]]).

- **Broader application settings:** Work covered diffusion-based emotional generation,
  linguistic-context prosody prediction, clinical and accessibility evaluation, watermark and
  provenance evaluation, and diffusion conditioned on self-supervised speech features
  ([[2509.25416|Emotion-Aligned Diffusion TTS]],
  [[2509.19231|Finding My Voice]], [[2507.03887|Traceable TTS]],
  [[2509.19812|Efficient Speech Watermarking]]).

- **New streaming and singing combinations:** Work included VAE-compressed streaming latents,
  GAN-based singing conversion, and hybrid multimodal singing systems. PerformSinger used
  synchronized visual cues,
  while Vevo2 joined speech and singing control in one framework
  ([[2509.22718|PerformSinger]], [[2508.16332|Vevo2]]).

Q3 also expanded multilingual and adaptation routes. Shared multilingual models, dialect-specific
expert routing, and lightweight adapters offered different balances between coverage and
specialization ([[2508.14049|MahaTTS]], [[2509.22727|DiaMoE-TTS]],
[[2508.18006|Lightweight TTS Adapters]]).

## Evaluation and evidence-quality changes

Evaluation had the most activity and produced some of the clearest changes in the evidence.

Evidence became strong that **automatic MOS predictors can rank systems differently from
listeners**. Disagreement was most important near a quality ceiling, outside the evaluator's
training domain, or with unusual generation systems
([[2508.00317|Speech Quality Assessment challenges]],
[[2509.24457|Neural-codec metric assessment]]). Evidence also showed that embedding-based speaker-
similarity scores can disagree with perceived identity
([[interspeech-2025-0438|LinearVC]],
[[interspeech-2025-1726|Voice Reconstruction for Assistive Communication]]).

For neural codecs, Q3 strengthened two practical conclusions. First, clean-speech reconstruction
scores do not describe behavior under noise, reverberation, bitrate changes, or domain shift.
Second, good reconstruction scores do not show how useful the tokens will be for a downstream
generator ([[2509.18823|Neural Audio Codec Embedding Distances]],
[[2509.24457|Neural-codec metric assessment]]).

Clinical and accessibility work became a separate evaluation family. Systems and metrics tuned on
typical speech need separate validation before use with dysarthric speech, neuroprosthetic
synthesis, audiological assessment, or assistive communication
([[interspeech-2025-1334|MiSTR]],
[[interspeech-2025-1726|Voice Reconstruction for Assistive Communication]],
[[interspeech-2025-2595|Voice Cloning for Audiological Assessment]],
[[interspeech-2025-2679|Dysarthric Voice Reconstruction]]).

Watermark and provenance evaluation also became a separate family. The papers studied
traceability, resistance to unauthorized cloning, and watermarking for autoregressive generation.
They treated provenance as a design requirement, not an afterthought
([[2507.03887|Traceable TTS]], [[2509.19812|Efficient Speech Watermarking]],
[[interspeech-2025-1993|Watermark-Aware Codecs]],
[[interspeech-2025-2328|Watermark for Autoregressive Speech Generation]]).

## Contested, weakened, or unresolved findings

The snapshot records **12 local clusters that became contested or first appeared as contested**.
“Contested” does not mean false. It means that the evidence supports a conditional answer, not a
simple yes or no.

- **Automatic judges have a bounded role.** LLM and audio-language-model judges can approximate
  some human ratings. Their use became contested for detailed prosody, paralinguistic qualities,
  and close comparisons. The Prosody Diversity Benchmark shows a useful automatic method. It also
  limits broader claims that automatic judges can replace listeners
  ([[2509.19928|Prosody Diversity Benchmark]],
  [[2509.13989|Instruction-Perception Gap]]).

- **Voice-cloning metrics need a narrower interpretation.** The claim that automatic
  intelligibility and speaker-similarity measures do not fully capture perceived cloning quality
  moved from strongly supported to contested. The mismatch did not disappear. Instead, Q3 evidence
  showed that some purpose-built metrics can measure specific dimensions well. Validate each
  metric for its intended dimension and domain.

- **Cross-lingual leakage varies by system and test.** Language, accent, and prosody can leak from
  a reference voice. The amount and type of leakage depend on representation, conditioning, and
  evaluation. New results narrowed a claim that had previously been too broad
  ([[2025.acl-long.598|Preference Alignment for Zero-shot TTS]],
  [[2508.14049|MahaTTS]], [[2509.22727|DiaMoE-TTS]]).

- **Disentanglement is not one solved operation.** Gradient reversal often reduces attribute
  leakage, but its effect on perceived quality varies. Generic latent methods also struggle to
  isolate pitch. Direct periodic or F0-aware methods can do better
  ([[interspeech-2025-0347|PeriodCodec]], [[interspeech-2025-1397|VibE-SVC]]).

- **Emotional coherence remains incomplete.** Systems improved at applying explicit and local
  emotion controls. Maintaining a suitable emotional path across realistic dialogue remained
  emerging. Control of one utterance does not prove robust conversational behavior.

No local claim moved to an explicit “weakened” status. The schema records reversals through changes
in status and confidence instead. The contested changes above are the main negative or limiting
evidence from the quarter.

## Attention versus evidence caveat

Publication count, concept membership, evidence strength, and adoption answer different questions:

- **Publication count** shows how many eligible records appeared during the quarter.
- **Concept membership** shows where papers connect to the wiki's concepts. Memberships overlap.
- **Assessment status** comes from reviewed supporting, contradicting, and refining evidence. It
  does not come from raw paper counts.
- **Adoption** needs evidence of deployment, use, or standardization. This snapshot does not measure
  those factors.

The 216 Q3 evaluation-metric memberships show strong attention in this corpus. They do not show
that evaluation improved in proportion to the count. The 19 newly represented method families
show more variety, not 19 established winners.
