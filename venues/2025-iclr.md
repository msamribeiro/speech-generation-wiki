## Overview

The five ICLR 2025 papers in the corpus span three distinct challenges in speech generation: waveform synthesis quality, codec representation efficiency, and long-form generation.

[[iclr-2025-tQ1PmLfPBL]] (PeriodWave) is the first flow-matching neural vocoder with an explicit multi-period generator, achieving better pitch accuracy and train-inference mismatch robustness than GAN vocoders while training in one-third the time.

[[iclr-2025-cuFzE8Jlvb]] eliminates RVQ quantization from autoregressive TTS entirely, replacing it with a GMM-VAE that produces continuous latent frames; a 51.5M-parameter GMM-LM trained on this continuous representation outperforms VALL-E across all metrics.

[[iclr-2025-dGSOn7sdWg]] (SyllableLM) compresses speech into syllable-rate units (5–8 Hz, ~60 bps) via loss-surface segmentation and iterative SSL distillation, enabling a 90M-parameter speech LM to match systems more than 100x larger on spoken language modeling benchmarks.

[[iclr-2025-868masI331]] (HALL-E) addresses the minute-long generation bottleneck in autoregressive codec TTS: Multi-Resolution Requantization post-trains any RVQ codec to 8 Hz via hierarchical distillation, and HALL-E adapts VALL-E to predict these tokens — together enabling stable synthesis of up to 180 seconds in a single inference pass.
