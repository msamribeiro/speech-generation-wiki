## Method-Family Evidence

### Pure Flow-Matching Systems

Models that use flow matching as the sole generative backbone, typically operating directly on mel spectrograms or a compact latent space, with no autoregressive component. This is now the largest and most heterogeneous family in the corpus: alongside the original OT-CFM mel-spectrogram TTS/VC pattern, it has absorbed emotion/environment/dialect control work, cross-lingual and low-resource adaptation, compact-architecture efficiency work, interpretability studies of FM DiT activations, and a first discrete-space flow-matching variant. All members share the property of achieving sub-linear RTF scaling with utterance length and requiring no separate AR token stage.

Family questions:
- What is the minimum data volume at which pure OT-CFM mel-spectrogram systems achieve competitive intelligibility, and can architectural changes (e.g. DiT vs. U-Net) shift this threshold?
- Does the Sway Sampling step scheduling principle generalise across all pure FM architectures, or is it specific to the DiT + ConvNeXt design of F5-TTS?
- This family now spans mel-spectrogram TTS, VC (Seed-VC), and latent-diffusion variants (MegaTTS 3) that use rectified flow rather than the canonical OT-CFM objective; does the family's shared 'no autoregressive component' property predict shared failure modes and scaling behaviour across these variants, or do task and target-representation differences dominate?
- A discrete flow-matching variant defined over factorized token subspaces (rather than continuous mel or latent targets) has now appeared as a single paper in this family; is discrete-space FM a viable third branch alongside continuous mel-spectrogram and latent FM, or a narrow niche specific to prosody/acoustic factorization?
- Interpretability findings (emotion encoded as linear directions in DiT activation space) and control findings (dual CFG for speaker/environment, early-injection dialect conditioning) have emerged independently across several papers in this family; do they reflect a single underlying geometric structure of FM DiT representations, or are they attribute-specific coincidences?

### Autoregressive LM + Flow-Matching Acoustic Model

Two-stage pipelines where an autoregressive language model generates discrete semantic or style tokens, and a separate flow-matching model decodes those tokens into continuous speech. The AR stage handles content and prosody at a semantic level; the FM stage handles fine-grained acoustic detail. This architecture trades the latency of AR decoding for stronger control over speaker style and paralinguistic attributes. Recent additions to this family increasingly focus their novelty on richer emotion/style conditioning of the AR-to-FM interface rather than on the FM stage itself.

### Continuous Autoregressive Models with Flow-Matching/Diffusion Heads

Single-stage architectures that generate continuous acoustic representations (mel frames, VAE latents, or fixed-length token blocks) autoregressively, using a flow-matching or diffusion sampling head as the per-step or per-block output distribution, rather than routing through a separate discrete-token acoustic decoder as in the ar_fm_hybrid pattern. The distinguishing property is a single jointly-trained model in which the flow-matching component is the output layer of the AR model itself, not a downstream module. Across the four papers in this family, this pattern consistently reduces AR sequence length or per-token NFE relative to discrete-token AR baselines without sacrificing — and sometimes improving — quality.

- Every paper in this family reports a non-monotonic relationship between the flow-matching/diffusion head's NFE (or FSQ/latent dimensionality) and downstream quality, with excess computation degrading rather than improving results; is there a shared theoretical account of why continuous AR heads saturate or degrade at high NFE, unlike the acceleration literature's typical framing of NFE reduction as a pure quality-speed trade-off?
- This family is new and entirely composed of 2025 papers; whether it displaces the two-stage discrete-token ar_fm_hybrid pattern, coexists as a latency-optimised alternative, or is absorbed into it (e.g. by hybridising continuous and discrete stages) is unresolved.

### Flow Matching Applied to Auxiliary (Non-Acoustic) Latent Targets

Systems that apply the flow-matching or rectified-flow objective not to the primary acoustic output (mel-spectrogram, waveform, or codec tokens) but to a compact auxiliary attribute space — prosody latents, stochastic prosody/duration/pitch predictors, or text-conditioned speaker embeddings — as a modular sub-component feeding into an otherwise separate, often non-FM, acoustic pipeline. This is a small but consistent pattern across three independently-authored 2025 papers, suggesting flow matching's simulation-free training and diverse-sampling properties transfer cleanly from full acoustic generation to lower-dimensional conditioning targets.
