## Executive Summary

> [!abstract]
> RLHF for speech synthesis encompasses post-training techniques — principally DPO, GRPO, and differentiable reward optimization — that align pre-trained speech generation models with human preferences on intelligibility, speaker similarity, naturalness, and expressiveness. As of 2026, multi-dimensional GRPO with composite reward models has become the production-standard post-training paradigm, replacing simple WER-only reward models and extending alignment across AR, flow-matching, diffusion, and masked generative architectures. The key open question is whether iterative alignment can exceed the base model's capability ceiling.

## Current Status

established — RLHF post-training is now a standard component of every major production TTS system (CosyVoice 2, Seed-TTS, GLM-TTS, Qwen3-TTS, Fish Audio S2); the field has moved from PPO-style reward modeling toward DPO and GRPO with composite multi-dimensional rewards, and from AR-only to architecture-general alignment frameworks. Adoption is high and growing; the primary research frontier is reward model design, distribution shift prevention, and multi-objective trade-off resolution.

## Why This Matters

Standard TTS pre-training maximizes likelihood on high-quality paired data, but this does not explicitly penalize failure modes such as word insertions, omissions, mispronunciations, or unnatural prosody. Pre-training also generalizes poorly to out-of-distribution conditions (tongue twisters, code-switching, cross-lingual synthesis) that are hard to collect at scale. Preference alignment allows targeted post-training on challenging scenarios using paired samples with relative preferences — even synthetic pairs, where the positive sample is generated by a stronger model or under better sampling conditions, can yield large improvements.

Preference alignment is computationally efficient relative to pre-training: a single DPO fine-tuning pass on a 250K-pair dataset on 8 H100 GPUs improves intelligibility across four domains simultaneously ([[2025.acl-long.598]]).

## Core Idea

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) for speech synthesis refers to post-training techniques that align a pre-trained speech generation model with human preferences, rather than maximizing a fixed supervised loss on reference audio. The most common practical variant is Direct Preference Optimization (DPO; Rafailov et al., 2023), which sidesteps explicit reward model training by reformulating the preference alignment objective in terms of log-likelihood ratios between a trained policy and a frozen reference policy. RLAIF (AI feedback) variants replace human annotations with automatic metrics (WER, automatic MOS predictors) or LLM-generated judgments to scale data collection.

In TTS, a preference pair (x, y_w, y_l) consists of a conditioning prompt x (text + reference speech for zero-shot TTS), a preferred synthesis y_w, and a dispreferred synthesis y_l. The model is trained to increase the relative probability of y_w over y_l, subject to a KL-divergence regularizer that keeps the policy close to the reference model.

## Methods and Variants

**DPO for autoregressive TTS (vanilla DPO).** The standard DPO loss applied to AR codec language models (VALL-E family, ARS). Log-probability of the entire codec token sequence is used. Prior work: SpeechAlign (Zhang et al., 2024), Koel-TTS (Hussain et al., 2025), and [[2025.acl-long.598]] (ARS).

**Differentiable reward optimization for codec LMs (DiffRO).** [[interspeech-2025-0704]] addresses two problems unique to codec LM-based TTS: (1) decoding codec tokens to audio for reward computation is expensive; (2) audio diversity from the same text is low, making pair construction hard. DiffRO trains a multi-task reward model (MTR) that predicts rewards directly from codec token sequences, bypassing the FM+vocoder stages entirely. Gumbel-Softmax makes the discrete token sampling differentiable, enabling direct backpropagation without policy gradient loops. The MTR model is jointly trained on ASR (WER), SER (emotion), SQA (MOS), and audio events, enabling multi-dimensional reward signals. ASR-reward DiffRO achieves WER 0.78% on seed-tts-eval zh (halving the CosyVoice 2.0 baseline of 1.56%), and MTR-reward DiffRO enables controllable emotion generation and multilingual WER improvement (Japanese WER: 9.13% → 6.36%) without explicit multilingual RL data.

**RLHF for diffusion TTS (DLPO).** [[interspeech-2025-0063]] is the first RLHF framework specifically designed for non-autoregressive diffusion TTS. The key insight is that existing image-domain RL methods (DDPO, DPOK, KLinR) fail for audio because they either use score-function estimators (which cannot handle audio's sequential complexity) or KL regularizers (which limit task-specific improvements). DLPO adds the original DDPM noise-prediction loss as a reward penalty: J_DLPO = E[-α·r(x₀,c) - β·||ε̃-εθ||²]. This prevents temporal incoherence and maintains the base model's probabilistic structure. Applied to WaveGrad 2R, DLPO achieves UTMOS 3.65 vs. 2.90 baseline, approaching ground-truth NISQA (4.02 vs. 4.37), while maintaining intelligibility (WER 1.2%).

**DPO for flow-matching models (DPO-FM).** Introduced by [[2025.acl-long.598]], derived by expressing the DPO log-ratio in terms of predicted vs. reference velocity fields at sampled timesteps t. The loss penalizes the model for predicting velocities that push the preferred sample further from the target than the reference policy, relative to the dispreferred sample. Applied to F5-TTS ([[2025.acl-long.313]]).

**DPO for masked generative models (DPO-MGM).** Also introduced by [[2025.acl-long.598]], extending DPO to non-autoregressive masked prediction models (MaskGCT, NaturalSpeech 3). The policy probability is the sum of log-probabilities over masked token positions.

**RLHF via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization).** Multiple 2025–2026 systems use GRPO instead of PPO or DPO. GLM-TTS [[2512.14291]] applies 4-reward GRPO (CER, SIM, emotion, laughter) with dynamic sampling for reward hacking prevention. Fish Audio S2 [[2603.08823]] uses Dr.GRPO (no value network) with a composite reward (intelligibility + quality + speaker similarity) where reward models are reused from the data pipeline. Qwen3-TTS [[2601.15621]] combines DPO for intelligibility with GRPO for stability and instruction-following capability. Vevo2 [[2508.16332]] uses multi-objective GRPO with intelligibility and prosody similarity rewards for unified speech+singing alignment.

**Dual-purpose data pipeline for RL.** Fish Audio S2 [[2603.08823]] addresses the distribution shift problem in RL reward design by using the same quality and ASR models as both data filters during pretraining and reward signals during RL post-training — by construction, the reward models never penalize outputs that are reasonable under the pretraining distribution.

**Emotion DPO / Emotion reward in GRPO.** GLM-TTS [[2512.14291]] includes an emotion reward in its GRPO framework; Seed-TTS [[2406.02430]] uses a separate RL-SER variant with speech emotion recognition accuracy as the reward signal. These reveal a pronunciation/emotion trade-off: optimizing emotion reward degrades CER and SIM, requiring careful reward weighting.

## Major Claims

Claims are generalised propositions aggregated from paper evidence. The full claim registry with supporting paper lists is in `wiki/concepts/_evidence/rlhf-speech.yaml`.

### Strongly Supported

- DPO-based preference alignment reduces word error rate by 31–46% relative across multiple TTS architectures without degrading speaker similarity, when constructed from WER-ranked synthetic preference pairs.
  Supporting: [[2025.acl-long.598]], [[2412.10117]], [[interspeech-2025-0704]]

- Multi-dimensional GRPO with composite reward models (intelligibility + quality + speaker similarity) outperforms single-objective RL, as single-objective optimization systematically degrades unconstrained dimensions.
  Supporting: [[2508.16332]], [[2512.14291]], [[2603.08823]]

- Preference pairs constructed from weaker generator outputs can improve stronger target models (weak-to-strong generalization), provided the pairs capture relative quality distinctions that generalize beyond the source model's failure modes.
  Supporting: [[2025.acl-long.598]]

### Emerging

- Differentiable reward computation from codec token sequences (bypassing audio decoding) is a viable and efficient alternative to waveform-domain reward models for AR codec LM-based TTS.
  Supporting: [[interspeech-2025-0704]], [[2412.10117]]

- RLHF is viable for diffusion-domain TTS when the original denoising loss is included as a reward penalty, preventing temporal incoherence that standard RL regularizers cannot handle.
  Supporting: [[interspeech-2025-0063]]

- Iterative self-alignment (training on model-generated preference data in successive rounds) yields diminishing returns that suggest base model capability is a ceiling for alignment gains.
  Supporting: [[2025.acl-long.598]]

### Contested

> [!warning]
> Whether using the same models for data filtering and RL reward evaluation (dual-purpose pipeline) eliminates distribution shift or introduces self-reinforcing bias remains unresolved.
> Supporting: [[2603.08823]] / Contradicting: (no direct contradicting evidence in corpus)

## Relationship to Other Concepts

### Extends or Builds On
- [[evaluation-metrics]] — RLHF reward models are operationalizations of evaluation metrics (WER, MOS, SPK-SIM); the quality of the alignment is bounded by the validity and coverage of the metrics used as rewards
- [[subjective-evaluation]] — human preference labels are the ground truth for RLHF pair construction; automatic metrics (UTMOS, WER) are proxies for human judgments, and alignment quality depends on how well these proxies correlate with human preference
- [[zero-shot-tts]] — the primary setting where RLHF has been applied; preference pairs are conditioned on text + reference speech, targeting zero-shot generalization to unseen speakers and challenging text domains

### Competes With
- [[flow-matching]] — supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on positive samples only is simpler but consistently weaker than full DPO in ablations ([[2025.acl-long.598]] Table 5): SFT on regular positive samples moves average WER 24.47 → 22.32 for ARS, while DPO moves it to 15.41. Negative pair contrast provides the additional discriminative signal. Data augmentation (adding hard cases to pre-training) is an alternative but requires high-quality reference audio for each hard case, which is difficult to obtain for tongue twisters or rare code-switching scenarios. Preference pairs need only relative judgments and can be produced automatically via WER comparison.

### Commonly Paired With
- [[instruction-conditioned-tts]] — instruction-following TTS systems increasingly combine RLHF post-training with instruction conditioning; GRPO with rule-based rewards ([[2601.15621]]) is used specifically to improve instruction-following stability
- [[spoken-language-model]] — SLM-based TTS systems (VALL-E family, CosyVoice) are the most common recipients of RLHF post-training; the codec token representation is directly compatible with DPO log-probability computation

## Representative Papers

### Foundational
- [[2406.02430]] — first large-scale demonstration of RL reward optimization (PPO-style) for TTS intelligibility and emotion control; identifies reward hacking (over-slow speech) as a fundamental challenge
- [[2412.10117]] — introduces differentiable ASR reward via Gumbel-softmax through FSQ codec indices, establishing the connection between codec-LM AR decoding and differentiable reward optimization

### Influential
- [[2025.acl-long.598]] — introduces INTP (250K preference pairs), derives DPO for flow-matching and masked generative models, demonstrates weak-to-strong generalization and 31–46% relative WER reduction across five TTS architectures
- [[interspeech-2025-0704]] — DiffRO: codec-token-level differentiable reward computation bypassing audio decoding; multi-task reward model enabling simultaneous ASR, emotion, and MOS optimization
- [[2512.14291]] — GLM-TTS: 4-reward GRPO with dynamic sampling; reveals the pronunciation/emotion reward trade-off and introduces hierarchical regularization for multi-objective RL

### Recent Highlights
- [[2603.08823]] — Fish Audio S2: dual-purpose pipeline where pretraining data filters double as RL reward models, addressing distribution shift at 5M-hour scale with Dr.GRPO
- [[2601.15621]] — Qwen3-TTS: sequential DPO + GRPO post-training combining targeted intelligibility alignment with instruction-following stability

### Cautionary / Negative Evidence
- [[interspeech-2025-0063]] — DLPO shows that standard image-domain RL methods fail for diffusion TTS due to temporal incoherence; validates the necessity of architecture-specific RL formulations rather than direct transfer from image generation

## Open Questions

- Does the diminishing return in iterative alignment saturate at the base model's inherent capability, or can curriculum design break through the ceiling?
- How well do WER-ranked synthetic pairs correlate with human intelligibility preferences for code-switching and cross-lingual synthesis?
- Can DPO-FM and DPO-MGM be extended to diffusion-based TTS (NaturalSpeech 2, DiTTo-TTS)?
- Is there a risk of anti-spoofing degradation from intelligibility alignment?
- GLM-TTS [[2512.14291]] shows emotion reward trades off against CER in GRPO; is this trade-off universal, and can reward scheduling or curriculum resolve it?
- Fish Audio S2 [[2603.08823]] uses the same models for data filtering and RL rewards; does this introduce a self-reinforcing bias, or does the distributional overlap actually help generalization?
- Vevo2 [[2508.16332]] shows that single-objective RL degrades the complementary dimension (intelligibility-only training drops melody from 65% to 50%); is multi-objective RL the general solution, or are there tasks where objectives are genuinely incompatible?
- DiffRO [[interspeech-2025-0704]] shows that codec-level reward prediction (MTR model) improves WER but has limited impact on audio MOS because FM+vocoder largely denoise the output; can reward models be designed to operate on the FM's target space rather than the LM's codec space?
- DLPO [[interspeech-2025-0063]] is validated only on single-speaker LJSpeech; does the diffusion-loss penalty scale to multi-speaker and zero-shot diffusion TTS systems?

## Trend Summary

2023–2024: RLHF via reward model (PPO-style) first applied to TTS intelligibility in Seed-TTS [[2406.02430]] and CosyVoice 2 [[2412.10117]]. DPO first applied to TTS by SpeechAlign (NeurIPS 2024). 2025: [[2025.acl-long.598]] introduces multi-model cross-comparison pairs and architecture-general DPO (FM + MGM extensions); weak-to-strong generalization; intelligibility improvements extend to code-switching. GLM-TTS [[2512.14291]] demonstrates multi-reward GRPO with laughter modeling; reveals pronunciation/emotion trade-off. Vevo2 [[2508.16332]] extends GRPO to multi-objective alignment spanning intelligibility and prosody. 2026: Fish Audio S2 [[2603.08823]] introduces the dual-purpose pipeline concept (same models filter data and serve as RL rewards), addressing distribution shift. Qwen3-TTS [[2601.15621]] combines DPO + GRPO in a sequential post-training pipeline at 5M-hour scale. The post-training paradigm for TTS has converged on GRPO with multi-dimensional rewards as the standard; DPO is now used primarily for targeted intelligibility alignment. Interspeech 2025: DiffRO [[interspeech-2025-0704]] introduces differentiable token-level reward computation (Gumbel-Softmax), bypassing audio decoding for reward evaluation; DLPO [[interspeech-2025-0063]] demonstrates that RLHF is viable for diffusion-domain TTS via task-specific loss regularization — expanding the RLHF paradigm beyond codec LM-based systems.
